## 1. The question

A fall velocity block ends in January. The season runs February to June. What does the lifting have to be, between those dates, for an 85+ arm to keep what he built — and what happens if it isn't?

Every strength coach in the sport answers this with some version of **"cut the volume, keep the intensity"** or **"cut both, lift three or four times a week at lower intensity."** Those are different prescriptions and they come from the same literature. §2 is what that literature says. §3 is the part of it that applies to a thrower, which is almost none.

## 2. The source everybody is quoting, read at source

**Spiering BA, Mujika I, Sharp MA, Foulis SA (2021). "Maintaining Physical Performance: The Minimal Dose of Exercise Needed to Preserve Endurance and Strength Over Time." *J Strength Cond Res* 35(5):1449–1458. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003964.** ✅ **READ IN FULL.**

The abstract's strength sentence, verbatim: *"Strength and muscle size (at least in younger populations) can be maintained for up to 32 weeks with as little as 1 session of strength training per week and 1 set per exercise, as long as exercise intensity (relative load) is maintained."*

### 2.1 Design and scope
- A **brief narrative review**, not systematic, not a meta-analysis. PubMed + Web of Science, standard search criteria.
- Inclusion rule: reduced-training periods **>4 weeks**, specifically to separate "reduced training research" from **tapering research** (short-term volume cuts aimed at shedding fatigue before competition). The taper literature is cited but excluded from the tables.
- Outcomes: **VO2max, 1RM strength, maximal isometric strength (MIS), muscle size/CSA.** Nothing else.

### 2.2 The four things that disqualify the number for a pitcher

| # | Finding | Verbatim from the paper |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Samples were untrained general-population adults** (F-708) | *"except where specifically indicated, the studies referenced in this section used relatively untrained participants of various ages from the general population. Therefore, caution is warranted when attempting to extrapolate these findings to special populations (i.e., athletes and military personnel)"* |
| 2 | **Power and RFD are out of scope** (F-709) | *"This review paper will not focus on maintaining other adaptations to training (e.g., muscle power, agility, flexibility), as relatively few studies have investigated the minimal dose of exercise necessary to maintain these variables"* |
| 3 | **The "1 set" half was never isolated** (F-710) | *"To our knowledge, no research has isolated the independent effects of reduced exercise volume (number of sets) on the maintenance of strength and muscle size"* |
| 4 | **Reduced intensity, for strength, has one study** (F-711) | *"To our knowledge, only one study has investigated the effects of reduced intensity on the maintenance of muscle strength"* → Morehouse 1967, *Res Q* 38:449–456, isometric |

Plus two more disclaimers: the abstract's *"Insufficient data exists to make specific recommendations for athletes or military personnel"*, and the Practical Applications' *"should be treated with the utmost caution because of the limited number of studies available, limitations in interpreting the existing studies, and the primary population studied."*

### 2.3 The studies underneath the figures (all snippet-only or as-described)

| Study | Manipulation | Duration | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bickel 2011**, *MSSE* 43:1177–1187 | 3 sessions/wk → **1**; 3 sets → **1**; intensity held at 8–12RM | **32 wk** | 1RM **further improved**; muscle size held in 20–35s, **not** in 60–75s |
| **Tavares** | 2–3 sessions/wk → **2**; 3–4 sets → **2**; 6–12RM vs 6–8RM | 8 wk | strength and size fully maintained |
| **Rønnestad 2011**, *JSCR* 25(10):2653–2660 | **1×/wk (n=7)** vs **1× per 2 wk (n=7)**, in-season | 12 wk | 1×/wk held squat 1RM + 40 m sprint; 1×/2wk **lost both**; **jump height unchanged in both** |
| **Morehouse 1967**, *Res Q* 38:449–456 | isometric, 9 wk at 100% MVC then reduced freq/vol/**intensity** | 8 wk | reps at 100% MVC maintained strength; lesser intensities did not |
| **Hickson** (endurance) | 10 wk at ~90–100% HRmax, then intensity cut 33% or 66%, **frequency and volume held** | 15 wk | VO2max and long-term endurance **not maintained in either** reduced-intensity group |

### 2.4 The endpoint-choice trap (F-713)
From the same review, same table set:
- maintaining **1RM** → **1 session/week**
- maintaining **maximal isometric strength** → **1 session every 4 weeks**

A **fourfold** spread, resolved by the authors in favour of 1RM on the stated grounds that 1RM tracked sprint speed in Rønnestad 2011. So the chain is: industry prescription → a 1RM-over-MIS choice → **one 12-week trial on 14 soccer players**. If a program's in-season battery is isometric (IMTP, dynamometry), it will read "maintained" on a dose that is losing the 1RM. **Neither test is velocity.** Fourth confirmed instance of the endpoint-choice sweep opened 2026-10-13 (F-689).

## 3. What the taper literature adds, and what it does not

Spiering's own summary of the excluded taper work: athletes maintain or enhance **strength** for up to **4 weeks** by cutting volume **30–70%** while maintaining or increasing intensity; and **endurance** by cutting frequency ≤~**20%** and volume **60–90%** at maintained intensity. That is a second, independent literature arriving at the same shape: **volume is cheap, intensity is not.** It is also only four weeks long and has no throwing outcome.

## 4. Cessation, and "power goes first" (F-719)

- **Bosquet 2013**, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 23:e140–149 (snippet-only): 103 of 284 screened studies. **Submaximal strength SMD −0.62** (−0.80 to −0.45); **maximal force SMD −0.46** (−0.54 to −0.37). Dose–response with duration; larger in >65s and in the inactive than in recreational athletes. 🚨 **The maximal-POWER SMD was truncated in every snippet and is not in this corpus.** This is **cessation**, not reduced training — it bounds what an off week costs.
- **Ruggiero & Gruber**, *J Physiol* narrative review (snippet-only): **unloading** produces a greater decline in explosive than maximal strength, gap narrowing with duration. Mechanisms: higher recruitment thresholds, lower firing rates, slower twitch kinetics, impaired excitation–contraction coupling, **falling tendon stiffness** (→ `tendon-stiffness.md`). **Dose is bed rest and immobilisation.** Grade: **MECHANISM**, not ESTABLISHED, for a training context.
- **Mujika & Padilla** (secondary): college swimmers held **gym strength** across 4 weeks of cessation while **swim power declined.** The most pitching-relevant observation in the topic and it is second-hand.
- **"Power diminishes in 7–10 days"** — VBT vendor material. **UNSOURCED. Do not repeat.**

## 5. The hole (F-714)

- **No in-season dose trial in baseball, at any level.** Searched both directions.
- **Zero throwing outcomes in the entire reduced-training and cessation literature.** Every endpoint is VO2max, 1RM, MIS, muscle size, sprint time or jump height.
- **The only in-season resistance work with a ball-throwing endpoint is handball, largely one research group, reporting +~22% limb muscle volume in 8 weeks, d = 7.00, η²p = 0.920, medicine-ball d = 6.44, n = 20 split two ways** (Hermassi 2019, *IJERPH* 16(22):4520, snippet-only). Those magnitudes cannot be real. Same signature as F-040 (g = 3.445) and F-194 (Differential Learning). **DO NOT CONVERT TO MPH** (F-717).
- **The study that would settle it is not affordable:** an in-season frequency × intensity manipulation with a mound-velocity endpoint needs **~106 arms** (F-681, F-289).

## 6. What the industry prescribes, and why it is backwards

| Source | Prescription | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Tread Athletics, *In-Season Training Blueprint* | 3–4 sessions/wk **at lower intensity** | **MARKETING/UNPROVEN** — cuts the protected variable |
| Rockland Peak Performance | 1–2 sessions/wk for younger athletes, **3–4 for college/pro** | **UNPROVEN** — inverts the dose-response, no data |
| Coaches Insider | 2–3 days/wk, heaviest day right after an appearance | **UNPROVEN** |
| VBT vendor material | power lost in "7–10 days" | **UNPROVEN** — direction plausible, number unsourced |
| Eric Cressey, *14 Reasons Pitching Velocity Decreases Over a Season* | skeptical of the standard justifications | **PROMISING as framing** — the only source treating the premise as contestable |

Every one cuts volume **and** intensity. The one conclusion with convergent support says hold intensity and give away volume. **The correction is free.**

Also on the record from the same review, as the *upper* bound: *"research has also shown that too much supplementary strength training during the competitive season can overstress the athlete and ultimately impair strength"* (Kraemer WJ, French DN, Paxton NJ, et al. 2004, *JSCR* 18:121–128, Big Ten soccer, starters vs non-starters). There is a dose too high and a dose too low, and **no study in any throwing population locates either.**

## 7. The deployable output (F-718)

**(a) THE RELATIVE-LOAD AUDIT — one spreadsheet, zero athlete time.** Per pitcher, per main compound lift: **mean %1RM actually used, by month, February→May**, plus **sets per week**, against the fall baseline. Falling %1RM with held set counts = reduced-intensity training, which has one 1967 isometric study behind it. Fix: hold the fall's relative load, cut to 1–2 sets, **never less than one heavy session per week** (F-712). **Scope: lower-body and trunk compounds. Overhead and arm-specific work is governed elsewhere (F-036) and nothing read here speaks to it.**

**(b) THE ONE-ARM TRAJECTORY PROBE**, priced off F-289 at the corpus working within-outing SD of 1.0 mph (two-sample, α = .05, 80% power):

| Contrast | Fastballs per bucket | ≈ starts per bucket |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0 mph | 4 | <1 |
| 1.5 mph | 7 | ~1 |
| **1.0 mph** | **16** | **~2** |
| 0.5 mph | 63 | ~4 |

**Innings 1–2 fastballs only** (so F-384's −0.2 mph/inning fade falls equally on both buckets), same radar unit, same mound where possible, **same feedback condition written down and never changed** (F-704). Bucket A = Feb/Mar, Bucket B = May.

**⭐ AND THE NULL IS NOT ZERO.** F-082: MLB starter velocity rises across a season — 90.1 (Mar/Apr) → 90.4 (May) → 90.7 (Aug), **+0.6 mph**, largest step April→May. The expected May-minus-March contrast for a healthy arm is **+0.3 to +0.6 mph**. **A flat trajectory is a relative decline of about the size you set out to detect.** Most programs score flat as a win.

**🚨 FREE PREREQUISITE, third consecutive day at the top of this vault:** the **1.0 mph within-outing SD is a corpus assumption**, never published for an 85+ arm, now load-bearing in F-289, F-659, F-684, F-689, F-706 and F-718. It is in the program's own radar log.
