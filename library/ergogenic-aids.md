# Ergogenic aids for the 85+ arm — caffeine, creatine, hydration
**Opened 2026-09-18.** Findings F-388 → F-398. Companion to `daily/2026-09-18-report.md`.

**⚠️ READ THIS FIRST.** This is the first topic in the corpus whose literature is almost entirely **randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover**. The vault runs ~30:1 cross-sectional to intervention; here the ratio inverts. **The marker-versus-lever problem does not arise — everything below was manipulated.** And the answer is still mostly no. That combination is what makes this topic worth keeping: it is the corpus's cleanest demonstration that a *clean design* and a *useful answer* are different things, and that the failure mode in good-design literature is **task mismatch**, not causal inference.

Three primary texts were read in full (F-389, F-390, F-391). Everything else here is snippet-only and is marked.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph answer

**Caffeine does not raise peak throwing velocity.** The pooled meta-analytic claim that it does is built on bench press throws, medicine balls and shot put — nine of ten studies — and does not survive restriction to overhead ball throwing, where the record is roughly one positive subtest out of six or seven across three studies. The best-designed study puts the 3 mg/kg effect at **+0.16 km/h [−1.82, +2.15]** while the *same dose in the same athletes on the same day* moved intermittent running by ~29% and reaction time by ~18.5%. **Creatine has never been tested with a throwing-velocity outcome, anywhere.** **Hydration has never been studied in pitching at all.** The usable residue is small and it is not a velocity residue.

---

## 2. What caffeine actually does, and why the pattern is coherent

At sporting doses (3–6 mg/kg) caffeine acts **centrally**, by adenosine-receptor antagonism: lower perceived effort, higher arousal and vigilance. The direct peripheral actions (sarcoplasmic calcium handling, catecholamine release) need concentrations an oral dose does not reach.

That single mechanism predicts the whole observed pattern:

| Task type | What limits it | Caffeine effect |
|---|---|---|
| Intermittent running (Yo-Yo IRT-1) | effort perception, fatigue | **+23–29%**, η²p = 0.415 |
| Reaction time (Flanker) | vigilance | **−18.5%** (−137 ms), η²p = 0.486 |
| Maximal overhead throw | technique, intersegmental coordination | **η²p = 0.004** |

**The ceiling argument:** in a maximal single-effort ballistic task performed by an expert, voluntary activation is already essentially complete. A drug that makes hard effort *feel* easier has nothing to buy when the constraint is coordination, not willingness. The *Life* (2026) authors reach this independently.

**This is a MECHANISM, and it is falsifiable** (cross-examination ①): it predicts caffeine's throwing effect shrinks as expertise, freshness and velocity rise. A positive peak-velocity effect in fresh, expert, high-velocity throwers would refute it. That study does not exist.

## 3. The evidence table — overhead ball throwing only

Everything that has ever thrown a ball overhead under caffeine:

| Study | n | Population | Dose | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Muñoz 2020 (PMC7464361, *Genes* 11:933) ✅ read in full | 31 | professional handball, **51 mph**, habitual caffeine **60 ± 25 mg/d** | 3 mg/kg | **1 of 4 throw tests significant.** 9 m: 82.55 → 84.90 km/h, p = .008, ES 0.31. 9 m+GK p = .093; 7 m p = .065; 7 m+GK p = .492. No multiplicity correction across 8 outcomes. |
| *Life* 2026 (PMC13302302, 16:954) ✅ read in full | 20 | trained female handball, **45.9 mph** | 3 and 6 mg/kg, **double-blind** | **NULL.** F = 0.04, p = .961, η²p = .004. +0.16 km/h [−1.82, +2.15]. **Positive controls fired in same session.** |
| Yildirim 2023, *IJDSHS* 6:107–115 ⚠️ snippet-only | — | trained female handball | low + moderate | **"Improve Aerobic Endurance but Not Throwing Velocity"** (title) |

**Everything else in the meta-analysis (F-389):** 4 × medicine ball (2/5/9 kg), 3 × bench press throw, 2 × shot put, 1 ballistic push-up; six of ten samples were resistance-trained non-throwers.

**⚠️ THE NULL CARRIES THE SAME POPULATION PENALTY AS THE POSITIVE RESULT.** 45.9 mph with a size-2 handball is *further* from an 85+ arm than Muñoz's 51 mph men. **The correct summary is "unmeasured at 85+, near-zero wherever measured" — never "zero."** What generalises from the *Life* study is its statement about **task type**, which travels better than a magnitude does, because it rests on a within-session contrast rather than on an absolute level.

## 4. Two errors to refuse

**(a) The percentage transfer.** Muñoz's significant gain is +2.85%. Scaled naively to 90 mph that is **+2.6 mph** — the number a supplement seller would quote. Nothing supports it. A 51 mph handball throw and a 90 mph pitch are different points on the force–velocity curve with a 3× implement-mass difference; percentages do not travel across that.

**(b) The pooled-SMD transfer.** SMD 0.24 for "throwing velocity" is, in substance, **bar velocity in a bench press throw and release velocity of a 2–9 kg medicine ball.** A 9 kg medicine ball is 62× the mass of a baseball. This is standing-brief check #3 — *does the number measure what the sentence says it measures?* — and it does not.

---

## 5. Habituation: the variable that decides applicability

- Muñoz's sample: **60 ± 25 mg/day** — under one cup of coffee. Authors scope the result to *"low habituation"* and warn of *"progressive tolerance to the ergogenic benefit of caffeine with chronic ingestion."*
- A 16 oz energy drink is 160–300 mg. ⚠️ snippet-only; **this corpus holds no measured intake distribution for college pitchers and none is published anywhere.**
- **The unmodelled one: every study mandated ~48 h abstinence.** In a habituated subject that **manufactures withdrawal**, biasing the placebo trial downward and inflating the apparent caffeine effect. In a habituated athlete a "caffeine benefit" may be nothing but withdrawal relief — and a habituated pitcher who skips caffeine on start day is *below* baseline, not at it.

---

## 6. The hole nobody has looked in: command

**Not one study in this topic — not one of the ten meta-analysed, not one of the three overhead — collected a throwing-accuracy outcome.** Velocity into an open goal is the entire outcome literature.

⚠️ **SNIPPET-ONLY, UNVERIFIED, DO NOT QUOTE:** a ScienceDirect review (S0266435623000426, host refused) is summarised as reporting ~2.5 mg/kg may induce fine hand tremor and ~5 mg/kg worsened dexterity, partly mitigated by tolerance. Primary sources appear to be Jacobson 1991/1992, *Perceptual and Motor Skills* — old, small, non-athlete.

**For a pitcher the risk is asymmetric**: the doses that might help ballistic output are the doses flagged for fine-motor cost, and command is half the job. No population in this literature plays a precision sport, so nobody has had a reason to look. **Measure with release-point SD (F-374), not strike rate** — continuous, uncontaminated by the catcher (F-309), far cheaper per pitch.

---

## 7. Creatine and hydration: two documented absences

**Creatine.** No randomised trial with a throwing-velocity outcome exists in baseball or any throwing sport (F-394). Ferrauti 2003 found only ~5 articles studying creatine under ballgame conditions. A softball loading study (PMID 14767409, 20 g/d × 1 wk) improved repeated-contraction endurance but not maximal strength or peak torque, **with no throwing outcome**. The plausible route to a pitcher is **indirect and slow — training tolerance → lean mass → velocity via F-001/F-002 — and the corpus already records that no mass-gain intervention with a velocity outcome exists at all.** Reasonable training-phase supplement; **not a velocity intervention.**

**Hydration.** No primary study of hydration and pitching exists, for any population (F-395). The 2% body-mass-loss threshold is borrowed from basketball (Baker 2007, PMID 17596779, n = 17, ⚠️ snippet-only) and a second elite-basketball study found **no** significant three-point decrement at 2.1%. **A guardrail from another sport, not a pitching finding.**

⚠️ **AND THE SEARCH ITSELF PRODUCED A LAUNDERING EVENT** — a summariser asserting, uncited, that *"dehydrated players show decreased fine motor control, reducing throwing accuracy and pitching command by measurable margins."* **No such measurement exists.** Third instance after F-274/F-275. **Where the literature is empty the content farms are loudest, and an absence is the easiest thing in the world to launder into a finding.**

## 8. Detection — and why this one is priced to be CLOSED, not opened

Paired crossover on session mean fastball velocity, 80% power, α = .05. With σ_p = 1.0 mph over 30 fastballs, σ_p/√n = 0.18 mph — negligible. **The binding term is σ_s, the session-to-session SD of an athlete's mean velocity, which nobody has measured** (eleventh entry in that pattern).

| σ_s | detect 1.0 mph | detect 0.5 mph |
|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 2 pairs | 8 pairs |
| 0.5 | 5 pairs | 18 pairs |
| 0.8 | 11 pairs | 42 pairs |

Against F-391's ±1.24 mph bound the realistic target is the 0.5 mph column: **8–42 paired sessions per pitcher. Don't run it.** ⚠️ **And any self-run version needs blinding that is practically unachievable — caffeine is felt.** Unblinded, the test measures expectancy.

---

## 9. What this topic actually licenses

1. **Do not sell caffeine as velocity.** It isn't one, and the best evidence is a null with its positive controls firing.
2. **Caffeine is an alertness and repeated-effort tool** — a flat day, a late warm-up, a doubleheader, a travel day into a night start. Those effects are large and real. Neither is a radar-gun effect.
3. **Audit habitual intake before anything else** (§5). Free, one week, decides whether the topic is live for a given arm, and surfaces the withdrawal problem.
4. **Keep caffeine off command days** until §6 has been measured.
5. **Creatine: judge it as a training-phase supplement, not a velocity aid.** Hydration: hygiene, 2% as a borrowed guardrail.
6. **The open question worth running (F-392): is caffeine a velocity-RETENTION agent?** Every study measured fresh maximal single throws — the one condition the mechanism says caffeine should do least for. **⚠️ This is a hypothesis and a proposed study. It is NOT advice, and the draft recommendation built on it was withdrawn under cross-examination.**
