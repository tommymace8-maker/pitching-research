# ECCENTRIC-EMPHASIS AND FLYWHEEL / ISOINERTIAL TRAINING — the modality named for a mechanism its own literature never verified

## 0. The one-paragraph state of the topic

**There is no evidence that eccentric-emphasis or flywheel training raises pitch velocity, and there is a structural reason to doubt the modality can currently support any claim at all.** A 2026 review concludes that flywheel exercises *do not necessarily produce eccentric overload*, and that the field has been inferring a force condition from velocity-derived metrics (F-762). The ratio that operationalises "overload" is disclaimed by the field's own editorial on reliability grounds (F-763). The inertia setting that produces overload is the setting that removes limb speed (F-764). Against matched conventional loading the modality is null for strength and hypertrophy and shows one small power advantage, **SMD 0.47 [0.10, 0.84]** (F-768) — while the effect sizes in circulation, **ES 1.19–1.33**, are within-study pre-post differences (F-769). The only pooled throwing-velocity estimate, **d = −0.15**, is a single n = 18 study wearing a meta-analysis's clothes (F-765, F-766). The only positive throwing trial has no control arm, is not randomised, is youth handball, and its flywheel group raised velocity while failing its own strength check (F-767). **No eccentric or flywheel study has ever been run on a baseball pitcher** (F-771). And the arithmetic says a single roster cannot test any of it: **144 athletes for the power effect, ~1,400 for the throwing effect** (F-774).

**The deliverable of this topic is a refusal, and a refusal is a legitimate deliverable.**

## 2. The structural results — why this topic matters beyond the topic

### 2.1 Mechanism-named tools (F-762) — the portable rule

| | outcome measured? | mechanism verified? |
|---|---|---|
| F-571 (stride) | ✗ wrong outcome | ✓ clean manipulation check |
| F-721 (plyometrics) | ✓ ball velocity | ✗ no SSC mediator, ever |
| **F-762 (flywheel)** | ✓ sometimes | ✗ **and the mechanism is in the product's name** |

⭐ **THE RULE: when a training tool is NAMED for a mechanism, treat the mechanism as unverified until a manipulation check is shown.** A purpose-built machine engineered to deliver eccentric overload cannot demonstrate that it does. An *implement* or *drill* named for a mechanism should be assumed to fail the same check. This advanced **Dispute #79** (is a plyo-ball "plyometric" or just another throw?) more than any evidence inside that dispute's own topic.

## 3. The mechanism, honestly stated (F-770, F-773)

**What eccentric loading uniquely does:** lengthen fascicles (sarcomeres in series) where conventional loading increases pennation angle. Banks et al. 2024 (*Eur J Sport Sci*, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.12215, correction PMID 40367320), n = 31, randomised, volume-matched, 10 weeks: flywheel → fascicle length + **distal** VL CSA + CMJ + broad jump (p ≤ 0.048); traditional → pennation angle + **proximal** VL CSA; **both equal for maximal isometric strength and fat-free mass.**

**The confound inside the mechanism:** 8 weeks of eccentric work **at long muscle length** → ≈ **8.5%** fascicle lengthening; **at short muscle length → no change** (*J Appl Physiol* 2025, DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.00859.2024). ≈ **5.2% at 11 weeks** in VL. **The active ingredient may be muscle length at loading, not loading mode — which makes the device irrelevant to its own best mechanism.**

**Why it does not obviously reach a pitch (F-773).** Pitching contains two eccentric events, both already priced here:
- **Lead-leg block:** ~**50–80 ms** braking (F-645), **~4–6% of between-pitcher velocity variance after mass control** (F-061: Z GRF r = 0.44 → **0.23**).
- **Shoulder deceleration:** **−201 ± 70 J** negative work (F-064), **tissue attribution UNTESTED** (F-636), zero cuff-stiffness trials anywhere.

Flywheel braking occupies **hundreds of ms**; the overload window inside it, where it exists, is **brief** (F-762). And the lower-limb quality that survives into a professional pitching sample is **reactive** — drop jump R² = 0.30, RSI p = .002, n = 53 pros, where CMJ (R² = 0.10) and squat jump (R² = 0.07) do not (F-006). **A 3-second lowering and a 60 ms block are different physiological problems.**

## 4. What to do — and the precise limit of what is licensed

### The decision (F-771, F-774)
**Do not build a velocity block around this modality.** The sentence to say out loud: *"nobody has tested it on a pitcher, the device may not do what it is named for, and the barbell arm matched it in the only throwing trial that exists."*

### If the flywheel is already in the building
Keep it as a **general-strength and lower-limb power tool**, chosen on **cost, joint comfort, accommodating resistance, unilateral loading and whether athletes will actually use it**. ⚠️ **This is a PREFERENCE, not a finding** — conceded in cross-examination 2026-10-19: at n = 20 a staff can no more detect "flywheel ≈ barbell" than it can detect "flywheel > barbell." **Prescribe by velocity, not by power**, per the primary's own recommendation (F-764).

### The one prescription the mechanism licenses (F-770)
**Loaded eccentrics at end range, lower body** — deficit RDL, Bulgarian split squat to full depth, slow eccentric step-down. **3–4 × 5–6, 4–6 s lowering**, in the existing general-strength slot, **replacing nothing on the throwing sheet.**
- **Cue:** *"We load you at long positions, under control, because that is where the tissue changes. The machine doesn't matter; the position and the control do."*
- **Video failure:** range cut short under load — that is the arm of the trial that got **nothing**.
- **Check:** the **training log** (did end-range loading happen, at depth, for 6+ weeks) plus end-range strength at the loaded position. ⚠️ **NOT velocity — no velocity check exists at n = 20** (F-774).

### The capacity prescription, stated as capacity (F-773)
**Eccentric-emphasis ER/IR, light, 3 × 8, 3–4 s lowering, twice weekly, inside the arm-care slot that already exists.** No new slot, no new volume, nothing removed.
- **Cue:** *"We do controlled eccentric work so your arm can absorb what it already absorbs 100 times a night. It is not going to add a mile per hour."*
- **Video failure:** speed on the lowering phase — the moment he drops it instead of resisting it, the stimulus is gone.
- **Check:** post-outing ER/IR strength retention, with the **non-throwing shoulder as his own control** (the free measurement F-622 identified, still unexecuted).
- ⚠️ **Loaded on an untested premise (F-636). Dose it at near-zero cost and attach no velocity claim.**

## 5. What is not known

1. **No eccentric or flywheel intervention in baseball pitchers, at any level, with any outcome** (F-771).
2. **No fascicle-length or muscle-architecture measurement related to pitch velocity, in any population** (F-772).
3. **No ICC/SEM/MDC for the eccentric:concentric ratio in any athletic population** — twentieth entry in the standing reliability gap (F-763).
4. **No throwing-outcome eccentric trial above youth/amateur level, and none in baseball.** Every object is handball, 425 g — subject to **Dispute #85**.
5. **No workload, soreness or tissue-cost accounting for eccentric-emphasis work in a throwing athlete** — the same hole F-757 found in the med-ball literature, in a modality with a far higher known soreness cost.
6. **The Sports 2026 trial's baseline throwing velocity and km/h tables are UNREAD** — one table decides whether F-767 is even directionally usable.
7. **The AEL-vs-constant-load meta-analysis's RFD and POWER outcomes are UNREAD** (PMC13388742) — the topic's missing numbers, and the most load-bearing unread object here.
8. **Does Spudić & Nosaka 2026 report a study count for the manipulation check — and is it 17 of 79?** One sentence. It is the difference between "the field sometimes fails to verify its mechanism" and a materially stronger claim this brief declined to make.

## 6. Fabrication watch

Nothing fabricated. Four items **held back** rather than reported:
1. **"17 of 79 studies"** (Spudić & Nosaka 2026) — appeared in one search pass, **did not reproduce** on a targeted second pass. **UNVERIFIED, withheld.**
2. **"Pitchers who did only pitching workloads with no resistance training saw velocity and arm health decline"** — a coaching blog's attribution to an unnamed 2025 study; no primary located. **UNVERIFIED. This is exactly the third-party-paraphrase failure mode this program exists to prevent.**
3. **Exxentric's "276 subjects from 9 studies, 2017, favouring flywheel"** — manufacturer page, not independently located. **MARKETING.**
4. **Exerfly's "braking must be delayed to the final part of the range for eccentric output to exceed concentric"** — a vendor's gloss on a paper this cycle could not open. Directionally consistent with the primary's stated conclusion; **not registered.**
