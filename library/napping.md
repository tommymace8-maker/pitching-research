# NAPPING AND THE PITCHER'S GAME DAY

**Opened 2026-10-10.** Findings **F-646 → F-656**. Disputes **#67**, **#68**.
⚠️ **Opened in the eleventh consecutive fully egress-blocked cycle (F-646). ZERO primary texts were read. Every literature claim in this file is SNIPPET-ONLY and is therefore a LEAD, not a finding.**

## 1. What this topic is, and why it was opened

Napping had been flagged twice and never opened — on 2026-09-19 (*"eight of the 25 studies in the sleep-intervention review used naps and the review calls the results conflicting"*) and in the standing gap list as *"the largest untouched sub-branch of this topic and the one a pitcher can actually execute on a game day."* Before 2026-10-10 the registry held **zero substantive nap findings**.

It is a **performance-development** topic, not a recovery-modality topic, for one reason: F-400 establishes that acute sleep loss costs **skill** about 3.3× what it costs **power**, and skill is command.

## 3. The two structural results

### 3.1 The outcome class is wrong, and F-406 predicted it before the literature was read

Three meta-analyses, 65 trials. Outcomes found: shuttle-run distance, fatigue index, muscle force, CMJ, sprint, agility, reaction time, mood, RPE, perceived recovery, heart rate, temperature, lactate, EEG. **Accuracy outcomes: zero. Throwing outcomes: zero. Baseball: zero.**

| Outcome class | Cost of ≤6 h sleep (F-400, source-verified) | Measured in the nap literature? |
|---|---|---|
| **Skill / target accuracy** | **−20.9%** [−27.0, −14.9], I² = 94.1 | **never** |
| Anaerobic power | −6.26% [−9.10, −3.41] | exhaustively |
| Strength | −2.85% [−4.47, −1.23] | exhaustively, and null |

And F-400's skill decrement by test time: **AM −14.2%, PM −22.9%.** The afternoon is where the deficit is largest and it is the only window a nap occupies. **If a nap works by repaying sleep pressure, its largest available effect is on accuracy, in the afternoon — the one cell nobody has looked in.** (F-647; Dispute #68)

**Dispute #27's twelfth consecutive venue.** At twelve without exception it is reclassified from an observation about topics to a property of the measurement culture.

### 3.2 The precision test that needs no full text

From Boukhris 2025's own abstract:

- **Significant:** mood 0.61, fatigue 0.91, RPE during 1.62, RPE after 1.11, perceived recovery 1.66.
- **Non-significant:** sleepiness 1.09, muscle soreness **1.57**, heart rate 0.62, temperature 0.66.

**A pooled SMD of 1.57 failing significance while 0.61 clears it means heterogeneity has consumed the estimates.** The significance pattern carries no biological information.

> ⭐ **REUSABLE TOOL, FREE, ONE ABSTRACT:** in any meta-analysis, compare the **largest non-significant** pooled effect against the **smallest significant** one. If the first exceeds the second, the significance pattern is uninformative. (F-648)

Note also that the two **largest** effects in the topic — perceived recovery 1.66 and RPE 1.62 — are **self-report inside an unblindable intervention. There is no placebo nap.** Every objective outcome is a null: muscle force 0.175 NS, heart rate 0.62 NS, temperature 0.66 NS, CMJ null after a 30-min nap.

## 4. What is genuinely manipulated — the dose inverts with sleep debt

`CAUSALITY: INTERVENTION` throughout. This inverts the corpus's usual 30:1 problem.

| Night before | Favoured dose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | **20–25 min** | 20 min improved RAST sprint, MCRT, sleepiness (n = 14 judokas); 90 min **decreased** repeated sprint, authors citing sleep inertia. Elite netball: CMJ peak velocity favoured 20 min over **both** no nap and >20 min. |
| **Restricted** | **60–90 min** | Romdhani's restricted arm (via *J Sports Sci* 2025, 10.1080/02640414.2025.2464440): both 20 and 90 min improved repeated sprint, reaction time, mood; **90 min stronger.** |
| **Totally deprived** | **90 min** | Apunts Sports Medicine 2026 (S2666506926000015), 40/60/90 min, male university soccer: 90 min largest gains — CMJ, RSA, HRmax, lactate, RPE, **p < .001** vs control and both shorter naps. ⚠️ **Deprivation ≠ restriction; these magnitudes do not transfer.** |

**Contradicted:** PMC12034280, collegiate soccer, **normal** night sleep — **both** 30 and 90 min improved anaerobic sprint, and **only 90 min** reduced fatigue index. Same sleep status as the judoka trial, opposite verdict on 90 minutes. → **Dispute #67.**

**The confound:** nap length and **wake-to-performance buffer** are not separable in the published record. The trial reporting harm tested **30 minutes post-wake**. A real pre-game nap ending at 14:30 before a 19:00 start has a **4.5-hour** buffer. **Every sleep-inertia result in this literature was measured at an interval far shorter than a real game-day nap.** (F-649)

## 5. Restriction of range, third form

| Form | Statistic | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Correlations | `r` | Luera 2020, n = 149 pros: r collapses to .17–.29 in an all-hard-throwing sample |
| Adaptation magnitudes | training effect | F-630/F-631: all three trained-athlete tendon studies showed **no change**; every large gain (+57%, +57.7%, "up to 61.6%", +20.1 ± 20.5%) came from untrained/older/patient samples; a professional **placebo** arm moved +4.6% |
| **Acute ergogenic magnitudes — new 2026-10-10** | acute effect | A nap acts on the gap between current sleep pressure and rested baseline. **F-650's own dose inversion is the within-literature evidence that the effect scales with the deficit.** |

**One argument in three variables: an adaptation responds to load above habitual; an acute restorative responds to deficit below habitual.**

**Falsifiable prediction:** the nap effect in a well-slept, well-resourced 85+ arm sits at the **small end** of every published estimate. **Consequence: the nap is a triage tool, not a team policy** — worth most to the pitcher sleeping worst. One on-population denominator exists: **22.5% of 200 professional players at one club's spring training reported ≤7 h/night** (F-653). (F-651)

## 6. The prescription — a wake-to-throw rule, not a nap program

The pitchers are already napping. **What is unmanaged is when they get up.**

| Situation | Nap window | Length | Awake and out of the room by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Night game, 19:00 first pitch, normal night** | 14:00–15:00 | **25 min** | **16:00** (≥2 h to bullpen) |
| **Night game, after a short night (<~6 h)** | 13:30–15:00 | **60–90 min** | **15:30** (≥2 h, longer buffer for inertia) |
| **Day game** | — | **no nap** | — |
| **Bullpen / training day** | post-session | 20–30 min | ≥60 min before next throw |

**No caffeine inside a starter's nap protocol** (F-655): a 5 mg/kg dose at 15:00 is still circulating at bedtime; caffeine's measured sleep cost in athletes runs to **−72.9 min** (391.4 ± 96.9 vs 464.3 ± 48.9), and **F-391 already measured caffeine as null for throwing velocity with its positive controls firing.** You buy the −6.26% power column and pay in the −20.9% skill column: ~3:1 against.

**Why this is defensible despite off-population evidence — Dispute #41's ninth encounter and its cleanest resolution.** What is prescribed is **not a benefit but the timing of a behaviour that already occurs.** Direction from manipulation rather than cross-section; cost zero; the one measured harm has a free mitigation. **A scheduling rule does not require an exchange rate. A velocity claim does, and none is made.**

🚨 **WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: this being written down as a velocity or command intervention. It is a schedule.**

## 7. The trial, priced

| Design | Outcome | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Two-arm, velocity | 1 mph | **106 pitchers** (F-623) |
| Two-arm, velocity | 0.5 mph | **422 pitchers** (F-623) |
| Paired, velocity | `d_z` = 0.5 | **32 pitchers** |
| Paired, velocity | `d_z` = 0.3 | **88 pitchers** |
| Crossover, **location SD** | 20% SD increase | **118 pitches/condition** (F-408 Design B) |
| Crossover, **location SD** | 10% SD increase | **431 pitches/condition** ≈ 17 bullpens of 25 each way |

⚠️ **F-408's price was powered against Craven's FULL-night −20.9% decrement.** A nap repays a fraction **f** of a smaller deficit; price scales as **1/f²**. **f = 0.5 → ~431/condition. f = 0.25 → ~1,700/condition, unaffordable.** The `f = 0.5` figure is **invented and bracketed, not a price.**

⚠️ **Every figure is a FLOOR.** F-482 item 7: pitches within a bullpen are not independent; design effect `1+(m−1)ρ`; **ρ unpublished**; at m = 25, ρ = 0.1 → **3.4×**.

**The defensible claim is a RANKING, not affordability:** the command-side crossover is the only design in this topic within an order of magnitude of a college season; the velocity-side design is not.

**Four binding instrument warnings:** ≥5 familiarisation sessions before the first scored block (F-404); never zone rate (F-309, F-310); never a jump mat as the readiness instrument (F-359); randomise or counterbalance nap/no-nap order — **no trial in this literature reports trial order**, the same hole Dispute #53 found in windup/stretch.

**Eighth time this corpus has concluded a continuous outcome is the only affordable one.** (F-654)

## 9. Gaps this topic leaves open

1. **No nap study with any throwing outcome, in any population.** Total. (F-653)
2. **No nap study with any accuracy outcome, in any population.** Dispute #27's twelfth venue. (F-647)
3. **No wake-to-performance interval reported retrievably for any nap trial.** One methods sentence per paper settles Dispute #67. **Cheapest open item in the topic.** (F-649)
4. **No trial-order report in any nap trial.** Same hole as windup/stretch.
5. **Nobody has crossed CHRONOTYPE with NAP.** The corpus holds the chronotype × start-time crossover in 18 MLB pitchers (WEAK, 2010 conference abstract). If evening types are already advantaged at 19:00 starts, the nap prescription may be for **morning types pitching at night** and near-worthless for the rest. **The moderator is a free questionnaire.**
6. **No ρ (within-bullpen location ICC) published anywhere.** Blocks every detection figure in §7 and in F-186, F-408 and F-482.
7. **No placebo-nap design exists**, so the topic's two largest effects are permanently confounded with expectancy.
8. **No reliever sub-case.** A 20-minute alertness window at 21:00 is a different question with a different caffeine calculus, and nobody has tested it. (F-655)
9. **Boukhris 2025's complete GRADE table is unretrieved.** One snippet reports **moderate** certainty for RPE; the rest are unknown.
