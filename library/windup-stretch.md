# Windup vs Stretch — the delivery you start from

**Opened 2026-10-02 · Findings F-547 → F-561 · Disputes #53, #54 · Flags F-069**

> ⚠️ **EVERY LITERATURE CLAIM IN THIS FILE IS SNIPPET-ONLY.** The cycle that produced it was the third consecutive fully egress-blocked run (F-560): zero primary texts opened. Four sections (§3, §4, §5, §6) are **source-independent** — arithmetic or logic against numbers the registry already holds — and those are the durable ones.

## 1 — Why this topic exists

`F-069` entered the registry in the August 2026 compilation as twelve lines reading *"Wind-up and stretch produce statistically similar velocity and kinematics in professionals,"* with `NUMBERS: effect sizes not recorded in the corpus` and a retired cue attached. It had never been examined. Meanwhile the industry is executing a visible migration to stretch-only deliveries, and F-069 is the finding that licenses it.

**Two things were wrong with F-069, and neither was the professional claim.** See §2 and §7.

## 2 — The literature: four studies, all within-subject, split by level

| Study | n | Level | Velocity result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dun S, Kingsley D, Fleisig GS, Loftice J, Andrews JR (2008), *AJSM*, PMID 17986632 | professional | pro | no difference |
| Hodakowski A, Dowling B, Brusalis CM, et al. (2024), *Sports Biomechanics* 23(12):3680–3692, PMID 39295086 | 52 (189.1 ± 4.8 cm, 92.8 ± 8.4 kg) | pro | no difference |
| Fleisig GS, Slowik JS, Kutz CB, Escamilla RF (2024), *AJSM* 52(7):1671–1675, PMID 38687464 | 221 (105 pro, 52 college, 64 HS) | three | **collegiate subgroup: windup > stretch, significant, below MCID (0.5 m/s = 1.118 mph)**; pro and HS n.s. |
| Escamilla RF, Slowik JS, Imamura R, Thompson IS, Asuncion R, Aguinaldo AL, Fleisig GS (2026), *J Appl Biomech* 42(3):165, PMID 41722544 | 18 | college, dirt mound | **windup > stretch, ≈1%, significant**; 74% of 35 comparisons null |

**Two professional nulls. Two collegiate positives. The positives agree on magnitude** (≈1% = 0.80–0.90 mph at any plausible sample velocity; the 2024 collegiate difference was < 1.118 mph). **Level is the apparent moderator, and the positives sit in this program's population.** (F-547)

**Three caveats that could each dissolve the collegiate result:**
- ⚠️ **Sample velocity is unretrievable for both collegiate samples** (F-557). Quote the percentage, never the mph.
- ⚠️ **The two collegiate studies share three authors and possibly a pitcher pool** (F-556). Unchecked. If they overlap there is one result, not two.
- ⚠️ **Trial order is unknown** in both (Dispute #53). If windup trials ran first, warm-up state is confounded with delivery.

### 2a — The 2026 study's eight "significant" parameters are one effect

Greater from the windup: shoulder horizontal adduction torque; shoulder anterior force; **lead knee height**; ball velocity; forward trunk tilt at release; upper-trunk angular velocity; elbow extension angular velocity; shoulder IR angular velocity.

**Lead knee height is the definition of a windup — a compliance check.** Six of the other seven **scale with ball velocity** (F-213 RULE 1 prices the tightest coupling at R² = 0.957 within an athlete). So this is **one output difference measured in six correlated channels**, not eight confirmations (F-549).

That cuts both ways. It deflates the apparent weight of evidence **and** it deflates the multiplicity objection — these are not 35 independent tests, and the significant set forms an ordered proximal-to-distal chain rather than a scatter.

## 3 — The causality correction (source-independent)

F-069 carried `CAUSALITY: CROSS_SECTIONAL`. **Wrong.** Every study had the same pitcher throw both deliveries in one session, compared with paired within-subject tests. **Correct tag: MANIPULATED, WITHIN-SUBJECT, ACUTE** — the tag F-323 already uses for the slide step.

This corpus runs ~30:1 cross-sectional to intervention and treats manipulated evidence as its scarcest asset. **It has been holding one of its rare manipulations in the cheap drawer since August.** Read correctly, the professional results are **MANIPULATED_NULLs** — the strongest grade in the registry — and the collegiate results are small genuine interventions. (F-548)

> **Standing audit item opened by this:** how many other registry entries describe a *within-subject condition comparison* and carry a `CROSS_SECTIONAL` tag? The pattern to look for is a paper that sounds observational but had one athlete perform both arms.

### 4a — "Below MCID" does not settle it

MCID = 0.5 m/s is a **per-pitch clinical threshold**. The decision applies the difference to roughly half of every pitch for a season. A quantity can be below the threshold at which a clinician calls one pitch different and above the threshold at which a season changes. **Treat a published "below MCID" as a statement about the authors' threshold, never about whether a coach should act.** (F-551)

## 5 — The field's argument, and why it does not identify anything

The industry's quantitative case: *"starters throw ~0.1 mph harder with runners on than with the bases empty, so the stretch costs nothing."* Bases-empty-vs-runners-on is a **proxy** for windup-vs-stretch. The corpus already holds the number that breaks it.

```
Observed = Δ_delivery + Δ_clock + Δ_intent
  +0.10  = Δ_delivery +   0.10  + Δ_intent        (F-328: 0.020 mph/s × 5 s)
      ⇒  Δ_delivery(stretch − windup) = −Δ_intent  ≤ 0
```

F-328 states its own confound direction: intent is higher with runners on, so **Δ_intent > 0** and the residual delivery term is **≤ 0**. Add dilution — bases-empty pitches are a **mixture** of windup and stretch throwers — and `Δ_windup = Δ_intent / w ≈ 1.7 × Δ_intent` at w = 0.6.

**This does not show the windup is faster in games.** It shows the field's headline number **cannot be read as evidence about deliveries in either direction**, because a confound the corpus already measured is the same size as the whole effect. (F-552)

⚠️ **And there is no windup/stretch database** — delivery type is not a tracked field in any public dataset, in any league (F-553). That is why the proxy is used, and why its confound is unavoidable rather than lazy. **A college program with its own video can close this gap locally in one season and hold a dataset nobody in the sport has.**

## 6 — The protocol

**THE COUNTERBALANCED DELIVERY TEST.** One athlete, two bullpens, radar only. Fastballs at competitive intent, **blocks of 5, A-B-B-A order, counterbalanced across sessions.** The block alternation and counterbalancing are the whole design — they are what the published studies may lack, and they cost nothing.

`n per condition = 15.70·σ²/Δ²` at α = .05 two-sided, 80% power, with σ = within-pitcher fastball SD (F-289 bracket):

| Detect | σ = 0.8 | σ = 1.0 | σ = 1.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.9 mph** | 13 | 20 | 28 |
| **0.6 mph** | 28 | 44 | 63 |
| 0.3 mph | 112 | 175 | 251 |

*Formula validated against the corpus's own cell: 1.0 mph at σ = 1.2 → 23 per condition (`running-game.md` §5.3). ✓*

**~25 fastballs per delivery — two bullpens — settles a ~0.9 mph gap. ~45 per delivery gets to 0.6. Below 0.3 mph is not measurable in a program and is not worth measuring.**

**DECISION RULE (individual, same shape as F-321):**
- **< ~0.3 mph or no clear signal** → not a velocity question for him; pick on feel and what he will repeat.
- **> ~1 mph** → real windup advantage; do not let him abandon it for a trend.
- **In between** → judgement; §4's table says which way his innings load pushes it.

**PHASE TWO if the gap is real** — four weeks weighting stretch volume, then re-run the identical test. **Gap closes → stretch unfamiliarity, and it is trainable. Gap holds → mechanical, keep the windup.** Nobody has run this.

**DO NOT** evaluate on command (§7) and **DO NOT** evaluate in one bullpen — one bullpen at σ = 1.0 detects about 2 mph. (F-558)

## 7 — What the stretch-only case actually rests on

Two reasons are given. **One is void and the other is unevidenced.**

**"It helps me control the running game."** **VOID BY INSPECTION.** The windup is used **only with the bases empty**. With runners on, every pitcher is already in the stretch. **The stretch-only switch buys exactly zero against baserunners.** This confuses the windup with the **slide step**, which is a different variable, applies only with runners on, and is a genuine trade (F-321, F-323).

**"A simpler delivery repeats better, so I'll command better."** The last link is **already retired here**. **F-175** (n = 344 MLB starters, 300,884 four-seams): release-point variability predicts K/9 and HR/9 — deception — and has essentially **no relationship with BB/9 (R² = 0.011)**. *"Repeat your release point and you'll command the ball"* was retired 2026-08-13. (F-554)

**This is not a refutation of stretch-only.** It is a statement of burden: **the cost is estimated and the benefit is not, and the mechanism usually offered for the benefit has already been thrown out.**

⚠️ **Nobody has ever measured command, strike percentage or location spread from the two deliveries, in any population** (F-555). And a program cannot close that gap either: F-186 puts ~200 tracked pitches behind a 2-inch command gain, so a two-delivery comparison needs **~400 tracked pitches**. **The velocity side takes a week; the command side takes a season.**

## 8 — Mechanism: where the difference lives

The deliveries are statistically indistinguishable in ~74% of measured parameters. What differs: **lead knee height** and **time from leg lift to foot contact**. Industry corroboration (n = 1, vendor-published, snippet-only): Tread Athletics reports deGrom holding **19–21 frames from peak leg lift to ball release in both deliveries** despite a much slower total time from the windup.

**This satisfies F-213 RULE 2 exactly — everything is decided by foot contact — and says the whole delivery difference is generated upstream of it.**

⚠️ **The corpus holds NO finding on leg-lift HEIGHT as a velocity variable in any population.** The nearest entries (F-060, F-061) concern centre-of-mass velocity generated before peak knee height and explicitly label it a measurement, not a cue. **Do not convert this into "lift your leg higher"** — knee height is the one variable that differs and nobody has manipulated it in isolation. It is a marker. (F-559)

**The better question it opens:** if the windup effect lives entirely in momentum built during leg lift, can a stretch-only pitcher recover it by changing what he does *within* the stretch? Untested.

---

## 9 — The stress line, closed

The windup's extra output is paid for in **shoulder horizontal adduction torque and shoulder anterior force**, and the 2026 authors conclude shoulder injury risk may be slightly lower from the stretch. **That is RULE 1 behaving as written — the same bill is payable by any other route to the same velocity.** It belongs in the conversation only if the athlete already has a shoulder reason to prefer the stretch, and it does not change §4's pricing, which is a performance valuation. (F-561) *One line, per the mission.*
