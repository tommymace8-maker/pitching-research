## 1. THE CENTRAL MOVE — "BREATHING WORK" IS THREE CLAIMS, NOT ONE

The industry sells one bundle. It contains three mechanisms, three literatures and three verdicts, and they do not stand or fall together. Separating them is most of what this topic required.

| | Channel A — VENTILATORY | Channel B — MECHANICAL | Channel C — AUTONOMIC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intervention** | IMT / RMT devices; apnea & CO₂-tolerance work | Breath state and abdominal bracing during the delivery | Slow-paced breathing; HRV biofeedback |
| **Mechanism** | Delay inspiratory muscle fatigue; attenuate the respiratory metaboreflex | Breath state sets intra-abdominal pressure → trunk stiffness → force transmission | Raise cardiac vagal activity; lower state anxiety |
| **Target outcome** | Endurance, within-outing retention | Velocity | Command |
| **Literature** | Large, meta-analysed, randomised | **One** manipulated study, not in throwers | Real, but wrong populations and wrong outcomes |
| **Mechanism verdict** | **DEAD in this task** (F-672) | **LIVE but direction unknown** (F-670) | **LIVE** (F-676) |
| **Cost** | Non-zero — adds training load (F-680) | Zero (a cue) | ~Zero |
| **VERDICT** | **DECLINE** | **REMOVE the cue, install nothing** | **ACT, with a process check only** |

## 2. CHANNEL A — WHY THE VENTILATORY STORY HAS NO PURCHASE

IMT's performance mechanism is not "stronger lungs." It is (i) delaying diaphragm and intercostal fatigue during **sustained** near-maximal ventilation, and (ii) attenuating the **respiratory metaboreflex**, the sympathetic limb vasoconstriction that fatiguing respiratory muscles trigger and which steals perfusion from locomotor muscle. **Both require minutes of near-maximal ventilation.**

Price the task against **F-132**: phosphagen-dominant; high-velocity portion **0.05–0.15 s**; delivery **1.5–2 s**; **15–25 s** between pitches; minutes between innings. **The pitcher never sustains the ventilation that generates a metaboreflex, and his inspiratory muscles are never the limiting tissue.** → **F-672.**

**What the literature shows, and what it does not:**

- Pooled sports performance for linear-workload IMT: **SMD 0.64 [0.35, 0.92]**; MIP **MD −29.42 cmH₂O [−35.65, −23.20]**. **No VO₂max effect** across 13 studies measuring it.
- **Every outcome contributing to that pooled effect is a continuous fixed-distance task** — 400 m, 800 m, swim splits.
- **Throwing outcomes in the entire RMT literature: ZERO** (F-673). The nearest randomised evidence in overhead athletes is **core/trunk** training (10 RCTs, standing-throw velocity improved, step and jump throws not), a different intervention.
- Two trials in progress (CrossFit, wrestlers) have outcomes of respiratory pressure, trunk endurance and balance. **The gap will still be open when they finish.**

**Causal ledger: unusually good — randomised and controlled throughout. Check #2 passes. Check #3 fails decisively.** The number is a real lever; it does not measure what the pitching claim says it measures.

**Apnea / CO₂-tolerance work (F-674).** 7 studies, n = 126: possible increase in **peak blood lactate**, no clear aerobic benefit. 2025 review: no haematological or aerobic improvement in novices, questionable in trained. **Per F-132 the pitch is alactic.** The one outcome this adaptation has any claim on is the one energy system the task does not use — **structurally identical to F-660**, two days apart. And the result's shape is **test-specific adaptation**: breath-hold capacity improves, performance does not — the F-571 pattern in a third domain.

**The screening instrument is debunked (F-675).** BOLT against Wingate and CPET in **n = 49 elite speedskaters: no significant relationship.** The authors say it "has not yet been scientifically validated in sports settings." The **<20 s / >30 s thresholds come from the test's commercial promoter**, not from validation research. ⭐ Note the sample: this is the rare case where restriction of range works *for* us — the null was found **in** a selected population, not inferred down into one.

**The cost (F-680).** A randomised trial in well-trained athletes found RMT "induces additional stress and training load," with its own authors flagging that the psychophysiological costs are understudied. **F-610's argument form closes the decision: a cost can be declined without an exchange rate when neither side of the trade is priceable in the outcome you care about.**

## 3. CHANNEL B — THE CUE, AND THE ONE STUDY THAT CONTRADICTS IT

**The cue, as the industry states it:** inhale as you receive the sign and set the grip, hold briefly as you come set, **exhale through the delivery** — commonly bundled with "draw the belly button in" or "hollow." The source is a mental-training content site that **cites no data.** Variants are near-universal in pitching instruction.

**The only study that ever manipulated breath/abdominal state against rotational output** — Kimura et al. (2026), *IJSPT* 21(3):262–271, PubMed 41777431, **crossover randomised, n = 15 healthy young adult males**, seated isometric trunk rotation, catheter IAP sensor, custom trunk-rotation dynamometer:

| Condition | IAP | Peak trunk rotation torque |
|---|---|---|
| Spontaneous rotation | **96.7 ± 31.4 mmHg** | **68.7 ± 20.2 Nm** |
| Abdominal drawing-in | **34.9 ± 15.9 mmHg** | **49.1 ± 17.7 Nm** |
| Change | **−63.9%**, p < 0.01, **d = 2.48** | **−28.5%**, p < 0.01, **d = 1.03** |

Authors' mechanism: drawing-in reduces inspiratory volume and abdominal circumference, **removing the mechanical basis for pressure generation.**

**Converging evidence.** Lumbar stiffness rises with respiratory effort and is **greatest at maximum expiration**, while during **tidal** breathing the IAP rise is "not sufficient to improve spinal stiffness" (*J Appl Physiol* 2002). In lifting, IAP rises **"to a greater extent with breath holding."**

**THREE OBJECTIONS, ALL SERIOUS:**

1. **POPULATION.** 15 healthy young adult males, training status unstated. **SAMPLE MISMATCH — DIRECTIONAL ONLY.**
2. **CONSTRUCT.** Seated **isometric** rotation against a dynamometer. A pitch is standing, ballistic, unresisted, trunk at **900–1,200 °/s**. **The most severe construct swap logged since F-564.**
3. **DETECTION FLOOR (F-671).** n = 15 paired → **d_min = 0.72**. IAP (d = 2.48) clears by **3.4×** and is robust. **Torque (d = 1.03) clears by 1.42×** — and torque is the half that bears on pitching.

**AND THE DIRECTION MAY BE BACKWARD.** IAP's mechanical job is transmitting torque to an **external resistance**. The dynamometer is near-infinite resistance; a 145 g baseball is almost none. In a ballistic unresisted rotation, high trunk stiffness may **impede** the proximal deceleration that passes energy distally — so the 28.5% could be measuring a quality a pitcher needs **less** of. → **Dispute #71, unresolved.**

**THE DEFENSIBLE ACTION IS REMOVAL, NOT REPLACEMENT.** Stop giving a mechanical breathing instruction that never had data behind it. Do **not** install "hold your breath through the delivery," which nobody has measured and which is exactly the instruction that produces a stiff, slow pitcher.

## 4. ⭐⭐ CHANNEL C — AND THE REFRAME THAT ORGANISES THE TOPIC

**Cornell et al. (2017), *JSCR* 31(1): 16 professional starting pitchers, 682 innings, in-game HR = 84.8 ± 3.9% of age-predicted HRmax.** Home innings 1–2 exceeded all others; **first inning home 87.3 ± 3.6% vs away 85.8 ± 3.8%.** The authors read the overall figure as evidence pitching is "mainly an anaerobic task."

**Put that next to F-132 and the two statements cannot both be metabolic.** A task working 0.05–0.15 s at a time with 15–25 s of rest **cannot** generate a genuine 85% HRmax metabolic demand. → **F-676: in-game heart rate in a pitcher is an AROUSAL signal, not a metabolic one.**

⚠️ **Do not quote 84.8% as precise.** Age-predicted HRmax (220 − age) carries an individual SEE of **±10–12 bpm**; at a true HRmax of 195 that moves "84.8%" to anywhere from ~80% to ~90%. **The dissociation survives the whole bracket** — no denominator in it makes the figure explicable by an alactic task. ⭐ **The stronger evidence is the home/away first-inning contrast (87.3 vs 85.8%): a within-study, within-subject comparison of two conditions with identical metabolic content, and a ratio of the same soft denominator, so the error cancels.**

**Three consequences:**
1. Reinforces F-132 — conditioning-for-starts remains folklore.
2. A pitcher's HR strap is an **arousal instrument**: usable as a process check, never as an outcome.
3. **The only breathing channel with a live mechanism is the autonomic one, and its target is COMMAND.** The entire respiratory-training literature is built for the channel this task does not have.

**What channel C's literature contains (F-677) — right mechanism, wrong populations, wrong outcomes:**

| Study | Design | Population | Outcome | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slow-paced breathing, n = 75 | Acute, within-subject | Athletes | Cardiac vagal activity (RMSSD, LF) **up in ALL SPB conditions vs control** | Single session; outcome is HRV, **and the effect was not specific to 0.1 Hz** |
| 4-wk SPB in weightlifting recovery | Controlled, 4 weeks | **Young academy baseball** | Resting HRV; anaerobic performance | **SAMPLE MISMATCH**; channel-A′ outcomes on a channel-C intervention |
| HRV biofeedback, n = 18 | **Randomised**, 20 min/d × 10 d | University baseball | **Cognitive anxiety reduced**; performance outcome was **BATTING**, unconfirmed | **d_min = 1.32 at 9/arm (F-678)** — uninformative about magnitude |
| Musicians | Randomised | Musicians | HF, LF/HF improved under performance anticipation | **Biofeedback added NOTHING over the breathing itself** |

**Not one study in this channel has a pitching outcome of any kind.**

**BUY NOTHING.** The musician trial is the operative one for equipment.

## 5. THE PRESCRIPTION, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

**ADD — between-inning down-regulation (channel C).** *"Before you pick up the ball: slow your breathing down, make the exhale longer than the inhale, about six breaths. It's for the first inning and for innings after you've been scored on."*

⚠️ **Deliberately imprecise, and that is the corrected version.** The first draft specified 4 s in / 6 s out at 0.1 Hz. That was withdrawn in cross-examination: resonance frequency is individual, the acute athlete evidence found vagal elevation across **all** slow-paced conditions rather than specifically at 6/min, and the subject is a pitcher at ~85% HRmax ninety seconds after an inning, not a seated non-athlete at rest. **False precision in a cue is the same error as false precision in a number.**

**REMOVE — the delivery breathing cue (channel B).** Stop saying it. If asked: "breathe however you breathe; don't suck your stomach in."

**DECLINE — devices (channel A).** No IMT/RMT device, no CO₂-tolerance protocol, no BOLT screening.

**ON VIDEO, THE FAILURES LOOK LIKE:** channel C — the pitcher walking off the mound straight into conversation at the dugout rail, i.e. never doing it; and in-game, a tempo that accelerates after a hit. Channel B — a visible abdominal hollow or rib flare at the set, and an audible exhale beginning **before** front-foot strike rather than after release.

**HOW WE KNOW IT IS WORKING — AND THE HONEST ANSWER (F-681):**

| Channel | Outcome | n needed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A / B | +1.0 mph fastball, two-arm | **106 pitchers** | Unavailable to a college program |
| A / B | +0.5 mph | **422 pitchers** | Unavailable |
| C | +10 pp zone rate, within-outing | **~392 pitches/condition** (F-374) | Unavailable to an individual |
| C | Same, clustering-corrected (m = 25, ρ = 0.1 → 3.4×) | **~1,330 pitches/condition** | Unavailable; and ρ is an assumption, so this is a **FLOOR** |

**The cruel arithmetic: the only channel with a live mechanism is the one this program cannot measure.** What IS available for channel C is a **PROCESS check** — with an HR strap, did between-inning HR recovery change, yes or no. **That is not evidence the pitch got better and must never be reported as if it were.**

## 6. ⭐⭐ THE DECISION RULE — the most portable output of this cycle

The standing objection to this corpus is that detection arithmetic turns it into a program of inaction. **Today it did not, and the reason is worth stating as a rule.** Under *identical* undetectability, the three channels earned three different verdicts:

1. **COST ZERO + MECHANISM LIVE → ACT**, and label the check a **PROCESS** check, never an outcome check. *(Channel C.)*
2. **COST NON-ZERO + MECHANISM DEAD → DECLINE.** *(Channel A — F-672, F-673, F-680.)*
3. **DIRECTION UNKNOWN → REMOVE the unfounded instruction and INSTALL NOTHING.** *(Channel B — F-670.)*

Undetectability is not a verdict. **It forbids claiming an outcome; it does not decide the action.** Mechanism and cost decide the action.
