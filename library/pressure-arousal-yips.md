## 0. Why this file exists, and what it did NOT find

INDEX §5 listed competitive anxiety, routines and focus under fatigue as **entirely uncovered**, and escalated it on 2026-09-13 when F-330 showed that the extra seconds a pitcher wants before a big pitch are metabolically the *cheapest* seconds available to him — relocating their value into a psychological channel this corpus did not cover.

There was a second, better reason to come here. **This corpus runs roughly 30:1 cross-sectional to intervention.** Sport psychology is one of the few adjacent fields where genuine manipulations are possible: you can randomise a person into a pressure condition in a way you can never randomise him into a stride length. The cycle came looking for levers.

**It found the same disease, in a field that hides it better.** In the current challenge/threat meta-analysis, **5 of 62 studies were direct-experimental and 57 were correlational or indirect** (F-372). That is 11.4:1 against this corpus's 30:1 — a difference of degree, not of kind, expressed throughout in the imperative voice. **Sport psychology is not the escape from the marker/lever problem. Anyone quoting challenge/threat state as a lever is doing what this corpus retired stride length and extension for doing.**

## 1. The one real manipulation — and what it can and cannot carry

**Oshikawa T, Morimoto Y, Adachi G, Akuzawa H, Kaneoka K (2020).** *Changes in lumbar kinematics and trunk muscle electromyographic activity during baseball batting under psychological pressure.* **International Biomechanics 7(1):66–75.** doi `10.1080/23335432.2020.1811765`. PMC8130725 / PMID 33998388. **READ IN FULL.**

### 1.1 The design

n = 14 male collegiate players, age 21 ± 1, **13 ± 1 years' experience, 4 h/day 6 days/week on an elite collegiate team**. Within-subject, three conditions: non-pressure; pressure (reward varied **±1 USD per ±1 km/h** of bat-tip velocity against their own baseline); emphasized pressure (after a **false** report of a 3 km/h improvement).

**The manipulation check passed decisively.** STAI Y-1 **32.4 ± 6.4 → 45.4 ± 6.9 → 44.4 ± 7.1**, F(2,26) = 36.545, p < 0.001, **partial η² = 0.738**.

### 1.2 What happened — output went UP

| Outcome | Non-pressure | Pressure | Emphasized | p | η²ₚ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bat-tip peak velocity (m/s) | 30.4 ± 3.2 | 32.6 ± 4.7 | **34.1 ± 3.1** | 0.003 | 0.367 |
| Swing-phase duration (ms) | 257.9 ± 48.7 | 219.0 ± 33.5 | **212.5 ± 28.4** | <0.001 | 0.609 |
| Peak lumbar flexion (°) | 27.9 ± 8.0 | 32.4 ± 9.4 | 35.3 ± 9.8 | <0.001 | 0.606 |
| ROM lumbar ext/flex (°) | 24.1 ± 7.1 | 29.8 ± 8.1 | 31.2 ± 8.8 | <0.001 | 0.692 |
| Peak ang. vel. lumbar ext. (°/s) | 234.4 ± 69.4 | 278.0 ± 72.4 | 285.4 ± 84.7 | 0.003 | 0.450 |

**+3.7 m/s = +12.2% of peak output. Swing phase −45.4 ms = −17.6%.** Preparation, weight-shift and follow-through durations did **not** change (p = 0.133, 0.319, 0.506) — only the business end compressed (F-366).

### 1.3 The nulls, which matter as much

External and internal oblique EMG: **no change under any condition.** Peak lumbar rotation angle (p = 0.076 lead, 0.228 back), rotation angular velocity (p = 0.201, 0.072), lateral-flexion ROM (p = 0.745), rectus abdominis, lumbar multifidus: all null. What *did* rise was bilateral **lumbar erector spinae** in the swing and follow-through (p = 0.009, 0.019).

The authors' explanation, and it is the most transferable idea in the paper: the obliques "were already extremely high in the non-pressure condition, and could not increase." **Pressure does not add output to structures already maximal. It loads the ones with headroom** (F-367).

⚠️ The p = 0.062–0.076 near-misses are **uninterpretable, not null**: the power analysis targeted partial η² = 0.14, so the study was built to detect only large effects.

### 1.4 THE THREE REASONS THE MAGNITUDE IS UNUSABLE

1. **The order confound is total, and the authors state it:** *"the trials of each condition could not be performed randomly because the psychological pressure must be increased gradually."* Non-pressure always came first, after an "optional" 10-minute warm-up. **A monotone 30.4 → 32.6 → 34.1 sequence is exactly what progressive warm-up produces.** There is no counterbalanced arm. The +12% is an upper bound on a pressure effect and is equally consistent with zero.
2. **Incentive is not anxiety.** The reward was tied *directly to the outcome variable*. Being paid per km/h and swinging harder is a motivation result, and the STAI cannot separate the two accounts. Note that in a game the incentive is aligned to **outcome**, not to effort — the opposite arrangement.
3. **The task is a dry swing.** No ball, self-paced, 5 reps with the middle 3 analysed. **There is no accuracy outcome, so the study cannot speak to choking at all** — and choking is what a coach means by pressure.

**What survives: the SIGN, weakly. Nothing else.**

## 2. Why the sign is nonetheless defensible — the physiology

The acute stress response is sympathoadrenal: circulating catecholamines, raised motor-neuron pool excitability, lowered recruitment threshold, raised firing rate. **Every item on that list raises maximal voluntary output.** This is the mechanism behind the universal observation that pitchers throw harder in games than in bullpens.

**And the same mechanism is the command tax.** Force output and force *variability* scale together — signal-dependent noise means raising the drive raises the variance of the drive. **You cannot take the velocity gift without the command tax; they are one neural event seen at two scales.**

⚠️ **GRADE: MECHANISM, NOT FINDING.** Challenged in cross-examination ① and conceded: *not one of the six texts read measured throwing accuracy under a pressure manipulation.* Signal-dependent noise is well established in laboratory isometric tasks; its magnitude at a 95 mph release is unmeasured. Read §2 as a prediction. The settling test is in §5.3.

**The ceiling argument (F-367) makes the prediction specific.** An 85+ arm at full intent is already at or near its tissue limit — the premise of half this corpus. So under leverage **the arm cannot contribute more, and everything extra arrives from the trunk and the lower half.** That is F-366's timing compression. It also means **"reach back for a little more" has no arm-side referent at this level**: whatever he finds, he finds downstream of the arm.

## 3. The yips — three source-verified results that dismantle both standard framings

### 3.1 "It's a mechanics problem" — DEBUNKED

**Aoyama et al. (2024),** Scientific Reports 14:2649, doi `10.1038/s41598-024-52332-9`, PMC10834996. n = 22 (12 yips, 10 control), 13 EMG channels, VICON 250 Hz, full-effort throws from the stretch to a bullseye **5 m** away, synergies via non-negative matrix factorization.

Four synergies extracted in both groups; **no significant difference in spatial or temporal pattern.** Phase proportions null: early cocking 81.9% vs 83.6% (p = 0.628), late cocking 14.9% vs 13.9% (p = 0.821), acceleration 3.5% vs 2.8% (p = 0.203).

And from the 2021 survey: **only 3 of 25 non-psychologically-triggered cases had a mechanics change as the trigger.**

### 3.2 "It's an anxiety problem" — TRUE FOR A MINORITY, SOLD AS TRUE FOR ALL

**Aoyama et al. (2021),** Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 3:652792, doi `10.3389/fspor.2021.652792`, PMC8424038. **n = 107**, First Division of the Tokyo Metropolitan Area Collegiate Baseball League, age 20.3 ± 1.3, experience 12.5 ± 2.1 y.

| Trigger group | n | % of 107 | % of the 49 yips cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Psychologically triggered | 19 | 17.8% | **38.8%** |
| Non-psychologically triggered | 25 | 23.4% | **51.0%** |
| Pain-triggered | 5 | 4.7% | 10.2% |
| Non-yips | 55 | 51.4% | — |

**49 of 107 = 45.8% reported a yips history. 61.2% of cases had no psychological trigger.**

Inside the psychological group: **"experience of throwing error" n = 11**, "anxiety about throwing" n = 5, "reprimand from others" n = 2, "fear-related throwing experience" n = 1. **The most common psychological trigger is a physical event — the bad throw came first.** Inside the non-psychological group: **22 of 25 had no identifiable trigger at all.**

Median onset **age 17**. Duration 10 months (psych) vs 5.5 (non-psych), n.s. Acute vs gradual 9/10 vs 5/20, **p = 0.054** (trend only).

And from 2024: **state anxiety yips 34.1 ± 5.9 vs control 34.5 ± 5.3, p = 0.865; trait 45.2 ± 5.4 vs 43.5 ± 4.9, p = 0.460.** ⚠️ n = 22 — this is "no *large* anxiety difference," not "no difference," and state anxiety was sampled while 9 of 12 were asymptomatic.

⚠️ **45.8% IS NOT A PREVALENCE RATE.** It is an anonymous self-report response to a question containing the word "yips," on one league, in one country. Read it as "this is common."

**Typing in the 2024 sample: 4 Type I (focal dystonia), 6 Type II (choking), 2 Type III (both). Roughly half ARE the anxiety-type presentation. The error is universality, in both directions.**

### 3.3 THE ONE THAT CHANGES WHAT A COACH DOES — it is invisible when it isn't happening

Only **3 of 12** yips players reproduced symptoms in the lab, despite a design deliberately built to provoke them (the authors chose 5 m because their own survey found symptoms far more likely at ≤10 m than ≥50 m). A **blinded neurologist** confirmed objective dystonic movement in all three. **Deviant synergy patterns appeared in 2 of those 3 — and in NONE of the 9 who stayed asymptomatic.**

> **A throw that looks normal IS normal at the motor-coordination level, even in an affected player.**
> **Film of a clean throw is not diagnostic. Stop collecting it. You have to capture the episode or you have nothing.**

⚠️ The positive result is **n = 2**. It carries no weight. The triage in §4 rests on §3.2, which is whole samples.

### 3.4 ⚠️ WHAT A COACH MUST NOT SAY

The onset profile (median age 17, ~a decade of daily stereotyped repetition) and symptom profile (task-, distance- and intent-specific involuntary movement) **match focal task-specific dystonia** — writer's cramp, embouchure dystonia, musician's dystonia — and Aoyama's own Type I/II/III scheme already carries that idea.

**It is a hypothesis with a matching profile. It is NOT a diagnosis, and no coach should voice the word to a player.** *(Corrected within cycle — see §7.)* The correct act is narrower: **describe what you observed to a physician and name nothing.**

## 4. What the coach actually does

### 4.1 Stop warning him about pressure

Every velocity-facing mechanism in §2 says the adrenaline is on his side. **"Pressure makes you worse" is manufacturing a problem the physiology does not have.**

> **SAY:** *"The nerves are not going to take velocity away from you."*
> **DO NOT SAY:** any number. "A mile and a half" attributes a magnitude to a confounded design (§1.4, §7).

### 4.2 Give the tax somewhere to go

"Pressure makes me worse" is unusable. **"Pressure buys me velocity and charges me the corner"** is usable — it is an in-game decision rule: *stop trying to paint, take the middle-third strike, let the extra velocity work.*

> **DRILL:** manufactured-leverage bullpen with the target on the **middle third, not the corner** (F-282's manufactured-count block is the template). Manufacture the leverage; do not manufacture a smaller target.
> **ON VIDEO:** at 240 fps, shorter foot-contact-to-release in the leverage set than the routine set, same day, same camera — trunk early, arm where it always is.

### 4.3 Sell the breath honestly

F-330 already established the pre-pitch seconds are metabolically nearly free. §6.2 shows breathing training moves **anxiety and breathing rate** and does not demonstrably move performance.

> **SAY:** *"The breath is for how the inning feels. I'm not going to tell you it adds a strike, because nobody has shown that."*
> Keep it inside the between-innings routine he already has (F-358). There it costs nothing. As a training block it is a promise the literature cannot keep.

### 4.4 THE TRIAGE — the cycle's one genuinely actionable output, and it is SUBTRACTIVE

Of the players carrying a "he's in his own head" label: **roughly three in five never had a psychological trigger, and as a group they are not measurably more anxious than anyone else.** The default response — more mental-skills work, more talking about it, more confidence cues — is aimed at a mechanism absent in the majority of them.

> **THE DRILL IS A ONE-PAGE TRIGGER AND DISTANCE AUDIT.** Two weeks. Every occurrence: distance, intent level, who was watching, game or practice, acute or gradual onset. **Bias sampling to short distances (≤10 m) at full intent** — where Aoyama's survey says symptoms reproduce.
> **THE OUTPUT IS A CLASSIFICATION AND A MATCHED REFERRAL:**
> - Acute onset traceable to a specific throwing event, symptoms tied to being watched → **sport psychology.**
> - Gradual onset, no trigger, short-distance-and-low-intent specific, visible involuntary movement → **physician. Describe the observation. Name nothing** (§3.4).
> **⚠️ Median onset age 17 means you will see this in freshmen.**

## 5. The detection arithmetic — derived in-cycle

Two-sample, α = 0.05 two-sided, 80% power: **n per group = 2(1.96 + 0.84)²σ²/Δ² = 15.68 σ²/Δ².**

### 5.1 Velocity — the corpus's second same-week instrument

Within-pitcher fastball SD bracketed at **σ = 0.8–1.2 mph** (F-289 — still unpublished anywhere, still one query against any radar log). To detect **Δ = 1.0 mph**:

| σ (mph) | Fastballs per condition |
|---|---|
| 0.8 | **10** |
| 1.0 | **16** |
| 1.2 | **23** |

**⚠️ THE DESIGN WAS CORRECTED WITHIN CYCLE (§7).** The first draft proposed *bullpen vs game*, which fails: those populations differ in warm-up, mound, ball, catcher, hitter presence, time of day and intent — the same order-confound family that killed §1. **The correct design is WITHIN-OUTING: high-leverage versus low-leverage fastballs inside the same starts**, holding warm-up, mound, ball and day constant. Accumulating 16–23 high-leverage fastballs takes a few starts, not one, and still lands inside a season.

### 5.2 Command — not available at all

Zone rate, p ≈ 0.5 so σ² = 0.25:

| Δ (pp of zone rate) | Pitches per condition |
|---|---|
| 10 | **392** |
| 15 | **174** |
| 20 | **98** |

And these are **optimistic** — they ignore the catcher contamination F-309 sized, and the count-composition confound (F-287, F-294).

> **THE ASYMMETRY, FOR THE FIFTH TIME IN THIS CORPUS:** what pressure gives you is measurable in a week; what it costs you is not measurable at all at the individual level. F-287, F-295, F-307 and F-320 are the same shape.
> **A coach who believes he has *seen* a pitcher's command degrade under pressure has seen about 30 pitches and a memory.**

### 5.3 The better outcome variable, and it is free

**Release-point SD, not zone rate.** A continuous measure carries far more information per pitch than a binary one, it is already in every TrackMan log, and it is the direct observable of the signal-dependent-noise prediction in §2. *Fifth entry in the pattern of F-264, F-289, F-295 and F-307: already in every program's own data, and nobody has published it.*

## 6. The mental-skills industry, priced

### 6.1 Arousal reappraisal — "turn your nerves into excitement"

**Bosshard M, Gomez P (2024),** Scientific Reports 14:7923, doi `10.1038/s41598-024-58408-w`, PMC10994935. **35 studies, 44 effect sizes, RCTs only.**

- Pooled **d = 0.23 [0.14, 0.33]**, I² = 52.6%.
- **Prediction interval [−0.18, 0.65]** — a competent future trial has a material chance of finding it makes performance *worse*.
- **Trim-and-fill imputed 11 missing studies; the effect falls to d = 0.14.** p-curve inconclusive.
- Subgroups: SAR-only k = 33, d = 0.22; **mixed interventions d = 0.45 — on k = 5.** That is the number the industry will quote, because it describes what practitioners actually do.
- **THE DECISIVE ABSENCE: the task taxonomy has two categories — cognitive written (k = 30) and public performance (k = 14). There is no motor-skill subgroup, because there were not enough motor-skill studies to make one.**

**The industry's most confident mental cue is established on maths exams and public speaking.** Not a debunking — the effect is probably real and small. The failure is transfer and confidence. **Quote the prediction interval or quote nothing.**

### 6.2 HRV biofeedback — and a published claim its own table contradicts

**Su Y-T, Huang P-H, Hsiao T-C (2025),** J Funct Morphol Kinesiol 10(1):65, doi `10.3390/jfmk10010065`, PMC11843864. **READ IN FULL.**

Abstract: *"Compared to the control group … batting performance improved significantly (9.8 ± 11.7 → 19.8 ± 12.0; p < 0.05)."*

**Table 7, as printed:**

| | Batting score Ctrl | Batting score Exp | Infield % Ctrl | Infield % Exp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trial 1 | 11.6 ± 11.7 | 9.8 ± 11.7 | 22 ± 22 | 17 ± 20 |
| Trial 2 | 18.4 ± 11.8 | 19.8 ± 12.0 | 35 ± 23 | 31 ± 15 |
| **Δ** | **+6.8** | **+10.0** | **+13** | **+14** |

**The control group improved by +6.8 and +13 with no intervention.** The asterisks mark *within*-group change for the experimental group only. **No group × time interaction is reported anywhere.** The between-group difference is +3.2 against SDs of ~12 with **n = 9 per group**; on infield ratio it is a dead heat. The parsimonious reading is ten days of familiarisation with a **120 km/h (74.6 mph) machine**, which both groups got. Reported power: 0.63 batting score, 0.34 cognitive anxiety, 0.2–0.56 on HRV indices. Sample: experience **6.4 ± 4.1 y** — a club-level team at a science-and-technology university. **SAMPLE MISMATCH.**

**THE HALF THAT STANDS:** CSAI-2 cognitive anxiety 23.56 → 20.11 (p = 0.036) vs control unchanged; stressor breathing rate 24.5 → 19.8 (p = 0.028). **HRVB plausibly did what breathing training does. It did not demonstrably improve hitting.**

> **NOTHING WAS FABRICATED HERE, AND THAT IS THE POINT.** Journal real, DOI resolves, authors real — every fabrication defence passes. **The error is in the one sentence everybody reads, and only the table catches it.** Second time in three cycles (F-353, F-354). **A control group that improves is a finding, and the abstract is where it goes to die.**

### 6.3 ⚠️ A NEW FABRICATION SPECIES

A search summariser emitted, unprompted and with no supporting link in its own result set, an attribution to *"The Athletic's 2026 report on the yips."* No such article was retrieved or linked, and this corpus has no route to check it.

**The hazard is that the claim is probably TRUE** — it paraphrases what Aoyama 2024 actually found, which this cycle read independently at source.

> **A fabricated citation attached to a correct claim is more dangerous than one attached to a false claim, because every check on the CONTENT passes — and content-checking is the defence that caught all 16 previously-logged fabrications.**

> **NEW STANDING RULE: an attribution is a separate claim from the proposition it carries, and must be verified separately. A correct fact does not launder its citation.** When a search summary names a source that does not appear as a link in its own result set, that attribution is **UNVERIFIED regardless of whether the claim is right**, and may not be written into a finding as a source.
