## 1. Why this topic was opened

It is the most widely practised and least examined conditioning decision in pitching. Before today the corpus held exactly two objects on it: **F-042** (Rhea 2008, n = 16 D1 **position players**, no velocity outcome) and **F-132** (the alactic-task physiology, graded MECHANISM). Both carried a practice recommendation — sprint, don't run poles — that nothing in the corpus had actually tested.

## 2. The citation audit, and why it matters most

**The field's founding citation against distance running is broken.** Potteiger, Williford, Blessing & Smidt (1992), vol. 6:2–6 — journal given as *J Appl Sport Sci Res* by one citing source and *J Strength Cond Res* by others.

| What the chain says | Source |
|---|---|
| "21 male NAIA collegiate baseball **players**, 4 ×/wk, 1-h sessions" | UNLV 2023 thesis |
| "a collegiate **pitching staff** in two groups of **eight**, over a season" | Cressey |
| "**18 Division II pitchers**" | third-party relay — **a CONFLATION with a different study** |
| "+**3.0%** velocity, +**4.2%** anaerobic power" | **TopVelocity**, a site selling a velocity program |

**Three incompatible sample descriptions of one paper, and the magnitudes in circulation originate with a vendor.** The comparison arm was **aerobic dance**, not running. The sprint arm **also lifted**. So the design cannot isolate the endurance variable, has no untrained control, and says nothing about distance running.

→ **F-683.** Two re-import vectors logged: the 3.0%/4.2% pair, and the "18 DII pitchers" descriptor.

## 3. The only real experiment in pitchers

**Rabena & Moran (2019), *Int J Exerc Sci* 12(6):726–734.** Randomised, **n = 13 collegiate pitchers**, 11 weeks, in season, steady-state vs interval.

| Outcome | p |
|---|---|
| One-mile run time (favouring steady state) | **0.007** |
| 30-m sprint | 0.15 |
| **Pitching velocity** | **0.25** |
| WHIP | 0.75 |
| Fatigue index | 0.79 |
| Soreness / readiness | 0.52 |

**The adaptation happened. Nothing a pitcher sells moved.** And the distance group **did not lose sprint speed** — the one direct test of the sprint-only camp's central claim, in pitchers, failed to show it.

⚠️ **n = 13 could not have detected anything a program cares about.** F-681 prices 1 mph of mean velocity between groups at **~106 pitchers**. The correct reading is *"the one real experiment was far too small,"* not *"modality doesn't matter."* → **F-684**.

## 4. What the general literature actually shows

**Schumann et al. (2022), *Sports Med* 52:601–612, 43 studies, n = 1,090** (coefficients read at one remove, NOT source-verified):

| Outcome | Pooled SMD | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength | ≈ −0.06 | 37 |
| Hypertrophy | ≈ −0.01 | 15 |
| **Explosive strength** | **−0.28, p = 0.007** | 18 |

**Failed as moderators** (concordant with an updated SportRxiv meta-analysis): modality, exercise type, exercise order, frequency, age, training status.
**Survived:** *same session* worse than separated sessions.

**So: the interference effect is real, small, and lands only in the column pitching lives in.** → **F-685**, **F-690**.

⚠️ **DO NOT CONVERT −0.28 INTO MPH.** The corpus holds no transfer coefficient from a jump/RFD SMD to ball velocity, and **F-438 → F-449** is why: the jump–velocity link in pitchers is **r ≈ 0.07 at n = 33**.

⚠️ **"Bike, don't run" is retired as a velocity argument.** Wilson 2012 said running was worse; Sabag 2018 is relayed as finding the reverse; the two newest pooled analyses say modality is not a moderator. **1 : 1 : 2.** → **F-686**, **Dispute #73**.

## 5. The pro-aerobic case, and its three inflation routes

***Sports* 4(1):14 (2016), PMID 29910262. n = 24 MLB pitchers (14 SP / 10 RP), seven seasons 2007–2013**, estimated VO2max (modified Bruce). Starters above vs below group-mean VO2max differed on performance; VO2max correlated with K, ERA, WHIP. Starters **49.49 ± 4.59** vs relievers **45.28 ± 5.72** mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (via a citing thesis). A second organisation reports pitcher mean **53.64** — the two means disagree by more than an SD, so **no norm can be set.**

**CROSS_SECTIONAL. Three routes by which it is inflated before anyone trains anything:**
1. **Median split** — 7 vs 7 starters, split on a between-subject variable that tracks body composition, age, role and training history.
2. **Multiplicity** — many pitching metrics against VO2max at n = 24, no correction retrieved.
3. **Survivorship** — seven seasons of panel data in which good pitchers keep appearing. The arrow plausibly runs **performance → fitness**, or both from a third thing.

→ **F-687.** **Never phrase this as an instruction.**

## 6. The recovery mechanism, and what it actually predicts

PCr resynthesis is oxidative, so the recovery argument has a genuine mechanism (F-132). But:

- **No PCr recovery kinetics has ever been measured in a pitcher, at any level.**
- **F-676** removed the indirect support: in-game HR = **84.8 ± 3.9% of age-predicted HRmax** in 16 pro starters over 682 innings, in an **alactic** task — so HR is an **arousal** signal, not a metabolic one, and cannot evidence a metabolic ceiling.
- **3–5 min restores PCr near-completely, and a half-inning is usually longer than that.** The mechanism's bite, if any, is **between pitches at 15–25 s**, inside the fast phase — which makes the **pitch clock**, not the conditioning program, the live variable.

Critically: **the mechanism does not predict more velocity. It predicts less velocity DECAY.** → **F-688**.

## 7. ⭐⭐ The one actionable output — swap the outcome, not the training

Every conditioning study in pitchers chose an endpoint it could not see. **F-289 already priced the endpoint the mechanism actually predicts.**

Fastballs needed **per bucket** to detect a within-outing drop (two-sample, α = .05, 80% power, SD = 1.0 mph):

| Drop | Fastballs per bucket |
|---|---|
| 0.5 mph | 63 |
| **1.0 mph** | **16** |
| 1.5 mph | 7 |
| 2.0 mph | 4 |

**A Friday starter supplies ~16 fastballs in innings 1–2 and ~16 in innings 5–6 with no special effort.** Against **~106 pitchers** for 1 mph of mean velocity (F-681). **Roughly an order of magnitude cheaper, and it is the right endpoint.**

**Design:** within-athlete. Decay = (innings 1–2 mean) − (innings 5–6 mean), fastballs only, ≥16 per bucket, across 4–6 starts before and 4–6 after a conditioning block. Each arm is its own control.

⚠️ **Two honest limits.** (1) The 1.0 mph within-outing SD is a **corpus assumption**, never published for an 85+ arm — measure it from the existing radar log first. (2) F-289 prices detecting the decay, **not** detecting a CHANGE IN the decay across a block, which needs the between-start SD of the decay itself — a quantity the corpus does not hold.

⚠️ **Do not run it as a between-subject correlation across a 12-man staff.** Restriction of range (Luera 2020, r = .17–.29 in all-hard-throwing samples) plus n = 12 makes that hopeless.

→ **F-689**.

## 8. Gaps (four-angle sweep, search-only)

1. ⚠️ **No conditioning study in pitchers reports its sample's mean velocity.** Not one. The 85 mph floor cannot be checked against any object in this topic.
2. No conditioning intervention of any kind in a confirmed 85+ sample.
3. **Zero throwing outcomes in the entire concurrent-training literature** — 43 studies, n = 1,090, all jump/RFD/1RM/hypertrophy columns.
4. No PCr recovery kinetics in a pitcher.
5. No study relates any aerobic-capacity measure to within-outing velocity decay — the one prediction the mechanism makes.
6. No within-trial manipulation of **session separation** against explosive output, in any population.

**Structural:** the topic's whole randomised in-pitcher evidence base is **n = 13 plus one uninterpretable 1992 design.** → **F-691**.

## 9. Field sweep — thin, and the emptiness is the finding

**Zero retrievable 2025–2026 Driveline or Tread Athletics items on pitcher conditioning.** The only Driveline object is *"Facts Behind Running Poles,"* ~10 years old, and it is **the most restrained public position in the industry** — against running poles from tradition AND against dropping aerobic work because critics call it "less explosive."

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Distance running costs a pitcher velocity" | **UNPROVEN** — citation broken (F-683); only direct test null on velocity *and* sprint (F-684) |
| "+3.0% velocity / +4.2% anaerobic power" (TopVelocity) | **MARKETING** — vendor paraphrase of an unopened paper; re-import vector |
| "Higher VO2max makes starters better" | **UNPROVEN** — cross-sectional, n = 24, three inflation routes (F-687) |
| Tempo runs / short sprints, long rests (SimpliFaster) | **PROMISING, UNTESTED** — sensible dose, no trial with a throwing outcome anywhere |
| "Poles the day after flush lactic acid" | **DEBUNKED** — already F-132 |
| "Build an aerobic base through anaerobic methods" / HIIT | **PROMISING, UNTESTED in pitchers** — Rabena & Moran is the test; interval work showed no additive benefit |

One genuinely new framing (March 2026 commentary, no data, standing unestablished): *"if conditioning focuses exclusively on endurance, explosive qualities may decline; if aerobic capacity is ignored entirely, recovery between repeated efforts may become the limiting factor."* → **F-692**.

## 10. The net position a staff should be given

**Sprinting is a defensible default — on mechanism, and on a small explosive-strength signal in general trainees. It is NOT defensible on evidence that distance running costs a pitcher anything measurable, because the only time anyone measured that in pitchers, nothing moved in either direction at n = 13.**

The one sentence today earns outright: **if you are going to condition, do not bolt it onto the end of the lift.** That is a calendar change, not a training block. ⭐ **Fifth time the corpus has found a topic's only actionable output to be a SCHEDULING decision** — after the between-start week, the between-inning break, the warm-up interval (F-419) and nap timing (F-646→). ⚠️ **The specific separation intervals in circulation ("under 3 h is worst," "separate by 6+ h") are UNSOURCED and must not be registered as thresholds.**
