## 2. THE KR SIDE — THE ONE GOOD STUDY

**Štirn I, Carruthers J, Šibila M, Pori P (2017).** *Frequent Immediate Knowledge of Results Enhances the Increase of Throwing Velocity in Overarm Handball Performance.* **J Hum Kinet 56:197–205.** DOI 10.1515/hukin-2017-0037 · PMID 28469758 · PMC5384067. **NOT OPENED.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **n** | **123** (50 F, 73 M physical-education students), **randomly assigned** |
| **protocol** | 2 × 10 maximal-effort handball set shots, 2×/week × **6 weeks**, identical in both arms |
| **the ONLY manipulated variable** | experimental arm saw radar velocity **immediately after every shot**; control arm received **no feedback** |
| **normal ball (size 2)** | **+8.1 ± 3.6 %** vs **+2.7 ± 2.9 %** |
| **heavy ball** | **+5.1 ± 4.2 %** vs **+2.5 ± 5.8 %**, also reported significant |
| **sex** | no differences except in the experimental arm's normal-ball throws |
| **retention test** | ⚠️ **NONE.** Authors: *"the participants were not available for a retention test."* |

**Arithmetic computed here from the reported means and SDs** (the only verification a blocked cycle can perform):
- pooled SD = √((3.6² + 2.9²)/2) = **3.27** → **d = (8.1 − 2.7)/3.27 ≈ 1.65**
- at ~61/62 per arm, α = .05, 80 % power the design detects **d ≈ 0.51**
- **n = 123 clears this corpus's own n ≥ 97 detection floor (F-519→F-532).** A large observed effect in a study powered for a moderate one.

**POPULATION — the binding limitation. SAMPLE MISMATCH, DIRECTIONAL ONLY.** PE students, handball set shot. **Absolute sample velocity NOT RETRIEVABLE** — the paper was not opened and the outcome is a percentage. The corpus's handball range is **45.9–51.3 mph**. The control arm alone gained **2.7 %** from nothing but 240 maximal throws, which is the signature of untrained throwers with large gain room. **8.1 % is a CEILING for an 85+ arm, not an estimate**; restriction of range (Luera 2020, r = .17–.29) says what survives at 90 mph is smaller.

### 2.1 ⚠️ One inconsistency inside the abstract (F-699)
The abstract says *"almost four times greater relative increase"* and reports **8.1 % vs 2.7 %**. **8.1/2.7 = 3.0.** Two innocent explanations (ratio on absolute m/s gains; or a subgroup) and **neither is checkable without the paper.** Registered as a **flag, not an error in the result** — the between-group difference stands on the means and SDs. **But a headline phrase and the numbers beside it disagreeing by 33 % is how "four times" enters coaching language.**

## 3. WHY THE GOOD STUDY CANNOT BE READ — AND THE MECHANISM THAT MAKES IT WORSE

**F-697.** With no retention test, two hypotheses are **observationally identical in this dataset**:

- **(A) LEARNING.** Six weeks of per-throw KR built a faster movement the athlete keeps. → *The gain persists with the gun off.*
- **(B) ACUTE OUTPUT UNDER MEASUREMENT.** The number raised central drive and effort on every throw **including the post-test**, while the unscored control arm — told "maximal effort" — did not give it. → *The gain is rented, not owned.*

**And (B) is sufficient on its own.** **Stien N et al. (2025)**, *Eur J Sport Sci*, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.70000 · PMID 40590195 · PMC12210026. **NOT OPENED.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **n** | **20 resistance-trained men, WITHIN-SUBJECT CROSSOVER** |
| **manipulation** | tested **with and without a live force-curve display**, across single, repeated and 30-s all-out IMTP |
| **headline** | feedback *"significantly enhanced most measures of peak and mean force outputs in all test variations,"* **ES 0.49–1.13, p < .001–.006** |
| **repeated-trial peak force** | **+7.9 % to +12.9 % on reps 2, 3, 4** (ES 0.68–0.99, p = .002–.036). **NOT significant on reps 1, 5, 6.** |
| **reliability** | single-trial peak-force CV **3.80 % → 3.38 %**, ICC steady 0.978. Repeated-trial CV ~4.5 % both ways. **30-s all-out: NO reliability gain** — authors blame pacing against the display. |
| **counter-case** | a 2026 technical note: real-time visual force feedback did **NOT** change maximal **grip** force but **did** change **RFD**. **Task- and muscle-dependent.** |

**Read together: a single acute exposure buys 7.9–12.9 % more peak force. Štirn's six weeks bought 8.1 % more throwing velocity with no washout. Same order of magnitude, and the acute explanation requires no learning at all.** That is not proof Štirn measured only intent — it is the most parsimonious reading available, and the missing retention test is why nobody can say otherwise.

⚠️ **Power, applied symmetrically (Dispute #63's rule).** n = 20 paired detects paired d ≈ 0.5 only at ~n = 34. **The bottom of the ES range (0.49) sits below its own detection floor**; the 7.9–12.9 % band (ES 0.68–0.99) is credible. **Reps 1, 5, 6 failing while 2, 3, 4 hit is not a clean dose-response** and is consistent with multiplicity across six reps × three variants. The potentiation-then-fatigue reading is **MECHANISM, speculative, and carries no weight.**

### 3.1 The mechanism in one paragraph (ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY)
**Voluntary maximal output is not maximal.** Central drive, recruitment completeness and rate coding under a true maximal voluntary effort are all submaximal relative to tissue capacity, and the gap is modulated by arousal, attentional focus and knowledge of the score. A live readout converts "throw hard" into a **scored task**, and a scored task recruits differently. **It is immediate — no adaptation timeline, nothing remodels, available on the first pitch and gone when the number is.**

### 3.2 🚨 And therefore it is not free
If the mechanism is raised central drive on **every** throw, a permanent per-pitch readout **raises the mechanical load of every training throw, including the ones meant to be submaximal.** The corpus's own constraint: the highest-risk single exposure in this population is a maximal-intent outing. **A gun pointed at a recovery pen converts it into a testing day.** Direction sound; **magnitude unpriceable** — no coefficient in this corpus converts IMTP force to arm load, and importing a proximal isometric test into an unresisted ballistic task is the exact error the 2026-10-12 structural sweep was opened to catch (this is its fourth instance).

## 4. THE GENERAL LITERATURE, AND ITS MOST-QUOTED NUMBER

**Jiménez-Díaz J, Chaves-Castro K, Morera-Castro M (2020).** *Effectiveness of Augmented Feedback on Motor Skills Performance: A Meta-Analysis.* **MHSalud 17(1).** **NOT OPENED.**

- **20 studies, 95 effect sizes, 949 participants.**
- **Acquisition (practising WITH feedback): ES = 0.87 [0.65, 1.10], I² = 74.41 %.**
- **"Acquisition-to-retention after feedback removed: ES = −0.74."** CI **not retrievable.**

⚠️ **CHECK 3 FAILURE — does the number measure what the sentence says?** Every snippet describes −0.74 as a **within-group** drop from acquisition to retention. **That happens in essentially every motor-learning dataset ever collected, feedback or not — it is warm-up decrement plus forgetting.** The contrast that would establish dependency is *feedback-group-at-retention vs no-feedback-group-at-retention*, and nothing retrievable states that is what was pooled. **So the topic's headline retention penalty is of UNKNOWN DESIGN and must not be quoted as a dependency effect.**

⚠️ **And I² = 74.41 % with no retrievable publication-bias assessment.** In this exact literature, bias correction took **external focus g = 0.26 → 0.01 (F-189)** and **self-controlled practice g = 0.44 → 0.107 (F-190)**. An ES of 0.87 from 20 heterogeneous studies with no funnel analysis is the precise object this registry has twice watched collapse.

> **Do not confuse this with the same first author's 2020 *J Motor Behav* meta-analysis on self-controlled feedback (18 studies, 86 ESs). Different paper.**

## 6. THE KP SIDE — THE GAP IS TOTAL (F-702)

**Four-angle sweep, all four empty:**

1. **Kinematic/biomechanical feedback × baseball pitching × any outcome: ZERO.** Only cross-sectional and retrospective work — Zappa & Lerch 2026 (markerless, NCAA D1 games, descriptive); a Super Learner model on **322 NCAA D1 pitchers, 2022–2024**; Lerch 2025 (measurement).
2. **Any other throwing sport: ZERO.** Javelin = observational technique description plus one single-group pretest–posttest, **no control, no retention** (~6–8 %, uninterpretable). Handball's one randomised trial is **KR about the ball**, not KP.
3. **Does the field know? YES.** The lower-body softball velocity paper states in its own discussion that *"longitudinal or intervention studies are warranted to understand how changes in lower extremity kinematics and kinetics can influence changes in pitch velocity."*
4. **Guidance hypothesis on a maximal-effort POWER task: DOES NOT EXIST.** The literature is accuracy, timing and force-maintenance tasks; one force study says the effect on maximal output *"remains unclear."*
5. **THE MISSING DESIGN, specified by today's cross-examination: a 2×2 — trained WITH/WITHOUT the readout × tested WITH/WITHOUT it.** Never run in any throwing task. Without it, §3's (A) and (B) stay observationally identical. **Note the asymmetry that makes this necessary: a retention test measured WITHOUT the gun would show nothing even if real adaptation occurred, because you removed the thing that expresses it.**

> **The sentence for a staff meeting: every mechanical cue this program delivers off a biomechanics screen rests on a cross-sectional association PLUS an untested assumption that telling the athlete his number changes his number. The first half this corpus has audited to death. The second half has never been tested in a pitcher at all.**

## 7. THE MEASUREMENT CEILING ON KP (F-703)

**Previously registered** (`biomechanics.md` §7.2): **Hawk-Eye MPJPE 56.6 ± 9.4 mm; Theia3D 52.0 ± 12.3 mm; n = 18 collegiate pitchers. Stride length agreed best across systems (CCC > 0.85); SHOULDER ROTATIONAL VARIABLES showed the greatest variability.** **Lerch 2025** (*J Biomech* 188:112775; 30 lab + 30 NCAA D1 in-game): **variability similar across systems, MEAN VALUES substantially different.**

**New today is only the inference, and it is a hard constraint on §6's missing trial:**

- ~5 cm of per-joint error, **concentrated worst in exactly the rotational shoulder variables coaches read off the report**, means a KP intervention targeting those variables feeds back a quantity **the instrument cannot resolve at the scale of the intended change.**
- **Lerch's split is the awkward one for feedback specifically.** A system that gets **variability** right but **means** wrong is adequate for *"is he repeating?"* and inadequate for *"what is his number?"* — **and the report hands the coach the number.**
- **Order of operations for anyone building the trial: establish the within-athlete MINIMAL DETECTABLE CHANGE for the target variable on your own system first, or the trial cannot interpret its own result.** **The corpus holds no such figure for any pitching kinematic variable, on any system** — the **eighteenth** entry in the F-264 / F-289 / F-295 / F-307 / F-320 / F-336 / F-348 / F-363 / F-396 / F-457 / F-507 / F-600 / F-659 / F-689 pattern.

### 7.1 The resulting split verdict on the lab
| use | verdict |
|---|---|
| **The report as a SCREEN** — deciding what to work on, checking whether a change took | **DEFENSIBLE**, for the reliable variables (stride length CCC > 0.85, trunk tilt) |
| **The report as FEEDBACK** — handing the athlete a number to chase | **ZERO evidence in a pitcher, plus a measurement problem concentrated exactly where coaches look** |

> **Keep the lab. Change what you say off it. Feed back the BALL, which you measure well. Use the body data to decide what to TRAIN, not what to TELL him mid-pen.**

## 8. THE DEPLOYABLE TEST (F-706)

**THE GUN-ON / GUN-OFF BLOCK TEST**, one arm, this week.

**Design.** A/B blocks on one arm, **counterbalanced ABBA within each session** so the warm-up ramp and within-outing fade (F-384's −0.2 mph/inning, borrowed from an **81 mph** sample and still unmeasured at 85+) fall equally on both conditions. Readout visible immediately after every fastball in A blocks; **screen turned away and no numbers called out** in B blocks. **Stadium TrackMan or the program's primary unit — NOT a consumer gun** (product testing reports consumer units varying **2–3 mph pitch to pitch**, snippet-only and UNVERIFIED, which would make the instrument noisier than the signal).

**Price, from F-289's table at the corpus working within-outing fastball SD of 1.0 mph:**

| difference to detect | fastballs PER CONDITION | total | pens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.0 mph** | **≈16** | **≈32** | **1–2** |
| 0.5 mph | ≈63 | ≈126 | 4–5 |

**Target the 1.0 mph contrast first:** if F-698's band transfers at all, 1 % of a 90 mph arm is **0.9 mph**.

🚨 **PREREQUISITE, AND IT IS FREE.** The **1.0 mph SD is a CORPUS ASSUMPTION**, never published for an 85+ arm, already load-bearing in every detection table this registry has built (F-289, F-659, F-684, F-689). **It is in your own TrackMan log. Pull it before running the test, or the test cannot tell you what it found.**

**What you tell the pitcher.** *"For the next five pens, some blocks you'll see the number after every fastball and some blocks you won't. Throw the same both ways."*

**What the failure looks like.** The B blocks drifting slower **across** the session rather than randomly — fatigue leaking into one condition, meaning the counterbalancing broke.

**What you do with a positive result.** Treat the readout as a **deliberate intent tool you deploy and withdraw by design** (§3), **not** a permanent fixture — because of §3.2's cost.

**Three limits, stated not hedged.**
1. **Unblindable.** The athlete always knows. The result will forever measure "feedback + knowing you're being studied."
2. **Acute only.** The persistence question is untouched; a washout block at 63 fastballs a bucket is a month of pens.
3. **One arm is one arm.** Do not generalise to the staff.

**Structural note.** This is the **second independent instance in two days** of the same escape route from the detection wall (F-506 / F-476 / F-623 / F-681, four arrivals). F-689 breached it by swapping the endpoint to within-outing decay; this breaches it by making the athlete his own control across within-session blocks. **The wall binds BETWEEN-SUBJECT designs on a college roster. The escape is always an endpoint the athlete serves as his own control on — and that qualifier should travel with the wall when it is promoted.**

## 9. THE REGISTRY-HYGIENE CONSEQUENCE (F-704)

If a live readout is worth **7.9–12.9 %** of peak output, then **feedback condition is an uncontrolled moderator sitting underneath an unknown fraction of this registry's magnitudes.** A test where the athlete saw the number and one where he did not **are not the same measurement**, and almost no study reports which it ran.

**Sixth candidate structural sweep: which registered magnitudes rest on a maximal-output test whose feedback condition is unreported?** Candidates: every pull-down and mound velocity figure in `velocity-development.md`; every CMJ and IMTP number imported as a proximal correlate (F-021, F-006, F-590); every bar-velocity figure in the VBT topic (F-590→F-600); every jump figure in `jump-transfer-audit.md`.

**Predictions registered in advance:** (a) the feedback condition is **unreported in the large majority**; (b) where it differs between the study's protocol and the setting a coach applies the number in, the imported magnitude is **biased upward** by roughly the F-698 band.

**Note the direction for VBT specifically:** bar-velocity autoregulation is a method in which the readout is **permanently visible by design**, so its reference values are F-698-inflated relative to any unmonitored set. That is internally consistent — **it becomes an error only when a velocity threshold measured on-display is applied off-display.**

**The free fix for a program:** whatever your velocity testing protocol is, **write down whether the athlete sees the gun, and never change it.** If October testing had the board on and January testing had it off, **you have manufactured a decline.**
