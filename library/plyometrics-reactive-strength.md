## 1. Why this topic exists, and why it is structurally unlike the rest of the corpus

`F-006` is the registry's single most tantalising cross-sectional finding: in **n = 53 professional pitchers**, drop-jump variables predict ball velocity (**R² = 0.30, F = 3.93, p = 0.005**; RSI specifically p = .002) where countermovement jump (R² = 0.10, NS) and squat jump (R² = 0.07, NS) do not. **Reactive strength is the only jump quality that survives into a professional pitching sample.**

`F-004` is the companion null: bilateral vertical jump height does not predict velocity at all (**r = 0.07, NS, n = 33**). `F-590` closed the door on reading the weight-room literature across: **the exchange rate from jump performance to mph is unknown in both directions**, with CMJ's interval at **[−0.280, +0.404]**.

So the corpus arrived here with one live marker and no lever. **The plyometric training literature is the literature that should convert it.** And the way it fails is new.

### The headline: this is the mirror image of F-571 (F-721)

`F-571` and `F-582` established the field's standard failure: **applied baseball science measures the construct the intervention obviously moves, not a pitching output.** Four randomised ROM trials in baseball players, all scoring ROM → ROM.

**Plyometrics does the opposite.** The trials measured **ball velocity** — the distal, hard-to-fake, sport-relevant outcome. Carter 2007 in mph. Cüre 2020 in mph. Kim 2024 in km/h. Deng 2023 pools throwing velocity across 30 studies. That is exactly the thing F-571 said nobody ever does.

**And the mechanism was never measured once.** No plyometric trial with a throwing outcome — in any population, at any level — reports **reactive strength index, drop-jump height, ground contact time, rate of force development, tendon or muscle stiffness, or any SSC variable.** Nor do the limbs match: F-006's marker is a **lower**-limb drop jump; every located trial trained the **upper** extremity.

> **This is a worse position than F-571's, not a better one.** F-571's literature at least produced a clean manipulation check. Here we have trials that moved the outcome and cannot say what they moved.

## 2. The evidence, trial by trial, with each study's detection floor

**The arithmetic used throughout.** For a between-group difference in change scores at α = .05 two-tailed and 80% power:

$$d_{\text{detectable}} = (z_{.975} + z_{.80})\sqrt{2/n} = 2.8016\sqrt{2/n}$$

⚠️ **The SD-of-change bracket (1.5–2.0 mph for an 8-week fastball-velocity change) is this corpus's assumption, not a published value.** It is the same missing quantity as **F-634** — no trial anywhere reports between-subject SD and within-athlete change together. It is stated, not hidden.

| Study | n / arm | d detectable | MDD at SD 1.5 | Reported effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Carter 2007** (NCAA D1) | 12 | 1.144 | **1.72 mph** (2.00 at SD 1.75) | **+2.00 mph** |
| **Cüre 2020** (youth) | 6 | 1.618 | **2.43 mph** | **+2.44 mph** |
| **Kim 2024** (HS, plyo arm) | 10 | 1.253 | **1.88 mph ≈ 3.0 km/h** | **+1.0–1.2 km/h** |

> **Every published positive in this topic sits inside its own minimum detectable difference. The two most quotable effects are, to within rounding, EQUAL to their studies' detection floors — and Cüre's matches to one hundredth of a mile per hour.** A literature in which every published effect equals its own detection floor is a literature whose effect sizes are set by its sample sizes. **When two such studies agree, the agreement is evidence about publishing, not about plyometrics.**

### 2.1 Carter et al. 2007 — the load-bearing paper (F-722, F-723)

*J Strength Cond Res* 21(1):208–215. "Effects of high volume upper extremity plyometric training on throwing velocity and functional strength ratios of the shoulder rotators in collegiate baseball players."

- **n = 24 NCAA D1**, assigned after rank-ordering by concentric internal-rotation strength.
- **"Ballistic Six"** — six upper-extremity plyometric exercises, **2×/week**.
- **Control: regular off-season training WITHOUT plyometrics.** A genuine non-training arm — rarer in this field than it should be, and the most valuable part of the paper.
- **83.15 → 85.15 mph (+2.00), significant vs control; control unchanged.**

**CHECK #1 — baseline 83.15 mph. The closest population match in this corpus's entire velocity-intervention literature, and still below the 85 floor.** The group crossed 85 only at post-test. **Directional for an 85+ arm; borderline rather than mismatched.**

**CHECK #2 — manipulated, with a control. INTERVENTION.**

**CHECK #3 — partly fails.** No retrievable source states whether "throwing velocity" was **mound fastball velocity** or a flat-ground/crow-hop throw, and the sample is "baseball players," not "pitchers" — position players may be included. **Unknown and material.**

**CHECK #4 — not applicable. No geometry in this literature.**

🚨 **Four things this corpus needs and does not have: the DURATION (secondary sources conflict — most say 8 weeks, at least one says 6, and duration is the topic's only significant moderator); the THROW COUNT ("high volume" is in the title and nobody quantifies a set, rep or throw number); the mound-vs-flat question; and all SDs.** The duration conflict is itself the tell: **when independent paraphrases of a paper disagree about its central design parameter, none of them has read it carefully.**

### 2.2 Cüre, Sterlace & Griffiths 2020 — not a replication (F-725)

*Gaziantep University Journal of Sport Sciences.* **n = 12 total, SIX per arm**, youth select-level, 6 weeks, quasi-experimental with a comparison group. **+2.44 mph (sig) vs +0.59 mph (NS).**

**SAMPLE MISMATCH — youth. Directional only, never a norm.** And its detection floor is **2.43 mph** against a reported **2.44 mph**. Under **F-439**'s n ≥ ~97 rule this is not a usable null, let alone a usable positive. ⚠️ The "12–15" in the abstract appears to describe how many players each team contributed, **not** an age range — do not report ages.

### 2.3 Kim, Jaber & Yim 2024 — the fifth Type B in eight weeks (F-724)

*Medical Science Monitor*, PMC11321950. **n = 30 HS pitchers, 16–19 y, ten per arm, THREE ACTIVE ARMS, NO NON-TRAINING CONTROL, 4 weeks, 2×/week, in-season (July–August).**

- **Compound: ~128.4 → ~134.3 km/h = +5.9 km/h = +3.67 mph.** Plyometric and kettlebell: **+1.0–1.2 km/h ≈ +0.6–0.75 mph** each. Post hoc: compound > both.

**Four defects.** (1) **TYPE B** — the design this corpus corrected F-039, F-044, F-508, F-510 and F-596 for; **fifth instance in eight weeks, so Type B is a base rate.** (2) **SAMPLE MISMATCH** — baseline **79.8 mph**, five mph below the floor. (3) **IMPLAUSIBLE MAGNITUDE** — +3.67 mph in four in-season weeks, against a controlled literature whose best case is +2.0 in 6–8 off-season weeks. (4) **The plyometric arm gained about a THIRD of what the design could resolve**, so its result is uninterpretable — and "compound beats plyos" compares one arm that cleared the floor against two that could not have.

✅ **Do not quote +5.9 km/h or the modality ranking.** ⚠️ **A laundering vector was caught live:** a Substack write-up claims the compound group improved "5–7×" more than the single-method groups. **That ratio is two numbers below a detection floor divided into a third** — noise with a multiplication sign in it. F-274's field-sweep hazard is still producing.

### 2.4 Deng et al. 2023 — the duration moderator, and a live attribution conflict (F-727, F-728)

*PLoS ONE* 18(7):e0288340, PMID 37459333. 30 studies; SCOPUS/PubMed/WoS/SPORTDiscus to 2023-05-03; **randomised designs with ACTIVE controls only**; random-effects; certainty **low to moderate**.

**THE RESULT THAT SURVIVED TWO INDEPENDENT SEARCH PASSES UNCHANGED:**
> **Throwing velocity: >7 weeks ES = 1.05; ≤7 weeks ES = 0.29; p = 0.011. Training length was the ONLY significant moderator.**

Coherent with the raw mph: **8 wk → +2.00; 6 wk → +2.44 (n = 6, discount); 4 wk → +0.70 (below floor).** ⚠️ **Confounded** — duration is collinear with total volume, total throw count and season phase. **And nobody has run a dose-matched duration comparison** (same total volume over 4 vs 8 weeks).

🚨 **THE ATTRIBUTION CONFLICT — the cycle's near-miss.** Two passes returned **ES = 0.78** attached to **two different outcomes**:
- *"small-to-moderate effect for throwing velocity ... ES = 0.78 (p < 0.001)"*
- *"For kicking velocity, the pooled effect was moderate (ES = 0.78, I² = 8.7%, Egger's p = 0.15)"*

The throwing cell's sports came back as **"handball, baseball, water polo"** once and **"handball, baseball, tennis"** another time; a third snippet flags the abstract's kicking range (0.37–0.44) conflicting with the main text's (0.67–1.47). **The I² and Egger figures attach to the KICKING reading, which is weak evidence that kicking is the correct attribution.**

> **UNVERIFIED. Do not quote 0.78 for throwing velocity in either direction.** Had this corpus run one search instead of two it would have registered the number today and been wrong. **The operational rule: any number that will carry weight must be returned by at least TWO independent passes, and when they disagree it is UNVERIFIED rather than averaged.**

### 2.5 Martínez-García et al. 2021 — the inflated pooled estimate (F-729)

*Int J Sports Sci Coach* 16(5), DOI 10.1177/17479541211002977. Strength training → throwing velocity. **16 studies, 424 participants**, randomised with control groups, ≥4 weeks. **Pooled ES = 1.10 [0.64, 1.57]**; male 1.12, female 1.22; **adults 1.34, teenagers 0.49 with a CI spanning zero.**

**424 / 16 = 26.5 per study ≈ 13 per arm** — the identical profile to every trial above. **No Egger, funnel or trim-and-fill figure retrievable.** **And a competing review reports a TRIVIAL effect** for resistance training on handball throwing performance (DOI 10.1186/s13102-024-00872-y). **ES 1.10 and "trivial" cannot both be right.**

This corpus has twice acted on the same signature: **F-040** discarded a periodization meta-analysis at g = 3.445 as small-study bias; **F-194** downgraded Differential Learning on single-lab and publication-bias grounds. **Treat 1.10 as inflated.**

⚠️ **No plyometric-vs-resistance-vs-combined modality subgroup is retrievable from either review** — so *is a plyometric minute better spent than a barbell minute?* is unanswerable from any retrievable source.

### 2.6 The handball meta-analysis — logged with a units flag

A 2025 *Scientific Reports* meta-analysis of plyometric training in handball reports improved **3-step running throw (MD = 2.78, p = 0.02), jumping throw (MD = 1.13, p = 0.04), penalty throw (MD = 1.43, p = 0.0002)**, with larger effects in adults than adolescents. ⚠️ **UNITS UNVERIFIED — no retrievable snippet states whether MD is in km/h or m/s, and the two readings differ by a factor of 3.6.** If m/s these are enormous and implausible. **SAMPLE MISMATCH regardless: handball throwing samples run 45.9–51.3 mph (F-396, F-398, Dispute #29).** Logged as a lead, not a magnitude.

## 3. 🚨 The method result — a pooled SMD does not transfer across a population boundary (F-726)

The most portable thing this cycle produced, and it applies to most meta-analytic effect sizes in this registry.

$\text{SMD} = \Delta / SD_{\text{pooled}}$, where the denominator is **the between-subject SD of the pooled samples.** To get mph back, you multiply by an SD. **Which one?**

- **Handball:** samples at 45.9–51.3 mph; between-player SD order **2–3 mph**.
- **Youth / unselected HS baseball:** 70–85 mph spread; between-player SD order **4–6 mph**.
- **An 85+ college staff:** range-restricted (Luera 2020, n = 149 pros, r = .17–.29); between-pitcher fastball SD order **2–3 mph**.

| SD used | ES 0.78 | ES 1.05 | ES 1.10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2.5 mph** (85+ restricted) | 1.95 mph | 2.63 mph | 2.75 mph |
| **5.0 mph** (youth / mixed) | 3.90 mph | 5.25 mph | 5.50 mph |

**The same effect size yields answers 2.5× apart, and the SD you are entitled to use is the pooled samples', not your roster's.** The conversion is not merely uncertain — **the standardised metric is not a transferable quantity across a population boundary at all.**

> **THE RULE: when a finding crosses a population boundary, carry the RAW units and discard the standardised effect. An SMD is a within-literature comparison device, not a magnitude.**

⭐⭐ **This generates the sixth queued structural audit, needs no egress, and is mechanisable in one pass over `FINDINGS.md`: how many of this registry's findings quote a pooled SMD across a population boundary, or read one as if it transferred?** It is **F-510's Type D defect in standardised-effect clothing**, and Dowling 2024 showed that defect moving a slope by ~23×. → **Dispute #80.**

## 4. Mechanism — what the tissue says, and where the argument is borrowed

A pitch is a **fast** stretch-shortening-cycle event at nearly every joint. The lead-leg block is a **~50–80 ms braking event** (F-061, F-006); shoulder external-to-internal rotation reversal is faster. Fast-SSC performance depends on **series-elastic stiffness**, **pre-activation and stretch-reflex gain**, and **rate of motor-unit recruitment** — not on maximal force. **Plyometric training is the only modality in common use whose stimulus matches that time domain:** heavy strength work loads the muscle for 300–1500 ms; a drop jump for 150–250 ms; a med-ball rebound throw for less.

**Three cautions, all cutting the same way.**

1. **The series-elastic adaptation timeline is ~8–12 weeks, not 4.** `library/tendon-stiffness.md` (F-628→F-645) puts lower-limb tendon stiffness change at **8 weeks minimum** under a protocol designed for it. **Deng's >7-week threshold matches a tissue timeline that was not fitted to it** — the strongest piece of mechanistic consistency in the topic, and it is consistency of a **dose**, not of an **effect**.
2. 🚨 **But at the shoulder the mechanism has never been measured in anyone.** **F-636:** no trial in any population reports a tendon-stiffness outcome for the rotator cuff or long head of biceps. **F-635:** nobody has related tendon or muscle stiffness to pitch velocity in any population. **The timeline above is the Achilles', not the cuff's.** Graded **MECHANISM**; it cannot be promoted. (The word "corroboration" was withdrawn in cross-examination, 2026-10-16.)
3. **Upper-extremity "plyometrics" may not be plyometric.** The Ballistic Six is med-ball and plyo-ball throwing. **Whether a 1 kg med-ball rebound throw elicits a fast SSC in the shoulder's series-elastic structures, or is simply a submaximal throw, is unmeasured** — nobody has put EMG or ultrasound on it. The label is anatomical folklore applied to a drill. → **Dispute #79.**

**And the ceiling argument, which points against the whole topic for this population:** reactive qualities are highly trainable in untrained people and least trainable in athletes who already do ballistic work daily. **A pitcher throwing 85+ already performs several hundred maximal-intent SSC events a week.** F-630/F-631 registered exactly this attenuation of adaptability in already-trained arms for tendon work. **85+ is the population where this should work least.**

## 5. Measurement — why RSI is the instrument and velocity is not (F-732)

**Within-athlete: MDC₉₅ = 1.96 × √2 × CV.**

| Assumed within-athlete CV of drop-jump RSI | MDC₉₅, ONE athlete |
|---|---|
| 5% | **13.9%** |
| 6% | **16.6%** |
| 8% | **22.2%** |

**A single pitcher needs a >14–22% RSI change before it may be called real.** At staff level (n = 12 paired pre/post), the detectable group-mean change is **≈ 4.5–7%** — comfortably inside what an 8-week block should produce if the mechanism is real.

**Contrast the velocity route: a 12-pitcher staff split 6/6 detects ~2.4 mph at best — it would MISS THE ENTIRE EFFECT CARTER REPORTED.** Run all 12 with no control and you have built a Type B design, and 8 weeks of off-season in a 19-year-old produces velocity gain for reasons unrelated to the intervention — which is precisely why Carter's control arm is the valuable part of that paper.

⚠️ **The CV is not published for a pitcher.** The 5–8% bracket is general trained-athlete reliability, snippet-level. **EIGHTEENTH entry** in the F-264 / F-289 / F-295 / F-307 / F-320 / F-336 / F-348 / F-363 / F-374 / F-385 / F-396 / F-457 / F-471 / F-493 / F-504 / F-507 / F-600 pattern — *already in the data of every program that owns a contact mat, gating a topic's whole detection arithmetic, published by nobody.* **Four weeks of Tuesday jumps.**

⚠️ **Protocol must be fixed across pre and post:** drop-jump technique itself changes RSI (countermovement-style drop jumps produce higher jump heights than bounce-style at 30, 45 and 60 cm — PMC5260527, not opened). Fix the drop height and the jump style or the comparison is meaningless.

⚠️ **And read the limit honestly (Dispute #81): RSI is the MANIPULATION CHECK, not the outcome.** It tells you whether the block changed the quality it was designed to change. It does **not** license *"his RSI went up so he will throw harder"* — **F-590** put the jump-to-mph exchange rate at unknown in both directions, and **F-006**'s R² = 0.30 is cross-sectional with no intervention evidence anywhere.

## 6. The prescription (F-733)

1. **Ballistic Six-pattern upper-extremity plyometrics, 2×/week, 8 weeks minimum.** Four weeks is below both the moderator and the tissue timeline and should not be programmed as a velocity intervention at all. Off-season or early pre-season.
2. **Log every rep on the pitching workload sheet.** This is unaccounted upper-extremity throwing volume; the source paper's own title says "high volume" and the count is unknown. `library/non-fastball-workload.md` and F-493 exist because unlogged throws are how arms get hurt. *(One line on injury, as the mission requires — then move on.)*
3. **Measure drop-jump RSI on a contact mat:** 3 trials per athlete per session, **≥2 separate days at each end**, protocol fixed.
4. **Read it at >14–22% for an individual, ~4.5–7% for a 12-man staff mean.**
5. **Do not read velocity as the test.**
6. **Free by-product:** 4 weeks of day-to-day RSI logging produces the pitcher RSI CV, which does not exist in print.

**Expected magnitude, stated honestly: somewhere in [0, 2.0] mph, and the restriction-of-range argument says expect the low end.**

**What you tell the pitcher:** *"Short, violent rebound work two days a week for eight weeks is one of the few things in the weight room with a real controlled study behind it in college baseball players. It's worth doing. I'm not promising you a number — and we're measuring your jump, not your fastball, because your fastball can't tell us."*

**What the failure looks like on video:** the athlete **absorbing** the med ball — a long, soft catch, elbow collapsing, a visible pause before the return throw. **If you can see a pause, the fast SSC is not happening and he is doing a strength exercise he thinks is a plyometric.**

**What this prescription is silent on, by necessity:** **command.** F-730 — zero accuracy outcomes in the entire plyometric throwing literature, Dispute #27's **ninth** venue. No evidence in either direction.

## 8. Gaps this topic leaves open

- 🚨 **No plyometric measurement of any kind in a sample above 85 mph.** The closest is 83.15 mph (Carter).
- 🚨 **No plyometric trial anywhere reports an SSC mediator** — RSI, drop jump, ground contact time, RFD, stiffness (F-721).
- 🚨 **No RSI or drop-jump TRAINING intervention with a throwing-velocity outcome, in any population** (F-006, restated).
- **No LOWER-body plyometric or sprint-based trial with a throwing outcome was located** — every trial this cycle was upper-extremity, while F-006's marker is a lower-limb drop jump and F-061 prices the lead-leg block at 4–6% of between-pitcher velocity variance. **Searched only incidentally; this is the obvious next cycle.**
- **No dose-matched duration comparison** (same volume over 4 vs 8 weeks) — the study that would de-confound F-728.
- **No modality subgroup** (plyometric vs resistance vs combined) in any retrievable review.
- **No EMG or ultrasound on a med-ball rebound throw at the shoulder** — the measurement that would settle whether "upper-extremity plyometrics" is plyometric (Dispute #79).
- **No pitcher-specific within-athlete CV for drop-jump RSI** — eighteenth entry in the missing-variance pattern (F-732).
- **Zero accuracy or command outcomes in the whole topic** — Dispute #27, ninth venue (F-730).
- **No throw count for the Ballistic Six as studied** — "high volume" is unquantified in every retrievable source.
