## 2. THE FIRST THING TO GET RIGHT — "COLD" IS THREE INTERVENTIONS, NOT ONE

| | **(A) Post-LIFT whole-body CWI** | **(B) Intra-outing LOCAL cooling** | **(C) Post-OUTING icing** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protocol | 10–15 °C tub, 10–15 min, after resistance training | palm or shoulder/forearm cooling between innings | ice to the dominant shoulder after the start |
| Outcome measured | fibre CSA, 1RM, lean mass | **ball velocity** | **dynamometer torque** |
| Evidence base | 13+ controlled trials, 2 meta-analyses, 1 acute meta of 22 RCTs | 2 crossovers, **67–70 mph samples** | 1 crossover, n = 16, velocity never measured |
| Where registered | **this file, F-603→F-610** | **F-360** | **F-581, F-582** |
| Verdict | a real, measured adaptation cost — small, and scoped to one timing | **unusable on population** | **measured the wrong outcome** |

**Their recommendations conflict.** (B) says cool the arm *during* the outing. (A) says do not cool the trained muscle *right after lifting*. (C) says nobody knows. **Any single-line advice about "ice" is incoherent across the three, which is exactly what went wrong with F-139.**

## 3. THE POST-LIFT EVIDENCE, NUMBERED

### 3.1 Strength — and the subgroup that is the whole argument

**Grgic J (2023), *Eur J Sport Sci* 23(3):372–380, PMID 35068365.** Ten studies, **n = 170**, 92% male, 11 comparisons / 22 groups.

| Analysis | ES | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall | **−0.23** | −0.45, −0.01 | 0.041 |
| **Trained-limb-only immersion** | **−0.31** | −0.61, −0.01 | 0.041 |
| **Whole-body immersion** | **−0.08** | **−0.53, +0.38** | 0.743 |

⚠️ **THE WHOLE-BODY ROW IS NOT A NULL — IT IS AN UNINFORMATIVE INTERVAL.** Width 0.91, i.e. *twice* the overall analysis's 0.44. That is the signature of an underpowered subgroup. **Contrast F-609's MVIC result, g = 0.08 [−0.11, +0.26], width 0.37, at moderate certainty — that is a real null.** The difference between those two readings is this corpus's entire method and it should be read twice.

**And nobody says the next part out loud: the flagship study sits on the limb-only side of the line.** Roberts LA et al. (2015), *J Physiol*, DOI 10.1113/JP270570 — n = 21 physically active men, 12 weeks 2×/wk, **waist-deep**, ~10 °C, 10 min, started **within 5 min** post-exercise, vs 10 min easy cycling. Active recovery: isokinetic work **+19%**, type II fibre CSA **+17%**, myonuclei/fibre **+26%**, all p < 0.05. CWI: none significant. Mechanism: fewer satellite cells at 24–48 h, blunted anabolic kinase phosphorylation. **"Waist-deep after lower-body training" is the trained muscle in the water.**

**The independent complement:** Fyfe JJ et al. (2019), *J Appl Physiol*, DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.00127.2019 — n = 8 vs 8, pair-matched on leg-press 1RM, 7 weeks, **whole-body** resistance training with whole-body CWI: *"...attenuates ... fiber hypertrophy, **but not strength gain**."* One study at n = 8 per arm settles nothing; its value is agreeing with Grgic's whole-body subgroup by a different route.

### 3.2 Hypertrophy

**Piñero A et al. (2024), *Eur J Sport Sci* 24(2):177–189, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.12074.** Eight studies, Bayesian.

- **Controlled: cSMD₀.₅ = −0.22 [95% CrI −0.47, +0.04]**, p(<0) = 0.957, p(<−0.1) = 0.834.
- **Uncontrolled arms: RT alone 0.36 [0.10, 0.61]** — reliable growth. **CWI + RT 0.14 [−0.08, +0.36]** — an interval containing zero.

Two honest riders. The uncontrolled pair is not a between-group comparison. And **p(<0) = 0.957 means a 4.3% posterior probability the effect favours the ice bath** — not nothing, in a literature whose publication-bias assessment is unretrievable (F-613).

### 3.3 🚨 The boundary condition that reframes the topic

Piñero's own stated scope: ***"The results of analysis apply solely to CWI application within 15 minutes of exercise cessation, which may not accurately reflect practical applications."***

**Every number in §3.1–3.2 is a number about plunging within fifteen minutes of racking the bar.** Nothing speaks to two hours later, or six, or tomorrow. **The question was never *whether*. It is *when*, and the literature answers exactly one *when*.**

### 3.4 The "wait 4–8 hours" fix is folklore as a dose

Four thresholds circulate at once: **~8 h** (Tornero-Aguilera et al. 2026, *Exp Physiol*, DOI 10.1113/EP094042 — a **narrative review**, recommending avoidance or delay "beyond approximately 8 hours, because repeated mTORC1 suppression accumulates into measurable losses of type II fibre cross-sectional area"); **4 h** and **4–6 h** (commercial); **24–48 h** (clinical). **Measured evidence for any of them: none. No delayed-CWI arm exists anywhere in the pooled literature.**

**Four incompatible rules circulating simultaneously is how you know none was measured.** Give the direction as a heuristic; never give the number as a finding (F-606).

## 4. THE ACUTE SIDE — IT INVERTS THE REASON PEOPLE USE IT

***PeerJ* 2025, article 21537, PMID 42473449.** Twenty-two RCTs, searched to 2025-09-01.

| Outcome | Pooled | Interval | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MVIC** | **g = 0.08** | −0.11, +0.26 (p = 0.42) | **a real null, moderate certainty — CWI does NOT restore strength** |
| CMJ, overall | 0.01 | −0.25, +0.28 | — |
| **CMJ, 0 h** | **−0.68** | −1.16, −0.20 | **transient explosive impairment, low certainty** |
| **CK** | −0.43 → **−0.16** | → **−0.44, +0.12** | **does not survive trim-and-fill** |

Corroboration on the acute decrement from *J ISAKOS* 2021 (S2059-7754(21)00072-9): ice beyond ~10 min linked to reduced muscular power.

**So: the tub does not give strength back (null, moderate certainty); it briefly takes explosiveness away; its one objective soreness marker is bias-fragile; and what survives is subjective — soreness, perceived recovery, parasympathetic reactivation.**

⚠️ **NEVER SUM THE ACUTE AND CHRONIC COSTS.** The −0.68 is cold muscle and slowed nerve conduction, gone in hours, fully reversible. The adaptation cost is a signalling deficit integrated across a mesocycle. **"The plunge cost him velocity that week" is wrong on the physiology.**

## 5. THE DECISION, AND WHY IT NEEDS NO EXCHANGE RATE

This is the structural mirror of 2026-10-05's velocity-loss problem (F-597, Dispute #60), and it comes out cleaner.

**Neither side of this trade is convertible into mph.**

- **Cost:** hypertrophy and strength. Their link to velocity is **F-001 r = 0.58 / F-002 r = 0.52**, NCAA D1, n = 33, **CROSS_SECTIONAL, never manipulated in any population (F-504)**. No mph-per-pound coefficient exists.
- **Benefit:** soreness, perceived recovery, parasympathetic reactivation — and **not** strength restoration (a null).

**The asymmetry that decides it:** the cost side has demonstrated effects in controlled trials on a variable plausibly upstream of velocity; the benefit side's demonstrated effects are perceptual. **Declining to plunge within fifteen minutes of a lift therefore forfeits only perception and avoids a measured biological cost — and that holds without any conversion at all.**

🚨 **STATED AT ITS CORRECT STRENGTH: this is NOT "skip the cold tub and you will throw harder."** It is: **a habit whose only demonstrated returns are how you feel is being paid for with the adaptation you went to the weight room to get.** Stopping a cost is not earning a return.

⭐ **THE GENERALISABLE FORM, which is the durable content of this file:** when **neither** side of a trade is priceable in the outcome you care about, the deciding question is not "which is bigger" but **"which side has a demonstrated cost on a variable plausibly upstream of the outcome?"** That form lets F-597's inference run without smuggling a cross-sectional correlation into an instruction, and it moves Dispute #60.

**The limit:** all of this is solid for *trained-limb* immersion. For the whole-body tub an athlete actually uses, the strength cost is an uninformative interval and the hypertrophy cost's interval touches zero (Dispute #61).

## 6. WHAT TO DO

**Offseason / velocity block (October):** **move the tub off the lift.** Not banned — moved. Nothing within ~2 h after resistance training; park it on non-lift days or in the evening. Keep it available, because the soreness benefit is real and block adherence matters more than block optimisation.

**Never in the ~2 h before throwing or testing.** g = −0.68 on CMJ at 0 h. Cold muscle is slow muscle. This is the one acute finding with a real scheduling consequence, because a lift → tub → throwing session does happen on a two-a-day fall schedule.

**In-season:** the adaptation argument largely dissolves, because you are not buying hypertrophy that week — you are buying the next appearance. A tub that helps a pitcher feel and sleep better between close outings is a legitimate purchase. **Know what you are not buying: strength restoration.** ⚠️ **And know that no study has ever tested this — F-611. The periodisation logic is the right shape; the evidence for it in a pitcher does not exist.**

**If he says it is how he sleeps:** he keeps it, moved off the lift. **F-399→F-412 make sleep the highest-leverage recovery variable in this corpus by a distance**, and an unpriceable hypertrophy decrement is not worth trading against a sleep habit that works.

**Do not act on the between-innings cooling result.** F-360: 67–70 mph, n = 8 and n = 22, post-hoc pain split, harm arm four times the benefit arm — and it is circulating in the industry with the magnitude quoted and the sample hidden.

### The measurable check

Measure what the intervention acts on, **never mph**:

- **Weekly body mass and lean mass** — same scale, same time of day, fasted — plus the block's main-lift 1RM or 3RM.
- **Detection:** the corpus holds no within-athlete weekly mass SD for an in-season college pitcher (F-507 item 6). Bracketed at **1.0–1.5 lb weekly SD, detecting a 4 lb divergence from the expected block trajectory needs ~6–8 weekly weigh-ins** — most of a block. **One week of weights proves nothing.**
- This is a decision made on mechanism and reviewed at the end of a block, not validated in a fortnight.
- **For the in-season use, the honest outcome is subjective** — a fixed 0–10 soreness and perceived-recovery scale. **Do not put a dynamometer on it and call it performance: that is the F-581 / F-582 error exactly.**

## 7. GAPS

1. 🚨 **No post-OUTING whole-body CWI study with a pitching outcome, in any population (F-611).** The nearest objects are F-360 (local, intra-outing, 67–70 mph) and F-581 (post-outing icing, dynamometer outcome). **This is the gap that decides whether §3–§4 applies to a baseball program at all — Dispute #62.**
2. **No delayed-CWI arm anywhere (F-605, F-606).** Three arms — 0 min / +6 h / none — hypertrophy and 1RM. **The cheapest unrun study in the topic, and the one that would convert it from "avoid" to "schedule."**
3. **Whether Grgic ran a between-subgroup test, and the k and n of the whole-body cell (F-603, Dispute #61).** One methods paragraph decides whether this file's headline is a finding or a subgroup artefact. **Verification priority #1.**
4. **No publication-bias assessment retrievable for either CWI meta-analysis (F-613)** — in a literature where bias correction drove F-189 from 0.26 to 0.01 and F-190 from 0.44 to 0.107, that is the most informative missing number here.
5. **`NCT06565468`** — "The Effect of Cold Water Immersion on Recovery and Performance in NCAA Division I Athletes." Sport, enrolment, protocol, status all unknown. **If the sport is baseball it is the only study that speaks to gap 1.**
6. **No baseball player anywhere in the CWI adaptation literature (F-613).** Check 1 fails in an unusual way: not "the sample was 78 mph" but "the sample never threw a ball."
7. **Zero accuracy, command or location outcomes** — Dispute #27's ninth venue.
8. **Immersion depth is never reported against intramuscular temperature in these trials**, so the limb/whole-body mechanism in §3.1 is an inference, not a measurement.
9. **Heat is unresolved and self-contradictory (F-612).** The one pitching-relevant strand — hot water immersion improving late-phase RFD recovery better than CWI — is on the variable closest to what a pitcher sells. Either a reading cycle settles it or it gets graded FOLKLORE.
