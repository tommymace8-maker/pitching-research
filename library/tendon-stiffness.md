# TENDON STIFFNESS, THE STRETCH-SHORTENING CYCLE, AND THE "ELASTIC ENERGY" LEVER

**Opened 2026-10-09.** Findings **F-628 → F-645**. Disputes **#65, #66**.
⚠️ **EVERY CLAIM IN THIS FILE IS SNIPPET-ONLY. Zero primary texts were opened (F-628 — tenth consecutive fully egress-blocked cycle).** No magnitude here may be quoted as verified.

---

## 1. Why the topic exists

The corpus's most-quoted mechanism claim, **F-064 ("the shoulder is a spring, not a motor")**, had never been pressure-tested on its own premises. Peak shoulder internal-rotation power of 11,838 ± 4,170 W in a 30–50 ms window and **−201 ± 70 J of negative work during cocking** (Roach & Lieberman 2014, *J Exp Biol* 217(12):2139–2149) imply stored energy returning. Nobody asked the follow-up: **are spring properties trainable, and by how much?**

Pre-cycle registry state: `stretch-shortening` 0 hits, `muscle-tendon` 0, `fascicle` 0, `heavy slow resistance` 0, `shear wave` 0, `vertical stiffness` 0, `tendon stiffness` 0 as a phrase.

---

## 2. What is ESTABLISHED

**Tendon stiffness is trainable by high-strain loading, and the adaptation is material, not morphological.**

| Property | ES | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Tendon stiffness | 0.70 | 0.51, 0.88 |
| Young's modulus | 0.69 | 0.36, 1.03 |
| Cross-sectional area | 0.24 | 0.07, 0.42 |

Bohm, Mersmann & Arampatzis (2015), *Sports Med Open* 1:7, DOI 10.1186/s40798-015-0009-9 — 27 studies, 37 interventions, **264 participants (~7.1 per intervention)**. Loading magnitude is the key variable; >12 weeks. Replicated in shape by Lazarczuk et al. (2022), *Sports Med* 52(10):2405–2429, DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01695-y.

**That is the whole of what is established, and it is a statement about tendons, not about throwing.** (F-629)

---

## 3. 🚨 THE POPULATION PROBLEM — the adaptation disappears in trained athletes

Lazarczuk's own limitations paragraph: *"only three studies recruited well-trained athletes... all of which displayed no change in PT stiffness even following high-intensity training."*

Corroborated three ways: professional athletes cross-sectionally (*Diagnostics* 2023 13(15):2541 — but cross-sectional, so it cannot speak to adaptation); adolescent handball high-load intervention (PMC7987778 — no stiffness or strain change); and **Lee 2025's placebo arm (professional footballers, 10 weeks of high-intensity work): +4.6%, d = 0.32.**

Against which, every large number and its population:

| Gain | Population |
|---|---|
| +57.3% (67.5 → 106.2 N/mm), RTD +36% — Kubo 2001, *J Appl Physiol* 91(1):26–32 | **untrained young men** |
| +57.7% at ~80% 1RM (0% at ~40%) | **older adults** |
| "up to 61.6%" — Oranchuk 2019, DOI 10.1111/sms.13375 | single-study maximum, not pooled |
| +51% / +66%, 6 wk, n = 11/group | recreational |
| +20.1 ± 20.5%, 12 wk, n = 15/arm | Achilles **tendinopathy patients** (⚠️ SD = mean) |

**F-630 / F-631 — the structural output: the restriction-of-range rule applies to ADAPTATION MAGNITUDES, not only to correlations.** An adaptation responds to load *above habitual*; a decade-trained tissue has a higher habitual, so the same protocol is a smaller relative overload — the same fact about selected samples that collapses r in Luera 2020, seen through a different statistic. ⚠️ **Graded EMERGING, direction only: the magnitude of the attenuation is withdrawn (three studies plus an n = 5 arm cannot establish it).**

## 4. 🚨 Where the causal chain breaks

The commercial argument: isometrics raise stiffness (**intervention**) → stiffer tendon transmits force faster (**assumed**) → higher RFD (**correlation**) → higher velocity (**nothing**).

Maffiuletti et al. (2016), *Eur J Appl Physiol*, DOI 10.1007/s00421-016-3346-6:
- *"existing data are inconsistent and **a causative relationship has not been clearly demonstrated**."*
- Achilles stiffness vs RFD **r² = 0.29 – 0.90, varying with the analyst's time window.** One choice, not one magnitude.
- Training-induced stiffness change *"typically <50%"* vs population variability *"~500%"*. ⚠️ **The derived ~10% trainable fraction is WITHDRAWN as a quantity** (one unreferenced parenthetical, possibly non-commensurable units — Challenge 2). **The structure survives without it** (F-634).
- **Bed-rest detraining: stiffness decline NOT correlated with RFD decline (n = 6).**

## 5. 🚨 The shoulder has no route at all

- **Zero trials, any population, report a tendon-stiffness outcome for the rotator cuff or long head of biceps.** The retrievable cuff-loading literature reports DASH, pain and function (RoCTEx PMC4318133, PASE PMC10880378, NCT01984203, NCT04073212). **Closest object in existence: NCT05969652** (heavy slow resistance vs eccentric, lists supraspinatus tendon *structure*) — **no results posted.**
- **No in-vivo strain-energy figure exists for the rotator cuff**, so no Achilles comparison is possible. Only cuff mechanics retrieved: an instrument validation (Hatta 2014, *J Ultrasound Med* 33(9):1641, r = 0.73).
- Every ranked stiffness modality is **Achilles or patellar** — long, series-elastic tendons. The cuff tendons are short and broad.
- ⚠️ **F-064's tissue attribution is an untested premise:** −201 J of negative work is consistent with capsule, ligament or eccentrically-loaded muscle, none of which a tendon protocol addresses.

**The structural fact: the one place with a documented elastic mechanism has no adaptation literature; the places with a rich adaptation literature are not where F-064's energy is stored.** ✅ **F-064's coaching line survives with one word guarded — say "a better spring," never "a stiffer tendon."** (F-636)

## 6. ⚠️ The target variable may be wrong

- Equal-work duration comparison (1 s pulses vs 20 s holds): long holds raised **stiffness**; short reps raised **elastic energy output** more. ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — blog-mediated, no author/journal/year. A lead, not a finding; nothing load-bearing rests on it.**
- **Named head-to-head, PMC5555899** (n = 11/group, 6 wk): both raised stiffness (+51%/+66%, no between-group difference), RFD gains **trends only**, and *"plyometric training (but not isometric training) enhances the extensibility of tendon structures during **ballistic** contractions."*
- The Achilles compliance trade-off, stated in review: a more compliant tendon stores more energy per unit force but has lower restoring force and higher strain.

**A pitch is a ballistic contraction. The modality ranked FIRST for static stiffness is the one that did NOT improve ballistic tendon behaviour. There is an optimum; its location is unknown for throwing.** → **Dispute #66.** (F-637)

## 7. The prescription, and why a college program cannot run it

**Fu et al. (2026), *Front Sports Act Living*, DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1782503, PMID 41908219** — 49 studies, **31 RCTs, n = 1,388**, 5 categories / 15 subtypes, searched to 18 Feb 2025:

| Outcome | Winner | SUCRA |
|---|---|---|
| Stiffness | long-term isometric plantar flexion | **89.3%** |
| Stiffness, 2nd | **low-load BFR** | — |
| Stiffness, last | short-term eccentric | lowest |
| CSA | short-term plyometric jump | **95.6%** (SMD 1.33) |
| — | stretching | low across outcomes |

⭐ **LL-BFR ranking second collides usefully with F-615→F-627: "BFR" is not one claim.** Its tendon-stiffness evidence is a different and better-populated literature than its proximal-hypertrophy evidence. (F-638)

**Arampatzis-line protocol (all snippet-only, primaries not opened):**
- **Stimulus: tendon strain 4.5–6.5%** ≈ 90% MVC *on average*.
- **5 sets × 4 reps, 3 s on / 3 s off, ~2 min rest, 3–4×/week.**
- **Time course:** stiffness change *"within the first eight weeks, no further significant changes thereafter"* across a 16-week design (DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-56840-6).
- **Dose-response:** 90% MVC → 4.55 ± 1.38% strain vs 55% MVC → 2.85 ± 0.99%; stiffness moved only at 90%.
- Screening threshold offered: MVC tendon strain **> 9.0%** = muscle–tendon imbalance.

🚨 **AND THE NUMBER THAT BREAKS IT: reaching 6.4% strain required relative loads "between 42 and 90% of the MVC."** %MVC is a **2:1-invalid individual proxy**. Prescribe "90% MVC holds" to twelve pitchers and you deliver the intended strain to some, double to others, nothing to a third group — and the only way to know requires ultrasound tendon strain synchronised to a dynamometer. **Dose unmeasurable, independent variable unmeasurable, outcome unmeasurable. Dispute #41's eighth encounter, in its hardest form yet.** ✅ One usable fact survives: **if run at all, eight weeks is the window.** (F-639)

## 8. Collagen — the one trained-athlete lever, and its trial has eleven people

**Lee, Robshaw & Erskine (2025), *Exp Physiol*, DOI 10.1113/EP092106, PMC12576002.** n = 11 (6 collagen / 5 placebo), Women's Championship first team. 30 g hydrolysed collagen + 500 mg vitamin C **before** each session, 3×/wk, 10 wk pre-season, high-intensity resistance + plyometrics + pitch work. **PT stiffness +15.4% vs +4.6%; Young's modulus +14.2% vs +3.4%; within-group d = 0.81 vs 0.32; P = 0.002. No hypertrophy in either group.**

🚨 **F-439's rule as extended to positives by F-616 the previous day.** Effective n per group = 2(6)(5)/11 = **5.45** → independent-groups MDE **d ≈ 1.70**. The reported separation is far below that, so the significance comes from the repeated-measures structure, not group size; the exact MDE is design-dependent and no single number is claimed. **What is claimable: an 11-athlete trial cannot estimate a magnitude precisely, and a significant result from one has passed the significance filter. Treat +15.4% as the ceiling of a wide interval.** (F-640)

⭐ **Second independent application of F-616's rule, in an unrelated literature, one day after it was invented. The rule generalises.**

**Contradicted:** Jerger et al. (2022), *Scand J Med Sci Sports*, DOI 10.1111/sms.14164 — stiffness and strength rose in **both** groups, **no between-group difference**. Dose split (PMC13028264): **15–30 g/day → between-group effects; ~5 g → within-group only**, and *"the two studies using higher doses"* is the entire high-dose evidence base. → **Dispute #65.** (F-641)

## 9. 🚨 THE GAPS — stated flatly

1. **Nobody has ever related tendon or muscle stiffness to pitch velocity, in any population.** Four-angle sweep (F-635).
2. **No tendon or muscle stiffness measurement exists in a confirmed 85+ sample.**
3. **The pitcher elastography literature has the wrong outcome every time** — five studies; the only correlation is infraspinatus shear-wave velocity vs **pitch count** (r = 0.467, p = 0.003, high school).
4. **Zero accuracy or command outcomes anywhere in this topic — Dispute #27's eleventh venue.**
5. **No shoulder tendon stiffness has ever been trained in a trial** (F-636).
6. **No trial reports between-subject SD and within-athlete change together** — the measurement that would convert marker-vs-lever from argument to arithmetic, registry-wide (F-634).
7. ⭐ **THE CHEAPEST OPEN ITEM: the javelin correlation.** Köykkä et al. (2026), DOI 10.1177/17479541261448854 **already measured vertical stiffness (6,102 ± 2,049 N/m) and throwing performance in the same high-level athletes.** One table would give the first stiffness-vs-throwing-velocity coefficient in existence.

## 10. What to actually do with an 85+ arm

**Decline the stiffness block. Keep the braking work. Change the readout.**

The only place the lower-limb tendon literature has a mechanical home in pitching is the lead-leg block — a **~50–80 ms braking event** served by series-elastic lower-limb tendons. The registered chain: **F-006** (drop jump R² = 0.30, RSI p = .002, **n = 53 professionals**, CMJ and squat jump null) + **F-445/F-642** (braking force survives the regression, **leg stiffness drops out**) + **F-050** (lead knee extension ≈ 0.035 mph/degree between professionals) + **F-061/F-063** (the block decelerates the centre of mass in a ~20 ms slice).

- **Tell the pitcher:** *"We're not training tendon stiffness, we're training how hard and how fast you can put the brakes on. The front leg is a post, not a shock absorber."*
- **Drill:** controlled-height drop jumps, repeat hops, and the heavy strength work underwriting them. **Nothing new is added.**
- **Video failure:** lead knee flexing *through* foot contact instead of arresting — at 240 fps, the knee angle still closing after the foot is down.
- **How we know it's working:** 🚨 **force plate, not radar — braking RFD and braking peak force across the block.** F-018's rule. **Velocity is undetectable at squad size** (F-623: 106 pitchers for 1 mph, 422 for 0.5 mph, from F-622's SD = 1.83 mph); command needs **~200 tracked pitches** for 2 inches (F-186).
- ⚠️ **Never attach "this will help your velocity" to it.** F-006 is CROSS_SECTIONAL and that belongs in the same sentence. (F-645)

**Collagen, if a pitcher already buys it:** 15–30 g with vitamin C, **before** the session. Unverifiable without an ultrasound; not the thing the block is built on.
