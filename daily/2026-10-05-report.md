🚨 **AND THE SPECIFIC MISMATCH IS SHARPER THAN THAT. ## 2. ⭐⭐ THE RESULT OF THE DAY — TWO INDEPENDENT ERROR ROUTES BOTH LAND AT ±10% OF 1RM

VBT's central selling claim is **precision**: your tested 1RM is stale, bar speed knows your true strength *today*, so the load is right. **Its own validation literature does not support that claim.** Two separate routes, computed independently, converge.

### Route A — the profile's prediction error (F-592)

- **Individualised load–velocity profiles: pooled standard error of estimate ≈ 9.8% of 1RM.** Sports Medicine 2023 IPD meta-analysis, doi 10.1007/s40279-023-01854-9, **434 participants, 20 studies.**
- **A tested 1RM's own between-trial variation: −5.6% to +4.8%.** The best velocity-predicted method's individual variation: **−5.5% to +27.8%.**
- Best-case predicted 1RM (5 warm-up sets up to 90% 1RM): **ICC = 0.92, SEM = 8.6 kg, CV = 5.7%.**

**DERIVED IN-CYCLE: the velocity-predicted 1RM is roughly TWICE as imprecise as simply testing the lift.** On a 400 lb squat, ±9.8% is **±39 lb**. A 5% load increment is 20 lb. **The prediction error is about two load steps wide — and two load steps is the entire resolution the method exists to provide.**

### Route B — the device's single-session detection limit (F-594)

- **GymAware mean-velocity typical error: 0.070 m/s** in use (ICC = 0.91, CV = 7%).
- **Published smallest worthwhile change: 0.05 m/s for mean velocity**, 0.10 m/s for peak velocity, free-weight back squat.

**The error exceeds the smallest worthwhile change.** Run it out the way this corpus ran the 6° hip-IR protocol (F-566):

> **MDC₉₅ = 1.96 × √2 × TE = 1.96 × 1.414 × 0.070 = 0.194 m/s.**

**A single-set mean-velocity change must exceed ~0.19 m/s before you may call it real in one athlete on one day. That is ~4× the smallest worthwhile change.** The whole usable squat velocity range from ~40% to ~90% 1RM is roughly 0.4–1.2 m/s, i.e. ~0.8 m/s wide; typical VBT zones run ~0.1 m/s per 5% of 1RM. **So 0.19 m/s ≈ two zones ≈ ~10% of 1RM.**

### ⭐ The convergence

**Route A (prediction SEE, 434 subjects, 20 studies) gives ≈ 9.8% of 1RM. Route B (device MDC from a single set) gives ≈ 10% of 1RM. Two unrelated error sources, one answer: the precision floor of velocity-based load prescription is about ±10% of 1RM.** That is wider than the 5% increments the method exists to fine-tune.

✅ **AND THE FIX IS FREE AND IS THE DAY'S ONE REAL PRESCRIPTION.** MDC shrinks as √k when you average k observations: **3 sets → 0.112 m/s; 8 reps → 0.069 m/s.** **Never react to one set.** This does not rescue Route A — averaging cannot fix a biased profile — but it makes within-session fatigue readings usable, which is the one use that survives today (§4).

### 2a. 🚨 The anchor of the whole method is its least reliable number (F-593)

**V₁RM — the minimum-velocity threshold, the velocity at which a true 1RM moves — is 0.24 ± 0.06 m/s with ICC = 0.42, SEM = 0.05 m/s, CV = 22.5%.**

**ICC = 0.42 is not "acceptable with caveats." It is a measurement that disagrees with itself.** Meanwhile mean and peak velocity at submaximal test loads are fine (**ICC ≥ 0.801, CV ≤ 2.36%**). **The device is reliable; the thing it is anchored to is not.** Every load–velocity extrapolation to 1RM passes through V₁RM, which is why Route A's error is as large as it is — this is the mechanism under that 9.8%.

⚠️ **AND A MEASUREMENT FLAG, REGISTERED HONESTLY.** The searches returned **two** GymAware mean-velocity error figures: **0.070 m/s** (described as typical error) and **0.01 m/s** (described as controlled testing). These differ by 7×, the choice changes every number in §2, and the vendor-facing material quotes the small one. **The reconciliation is almost certainly bench-rig accuracy against a known displacement versus in-use reliability with a human lifting free weights — but I could not open either paper to confirm it.** Registered as a flag, not a finding. **The CV = 7% figure is independently consistent with the larger number** (7% of a 0.7 m/s working velocity ≈ 0.049 m/s), which is why §2 uses it.

## 3. ⚠️ THE ONE VBT TRIAL WITH A THROWING OUTCOME — AND WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT

**Abuajwa, Hamlin, Hafiz & Razman (2022), *PeerJ* 10:e14049, PMID 36193438, PMC9526411.**

**n = 22 university handball players**, randomised to two arms, **3×/wk × 5 weeks**:
- **G1:** bar speed **0.75–0.96 m/s** (≈ **60% 1RM**)
- **G2:** bar speed **1.03–1.20 m/s** (≈ **40% 1RM**)

Results, **both arms**: skeletal muscle mass **+3.1% / +3.5%** (p < .01); 1RM **+15.5% / +15.0%** (p < .01); **throwing velocity +18.7% / +18.3% (p < .01).** Authors' own conclusion: both prescribed velocities "elicited similar changes."

**FOUR THINGS ARE WRONG WITH USING THIS, AND THE FIRST TWO ARE FATAL.**

**(1) NO NO-TRAINING CONTROL GROUP (F-595).** Two active arms only. This is the exact design this corpus corrected F-039 for ("do not re-import 'removal design'") and corrected the Mah 2011 sleep-extension entry for (uncontrolled pre-post, practice effects). **A pre-post with two active arms and no control cannot separate training from familiarisation, regression to the mean, or a maximal-throw test the subjects had never performed before baseline.**

**(2) +18.7% IN FIVE WEEKS IS NOT A TRAINING EFFECT.** Price it on this population: **18.7% of 85 mph is +15.9 mph in five weeks.** Price it on handball's own velocity — which this corpus has flagged at **45.9–51.3 mph** under Dispute #29 — and it is still **≈ +9 mph in five weeks** in trained collegiate athletes. **Both are physically implausible and both arms produced it identically.** An effect that appears equally in two arms that differ by 20% of 1RM is not attributable to what distinguishes them. **The most parsimonious reading is an unfamiliar baseline test.**

**(3) 🚨 IT IS MISLABELLED, AND THIS IS A TYPE B FAILURE IN THE CORPUS'S OWN TAXONOMY (F-596).** The field cites this as evidence for VBT. **It contains no percentage-based arm.** Both arms were assigned by velocity; what differs between them is **load** (60% vs 40% 1RM). **It is a load-zone comparison wearing VBT's clothes, and it cannot answer "is VBT better than percentages" because it never ran that contrast.** This is the 2026-09-30 audit's Type B — *the study never ran the comparison the field attributes to it* — the same error as F-044's missing normal-stride condition, found in a fresh literature.

**(4) POWER.** n = 22, **11 per arm**. The between-arm "no difference" fails the F-439 n ≥ 97 rule **by a factor of about nine**. It is a detection floor, not an absence.

⚠️ **SAMPLE MISMATCH — DIRECTIONAL ONLY, and barely that.** Handball, collegiate, ~48 mph against an 85 mph floor.

**What survives: the existence of the trial, and its own stated conclusion that two load zones 20% of 1RM apart produced indistinguishable throwing gains. Nothing about magnitude. Do not quote +18.7% in any direction.**

## 4. THE VELOCITY-LOSS LITERATURE — WHERE VBT ACTUALLY HAS AN ANSWER, AND WHY IT INVERTS FOR A PITCHER

**This is the strongest part of the topic and it is a different variable than everything above.** Velocity loss (VL) within a set is a **volume/fatigue** dial, not a load dial. The literature is real and reasonably consistent:

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Sports Medicine 2022**, doi 10.1007/s40279-022-01754-4 | VL threshold choice **does not affect** strength or muscle-endurance gains. **Higher VL superior for hypertrophy. Lower VL superior for jumping, sprinting, and velocity against submaximal loads.** |
| **Sagepub 2024**, doi 10.1177/17479541241244581 | **VL ≤ 25% > VL > 25% for strength** — but primarily when additional exercises were performed alongside. |
| **Proximity-to-failure meta** | **VL > 25% likely greater hypertrophy than < 20%**; similar to 20–25%. |
| **Rodiles-Guerrero et al.**, 8 wk, trained men (22.7 ± 1.9 yr), squat 69–85% 1RM | **Higher VL (> 20%) maximised hypertrophy and GH; 10% and 20% VL gave the greatest CMJ gains; 30% and 40% gave considerably less.** High VL (~40%) full squat → greater hypertrophy than 10–20%. |

### 4a. ⚠️ CHECK 4 — one of those three outcomes is nearly a training-specificity identity (F-598)

"Lower VL improves **velocity against submaximal loads**" is close to restating the intervention. **Training at low velocity loss means, by construction, spending your reps at high bar velocities; the outcome measured is bar velocity at submaximal load.** That is the test being the training. It is not a geometric identity in the F-534 sense, but it is the same class of error — **an outcome that cannot fail to move in the direction of the assignment**, and it should not be counted as a third independent benefit alongside jump and sprint.

### 4b. ⭐ AND THE TRADE-OFF INVERTS FOR A PITCHER (F-597)

The field's standard VBT advice is **use low VL thresholds (10–20%) to preserve power, CMJ and sprint.** Lay that over what this corpus already holds:

- **Low VL buys: CMJ height** — the channel whose mph exchange rate is **unknown**, CI [−0.280, +0.404] (F-004/F-438), and **sprint**, for which no velocity link is registered at all.
- **High VL buys: hypertrophy** — i.e. **body mass and lean mass**, which are **r = 0.58 (p = .0004)** and **r = 0.52** against fastball velocity in n = 33 D1 (F-001, F-002), **the strongest physical correlates in the entire literature.**

**So the usual justification for choosing a low VL threshold does not apply to a pitcher: the outcome it protects is the one this corpus cannot price, and the outcome it sacrifices is the one with the strongest (if cross-sectional) link to mph.**

🚨 **STATED AT ITS CORRECT STRENGTH, AND NO STRONGER. This is NOT "chase hypertrophy and you will throw harder."** F-504 is explicit: **there is no within-athlete mass-change → velocity-change slope of any kind, in any population, at any level.** The mass correlation has never been manipulated. **What is claimed is strictly narrower and strictly negative: the standard reason for paying the cost of a low VL threshold is not a reason for a pitcher.** That is a reason to stop paying a cost, not a promise of a return — and the distinction is the whole point of this file.

⚠️ **AND BOTH SIDES ARE OFF-POPULATION.** Every VL study above is trained non-throwers, mostly 20-something men in squat and bench. No VL study has ever been run in baseball players (F-600).

### 4c. The one defensible operational use, and it is F-086 with a number attached (F-599)

**Velocity loss as a set-termination and session-termination cue is the single use of a bar-speed device that survives today**, because it is the one use where the lever and the measurement are the same physical quantity **within one session** — no 1RM extrapolation, no V₁RM, no day-to-day stability required, and the comparison is rep 1 against rep n minutes apart on the same bar with the same device.

This is exactly **F-086**'s industry protocol (4–6% / 6–10% session termination, ~6% weekly target) — registered in August as "a starting heuristic, not a threshold with evidence behind it." **Today supplies the missing evidence layer and it mostly agrees on structure while disagreeing on scale**: the research VL thresholds that change adaptations are **10–40% within-set**, an order of magnitude above F-086's 4–10% across-session numbers. **These are different quantities and should never be compared directly — but nobody has validated either in a pitcher.**

✅ **AND THE DETECTION ARITHMETIC APPLIES HERE TOO AND IS FAVOURABLE:** within-set VL is a large signal (10–40%) read against the same 0.070 m/s error. At a 0.7 m/s first-rep velocity, a 20% VL is **0.14 m/s** — below the 0.194 m/s single-measure MDC₉₅, **but comfortably above it once you average the first three reps against the last three** (MDC ≈ 0.112 m/s). **Three-rep averaging is what makes the one defensible use actually readable. One rep does not.**

## 5. THE ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY PASS — does the mechanism survive?

**On the VL trade-off: yes, and it is the least speculative mechanism in this file.** The hypertrophy/CMJ divergence is a dose-vs-quality story with known tissue correlates. Reps taken deep into a set accumulate metabolic stress and mechanical tension at low velocities — the stimulus profile associated with cross-sectional-area change (and in Rodiles-Guerrero, with a GH response). Reps terminated early keep motor-unit discharge rates high and fatigue low, which is the stimulus profile associated with rate-of-force-development qualities. **Two different adaptations, genuinely in competition for the same sets.** The trade-off is not a statistical artefact; it is what you would predict from the tissue.

**On the precision claim: the mechanism is actively against VBT, and this is the part the industry does not say.** Bar velocity at a fixed load is not a readout of strength. It is a readout of strength **plus** arousal, caffeine, sleep, warm-up completeness, intent, skill at that bar path, and shoe-to-floor friction. **Those are the same inputs this corpus has already shown move a countermovement jump by 2–5% within a day (pre-game warm-up / PAPE, F-388-area).** So the day-to-day swing in bar speed is **partly real readiness and partly everything else**, and the device cannot separate them. **V₁RM's CV = 22.5% is that confound showing up in the data.** The method's premise — that today's bar speed tells you today's true capacity — requires the non-strength inputs to be small, and they are not.

⚠️ **RULE 1, one line, and then move on:** nothing in today's material bears on elbow or shoulder load. VL thresholds are a barbell variable; no VBT or VL study measured arm kinetics. **The injury constraint is neutral here, which is unusual and worth noting rather than manufacturing a caution.**

## 7. THE COACH ASKS — four pointed questions

### Q1 (to the biomechanist): "I have a GymAware. Should I trust Tuesday's squat bar speed to tell me how the kid is doing?"

**Not from one set, and not for load.** Single-set MDC₉₅ is **0.194 m/s** against a smallest worthwhile change of **0.05 m/s** — you cannot resolve a meaningful change in one reading. Average the first three reps and the number becomes **0.112 m/s**, which is readable against a within-set velocity loss of 15–20% but still not against a day-to-day readiness swing.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"the first three reps are the measurement — one rep is noise."* **The drill is:** average the first three reps of the first working set, every session, same load, same device, same spot on the platform. **On video the failure looks like** a coach chasing a single slow rep into a deload, or a pitcher who warms up less thoroughly on Tuesday and reads it as fatigue. **Here's how we know it's working:** you are no longer able to generate a "he's down today" call from a single set, because the three-rep average has to move **0.112 m/s** before the device permits the sentence.

### Q2 (to the biomechanist): "Percentages off a tested max, or velocity off a profile?"

**Test the max.** A tested 1RM's between-trial variation is **−5.6% to +4.8%**; the velocity-predicted 1RM's pooled SEE is **≈ 9.8% of 1RM**, with individual variation running to **+27.8%**. **The thing VBT exists to replace is about twice as precise as the replacement.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** nothing — this is a coach-facing decision and he should never hear about it. **The drill is:** test or estimate the max the way you already do, program percentages, and use the device for within-session fatigue only (Q3). **On video the failure looks like** two pitchers on adjacent racks at loads 40 lb apart because the profile fitted differently that morning. **Here's how we know it's working:** session-to-session prescribed loads stop moving in ways you cannot explain from the program.

### Q3 (to the anatomist): "Then what is the device actually for?"

**Deciding when to stop a set, and when to stop a session.** It is the one use where the lever and the measurement are the same physical quantity inside one session — no 1RM extrapolation, no V₁RM, no overnight stability required. **This is F-086 with detection arithmetic attached, not a new recommendation.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"the set ends when the bar slows down by about a fifth, not when you hit a number."* **The drill is:** first-three-rep average vs last-three-rep average; terminate at the assigned VL. **On video the failure looks like** three grinding reps at the end of a set that the rep target demanded and the bar speed had already vetoed. **Here's how we know it's working:** at the assigned 20% VL and a ~0.7 m/s first-rep velocity, the terminating difference is **≈ 0.14 m/s** — above the three-rep MDC of 0.112, so the call is readable. Log it; if your actual VL at termination is scattering from 8% to 35%, the device is decorating a rep scheme rather than driving it.

### Q4 (to both): "High velocity loss or low, for a guy at 89 chasing 93?"

**Lean high — 20–25% rather than 10% — and understand that this is a decision to stop paying a cost, not a bet on a return.** Low VL protects CMJ height and sprint. CMJ height's mph exchange rate has a 95% CI of **[−0.280, +0.404]**; sprint has no registered velocity link. High VL buys hypertrophy, i.e. mass and lean mass, **r = 0.58 and r = 0.52** against velocity. **Nobody has manipulated mass and measured mph (F-504), so neither branch is a promise.** But only one branch's justification survives contact with this file.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"we're taking sets a little deeper this block, and the goal is you weigh more in February than you do now."* **The drill is:** assign 20–25% VL on the main lower-body lift in the off-season accumulation block instead of 10–15%; keep one low-VL day for the explosive work if you want it, and do not pretend it is buying mph. **On video the failure looks like** nothing — this is a weight-room choice and it is invisible on the mound, which is exactly why it needs a measured check. **Here's how we know it's working:** **scale and DEXA/BodPod, not the radar gun.** Body mass and lean mass are the proximal outcomes with the evidence behind them; the mph claim is unavailable at any sample size this program can reach. ⚠️ **AND THE HONEST CAVEAT HE SHOULD HEAR: we are choosing this because the usual reason not to does not apply to pitchers, not because anyone has shown it adds a tick.**

## 8. CROSS-EXAMINATION

### The PITCHING-COACH challenges the BIOMECHANIST — "your ±10% convergence is two restatements of one error, not two independent routes."

**THE CHARGE.** §2 claims Route A (9.8% SEE) and Route B (0.19 m/s MDC ≈ 10% of 1RM) independently confirm each other. **They may not be independent.** If Route A's prediction error is *caused* by measurement noise in the velocities the profile is fitted to, then both routes are measuring the same device error twice and the convergence is arithmetic, not corroboration. **The corpus has been burned by exactly this — a claimed R² = .945 that was a geometric identity restated.**

**THE BIOMECHANIST CONCEDES PART AND DEFENDS PART.** **Conceded: the two routes are not fully independent, and the word "convergence" is doing more rhetorical work than it has earned.** Device noise propagates into the profile fit, so some of the 9.8% is Route B's error wearing Route A's clothes. **Defended: they are not the same number either.** Route A contains at least two error sources Route B does not — **V₁RM's biological unreliability (ICC = 0.42, CV = 22.5%, which is a property of the athlete, not the device)** and the model-form error from extrapolating a linear or near-linear fit to a load nobody lifted. Route B contains only within-session device-plus-performer noise. **The defensible statement is weaker and is what the finding will be registered as: both routes land near ±10% of 1RM, they share an unknown fraction of their error, and the shared fraction cannot be partitioned without opening the papers.** → **Dispute #59, and it is Dispute #41's cousin: a number the corpus cannot decompose but which does not change the action either way, since ±10% and ±7% both exceed a 5% load step.**

### The ANATOMIST challenges the PITCHING-COACH — "your Q4 answer smuggles F-001's correlation into an instruction."

**THE CHARGE.** Q4 tells a pitcher *"the goal is you weigh more in February."* **F-001 is cross-sectional, n = 33, and F-504 says no within-athlete mass→velocity slope exists anywhere.** This is the stride-length failure mode exactly: an observed association phrased as a prescription. **Stride length and extension both collapsed this way; INDEX.md flags F-049 as sitting in the same position right now.**

**THE COACH DEFENDS, AND THE DEFENCE IS NARROW.** **The instruction issued is not "gain weight to throw harder."** It is *"between two VL thresholds you must choose anyway, the usual tie-breaker does not apply to you."* **A VL threshold must take some value; there is no null option.** The choice is being made today on a justification (protect CMJ) whose downstream link is an interval spanning zero. **Declining to pay a cost for an unpriceable benefit is not a causal claim.** ⚠️ **CONCEDED ON THE PHRASING, AND THE REPORT TEXT IS CHANGED: the pitcher-facing sentence is a mass outcome check, not a velocity promise, and the caveat is now spoken aloud to him in Q4 rather than kept in the coach's head.** **The underlying worry is legitimate and is registered rather than argued away** → **Dispute #60.**

### The BIOMECHANIST challenges the ANATOMIST — "you called the VL trade-off 'the least speculative mechanism in this file.' On what evidence?"

**THE CHARGE.** §5's hypertrophy-vs-RFD story is a textbook narrative, and the GH observation in Rodiles-Guerrero is **a hormonal marker, not a mechanism** — acute GH response has repeatedly failed to predict hypertrophy in better-controlled work. **Citing it dresses up a plausible story as a measured pathway.**

**THE ANATOMIST CONCEDES THE GH CLAUSE ENTIRELY.** **Withdrawn: GH is removed as supporting evidence** and survives in §4 only as a reported result of that trial, not as mechanism. **Defended: the trade-off itself does not need GH.** It needs only that reps taken at low velocity late in a set differ in stimulus from reps terminated early — which is directly observed in the VL literature's own divergent outcomes (hypertrophy up, CMJ down, proportionally with VL, across multiple independent trials). **The mechanism claim is downgraded from "least speculative in this file" to "the only dose-response relationship in this file with a consistent direction across more than two independent samples."** That is accurate and smaller.

## 10. WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK — one thing, with an 85+ arm

**Change the velocity-loss threshold on the main lower-body lift from 10–15% to 20–25% for the accumulation block, and read it off a three-rep average rather than a single rep.**

- **What:** Main squat or trap-bar pattern. Terminate the set at **20–25% velocity loss** from the first-three-rep average, not at a rep count.
- **Why this and not the rest of VBT:** it is the only use of a bar-speed device where the measurement and the lever are the same physical quantity inside one session. Load prescription off a predicted 1RM carries **±9.8% of 1RM** and should not be used.
- **How he'd know it worked — and this is a mass check, not a velocity check:** **body mass and lean mass across the block** (scale weekly, same time of day, fasted; DEXA or BodPod at both ends). Those are the outcomes with the strongest registered link to velocity and the only ones this program can actually detect.
- **The detection honesty, stated out loud:** **you cannot evaluate this in mph.** F-586's arithmetic applies — at an assumed between-session fastball SD of ~1.0 mph, detecting 0.5 mph needs **≈ 32 paired observations**; a college starter makes 15–17 starts. **Any mph claim from one off-season block on one pitcher is unsupported at the sample size available.**
- **Free and first, before any of it:** log the **actual** VL at which sets are currently ending. If it is already scattering 8–35%, you do not have a threshold to change — you have a rep scheme with a device next to it.
