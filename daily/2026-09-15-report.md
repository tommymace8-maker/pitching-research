## 3. THE RESEARCH — the between-inning break

### 3a. ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY pass: two channels, opposite signs (F-355)

Sitting between innings does two things, and they disagree.

| Channel | Direction | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Phosphagen resynthesis | longer is **better** | fast — half-time 20–30 s (F-132) |
| Muscle temperature / neuromuscular readiness | longer is **worse** | slow — τ of order 300–1800 s |

The metabolic channel is **roughly an order of magnitude faster.** Therefore the ratio of metabolic benefit per second to thermal cost per second **falls monotonically across the break, by a factor of 11×–59%** over 120 seconds, for every parameter pair in the bracket.

**A crossover beyond which extra sitting is net negative must exist, and must be unique.**

⚠️ **Its location is NOT computable.** The two channels are denominated in different units — a fraction of an unmeasured per-pitch depletion `d` versus a percent of lower-body power — and nothing converts between them. **This is the shape argument F-327 made against the pitch clock, with the same limitation.**

**What it licenses:** "more rest is always better between innings" is FOLKLORE on structure alone.
**What it does not license:** any statement about where the crossover is.

### 3b. BIOMECHANIST pass: the thermal numbers, and where they came from (F-356)

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** From González Fernández et al. (2023), *Biol Sport* 40(2):335–344, PMC10108754, read in full: a passive **15-minute** half-time induces a muscle-temperature drop of **~1.5 °C**. From PMC10535876, read in full: **~3% loss of lower-body power per 1 °C.**

Scaling to 120 seconds:

| τ_T | ΔT at 120 s | share of half-time drop | power cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 s | **0.52 °C** | 34.7% | 1.56% |
| 600 s | **0.35 °C** | 23.3% | 1.05% |
| 1800 s | **0.25 °C** | 16.4% | 0.74% |
| linear | **0.20 °C** | 13.3% | 0.60% |

**0.20–0.52 °C; best estimate ~0.30–0.35 °C; ~0.6–1.6% of lower-body power.** The drop is **front-loaded** — a seventh of the time buys a quarter of the cooling.

> ⚠️ **AND HERE THE BIOMECHANIST STOPS.** F-004: bilateral vertical jump height has **no** significant relationship with fastball velocity (r = 0.07 NS). F-005: power per kg, r = 0.19 NS. **There is no published conversion from "percent of lower-body power" to "mph," and this corpus will not invent one.** Anyone who hands you a mph figure derived this way has done arithmetic the literature does not support.

### 3c. The metabolic table — the one with quotable cells (F-357)

**DERIVED**, τ = 28.9–43.3 s (F-132/F-327 bracket).

| Elapsed | recovery (τ=28.9) | (τ=43.3) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 64.6% | 50.0% |
| 60 s | 87.5% | 75.0% |
| 90 s | 95.6% | 87.5% |
| **120 s** | **98.4%** | **93.7%** |
| 150 s | 99.4% | 96.9% |
| 180 s | 99.8% | 98.4% |

> ⚠️ **THIS TABLE IS INVARIANT TO `d`.** The unmeasured depletion scales the absolute deficit and **cancels out of the fraction recovered.** An F-307-class invariance, and the reason these cells are quotable where F-327's are not.

**First 30 seconds buy 50.0–64.6 points. Last 30 (90→120 s) buy 2.9–6.3. Ratio 8.0×–22.5×** — against F-330's inter-pitch ratio of 1.60×. **95% recovery arrives at 86–130 s, which brackets the NCAA cap almost exactly.**

### 3d. The warm-up decision, priced (F-362)

| Last warm-up at | time left | share of that pitch's `d` repaid |
|---|---|---|
| 40 s | 80 s | 84–94% |
| 60 s | 60 s | 75–87% |
| 80 s | 40 s | 60–75% |
| 100 s | 20 s | 37–50% |
| 110 s | 10 s | 21–29% |

Worst case — last warm-up moved from 40 s to 110 s — leaves **~0.6 of ONE pitch's depletion** unpaid, against a standing inter-pitch deficit of **1.0–1.7 × d** carried on every pitch of the outing. The thermal benefit of the same shift is **0.1–0.3 °C ≈ 0.3–0.9%** of lower-body power, which §3b forbids converting.

**Both channels are small and they partly cancel.**

**The one asymmetry:** past the first minute he is already 94%+ recovered, so a late warm-up costs almost nothing metabolically while the thermal benefit is monotone. **"Take the break, then warm up at the end of it" — a tiebreaker, not a lever.**

### 3e. ⚠️ THE TRANSFER IS BROKEN AT BOTH ENDS (F-358, F-359)

**End one.** The re-warm-up meta-analysis (PMC10108754, read in full): 892 records screened, **4 studies reviewed, 3 meta-analysed.**

| Outcome | ES | p | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vertical jump height** | **0.66** | **0.001** | 0.0% |
| **Linear sprint time** | 0.19 | 0.440 | 38.4% |

Risk of bias: one "some concerns," two "high," one "some concerns." **No study rated low.**

**The one quality it reliably improves is the one this corpus established does not predict velocity.** The quality that did *not* move — a horizontal, cyclic, whole-body sprint, the closer analogue to a delivery — is the one you wanted.

**End two.** PMC6028199, read in full: 18 collegiate pitchers, **117 maximal pitches over 9 innings**, 13/inning, 5-minute breaks.
- Ball velocity 130.3 ± 6.2 → 127.8 ± 6.8 km/h (**−1.55 mph**, P = 0.001, d = 0.38); significant by the 7th.
- Isometric hip abduction 450.2 → 426.7 N (P = 0.009); adduction 450.7 → 421.4 N (P = 0.001); 9th-inning velocity loss correlated with adduction loss (r = 0.583, P = 0.011).
- **Squat jump: height 0.41 → 0.42 m; mean power 2,714.9 → 2,704.8 W; peak power 6,084.2 → 6,291.9 W; mean velocity 1.61 → 1.59 m/s; peak velocity 3.17 → 3.20 m/s. ALL NON-SIGNIFICANT. Two moved the wrong way.**

⚠️ **SAMPLE MISMATCH: 130.3 km/h = 80.96 mph.** Four mph below the floor. Directional only.

> **The re-warm-up literature moves jump. Jump does not respond to pitching. Both links break independently.**
>
> **The warning:** this argument will arrive wearing the strongest credential in sports science — *randomised, controlled, meta-analysed*. **A real intervention on the wrong outcome is not weaker evidence than a cross-sectional study on the right one. It is a different error, and it is harder to see, because the design looks impeccable.**

### 3f. ⚠️ THE POPULATION AUDIT (F-361)

| Study | Design | Mean velocity |
|---|---|---|
| F-323 slide step (PMID 22487194) | within-subject | **76 mph**, age 17.6 |
| F-329 Yang 2016 (PMID 27434082) | randomised counterbalanced, n=7 | **not reported anywhere** |
| F-359 simulated 9 innings (PMC6028199) | repeated measures, n=18 | **80.96 mph** |
| F-360(b) Bishop 2016 (JSCR 30(4)) | crossover, n=8 | **~69.8 mph** |
| F-360(a) palm cooling (PMC12360938) | crossover, n=22 | **~67.0 mph**, throws not pitches |
| F-358 re-warm-up meta-analysis | RCTs, k=3 | **soccer; no throwing outcome** |

**Median pitching sample n = 13. Not one clears 85 mph.**

> **The rule this forces: the causality tag and the population tag point in opposite directions, and the population tag wins.** An INTERVENTION at 67 mph is not better evidence for an 88 mph starter than a CROSS_SECTIONAL study at 92.
> **This is the mirror image of the corpus's founding error.** F-043/F-044/F-045 was a cross-sectional finding sold as a lever. **This is a lever, correctly identified as a lever, measured on somebody else's arm.**

### 3g. Against interest — a source-verified result pointing the wrong way on F-127, and why it does not count (F-364)

PMC6028199 reports velocity falling significantly while **accuracy did not change** — the reverse of F-127's ordering, which underwrites F-348 and half of Dispute #26.

**It does not survive a power check.** Accuracy was the proportion of **9 fastballs per inning** through the zone (1st 53.1% ± 21.1%, 2nd 46.9% ± 24.3%, 3rd 48.1% ± 18.7%). With 9 trials per pitcher per inning and n = 18, the SE of an inning mean is **~5 pp** given the reported between-pitcher SDs. The observed ~7 pp drop is **~1.4 SE.** The design could not have detected a change of the size F-127 predicts.

> **Recorded because the recording matters more than the verdict.** An absence produced by a weak instrument is not evidence of absence — and this program should expect to meet this exact shape again, because *strike rate on a handful of pitches* is how almost everyone measures command in a lab.
> **Dispute #26 is NOT moved.** Today supplied a candidate counter-example and then disqualified it on power. That leaves the dispute where it was, and saying so plainly beats scoring it.

## 5. TRANSLATE — the coach's four questions

**Q1 (to the anatomist): "Is the 120-second clock costing my starter anything?"**

No, not on the recovery channel, and the margin is not close. By 120 seconds he is **93.7–98.4%** of the way through fast-phase phosphagen resynthesis, and 95% arrives at **86–130 seconds** — the rule sits right at the knee. The seconds a longer break would add are worth about **an eighth to a twenty-third** of the seconds he already has (F-357).

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** "The clock between innings is not your problem. By the time you've had a drink and picked up the ball you're back." **The drill is** nothing — this is a thing to stop worrying about. **On video the failure looks like** a pitcher rushing his between-innings routine because he thinks he's short of time, and arriving at his warm-ups unsettled. **Here's how we know it's working:** we don't measure this; we stop spending attention on it. That is the deliverable.

**Q2 (to the biomechanist): "Then how many warm-ups, and when?"**

The whole decision is worth about **0.6 of one pitch's depletion** and **0.3–0.9% of lower-body power**, and the two partly cancel (F-362). There is one weak asymmetry: late is better on both channels once he's past the first minute.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** "Take the break. Warm up at the end of it, not the start. Throw the number that gets your rhythm right and gets your catcher his throw-down — that's what it's for." **The drill is** a fixed between-innings sequence he owns, timed against the clock in a scrimmage until he never looks at it. **On video the failure looks like** him throwing five at 15 seconds in, then standing on the back of the mound for ninety seconds with a jacket on. **Here's how we know it's working:** compliance only — does he start his warm-ups in the last 45 seconds, 8 innings out of 8? **Never outcome.** There is nothing here an outcome check could resolve.

**Q3 (to both): "My offence just batted around for eighteen minutes. What do I do with him?"**

**This is the real question in the topic, and it is the only part that is not small.** The soccer evidence is calibrated to a **15-minute** interval — which is not the 2-minute break; it is exactly this. **1.5 °C and ~3% of lower-body power are in range here** (F-356), even though F-004 forbids converting that to mph.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** "If we're batting long, you don't sit. You get up at the seven-minute mark and you move — the same movement prep you do before the first inning, shortened." **The drill is** a 2–3 minute re-warm-up (the literature finds 3 minutes ≈ 7 minutes), **not extra throwing** — throwing spends `d`, movement doesn't. **On video the failure looks like** him sitting in a jacket through a twenty-minute rally and then taking his eight warm-ups cold. **Here's how we know it's working:** see Q4 — and it is the one recommendation this cycle that has a real check.

**Q4 (to the biomechanist): "How would I actually know?"**

Two ways, and **prefer the second** (F-363).

**Observational, staff-pooled:** first-fastball velocity of each half-inning, split by the duration of the preceding offensive half-inning. At within-pitcher release-speed SD = 1.0 mph, detecting **0.5 mph needs 63 per group.** One starter supplies **11–14** long-inning first pitches a season — five seasons short. **The whole staff supplies 74–99 in one season.** ⚠️ **Confounded:** long half-innings mean you're scoring, which moves score margin, leverage and who's hitting.

**Bullpen A/B, and this is the one to run:** paired within-pitcher, 120 s sit vs 120 s sit + re-warm-up. **0.8 mph in 8–18 pitch-pairs; 0.5 mph in 21–46.** One to two sessions, no confounds.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** "We're going to find out on you, this week, in a pen." **The drill is** 25 fastballs alternating condition in blocks, radar on every one. **On video the failure looks like** nothing — this is a measurement, not a mechanics fix. **Here's how we know it's working:** if the re-warm-up is worth less than 0.5 mph *for him*, it is below what one bullpen can see and below what should change his routine. **One bullpen answers it for one arm. It does not answer it for the field, and nobody has answered it for the field.**

## 6. CROSS-EXAMINE

**① BIOMECHANIST → ANATOMIST.** *"Your two-channel model is two exponentials with invented time constants. τ_T is 'of order 300–1800 seconds' — that is a factor of six, chosen by you, and the whole crossover argument rests on it."*

**ANATOMIST — CONCEDED IN PART, DEFENDED ON THE INVARIANT.** Conceded: τ_T is not measured and the 300–1800 s bracket is a guess about a quantity nobody has measured in a throwing arm. **Defended:** the crossover argument does not need τ_T's value, only that τ_T ≫ τ_m. The ratio falls **11×–59×** across the break at *every* corner of the joint bracket — the weakest corner (τ_m = 43.3, τ_T = 300) still gives **11×**. **To break the argument you need τ_T within a factor of ~3 of τ_m**, i.e. muscle cooling with a time constant under two minutes, which contradicts the 15-minute measurement the model is calibrated to. **Status: the shape survives; the location was never claimed.**

**② COACH → BIOMECHANIST.** *"You corrected F-309 by a factor of ten using a KBO number pushed through f₀, which Dispute #23 says is a model output. You have replaced one assumption with a measurement multiplied by the same assumption."*

**BIOMECHANIST — CONCEDED, AND IT NARROWS THE CLAIM ON THE SPOT.** He is right about the inches.
