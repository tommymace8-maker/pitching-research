Design, every one of them: **the same pitchers threw both deliveries in the same session, compared with paired within-subject tests.** That is a manipulation, not an observation (§2a).

**The MCID the 2024 paper used was 0.5 m/s = 1.118 mph.** The collegiate difference fell under it. The 2026 paper's ≈1% is, at any plausible sample velocity (80 / 85 / 90 mph), **0.80 / 0.85 / 0.90 mph** — also under it. **The two college results agree with each other on magnitude: a real windup advantage, smaller than 1.1 mph.**

**Two professional nulls, two collegiate positives. Level is the apparent moderator, and the positives are in our population.** (F-547)

### 1a — What else the 2026 study found, and why it is one finding and not eight

Eight of 35 parameters were significantly greater from the windup: **maximum shoulder horizontal adduction torque, maximum shoulder anterior force, maximum lead knee height, ball velocity at release, forward trunk tilt at release, maximum upper trunk angular velocity, maximum elbow extension angular velocity, maximum shoulder internal rotation angular velocity.** 74% of comparisons were null.

**BIOMECHANIST:** that list reads like eight confirmations and is nothing of the sort.
- **Lead knee height is the manipulation check.** A windup has more leg lift *by definition*. Reporting it as a finding reports that the pitchers complied.
- **Six of the remaining seven scale with ball velocity.** Upper-trunk angular velocity, elbow extension angular velocity, shoulder IR angular velocity, forward trunk tilt at release, shoulder anterior force and shoulder horizontal adduction torque all co-move with output; this corpus already prices the tightest of those couplings at **R² = 0.957 within an athlete** (F-213 RULE 1). If ball velocity is 1% higher, these are *expected* to be higher. They are the same effect measured six ways.

**So the honest reading: one effect — slightly more output — detected in a tightly correlated bundle, plus a compliance check.** (F-549)

That cuts both ways, and the second direction favours the result. A multiplicity objection ("8 of 35 at α = .05") is weaker than it looks, because these are **not 35 independent tests**; and the significant ones form a **coherent proximal-to-distal chain** (knee lift → trunk → elbow → shoulder → ball) rather than a scatter. An artifact of multiple comparisons does not usually arrange itself along the kinetic chain in order.

### 1b — The stress side, in one line

The windup's extra output is paid for in **shoulder horizontal adduction torque and shoulder anterior force**, and the 2026 authors conclude shoulder injury risk may be *slightly lower* from the stretch. That is RULE 1 behaving exactly as written: no velocity without its stress cost. It does not change the recommendation; it prices it. (F-561) *Injury line closed.*

## 2 — BIOMECHANIST pass

### 2a — ⚠️ F-069 IS MIS-TAGGED, AND THE ERROR UNDERSELLS IT

F-069 carries `CAUSALITY: CROSS_SECTIONAL`. **That is wrong.** Every study in §1 had the same pitcher throw both deliveries in one session and compared them within-subject. The variable was **manipulated**. The correct tag is the one F-323 already uses for the slide step: **MANIPULATED, WITHIN-SUBJECT, ACUTE.**

This matters because the corpus runs a 30:1 cross-sectional-to-intervention ratio and treats intervention evidence as its scarcest asset. **It has been holding one of its rare manipulated comparisons in the cheap drawer.** (F-548)

Note the asymmetry it creates: the professional *nulls* are **MANIPULATED_NULL** — the strongest grade in this file — and the collegiate *positives* are genuine small interventions. Both halves of F-069 just got stronger, in opposite directions.

### 2b — "Below the MCID" is a category error for this decision

The 2024 authors set MCID = 0.5 m/s and concluded the collegiate difference was not clinically meaningful. **MCID is a per-pitch clinical threshold. The decision in front of a college pitcher is not per-pitch.** He is choosing a delivery he will use on roughly half of every pitch he throws for a season.

A difference can be simultaneously (a) below the threshold at which a clinician would call one pitch different and (b) worth more than the entire times-through-the-order question across 90 innings. Those are not in conflict; they are different questions with different denominators. **Quoting "below MCID" as though it settles the coaching decision is importing a clinical convention into a season-aggregate problem.** (F-551)

So: price it.

### 2c — 🧮 PRICING THE WINDUP (derived in-cycle, source-independent)


Named adopters: Strasburg, Darvish, Alex Wood, Brad Peacock, Festa (2025). Stated reasons, uniformly: **simplify the delivery to aid repetition**; "more balanced from the stretch."

**VERDICT: PROMISING AS A PRACTICE, UNPROVEN AS A CLAIM.** The practice may well be right for an individual. The *reason given* has no evidence behind it in any population (§3c, §4 q3).

### 3b — 🚨 THE FIELD'S ONE EMPIRICAL ARGUMENT IS CONFOUNDED, AND THE CORPUS'S OWN NUMBER EATS IT

The quantitative case circulating in the industry is this: **"last year the average starting pitcher threw his average fastball 0.1 mph *harder* with runners on base than with the bases empty — so working from the stretch costs nothing."**

Bases-empty-vs-runners-on is being used as a **proxy** for windup-vs-stretch. It is a badly confounded one, and **this corpus already holds the number that breaks it.**

**F-328** (derived 2026-09-13) used MLB's two-tier pitch clock — 15 s bases empty, 20 s with runners on — as a natural experiment and estimated **0.020 mph per second of rest → +0.10 mph for the 5-second difference.**

```
Observed(runners on − bases empty)  =  Δ_delivery  +  Δ_clock  +  Δ_intent
            +0.10 mph               =  Δ_delivery  +  0.10     +  Δ_intent
        ⇒   Δ_delivery(stretch − windup)  =  − Δ_intent
```

**The entire observed +0.1 mph is accounted for by the clock alone.** F-328 further states the direction of its own confound: pitchers throw with higher intent with runners on, so **Δ_intent > 0**. Therefore the residual delivery term is **≤ 0** — i.e. the game data, correctly decomposed, show the windup at parity *or better*, not the stretch.

And there is a dilution term that pushes the same way. Bases-empty pitches are a **mixture**: some from the windup, some from pitchers who already work stretch-only. If a fraction *w* of bases-empty pitches come from the windup, the observed delivery term is `w × Δ_windup`, so **Δ_windup = Δ_intent / w ≈ 1.7 × Δ_intent** at w ≈ 0.6.

**The conclusion is not "the windup is worth X." It is that the field's headline number does not say what the field says it says, and the quantity it actually bounds — the runners-on intent premium — has never been measured by anyone.** (F-552, and a new gap)

**VERDICT on the field's argument: DEBUNKED AS STATED.** The observation is probably real; the inference from it is not available.

### 3c — 🚨 THERE IS NO WINDUP/STRETCH DATABASE

Repeatedly confirmed across the sweep, and stated outright by the FanGraphs piece: **no public or league data source records which delivery any pitch was thrown from, in any league.** That is why everyone uses the bases-empty proxy, and why §3b's confound is unavoidable rather than lazy.

Consequence: **the sport's biggest current delivery decision is being made on anecdote plus a proxy that cannot be cleaned.** (F-553)

### 3d — Tread Athletics on tempo — a mechanism observation worth keeping

**Source:** [Tread Athletics, *All About Tempo*](https://treadathletics.com/tempo/) (snippet-only). The claim: deGrom holds **19–21 frames from peak leg lift to ball release in both deliveries**, despite a much slower total time to the plate from the windup.

If that generalizes, it localizes the entire windup/stretch difference to the segment **before peak leg lift** — which is to say, before the landmark this corpus says decides everything (F-213 RULE 2: everything is decided by foot contact). The published kinematics agree: the differences cluster at **lead knee height and time from leg lift to foot contact**, and 74% of everything downstream is identical.

**VERDICT: PROMISING, single-athlete, vendor-published, n = 1.** But it is the most useful mechanistic statement found today (F-559).

## 4 — PITCHING-COACH pass: four pointed questions

**Q1 — You just told me the windup is worth up to 2 runs. Last week you told me not to trust cross-sectional findings. Which is this?**

Neither, and that is the point. **The variable was manipulated within-subject, in the same session, by four independent research groups** (§2a). This is one of the small number of entries in this registry where somebody actually moved the thing and measured the result. The weakness is not design — it is **sample size (n = 18 in the one with the cleanest result), unknown sample velocity, and possible sample overlap between the two college studies** (F-556, F-557).

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"The windup is probably worth half a mile an hour to you, and nobody can tell you whether it's worth it for YOU without measuring it — which takes two bullpens."*
> **The drill is:** the counterbalanced delivery test in §6.
> **On video the failure looks like:** nothing. There is no video tell. This one is radar-only.
> **How we know it's working:** the test either clears the detection threshold in §6 or it does not. If it does not, the difference is smaller than you can act on, and you should pick your delivery on other grounds.

**Q2 — Everyone says they go stretch-only to control the running game. Does it help?**

**No. It cannot.** With a runner on base, *every* pitcher is already in the stretch. The windup is used **only when the bases are empty** — the one situation in which there is no running game to control. **The stretch-only switch buys exactly zero against baserunners.**

This is the single most commonly stated reason for the switch and it is **void by inspection.** It is being confused with the *slide step*, which is a different variable (F-321, F-323), applies only with runners on, and is a genuine trade.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Going stretch-only does nothing about the running game, because you're already in the stretch when it matters. If that's your reason, you don't have a reason."*

**Q3 — Then what IS the case for stretch-only?**

One claim: **a simpler delivery repeats better, and repeating better means better command.** The last link in that chain is already **RETIRED in this corpus.**

**F-175** (n = 344 MLB starters, 300,884 four-seams): release-point variability predicts K/9 and HR/9 — deception — and has **essentially no relationship with BB/9** (R² = 0.011). The cue *"repeat your release point and you'll command the ball"* was retired 2026-08-13.

So the chain "simpler → more repeatable → better command" is broken at the step where it would have to pay. (F-554)

That is **not** a refutation of stretch-only. It is a statement about the burden of proof: **the cost is measured and the benefit is not, and the mechanism usually offered for the benefit has already been retired here.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"The windup's price tag is on the table — about half a run to a run across a season. Nobody has ever measured what simplifying buys you. If you want to go stretch-only, fine, but you're paying a known cost for an unknown benefit, and you should be the one to decide that, not a trend."*

**Q4 — Can I measure the command side myself?**

**No, and this is the honest answer.** F-186 puts ~200 tracked pitches behind a 2-inch command gain. A *comparison* of two deliveries needs that on each side — **~400 tracked pitches, ten-plus bullpens, before you learn anything.** The velocity side takes two bullpens. The command side takes a season.

**That asymmetry is itself the practical finding:** you can settle the cost in a week and you cannot settle the benefit at all inside a program. (F-555, F-558)

## 5 — CROSS-EXAMINATION

**BIOMECHANIST → COACH.** *"You priced a result from n = 18 to two decimal places and put it on a ladder next to findings built on 344 MLB starters."* **CONCEDED in form, defended in substance.** The cells are not quotable and the report says so three times. What survives is the **ordering** — that this decision is not negligible and sits in the same tier as questions coaches spend weeks on. The ordering is robust across the entire bracket: even the low cell (0.20 R) is not zero, and even the high cell (2.16 R) is below a mix error.

**BIOMECHANIST → BIOMECHANIST (the order-effect objection).** *"In a session where each pitcher throws windup trials and stretch trials, if windup came first, warm-up state is perfectly confounded with delivery."* **NOT RESOLVABLE — no methods section could be opened.** This is the single most important unread paragraph in the topic and it goes straight to the verification queue. Note the direction: if windup trials ran first, a pitcher is **less** warm, which would bias *against* the windup — so the observed windup advantage would be an underestimate. If stretch ran first, the bias runs the other way. **Unknown sign, and that is why it is a dispute (#53) rather than a caveat.**

**ANATOMIST → BIOMECHANIST.** *"Why would the effect exist in college and vanish in professionals? If it is mechanical, the mechanics do not know what league they are in."* **DEFENDED, with three candidates and no way to choose between them today:** (1) professionals have thrown vastly more stretch reps and have equalized through exposure; (2) restriction of range — the professional sample is selected on having succeeded from both; (3) chance, since four comparisons produced two positives at small effect sizes. **Candidate (1) is the one with a training implication**: it says the windup advantage is a *deficit in stretch proficiency*, not a property of the windup — and that it is trainable away. Nobody has tested it.

**COACH → ANATOMIST.** *"If it's just stretch unfamiliarity, then the answer isn't 'keep the windup,' it's 'throw more stretch reps.'"* **CONCEDED as the most useful hypothesis of the day,** and it is testable in a program: measure the gap, spend a block on stretch volume, re-measure. **If the gap closes, it was familiarity. If it does not, it is mechanical.** That is a cheap, decidable experiment and it is written into §6 as the second phase.

**COACH → BIOMECHANIST, on §3b.** *"You used a corpus-derived number (F-328) that F-328 itself grades WEAK with snippet-only inputs, to overturn a field claim."* **CONCEDED, and the conclusion is narrowed accordingly:** §3b does **not** establish that the windup is faster in games. It establishes that **the field's inference is unavailable** — the observed +0.1 mph is the same size as a confound the corpus has already identified, so it cannot be read as evidence about deliveries in either direction. That narrower claim does not depend on F-328's magnitude being right, only on it being roughly the right order.

## 6 — 🎯 WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK

**THE COUNTERBALANCED DELIVERY TEST.** One athlete, two bullpens, radar only.

**WHAT:** fastballs only, at competitive intent, alternating in **blocks of 5** in an **A-B-B-A** order (windup-stretch-stretch-windup), repeated. Counterbalance which delivery starts, across the two sessions. **The block alternation and the A-B-B-A order are the whole design** — they are what the published studies may not have, and they cost nothing.

**HOW MANY.** Two-condition detection at α = .05, 80% power, `n = 15.7·σ²/Δ²` per condition, with the corpus's within-pitcher fastball SD bracket σ = 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 mph (F-289):

| Difference to detect | σ = 0.8 | σ = 1.0 | σ = 1.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.9 mph** | 13 | 20 | 28 |
| **0.6 mph** | 28 | 44 | 63 |
| 0.3 mph | 112 | 175 | 251 |

*(Formula validated against the corpus's own published cell: 1.0 mph at σ = 1.2 → 23 per condition, `running-game.md` §5.3. ✓)*

**So: ~25 fastballs per delivery — two bullpens — settles whether he has a difference of about 0.9 mph. ~45 per delivery, three or four bullpens, gets you to 0.6. Below 0.3 mph is not measurable in a program and is not worth measuring.** (F-558)

**THE DECISION RULE, individual, same shape as F-321:**
- **Gap under ~0.3 mph (or the test does not clear):** the delivery is not a velocity question for him. Pick on feel, command and whatever he will actually repeat.
- **Gap over ~1 mph:** he has a real windup advantage. Do not let him abandon it for a trend, and especially not for the running-game reason, which is void (§4 q2).
- **In between:** a genuine judgement call, and the table in §2c says which way his innings load pushes it.

**PHASE TWO, if the gap is real** — the anatomist's hypothesis, testable: spend a four-week block weighting stretch reps heavily, then re-run the identical test. **Gap closes → it was stretch unfamiliarity and it is trainable. Gap holds → it is mechanical and he should keep the windup.** Nobody in the literature has run this.

**WHAT NOT TO DO:** do not evaluate this on command. You cannot — ~400 tracked pitches (§4 q4). Do not evaluate it in one bullpen; one bullpen at σ = 1.0 detects about 2 mph, and the effect is half that at most.
