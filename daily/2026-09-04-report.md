## 2. THE ARITHMETIC — the only source-independent work here, and the substance of the day

### 2.1 Model, stated so it can be attacked

One-dimensional flight along the release-to-plate line. Ball mass `m = 0.145 kg`, radius `r = 0.0366 m`, `A = πr² = 4.208e-3 m²`, drag coefficient `Cd = 0.33`, air density `ρ = 1.196 kg/m³` (sea level, 20 °C). Quadratic drag gives `k = ρ·Cd·A / (2m) = 0.005727 m⁻¹`, and closed forms:

```
v(x)  = v₀·exp(−kx)
t(L)  = (exp(kL) − 1) / (k·v₀)
x(t)  = ln(1 + k·v₀·t) / k
```

Gravity and Magnus are omitted from the **timing** term (they act transverse to flight and change path length by well under 1%). Travel distance is `60.5 ft − extension`.

**Everything below is reproducible from `/tmp/flight.py` as run this cycle. It depends on no source I could not open.**

### 2.2 The clock, at 95 mph

| Extension | Travel | Flight time | Plate speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.0 ft | 54.5 ft | **410.4 ms** | 86.4 mph (90.9%) |
| **6.5 ft** (MLB 4-seam mean, F-153) | 54.0 ft | **406.4 ms** | 86.5 mph (91.0%) |
| 7.0 ft | 53.5 ft | **402.5 ms** | 86.5 mph (91.1%) |
| 7.5 ft | 53.0 ft | **398.5 ms** | 86.6 mph (91.2%) |

Sensitivity: across `Cd = 0.30–0.36` and `ρ = 1.06–1.196`, flight time at 95 mph / 54.0 ft spans **402.7–408.2 ms**. The number is robust to ±2 ms.

> **The naive no-drag figure is 387.6 ms. Drag adds ~19 ms — about 5%.** F-250 already flagged this qualitatively ("with in-flight drag the true figure is ~7.5 ms" for the extension delta); this cycle computes the whole clock rather than the delta. My extension delta comes out **7.88 ms per foot** at 95 mph, consistent with F-250's ~7.5 and its conservative 7.2 — the spread is entirely `Cd` assumption. **No correction to F-250 is warranted; it is refined, not wrong.**

**Plate speed is 91.0% of release speed**, essentially independent of velocity and extension over this range.

### 2.3 The commit instant — where the ball is when the decision is already made

Take the swing itself as ~150 ms from initiation to contact (**this input is snippet-only and is the load-bearing assumption of §2.3–2.4** — see Q1 in §5). Then:

| Swing duration assumed | Commit at | Ball has travelled | Distance still to go |
|---|---|---|---|
| 130 ms | 276 ms after release | 37.3 ft | **16.7 ft** |
| **150 ms** | **256 ms after release** | **34.7 ft** | **19.3 ft** |
| 175 ms | 231 ms after release | 31.4 ft | **22.6 ft** |

And on the front end: **in the first 100 ms of flight the ball covers only 13.8 ft.** It is still 40 ft from the plate. Whatever the hitter is doing early, he is doing it on a ball that has barely left the pitcher.

### 2.4 The finding: velocity separation and movement separation are not the same size

At the commit instant (`t = 256 ms`), how far apart are two pitches released identically but at different speeds?

| Pair | FB position | Off-speed position | **Positional gap at commit** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 95 vs 92 | 34.66 ft | 33.59 ft | **12.8 in** |
| 95 vs 88 | 34.66 ft | 32.17 ft | **29.8 in** |
| 95 vs 85 | 34.66 ft | 31.11 ft | **42.6 in** |
| 95 vs 80 | 34.66 ft | 29.32 ft | **64.0 in** |

Now the movement channel. Induced movement is, to first order, a constant transverse acceleration, so separation grows as `t²`. At the commit instant `t/T = 256.4/406.4 = 0.631`, so **`(t/T)² = 39.8%` of the eventual plate-level movement separation has appeared.**

| Total movement separation at the plate | Visible at commit |
|---|---|
| 6 in | 2.4 in |
| 12 in | 4.8 in |
| 18 in | 7.2 in |
| 24 in | 9.6 in |

> **A 10 mph velocity gap puts 42.6 inches of positional discrepancy in front of the hitter at the moment he must commit. An 18-inch movement difference — a big one, a fastball against a sweeper — has produced 7.2 inches by the same instant. That is roughly a factor of six, in favour of velocity.**

That is a mechanism for F-159/F-232 and simultaneously an explanation for F-163. Tunneling is not measuring a fake thing; **it is measuring the weaker of the two channels, at the point in flight where that channel has delivered least.** Movement separation is back-loaded — 60% of it arrives after the decision is gone.

### 2.5 A real internal inconsistency in the tunneling literature, computed

F-163 records both of Baseball Prospectus's tunnel-point definitions: **"23.8 ft from the plate (~175 ms)"**, later **"revised to 150 ms."** Run those through the clock at 95 mph:

| Point | Distance from plate | Time remaining to plate |
|---|---|---|
| BP original | 23.8 ft | **183.9 ms** |
| BP revised | **19.3 ft** | 150 ms |

BP's own "~175 ms" gloss on 23.8 ft is close (my 183.9 ms; the gap is `Cd` and their assumed velocity). **But the revision from 175 ms to 150 ms moved the tunnel point 4.5 feet closer to the plate, and the 23.8 ft figure kept circulating.** Anyone drawing a "24-foot tunnel window" while citing the 150 ms angular-eye-velocity justification is drawing the window in the wrong place — the two numbers that travel together in the industry describe points 4.5 ft apart.

This is not a large error in outcome terms (given §2.4, the whole tunnel channel is the weak one). It is registered because it is exactly the kind of number-drift the corpus exists to catch, and because it is **computable, not citable** — it needed no source at all.

## 5. THE COACH INTERROGATES — four questions, answered

### Q1 — "You built §2.3 and §2.4 on a 150 ms swing. Where did that number come from, and what happens if it's wrong?"

**Biomechanist:** It came from a search summary. I did not open a source for it. It is the single load-bearing unverified input in this report and I am flagging it rather than burying it.

**What happens if it's wrong** — I computed the sensitivity precisely because I could not verify it, and the answer is that **the qualitative conclusion is robust and the exact numbers are not.** Across 130–175 ms of swing duration, the commit instant moves from 276 ms to 231 ms after release, and the ball's remaining distance moves from 16.7 ft to 22.6 ft. The `(t/T)²` movement fraction moves from 46% down to 32% — i.e. **a longer swing makes the movement channel *worse*, not better.** The velocity-versus-movement ratio in §2.4 holds, and widens, across the whole plausible range. **No conclusion in §2.4 flips anywhere in that band.**

**Coach:** Then say the sentence with the band in it, not the point estimate. *"He's decided somewhere around a quarter of a second after the ball leaves my hand, when it's still fifteen to twenty-five feet away from him."* That is true across the whole range and I do not have to defend 150.

### Q2 — "Is 'throw a bigger velocity gap' a LEVER, or have I just watched you invent a new stride length?"

**Biomechanist:** Fair, and this is the right question to ask. Take it in three pieces, because they have different answers.

1. **The geometry in §2.4 is `MECHANISM`.** A 10 mph gap produces 42.6 inches of positional discrepancy at commit. That is arithmetic. It is not an association and it cannot collapse.
2. **The link from that discrepancy to whiffs is `CROSS_SECTIONAL`.** F-159/F-232 record the velocity-gap→whiff chain as the source's argued chain, and it is *argued*, not manipulated. Nobody has assigned pitchers to a bigger gap and measured outcomes.
3. **Crucially — and this is where it differs from stride length — the variable is under conscious, pitch-by-pitch control.** Stride length collapsed as a lever because two independent groups moved it and got nothing or worse (F-043/F-044/F-045); a pitcher cannot decide mid-delivery to stride 8% further. A pitcher **can** decide which pitch to throw, and can change a grip in a bullpen and see the velocity move that afternoon.

**So: the mechanism is established, the outcome link is not, and the manipulability — the thing that killed stride length — is the one part that is unambiguously fine here.** That is a genuinely different evidentiary shape, and I will not let it be written as "science says throw a bigger changeup gap."

**Anatomist:** One constraint and I will get out of the way. **The gap must come from the ball, not from the arm.** A changeup thrown with a decelerated arm is a different motor pattern, it is the classic tell, and it removes the very release-kinematics congruence that makes the gap deceptive in the first place. The pitcher is not trying to throw slower; he is trying to throw a slower ball at the same effort. That is a grip-and-pressure problem, not an intent problem.

### Q3 — "The corpus retired tunneling as folklore (F-163). Does today put it back?"

**Coach:** No, and I want that stated flatly because I am the one who retired it.

**Biomechanist:** Today does something more useful than reviving it — it **explains** it. Bryant's `r = 0.07` against run value was never evidence that the tunnel *window* is imaginary; the window is real geometry and BP's 150 ms figure lands at 19.3 ft, which my clock reproduces. What §2.4 shows is that **the tunnel metrics score the channel that has delivered least by the time the decision is made.** Only ~40% of movement separation exists at commit, and even the whole of it is small next to what a velocity gap does. A metric aimed at the weak channel, measured at the moment that channel is weakest, correlating at 0.07 with outcomes, is exactly what you would predict.

**Coach:** Then F-163's coaching line stands unchanged — *"train release consistency, do not build sequences around tunnels"* — and it now has a reason attached instead of just a null. That is an upgrade to a finding, not a reversal, and I will take it.

### Q4 — "Give me one thing to do this week with an 85+ arm, and tell me how I'd know it worked."

**Coach:** **Measure the fastball–changeup velocity gap and its stability — mean and SD — and treat the SD as the target, not the mean.**

- **What I tell the pitcher:** *"Your changeup's job is the gap, not the movement. He commits with the ball still twenty feet away — by then he has seen almost none of your movement, and he has seen all of your speed."*
- **The drill:** FB/CH pairs off the mound, reading **only** the velocity delta off Trackman/Rapsodo. Do not show him movement plots during this block; they are the distractor. A/B two changeup grips against the same fastball.
- **What the failure looks like on video:** the gap arriving from a **slowed arm** rather than the grip. At 240 fps, compare arm-speed and the foot-contact→release interval on the fastball and the changeup. If the changeup's interval is longer, he bought the gap with the tell, and the deception is gone even though the radar looks right. This is the one thing to check and it is checkable with the camera already in the bag.
- **How we know it worked — with the sample size, because that is the rule:**

| Question | Detectable change | Sample needed |
|---|---|---|
| Did the mean gap move? | 1.0 mph, within-pitcher SD ~1.0 mph | **~16 pitches of each type** (α=.05, 80% power) |
| Did the mean gap move? | 0.5 mph | ~63 of each type |
| Did the gap get more consistent? | SD change | ~40–90 of each type, and treat it as descriptive |
| **Did whiff rate improve?** | 30% → 35% whiff/swing | **~1,374 swings per arm per batter-side** |
| Did whiff rate improve? | 30% → 40% | ~353 swings per side |

> **This is the honest shape of it: the mechanical check is cheap and the outcome check is unaffordable.** Two bullpens settle whether the gap moved. **Nothing you can collect in a college season settles whether it bought whiffs on one arm.** Say so to the pitcher up front, and coach the input on the mechanism — do not promise him the outcome and then go looking for it in 40 innings.

## 6. CROSS-EXAMINATION — each agent challenges the others

**ANATOMIST → BIOMECHANIST.** *The claim:* that a 42.6-inch positional gap at the commit instant "is" the deception. *Why I doubt it:* the visual system does not read absolute position. It reads optical expansion — the rate of growth of the retinal image — and derived time-to-contact quantities. Two balls at 34.7 and 31.1 feet subtend different visual angles, yes, but the perceptual variable is expansion rate, and you have not shown that the 42.6 inches maps monotonically onto anything a hitter's brain computes. *What would settle it:* recompute the same comparison in units of optical expansion rate and time-to-contact error rather than feet, and check whether the ranking survives.

**BIOMECHANIST — CONCEDED, with a boundary.** The objection is correct and I will not defend the positional framing as perceptual. What I will defend is the weaker and sufficient version: **the two channels differ by roughly a factor of six in whatever units you choose, because the velocity channel is linear in `t` and the movement channel is quadratic in `t`, and the decision happens at `t/T ≈ 0.63`.** That ordering is a property of the exponents, not of the units, and it survives translation into expansion rate. **The factor of six is unit-dependent and I am withdrawing it as a precise number. The ordering is not, and I am keeping it.** Registered as F-254 with the framing restricted accordingly, and the unresolved half goes to `open-disputes.md` as Dispute 17.

**COACH → ANATOMIST.** *The claim:* that a changeup thrown with a decelerated arm loses its deception. *Why I doubt it:* every coach in America says this and I have never seen it measured. It is the most confidently repeated unverified sentence in changeup instruction, and its confidence is exactly the profile of the six claims that got corrected on 2026-08-13. *What would settle it:* an occlusion study showing hitters discriminate a decelerated-arm changeup earlier than a grip-produced changeup at matched velocity — which, as far as this cycle can tell, does not exist.

**ANATOMIST — DEFENDED, but downgraded.** The mechanism is sound: arm speed is available in the pre-release kinematics that the anticipation literature says experts read, and a changed foot-contact→release interval is a large, early, high-contrast signal. But the coach is right that **I have no measurement**, and I am relabelling it. **This is now `FOLKLORE` with a plausible mechanism, not an established finding.** It stays in the drill in §5 Q4 as *what to look at*, not as *what is known.* Registered F-256.

**BIOMECHANIST → COACH.** *The claim:* the §5 Q4 recommendation, offered on a day when nothing could be verified. *Why I doubt it:* you are recommending a training focus whose outcome link is `CROSS_SECTIONAL` and resting on F-159/F-232, a finding that itself entered this corpus **through a reversal correction.** *What would settle it:* nothing available this cycle.

**COACH — PARTIALLY CONCEDED.** Correct on the provenance and I have narrowed the recommendation because of it. **What I am recommending is a measurement, not an outcome.** Measure the gap and its SD; find the arm-speed tell on video. Every part of that is checkable inside two bullpens. **What I am explicitly NOT saying is "a bigger gap will get you more whiffs"** — §5 Q4 states outright that no sample a college program can collect will establish that on one arm. **The recommendation is priced at what today's evidence can pay for, and no higher.**
