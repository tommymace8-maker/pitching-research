## 2. BIOMECHANIST — the power arithmetic settles more than the studies do

α = .05 two-tailed, derived in-cycle, source-independent.

| Study | n per arm | **d detectable @ 80%** | d @ 50% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lambert 2023 | 15 / 13 | **1.062** | 0.743 |
| Brumitt 2020 | 23 / 23 | **0.826** | 0.578 |

**Lambert's published effect sizes are ES = 1.0 to 1.4. Its floor is d ≈ 1.06.**

> ⭐⭐ **A study reporting effects at its own detection floor is reporting the only effects it could have declared significant — the largest ones. Assume the true effect is smaller than published (F-616).**

This is F-439's logic — the corpus's standing n ≥ 97 null rule — **applied for the first time to a POSITIVE result.** It immediately opened a dispute (#63) and generated a new structural audit (§7).

### 2b. And the apparent contradiction dissolves (F-618)

The field reads Lambert (positive) against Brumitt (null) as a disagreement. **It is not one.** A single true effect of **d ≈ 0.5** predicts both results exactly: Brumitt reports NS at ~36% power; Lambert declares significance only on the inflated tail.

| true d | per arm | **total to settle it** |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 175 | **350** |
| 0.40 | 99 | **198** |
| 0.50 | 63 | **126** |

**The existing literature has 74 randomised participants.**

⚠️ **And a reporting hazard: the published synopsis of Brumitt is titled "Rotator cuff strength is NOT augmented by blood flow restriction training" (*Phys Ther Sport*). That is a 46-person detection floor presented as an absence, in a title** — the exact error F-439 exists to catch.

> **Do not say "the evidence is mixed." Say "the evidence is 74 people and cannot see an ordinary effect."**

### 2c. The headline effect size is the wrong kind of effect size (F-617)

Reported: BFR **+227 ± 60 g** vs control **+75 ± 37 g** shoulder lean mass, P = .018, "ES = 1.0".

- **The ± convention was unstated in every snippet and is resolved by self-consistency:** read as SDs the same numbers give t = 8.18, **p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁸** — irreconcilable with the paper's own P = .018. Read as **SEs**: t = 2.16, df = 26, p = 0.040. **They are standard errors.** Implied SDs 232 g and 133 g.
- **"ES = 1.0" is 227/232 = 0.98 — a WITHIN-GROUP effect size.** In an RCT that is not the treatment effect; the control group changed too.
- **The between-group treatment effect is 152 g, pooled d = 0.79.**

**Scale check, and it is the point: 152 grams of regional lean tissue in one shoulder over eight weeks — about a third of a pound.** F-504 says this corpus holds **no within-athlete mass→velocity slope of any kind**, so there is no licensed conversion to mph, and the order of magnitude says not to go looking for one.

⚠️ **Unresolved and needs the paper: regional DXA lean-soft-tissue least-significant-change is plausibly of the same order as 152 g. Whether this effect clears its own instrument's precision is unknown.** That is Check #4 in its proper form — not "is the R² an identity" this time, but "is the number inside measurement error."

## 3. ANATOMIST — the muscle that adapted was never occluded (F-620)

**This is the strongest objection in the topic and it is pure anatomy.**

A BFR cuff goes on the **proximal upper arm**. The rotator cuff — supraspinatus, infraspinatus, subscapularis, teres minor — **originates on the scapula**, entirely upstream of the tourniquet. **All four muscles are above the cuff line and are never occluded.**

So the standard mechanism — local hypoxia and metabolite accumulation driving high-threshold motor-unit recruitment *in the restricted muscle* — **cannot be the mechanism for cuff adaptation.**

The literature names the problem rather than solving it. Hedt et al. (2022, *Arthroscopy Sports Med Rehab*) call it **"the paradox of proximal performance,"** rate their own evidence **LEVEL V, EXPERT OPINION**, and conclude that further research is needed to characterise proximal responses. The candidates offered — metabolic stress sensing, metabolite spillover, mechanotransduction via cell swelling, systemic hormonal response — are hypotheses, none demonstrated for a non-occluded muscle.

> ⭐ **THE FREE DECISIVE TEST NOBODY HAS RUN: measure the NON-THROWING shoulder.** A systemic mechanism must act on the untrained side; a local one cannot. **Lambert used DXA, which images both shoulders by default — the discriminating data may already sit inside that trial's own scans.** No new subjects, no new instrument.

⚠️ **Note which way the sample mismatch runs: untrained people gain from almost anything, so these are UPPER BOUNDS for an 85+ arm.**

## 5. ⭐⭐ The cycle's best output — a number the corpus already owned

### 5a. F-072's dispersion, never extracted (F-622)

F-072 has been quoted for two months for **+0.65 mph** in 88+ arms — "the number to say to an 88 mph athlete on day one." **Its dispersion was never taken out. The published tails determine it.**

- n = 58, entry ≥ 88 mph, mean **+0.65 mph**, **41.95% gained ≥ 1 mph**, **18.39% lost > 1 mph**
- P(X < −1) = 0.1839 → z = −0.90 → **SD = (−1 − 0.65)/(−0.90) = 1.83 mph**
- **Independent back-check on the opposite tail:** z = (1 − 0.65)/1.83 = 0.191 → **42.4% predicted vs 41.95% observed**

**A two-point fit that closes to half a percentage point, with the parameter fitted on the opposite tail.** The change distribution is near-normal and **SD ≈ 1.8 mph**.

The dispersion is robust to F-072's design weakness in a way the mean is not: a missing control group biases the *mean*, but the *spread* is a property of the measured distribution.

> **Say this to an 88 mph arm alongside the +0.65: the spread is about ±1.8 mph — nearly three times the average gain.** One athlete's +3 proves nothing about a program; one athlete's −2 proves nothing against it. **This is what makes F-072 usable rather than merely quotable.**

### 5b. What that SD costs every velocity trial (F-623)

| Effect to detect | per arm | **total pitchers** |
|---|---|---|
| **0.50 mph** | 211 | **422** |
| **0.65 mph** (F-072's whole mean) | 125 | **250** |

**A D1 staff is 12–18 arms. An SEC season fields on the order of 200 pitchers across fourteen programs.** Detecting half a mile per hour needs **more pitchers than the conference has**; detecting a full mph needs **six to nine programs acting as one trial**.

> ⭐⭐ **THE DECISION RULE, AND IT GENERALISES FAR PAST BFR: a college program cannot validate ANY velocity intervention on its own athletes. "Did it work for us?" is not an answerable question and should not be asked.**
>
> **What a program CAN ask is whether an intervention is cheap, safe and mechanistically sound — and accept the velocity effect as unmeasurable.**

**This is the third independent arrival at the same wall** — F-506 (nutrition), F-476 (imagery), now F-623. **It is beginning to look like a property of the sport rather than a feature of three topics.**

⚠️ **Corollary: any facility claiming to have MEASURED a sub-mph velocity benefit on a roster-sized sample is reporting noise. The arithmetic forbids the claim regardless of the intervention.**

## 8. WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK WITH AN 85+ ARM

**The honest headline: nothing in this topic should change what a healthy 85+ pitcher does in an offseason that can already lift heavy.** That is the finding, and it is worth having, because the marketing says otherwise.

**Where BFR has a defensible use:**

1. **In-season load sparing.** The one channel BFR genuinely matches heavy lifting on is hypertrophy (§4), at 20–30% of 1RM. Mid-season, when heavy lower-body work competes with outings, that trade is real. **PROMISING, population-mismatched.**
2. **A compromised week** — tweaked elbow, travel, post-op. BFR's rehab home, where the evidence is strongest.
3. **🚨 NOT INSIDE 48 h OF AN OUTING.** A 2023 *Sports Med* review warns that inappropriately prescribed BFR "could significantly impair neuromuscular performance by generating considerable fatigue." **Fatiguing stimulus, unmeasured acute cost, no demonstrated acute benefit.** This is the one unambiguous practical line the cycle produces.

**THE MEASURABLE CHECK — stated honestly, including what it cannot do:**

> **Isometric cuff strength (IR and ER at 0° and 90°) by handheld dynamometer, BOTH shoulders, every 4 weeks.** Handheld dynamometry carries roughly **5 kg of between-day noise** per athlete, so **3 pre and 3 post measurements per athlete** are needed to see a 2 kg change — a single reading is worthless.
>
> **And track the NON-THROWING shoulder, because that is §3's free mechanism test and nobody has published it.** If the untrained side moves, the mechanism is systemic; if it does not, the proximal claim needs an explanation anatomy does not supply.

**On video the failure looks like: nothing.** There is no mechanical signature of BFR — **which is part of why it is hard to evaluate and easy to sell.**

**What you cannot check:** velocity. 106 pitchers for one mph, 422 for half (§5b).
