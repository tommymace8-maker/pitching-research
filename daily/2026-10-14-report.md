## 2. THE RESEARCH

### 2.1 ⭐⭐ THE RESULT OF THE DAY — THE ONLY ADEQUATELY POWERED RANDOMISED THROWING-VELOCITY FEEDBACK TRIAL EXISTS, IT IS POSITIVE, AND IT IS ABOUT THE RADAR GUN (F-696)

**Štirn I, Carruthers J, Šibila M, Pori P (2017).** *Frequent Immediate Knowledge of Results Enhances the Increase of Throwing Velocity in Overarm Handball Performance.* **J Hum Kinet 56:197–205**, DOI 10.1515/hukin-2017-0037, PMID 28469758, PMC5384067.

- **n = 123** (50 female, 73 male physical-education students), **randomly assigned** to experimental or control.
- **Protocol:** 2 × 10 maximal-effort handball set shots, **2×/week for 6 weeks**.
- **The only difference between arms:** the experimental group **saw the radar-gun velocity immediately after every single shot.** The control group received **no feedback**. Same volume, same intensity instruction, same duration.
- **Result, normal ball (size 2):** relative velocity increase **+8.1 ± 3.6 %** (feedback) vs **+2.7 ± 2.9 %** (control).
- **Result, heavy ball:** **+5.1 ± 4.2 %** vs **+2.5 ± 5.8 %**, also reported as significant between groups.
- **Sex:** apart from the experimental group's normal-ball throws, no sex differences in velocity change.

**Why this matters more than anything else found today.** Three reasons.

1. **It is a LEVER, not a marker.** Somebody manipulated the variable — the presence of the number — held everything else constant, randomised, and measured ball velocity. The corpus runs ~30:1 cross-sectional to intervention. This is on the right side of that ratio.
2. **It clears this corpus's own detection floor.** The null audit (F-519→F-532) established **n ≥ 97** as the threshold below which a study cannot detect anything short of a large effect. **n = 123 clears it.** At ~61/62 per arm, α = .05, 80% power, the design detects **d ≈ 0.51**. My own computation of the observed effect from the reported means and SDs: pooled SD = √((3.6² + 2.9²)/2) = **3.27**, so **d = (8.1 − 2.7)/3.27 ≈ 1.65**. A large effect in a study powered for a moderate one. **This is one of a handful of objects in the entire registry that is both a manipulation and adequately powered.**
3. **It is free to install.** Unlike every other lever in the corpus, this one requires no new equipment, no training block, and no load.

**POPULATION — AND THIS IS THE BINDING LIMITATION. SAMPLE MISMATCH, DIRECTIONAL ONLY.** Physical-education students throwing a handball set shot. **The sample's actual throwing velocity is NOT RETRIEVABLE from any snippet** — I could not open the paper and the reported outcome is a *percentage*, not an absolute. The corpus's own handball range is **45.9–51.3 mph** (ergogenic-aids topic, F-389 region). An 85-mph floor population is nowhere near this sample, and these are **untrained throwers with enormous gain room** — the control arm alone gained 2.7% from nothing but 240 max-effort throws. **The 8.1% does not transfer as a number.** What transfers is the existence of a causal arrow.

### 2.2 ⭐⭐ AND IT HAS NO RETENTION TEST — THE AUTHORS SAY SO THEMSELVES (F-697)

From the paper's own limitations, via snippet: *"the participants were not available for a retention test to evaluate the long-term effects of the intervention."*

**This is the single most important sentence of the cycle.** It means the +8.1% was measured with the feedback contingency still in force, or at best immediately at the end of a six-week block with no washout. Therefore **the study cannot distinguish between two completely different mechanisms with opposite coaching implications:**

- **(A) LEARNING.** Six weeks of per-throw KR built a better, faster movement. The gain is the athlete's to keep. → *Install the gun permanently, and the gain persists when you take it away.*
- **(B) ACUTE OUTPUT UNDER MEASUREMENT.** The number raised effort/intent on every throw, for six weeks, including the post-test. The control group was told "maximal effort" and, being unscored, didn't give it. → *The gun is not a training tool, it is an intent tool, and the gain is rented, not owned.*

The guidance-hypothesis literature the corpus already holds (§10.5, FOLKLORE verdict on faded/bandwidth KR as learning accelerators) was built almost entirely on **accuracy and timing** tasks, where dependency is the predicted failure. **Nobody has tested the guidance hypothesis on a maximal-effort power task** — searched today, four angles, not found (F-702 gap 4). So the corpus cannot even borrow a prior.

### 2.3 ⭐⭐ THE MECHANISM FOR (B) IS MEASURED, MANIPULATED, AND LARGE (F-698)

**Stien N et al. (2025).** *The Impact of Real-Time Visual Feedback on Maximal Force Output and Reliability During Isometric Mid-Thigh Pull Testing in Resistance-Trained Men.* **Eur J Sport Sci**, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.70000, PMID 40590195, PMC12210026.

- **n = 20 resistance-trained men**, **within-subject crossover** — each man tested **with and without a live force-curve display**, across single, repeated, and 30-s all-out IMTP variations.
- **"Visual feedback significantly enhanced most measures of peak and mean force outputs in all test variations," ES 0.49–1.13, p < 0.001–0.006.**
- **Repeated-trial peak force: +7.9 % to +12.9 %** on repetitions **2, 3 and 4** (ES 0.68–0.99, p = .002–.036). **NOT** significant on reps 1, 5, 6.
- **Reliability:** single-trial peak-force CV **3.80 % → 3.38 %** with feedback, ICC steady at 0.978. Repeated-trial CV ~4.5 % both ways (ICC 0.987 → 0.983). The 30-s all-out test showed **no** reliability gain — the authors attribute it to pacing against the display.
- **Counter-case, same literature:** a 2026 technical note found real-time visual force feedback **did NOT change maximal grip force** but **did** change rate of force development. **Task- and muscle-dependent.**

**Read these two studies together and the cycle's central claim falls out.** A single acute exposure to a live readout buys **7.9–12.9 % more peak force** in trained men. Štirn's six-week block bought **8.1 %** more throwing velocity with per-throw readout and no washout. **The magnitudes are the same order, and the acute explanation requires no learning at all.** That is not proof that Štirn measured nothing but intent — but it is the most parsimonious reading available, and **the study's own missing retention test is why nobody can say otherwise.**

**Caveat on power, applied symmetrically (Dispute #63's rule).** n = 20 paired. For a paired d = 0.5 at 80% power you need ~34. **So the bottom of Stien's ES range (0.49) sits BELOW its own detection floor and the significance attached to it should be discounted; the top (1.13) is comfortably above it.** The 7.9–12.9% peak-force band on reps 2–4 (ES 0.68–0.99) is in the credible region. The pattern of reps 1, 5, 6 failing while 2, 3, 4 hit is **not** a clean dose-response and is consistent with multiplicity across six repetitions and three test variants.

### 2.4 THE GENERAL AUGMENTED-FEEDBACK LITERATURE, AND WHY ITS HEADLINE NUMBER DOES NOT SAY WHAT IT IS QUOTED AS SAYING (F-700)

**Jiménez-Díaz J, Chaves-Castro K, Morera-Castro M (2020).** *Effectiveness of Augmented Feedback on Motor Skills Performance: A Meta-Analysis.* **MHSalud 17(1)**.

- **20 studies, 95 effect sizes, 949 participants.**
- **Acquisition (practising WITH feedback): ES = 0.87 [0.65, 1.10]**, **I² = 74.41 %**.
- **"Acquisition-to-retention after feedback was removed: ES = −0.74."** CI **not retrievable** from any snippet.

**CHECK 3 — DOES THE NUMBER MEASURE WHAT THE SENTENCE SAYS?** This is where the topic's most-quoted figure fails. The −0.74 is described in every snippet as a **within-group drop from the acquisition phase to the retention phase**. A within-group drop from end-of-practice to a delayed retention test happens in **essentially every motor-learning dataset ever collected**, feedback or not — it is warm-up decrement plus forgetting. **Without a no-feedback control arm measured at the same retention interval, −0.74 is not evidence of feedback dependency. It is evidence that people get worse when they stop practising.** I could not open the paper to establish which contrast it actually is, so the honest registration is: **the headline retention penalty in the augmented-feedback literature is of UNKNOWN design and must not be quoted as a dependency effect.**

**And I² = 74.41 % with no retrievable publication-bias assessment.** In this exact literature, bias correction took **external focus from g = 0.26 to 0.01 (F-189)** and **self-controlled practice from g = 0.44 to 0.107 (F-190)**. An ES of 0.87 assembled from 20 heterogeneous studies with no funnel analysis is the precise object this registry has twice watched collapse.

### 2.5 THE ONLY SPORT-SPECIFIC META-ANALYSIS IS NON-SIGNIFICANT AND HAS NO ISOLATED FEEDBACK ARM (F-701)

**Tissera K, Orth D, Huynh M, Benson AC (2022).** *The impact of augmented feedback (and technology) on learning and teaching cricket skill: a systematic review with meta-analysis.* **PLoS ONE 17(12):e0279121**, PMID 36525446, PMC9757599.

- 8,262 records screened → **11 studies included → only 5 in the meta-analyses.**
- **Overall performance success: Hedges' g = 0.55, 95% CI [−0.39, 1.50] — NOT SIGNIFICANT.**
- **Anticipation accuracy: g = 1.21, 95% CI [−0.37, 2.78] — NOT SIGNIFICANT.**
- Authors' own caution: interpret with care given the wide intervals.
- **One included study had n = 1** (a 21-year-old semi-professional bowler).
- 🚨 **The authors state that NO STUDIES WITH AN ISOLATED FEEDBACK INTERVENTION ARM WERE IDENTIFIED.** Every pooled intervention bundled augmented feedback **with other components.**

**So the closest thing to a sport-specific evidence base for the biomechanics report is: five studies, one of them a case report, both pooled effects straddling zero, and not one of them capable of attributing its result to feedback at all.** By Dispute #63's rule — a result at or below its own detection floor is evidence of nothing — this is **evidence of nothing**, in both directions. It does not support the practice and it does not refute it.

### 2.6 🚨 THE GAP — NOBODY HAS EVER GIVEN A PITCHER HIS OWN KINEMATICS AND MEASURED WHAT HAPPENED (F-702)

Four-angle sweep, all four empty:

1. **Kinematic/biomechanical feedback × baseball pitching × any outcome.** Zero. Searches returned only cross-sectional and retrospective work: Zappa & Lerch 2026 (markerless, NCAA D1 games, *descriptive*), a Super Learner model on **322 NCAA D1 pitchers, 2022–2024** (cross-sectional), Lerch 2025 markerless-vs-lab variability (measurement).
2. **Any throwing sport.** Javelin returns observational technique description plus one single-group pretest–posttest with **no control and no retention** (~6–8% gain, uninterpretable). Handball's one randomised feedback trial is **KR about the ball** (§2.1), not KP about the body.
3. **Does the softball/baseball literature know it's missing?** Yes — the lower-body softball velocity paper states in its own discussion that *"longitudinal or intervention studies are warranted to understand how changes in lower extremity kinematics and kinetics can influence changes in pitch velocity."* The field names the gap.
4. **Guidance hypothesis on a maximal-effort power task.** Does not exist. The literature is accuracy, timing and force-maintenance tasks. One force study explicitly says the effect on maximal output *"remains unclear."*

**Every mechanical cue in this corpus that a coach delivers off a biomechanics screen is delivered on the strength of a cross-sectional association plus an untested assumption that telling the athlete the number changes the number.** That assumption has never been tested in a pitcher.

### 2.7 THE MEASUREMENT CEILING ON KP — THE CORPUS ALREADY HELD THE NUMBER, NOT THE INFERENCE (F-703)

**Already registered (F-~250 region, `biomechanics.md` §7.2): Hawk-Eye MPJPE 56.6 ± 9.4 mm; Theia3D 52.0 ± 12.3 mm; n = 18 collegiate pitchers. Stride length agreed best across systems (CCC > 0.85); SHOULDER ROTATIONAL VARIABLES showed the greatest variability.** Lerch 2025 (30 lab + 30 NCAA D1 in-game) found pitch-to-pitch **variability** similar across systems while **mean values differed substantially**.

**New today is only the inference, and it is a hard constraint on §2.6's missing trial.** ~5 cm of per-joint position error, concentrated **worst in exactly the rotational shoulder variables coaches read off the report**, means a KP intervention targeting those variables is **feeding back a quantity the instrument cannot resolve at the scale of the intended change.** And Lerch's pattern is the awkward one for feedback specifically: a system that gets **variability** right but **means** wrong is fine for "is he repeating?" and wrong for "what is his number?" — and the report hands the coach the number.

**This also sets the order of operations for anyone building the missing trial: establish the within-athlete minimal detectable change for the target variable on your own system first, or the trial cannot interpret its own result.** The corpus holds no such figure for any pitching kinematic variable — the **eighteenth** entry in the F-264 / F-289 / F-295 / F-307 / F-320 / F-336 / F-348 / F-363 / F-396 / F-457 / F-507 / F-600 / F-659 / F-689 missing-within-athlete-variance pattern.

### 2.8 ⭐ THE STRUCTURAL ITEM — EVERY TEST IN THIS REGISTRY THAT SHOWED A LIVE READOUT MEASURED A DIFFERENT QUANTITY (F-704)

If Stien 2025 is right that a live display adds **7.9–12.9 %** to peak force, then **feedback condition is an uncontrolled moderator sitting underneath an unknown fraction of this corpus's registered magnitudes.** A velocity test where the athlete saw the gun and a velocity test where he didn't are **not the same measurement**, and almost no study reports which it ran.

This is a **sixth candidate structural sweep**, and it is mechanisable: *which registered findings rest on a maximal-output test whose feedback condition is unreported?* The candidates are everywhere — every pull-down and mound velocity figure, every CMJ and IMTP number imported as a proximal correlate, every bar-velocity figure in the VBT topic (F-590→F-600), every jump figure in `jump-transfer-audit.md`. **Prediction, registered in advance: the feedback condition will be unreported in the large majority, and where it differs between a study and the setting a coach applies it in, the imported magnitude is biased upward by roughly the Stien band.**

## 5. STEP 3 — TRANSLATION: THE COACH INTERROGATES THE OTHER TWO

### Q1 (COACH → BIOMECHANIST): "Štirn is handball students. Why should I care at all?"

**BIOMECHANIST.** You shouldn't care about the 8.1%. You should care that it is **the only randomised, adequately powered, isolated manipulation of a feedback variable with a throwing-velocity outcome in existence.** Everything else in this topic is either cross-sectional, bundled, underpowered, or in a different task. **What crosses over is the arrow, not the magnitude.** And the direction of the population mismatch is knowable: untrained throwers with huge gain room, so **8.1 % is a ceiling for an 85+ arm, not an estimate.** Restriction of range (Luera 2020, r = .17–.29 in all-hard-throwing samples) says whatever is left at 90 mph is smaller.

> **SO WHAT I TELL THE PITCHER IS:** nothing about this study. I tell him we're going to find out on him.
> **THE DRILL IS:** the gun-on/gun-off block test in Q4.
> **ON VIDEO THE FAILURE LOOKS LIKE:** nothing — this is not a mechanical intervention and there is nothing to see. That is a feature. It is also why it's cheap.
> **HOW WE KNOW IT'S WORKING:** §5/Q4's arithmetic, on your own TrackMan, on one arm.

### Q2 (COACH → ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY): "Is there a tissue-level story for why a number on a screen makes a man throw harder, or is this just psychology?"

**ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY.** There is a real mechanism and it is not mysterious: **voluntary maximal output is not maximal.** Central drive, motor-unit recruitment completeness and rate coding under a true maximal voluntary effort are all submaximal relative to what the tissue can produce, and the gap is modulated by arousal, attentional focus and knowledge of the score. **That is the entire basis of Stien's 7.9–12.9 %.** A live readout converts "throw hard" into a scored task, and a scored task recruits differently.

Two consequences the coach should hold onto, and one warning:

- **It is immediate.** There is no adaptation timeline. No tissue remodelling is required and none occurs. It is available on the first pitch and gone when the number is.
- **Which is exactly why it is NOT free.** If the mechanism is raised central drive and fuller recruitment on every throw, then **installing a permanent per-pitch readout raises the mechanical load of every training throw**, including the ones that were supposed to be submaximal. The corpus's own strongest injury-adjacent constraint is that **the highest-risk single exposure in this population is a maximal-intent outing** (F-~146 region, non-fastball workload). **A gun pointed at a recovery-day pen converts it into a testing day.** One line, as the brief requires, and then move on — but it is the one real cost.
- **Stien's reps 1, 5, 6 failing while 2, 3, 4 hit** has a plausible physiological reading beyond multiplicity: rep 1 is pre-potentiation, reps 5–6 are fatiguing, and the feedback effect lives in the window where there is both headroom and freshness. **Untested. MECHANISM only.**

### Q3 (COACH → BIOMECHANIST): "We paid for the markerless lab. Should I stop showing guys their reports?"

**BIOMECHANIST.** Separate two uses and the answer stops being uncomfortable.

- **The report as a SCREEN — selecting what to work on, and tracking whether a change took.** Defensible, and the corpus supports it: stride length has **CCC > 0.85** across systems, trunk tilt is reliable, and these are the variables you can act on.
- **The report as FEEDBACK — handing the athlete a number to chase.** This has **zero** supporting evidence in a pitcher (F-702) **and** a measurement problem that bites hardest exactly where coaches look: **~5 cm MPJPE, worst in the shoulder rotational variables** (F-703). And Lerch's split — variability right, means wrong — means the number on the page is the least trustworthy thing on it.

**So: keep the lab, change what you say off it.** Feed back **the ball**, which you measure well, and use the body data to decide **what to train**, not **what to tell him mid-pen.**

### Q4 (COACH → BOTH): "Fine. What do I actually do on Thursday, and how many pitches before I believe it?"

**All three agree on this one, which is rare enough to flag.**

> **SO WHAT I TELL THE PITCHER IS:** *"For the next five pens, some blocks you'll see the number after every fastball and some blocks you won't. Throw the same both ways."*
>
> **THE DRILL IS:** the **gun-on / gun-off block test**, on one arm, counterbalanced **ABBA within each session** so warm-up ramp and within-outing fade (F-384's −0.2 mph/inning, borrowed from an 81-mph sample and still unmeasured at 85+) fall equally on both conditions. Readout visible immediately after each pitch in A blocks; screen turned away and nobody calls out numbers in B blocks. **Stadium TrackMan or the program's primary unit — not a consumer gun** (§4).
>
> **ON VIDEO THE FAILURE LOOKS LIKE:** the B blocks drifting slower across the session rather than randomly — that's fatigue leaking into one condition, and it means your counterbalancing broke.
>
> **HOW WE KNOW IT'S WORKING — with the sample size, from this corpus's own arithmetic (F-289):** at the corpus working within-outing fastball SD of **1.0 mph**, to detect a mean difference of **1.0 mph** you need **≈16 fastballs per condition**; for **0.5 mph**, **≈63 per condition.** So: **~32 fastballs total for a 1-mph effect — one or two pens. ~126 total for a 0.5-mph effect — four or five pens.** If Stien's band transfers at all, 1% of a 90-mph arm is 0.9 mph, so **target the 1.0-mph contrast first: it is one bullpen's worth of work and it is inside your reach today.**
>
> **AND THE PART THAT MAKES IT HONEST:** that **1.0 mph SD is a CORPUS ASSUMPTION, not a measured value** — it has never been published for an 85+ arm and it is already in every detection table this registry has built (F-289, F-659, F-684, F-689). **It is sitting in your own TrackMan log right now. Pull it before you run the test, or the test cannot tell you what it found.** That one query remains the vault's top applied item for a second day.

**Three limits on this recommendation, stated rather than hedged.**
1. **It is unblindable.** The athlete always knows which condition he's in. Nothing fixes that; it is the nature of the manipulation, and it is why the result will measure "feedback + knowing you're being studied" forever.
2. **It measures the ACUTE effect only** — Štirn's unanswered question (does it persist?) stays unanswered by this design. To touch that you'd need a washout block, and at 63 fastballs a bucket that is a month of pens.
3. **A single arm's result is a single arm's result.** Do not generalise it to the staff; the detection wall (F-506 / F-476 / F-623 / F-681, four independent arrivals) says a 12-man roster cannot validate a velocity intervention. **This design evades the wall the way F-689 does — by changing the endpoint to a within-athlete contrast — and that is the only reason it is affordable.**
