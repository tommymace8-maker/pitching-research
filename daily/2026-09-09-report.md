## §3 — THE RESEARCH

### §3a — ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY opens: there is no tissue in this topic, and that is itself the finding

*The anatomist, stating his scope honestly rather than inventing a role:*

**Sequencing has no anatomical content. There is no structure that executes a transition matrix, no adaptation timeline for "pitch selection," and no trainable capacity that makes a pitcher better at choosing.** Every other topic this corpus has opened has a tissue story underneath it — force production, elastic energy, collagen turnover, motor-unit recruitment. **This one has none, and the recommendation section must not pretend otherwise.**

**Two contributions the anatomist will make, both constraints rather than mechanisms:**

1. **A sequencing change is FREE in load terms, and that is unique.** Every other lever in this corpus — velocity (F-094: within-athlete velocity and varus torque locked at $R^2 = 0.957$), the lead-leg block (F-062, raises torque), weighted implements (F-024–F-032), arm slot (F-070) — is bought with tissue cost. **Changing which pitch follows which costs nothing.** This matters for prioritisation: a 1-run-per-season sequencing gain with zero stress cost may outrank a 1-run velocity gain that carries load.
   ⚠️ **One caveat, and it is not nothing: a mix shift toward breaking balls is not load-neutral.** The corpus records non-fastball workload accounting as entirely uncovered (INDEX §5). **A sequencing recommendation that incidentally moves the arsenal 15 points toward sliders has left the free regime and nobody knows the price.**
2. **The execution constraint is real and is upstream of the strategy.** F-127 documents the within-outing degradation order — command drift first, then breaking-ball finish, then slot, then velocity. **A sequencing plan that requires a precisely-located third pitch in the sixth inning is a plan that expires before the inning it was written for.** The strategy layer must be written to degrade gracefully, and nothing in the sequencing literature acknowledges that the executor changes during the outing.

### §3b — BIOMECHANIST: sequencing is a MATRIX where mix is a VECTOR, and the season did not get bigger

The decision object:

- **Mix**: choose $p_i$, $\sum p_i = 1$ → $k-1$ free parameters.
- **First-order sequencing**: choose $P(j \mid i)$, one row per previous pitch → $k(k-1)$ free parameters.
- **Sequencing × count**: $12k(k-1)$.
- **Second-order** (previous two pitches): $k^2(k-1)$.

| arsenal $k$ | mix | 1st-order seq | seq × 12 counts | 2nd-order seq |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 2 | 24 | 4 |
| 3 | 2 | 6 | 72 | 18 |
| **4** | **3** | **12** | **144** | **48** |
| 5 | 4 | 20 | 240 | 100 |

**A four-pitch college starter's sequencing decision has 12 free parameters against the mix decision's 3 — and 144 if you condition on count.** The season is still 1,600 pitches.

**Pitches per cell, 1,600-pitch NCAA starter season:**

| conditioning | $k=3$ cells / n | $k=4$ cells / n |
|---|---|---|
| pitch type only (mix) | 3 / **533** | 4 / **400** |
| previous pitch | 9 / **178** | 16 / **100** |
| previous pitch × batter hand | 18 / **89** | 32 / **50** |
| previous pitch × count (12) | 108 / **15** | 192 / **8** |

**This is F-286's structure — the detection wall — arriving through a completely different door.** There the sample was fixed and the effect was tiny. Here the effect may be large and **the sample is being shredded by the conditioning itself.** Registered **F-294**.

### §3c — The result the biomechanist did not expect: THE OPPONENT CAN MEASURE YOUR SEQUENCING AND YOU CANNOT

Two different questions, two different statistics.

**Question A — "does this pitcher have a tendency?"** A *frequency*. One-sample proportion test, $\alpha = .05$ two-sided, 80% power, on $n$ observations of the conditioning event:

| baseline $p_0$ | +5pp | +10pp | +15pp | **+20pp** | +25pp | +30pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.35 | 726 | 183 | 82 | **46** | 29 | 20 |
| 0.40 | 761 | 191 | 85 | **47** | 30 | 20 |
| 0.50 | 783 | 194 | 85 | **47** | 29 | 19 |

**A 20-point tendency is established in about 47 observations of the conditioning event.** A college season supplies 178 per previous-pitch cell ($k=3$) or 100 ($k=4$). **The opponent has two to four times what they need.**

**Question B — "did changing it help?"** A *run value*. Two-sample, per group, on F-270's bracketed SD:

| true gap | SD .20 | SD .25 | SD .30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 R/100 | 25,116 | 39,244 | 56,512 |
| 1.0 R/100 | 6,279 | 9,811 | 14,128 |
| 2.0 R/100 | 1,570 | 2,453 | 3,532 |
| 5.0 R/100 | 251 | 392 | 565 |

**Putting them together at the same effect size.** A +20-point tendency, priced through the anticipation slope $D$ (§3d), against the 47 observations the opponent needs:

| $D$ (R/100) | sequence gap it implies | opponent needs | **you need** | **ratio** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.20 R/100 | 47 | 245,277 / group | **5,208×** |
| 2 | 0.40 | 47 | 61,319 | **1,302×** |
| 4 | 0.80 | 47 | 15,330 | **326×** |
| 8 | 1.60 | 47 | 3,832 | **81×** |

> **THE HEADLINE OF THIS CYCLE. Your opponent's advance report on your pitcher is on FIRMER STATISTICAL GROUND than your own evaluation of your pitcher — by two to three orders of magnitude, at every value of the unknown parameter.** They are measuring a categorical frequency. You would be measuring a run-value difference. **A 1,600-pitch season is comfortably enough for the first and roughly 1/2,000th of enough for the second.**

**This is the input-vs-outcome asymmetry (F-257, F-289) with a genuinely new feature: it is asymmetric BETWEEN THE TWO PARTIES, not merely between two measurements of the same thing.** Registered **F-295**.

**The prescription it forces is unusual and is the practical core of the cycle:** the pitcher's own transition matrix is **the only thing in the entire topic he or his coach can actually measure within a season** — and it is measurable *because* it is the same statistic the opponent uses. **Audit your own scouting report. Do not attempt to evaluate outcomes.**

### §3d — The predictability tax: what does being readable actually cost?

Mapping onto the corpus's existing anticipation model (F-268, F-269): $v_i(p_i) = a_i + b_i p_i$, where the mechanism is that the hitter's subjective probability drives the penalty, and at equilibrium that probability equals the usage share. **A detectable tendency lets the hitter raise his local subjective probability from the marginal $p$ to the conditional $q$ while the pitcher pays as though it were $p$.** Excess cost in that cell is $D(q-p)/100$ runs per pitch, with $D$ the anticipation slope in runs per 100 across a full usage swing.

### §3f — The count-composition confound, and why the free fix is not affordable

A sweep snippet raised the right objection: *"pitch usage can be constrained by the count, and pitchers regularly finding themselves behind in the count could create the false appearance of sequences that are really driven by context."*

**This is F-287's lineup-slot composition bias, transplanted.** "The pitch after a fastball" is not a random subset — the previous pitch's strike rate determines which counts you land in, and count drives usage. **So an apparent sequence effect can be pure composition with zero true sequence dependence.**

Model it with usage depending **only** on count state:
$$\text{apparent gap} = \Delta(\text{strike rate}) \times \Delta(\text{usage between count states})$$

| Δ strike rate | Δusage .20 | .25 | .30 | .35 | .40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5pp | 1.0 | 1.2 | 1.5 | 1.7 | 2.0 |
| 10pp | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 |
| 15pp | 3.0 | 3.8 | 4.5 | 5.2 | 6.0 |
| **20pp** | 4.0 | 5.0 | 6.0 | **7.0** | **8.0** |

**1–8 points of apparent sequence effect from composition alone.**

**Two honest observations, and the second is the sharp one:**

1. **This is NOT F-287.** There the bias was **2.5× the effect** and fatal. Here it is 1–8 points against a ~20-point detection floor — **below what a season can resolve.** The corpus should not inflate a real confound into a crisis because the last one was a crisis. **It matters for interpretation, not for detection: any apparent sequence effect under ~10 points should be attributed to count composition before it is attributed to the pitcher.**
2. **The free fix is NOT free here, and that is new.** F-287's fix cost nothing — compare slots 1–4 vs slots 1–4. The fix here is to compare *within count*, which takes $k^2$ cells to $12k^2$: $k=3$ goes 9 → 108 cells (n: 178 → 15); $k=4$ goes 16 → 192 (n: 100 → 8). **Both fail the §3e budget by 3–6×.**

> **THE BIND: the confound is real, the correction is textbook, and the correction costs more sample than the season contains.** You can have an unconfounded estimate or an adequately-powered one. Not both. Registered **F-298**.

### §3g — The equilibrium argument: an observed sequence effect is evidence of a MISTAKE, not a lever

**This is the marker-vs-lever rule applied to the transition matrix, and it is the conceptual result of the cycle.**

Apply F-268's stationarity condition to sequences rather than pitch types. If the hitter's anticipation responds to *conditional* frequencies the way it responds to marginal ones, then at an interior optimum **every sequence the pitcher actually uses has equal marginal value.** Therefore:

**An observed run-value gap between two sequences in game data is not a lever the pitcher can pull. It is one of three things:**
1. **Noise** — overwhelmingly the most likely (§3c: 3,832–245,277 pitches to resolve a real gap; a season has 1,600 across the whole arsenal).
2. **Composition** — count context masquerading as sequence (§3f).
3. **A genuine disequilibrium** — a real, exploitable error.

**Only (3) is actionable, and it is the rarest.** "His slider plays better after a fastball, so throw more sliders after fastballs" is **F-269's error — the more-used option should look BETTER at an optimum — transplanted from the marginal distribution to the transition matrix.** It is the stride-length error (F-043/F-044/F-045) in its third venue.

**The asymmetry that makes this NOT purely academic, and the coach is right to press it (§4):** equilibrium requires the adapting party to observe enough to adapt. A college hitter faces a given conference starter perhaps twice a season — **eight plate appearances.** He cannot personally equilibrate. **The adaptation runs through the ADVANCE REPORT, not through the hitter's experience.** So the equilibrium argument's force is exactly proportional to the opposing program's analytics capability — **which in the SEC is high and in a non-conference midweek is not.** Registered **F-299**.

### §3h — Over-alternation: the one place a real mechanism might live

**If** Kovash & Levitt's negative-serial-correlation result survives its 2024 rebuttal (§2a), the mechanism is well-established general psychology: humans asked to produce random sequences **over-alternate** — they switch too often, because streaks feel non-random. Walker & Wooders found the same structure in tennis serves: correct frequencies, incorrect serial independence.

**Applied here, it predicts a specific, checkable, and free-to-measure signature: a pitcher's REPEAT rate should sit BELOW his marginal usage rate.** If he throws 45% sliders, he should throw a slider after a slider about 45% of the time. Over-alternation shows up as, say, 33%.

**And it is detectable at the same cost as any other tendency:**

| marginal $p$ | −5pp | −10pp | −15pp | **−20pp** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.40 | 742 | 181 | 78 | **42** |
| 0.50 | 783 | 194 | 85 | **47** |
| 0.60 | 761 | 191 | 85 | **47** |

**A 20-point over-alternation is established in ~42–47 same-pitch observations. A college season supplies far more than that on a primary and secondary pitch.**

## §4 — TRANSLATE: the coach's four questions

### Q1 (to the biomechanist) — "You just told me my opponent can measure my guy better than I can. Do I actually believe that, or is it a trick of two different statistics?"

**Answer: believe it, and it is not a trick — it is the point.** The two parties are not measuring the same thing. **The opponent needs to know WHAT he throws (a frequency, ~47 observations). You would need to know WHETHER CHANGING IT HELPED (a run value, 3,832–245,277).** Nothing about the pitcher differs between the two; the *questions* differ, and one of them is 81–5,208× harder.

**The consequence is genuinely counter-intuitive and it is the cycle's one real strategic insight: the cheapest and most reliable analysis of your own pitcher is to build the report your opponent is building.** You are not disadvantaged — you have the same data and better access. You are only disadvantaged if you insist on asking the harder question.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"I can't tell you whether throwing the slider after the heater works. Nobody can — that needs about fifty seasons. What I can tell you, off this season, is that after a first-pitch fastball to a righty you go slider 61% of the time and your season rate is 40%. Vanderbilt's staff can run that same number in an afternoon, and they have."*
> **The drill is:** the **transition-matrix audit** — §5.
> **On video the failure looks like:** nothing. **There is no video failure mode for this and the coach must say so.** This is the first recommendation in the corpus with no visual signature — it lives entirely in the pitch log.
> **Here's how we know it's working:** the tendency closes toward the marginal, measured on ≥47 observations per cell across ≤32 cells. **You verify COMPLIANCE, never OUTCOME** — F-273's architecture, third application.

### Q2 (to the biomechanist) — "Our analytics guy hands me a sheet with sixteen split cells and one of them is circled. What do I do with it?"

**Answer: ask how many cells were on the sheet before he circled one.**

**If 16 cells (previous pitch, four-pitch arsenal), the largest one is +8.6 points in expectation ON A PITCHER WITH NO TENDENCIES, and there is a 56% chance at least one clears p<.05.** At 192 count-conditioned cells the number is **+47 points and the chance is 100%.**

**Rule to hand the analyst, and it is one line:**
> **Sixteen cells maximum. Report every cell, never the extreme one. Anything under 10 points is count composition (§3f). Nothing conditioned on count — the season cannot pay for it.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** nothing. **This one never reaches the athlete.** Telling an 88-mph arm he has a tendency that is 56% likely to be an artefact is how you get a pitcher thinking about the wrong thing in a 2-2 count.
> **How we know it's working:** the circled cell survives on **next** season's data. **A tendency that does not replicate was noise, and roughly half of them will be.**

### Q3 (to the anatomist) — "Is there anything to TRAIN here, or am I just reading a spreadsheet?"

**Answer: you are reading a spreadsheet, and the anatomist will not pretend otherwise (§3a).** There is no tissue, no adaptation, no capacity.

**But two things follow that are worth a coach's time:**

1. **It is the only free lever in the corpus.** Zero stress cost — unique among everything in `velocity-development.md` and `coaching-translation.md`. At 0.8–6.4 runs it outranks times-through-the-order (~1 run, F-288) and 0-2 counts (~0.2 runs, F-280) **and costs no tissue to collect.**
   ⚠️ **Unless the fix moves the arsenal.** Shifting 15 points toward breaking balls to break a pattern leaves the free regime, and non-fastball workload accounting is an open corpus gap.
2. **Write the plan so it degrades.** F-127: command goes first, breaking-ball finish second. **A sequence requiring a located third pitch in the sixth is a plan for a pitcher who no longer exists by then.** Nothing in the sequencing literature models a changing executor.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"This isn't something you train. It's something we fix on paper in February and then leave alone."*
> **The drill is:** there isn't one. **Saying so is the honest answer.**
> **How we know it's working:** compliance in the pitch log, ≥47 per cell. Nothing else.

### Q4 (to both) — "If it's all equilibrium and noise, why would I bother at all?"

**Answer: because equilibrium requires an ADAPTING OPPONENT, and college baseball only partly has one (§3g).**

The equilibrium argument says an exploitable sequence edge should already be arbitraged. **But arbitrage runs through the advance report, not the hitter's memory** — a college hitter sees a given conference starter ~8 PA a season and cannot personally equilibrate. **So the correct posture is program-dependent, and this is the one place the corpus can give a genuinely conditional recommendation:**

- **vs. a high-analytics conference opponent (SEC weekend):** assume the report exists. **Audit and break your top one or two tendencies. This is where the 0.8–6.4 runs live.**
- **vs. a low-analytics midweek opponent:** the tendency is not being read. **Spend the time elsewhere.**

⚠️ **This entire recommendation rests on an assumption nobody has tested: that a pitcher can deliberately alter his own transition matrix and have it hold in a game.** That is **F-197 — no bullpen-to-game transfer study exists in baseball** — for the third consecutive cycle (Dispute #20a, F-282's manufactured count, and now this). **Stated, not hidden.**

## §5 — WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK: THE TRANSITION-MATRIX AUDIT

**Cost: one afternoon of an analyst's time, per pitcher. No new instrumentation. No athlete time. Uses data you already own.**

**Step 1.** Pull last season's pitch log for one 85+ starter. **Exclude the first pitch of every plate appearance** (no previous pitch).

**Step 2.** Build **one** table — previous pitch type (rows) × current pitch type (columns), **split by batter handedness and NOTHING ELSE.** For $k=3$ that is 18 cells at ~89 each; for $k=4$, 32 cells at ~50. **Do not condition on count.** §3e.

**Step 3.** Next to each row, print the pitcher's **marginal** usage for that batter hand. **The row IS the tendency; the marginal IS the null.**

**Step 4.** Flag a cell only if **all three** hold:
- ≥47 observations in the conditioning cell;
- deviation from marginal **≥15 points** (10 points is count composition — §3f);
- it is not the single most extreme cell on the sheet, **or**, if it is, you have accepted the 56–81% false-positive rate and said so out loud.

**Step 5 — the free extra, and run it first.** Compute the **repeat rate** for the primary and secondary pitch: $P(\text{slider} \mid \text{previous} = \text{slider})$ vs the marginal slider rate. **A repeat rate well below the marginal is the over-alternation signature (§3h).** ~42–47 same-pitch observations. **It is one number per pitch type, and it tests the only claim in this topic with a real mechanism behind it.**

**Step 6 — what "working" looks like, and it is NOT run values.** Re-run in-season on ≥47 observations per cell. **Success = the flagged tendency has moved toward the marginal.** **Failure = it hasn't.** **You will never know if it scored runs, and you must tell the athlete that up front** — F-273's script, third application:

> *"We're changing this on principle, not on evidence. If your ERA is better in April I won't credit it and if it's worse I won't blame it. Neither reading would be real."*

**What NOT to do, stated because all three are the field's default behaviour:**
- ❌ Do not build a count-conditioned sequencing report. **It is 100% guaranteed to produce a fake tendency and the expected size of the biggest one is 47 points.**
- ❌ Do not compare run values between sequences. **Needs 3,832–245,277 per group.**
- ❌ Do not tell the pitcher about a tendency you found in the most extreme of sixteen cells.

## §6 — CROSS-EXAMINATION

### §6.1 — The COACH challenges the BIOMECHANIST on F-295 (the 81–5,208× asymmetry)

**"You've compared a one-sample proportion test against a two-sample run-value test and called the ratio a discovery. Those are different tests of different quantities. Change either convention and your headline number moves. I think you've dressed up 'frequencies are easier to measure than run values' as a finding, and every coach already knows that."**

**Biomechanist — CONCEDED IN PART, DEFENDED IN PART.**

**Conceded:** the *ratio* is convention-dependent and should not be quoted as a precise quantity. It spans 81× to 5,208× purely on the unmeasured $D$, and would move again under different power conventions. **The cell values go the way of F-269's magnitude table: say the direction, never the number.** F-295's NUMBERS field is written to reflect this.

**Defended:** the *direction and order of magnitude* survive every reasonable variation, because they do not come from the test conventions — they come from the fact that **a proportion has a bounded variance ($\le 0.25$) and a per-pitch run value has an SD of 0.20–0.30 runs against effects of order 0.002–0.016 runs.** The signal-to-noise ratios differ by orders of magnitude *before* any test is chosen. **To collapse the asymmetry you would need $D$ near 100 R/100, which is roughly forty times the largest bracketed value and would mean a pitch thrown 100% of the time is worth a run per pitch more than one thrown never.**

**Coach's response: accepts the defence, holds the objection to the specific numbers.** Logged as part of **Dispute #22**.

### §6.2 — The ANATOMIST challenges the COACH on the §5 recommendation

**"You've written a six-step protocol on top of a mechanism you told me in §3a doesn't exist. There is no tissue, no adaptation, and no evidence a pitcher can deliberately change his own transition matrix in a game. F-197 says the transfer study doesn't exist. You have made a recommendation out of an assumption for the third cycle running, and each time you flag it and proceed anyway. At what point does flagging it stop being honesty and start being a habit?"**

**Coach — CONCEDED IN FULL, and the concession is the sharpest thing in this brief.**

**He is right, and the pattern is now documented three cycles deep:** F-282 (the manufactured full count), Dispute #20a, and now this. **Three consecutive cycles have produced exactly one executable recommendation each, and all three rest on the same untested assumption — that deliberate practice of a decision-layer skill transfers to competition.**

**What the coach changes as a result, rather than merely noting it:**
- **§5 is downgraded from a training intervention to a MEASUREMENT protocol.** Steps 1–5 are an audit — they describe the athlete and assume nothing about changing him. **Only Step 6 assumes transfer, and it is now labelled as the assumption it is.**
- **The audit is worth running EVEN IF transfer is zero**, which is the real defence: it tells you what the opposing report says about your pitcher, and that is a fact about the world regardless of whether he can act on it.

### §6.3 — The BIOMECHANIST challenges the COACH on the SEC-vs-midweek conditional (F-299)

**"You've built a program-dependent recommendation on a claim about opposing analytics staffs that you have not measured and cannot measure. You don't know whether Vanderbilt's staff runs transition matrices. You've invented a scouting-capability gradient to rescue a finding the equilibrium argument would otherwise kill."**

If none were, the tendency is unexploited and the answer is "don't bother" for the opposite reason.** The recommendation only has force in the middle, and **the middle is where college baseball obviously is.**

**Conceded within that:** the coach cannot say *which* opponents, and **the SEC/midweek split is an illustration, not a measurement.** He will not name a conference threshold.
