# Daily Brief — 2026-08-12
## Anatomy & Physiology of the Throwing Body (Day 1 foundation)
**Population: elite only. 85 MPH floor.** Showcase-circuit HS prospect → college → pro.
Full report: `research/library/anatomy-physiology.md`

### The one thing to internalize
Peak elbow varus torque in adult pitching is **~100 N·m**, far exceeding the isolated UCL's tensile capacity. Modeling splits the load roughly **one-third UCL / one-third flexor-pronator mass / one-third bony articulation**. At 85+ this is not a safety margin — it's the operating condition of every pitch. **The ligament survives only because muscle shields it.** That single fact justifies every arm-care decision below. **[ESTABLISHED]**

### Five findings that change how you coach an 85+ arm

**1. "Abnormal" imaging is the baseline in this population. ▶ ELITE-SPECIFIC [ESTABLISHED]**
- ~**79%** of *asymptomatic* pro throwing shoulders show labral abnormality (55% signal change, 45% frank tear) — Miniaci, *AJSM* 2002.
- Ultrasound in **368 asymptomatic pro pitchers**: dominant-arm hypoechoic foci **28.0% vs 3.5%**, calcifications **24.9% vs 1.6%**; 13 of 26 MRI'd showed **scar remodeling of the anterior bundle of the UCL**.
- **Elbow MRI findings do not predict future injury-list placement** in asymptomatic pro pitchers (Wilcox 2017). Shoulder MRI findings likewise didn't predict IL placement.
- **Operational read:** a scan cannot clear *or* condemn an asymptomatic elite arm. Symptoms, ER strength, ROM trajectory, and performance are the operative signals.

**2. Fatigue neurally *disconnects* the deceleration muscle. [ESTABLISHED]**
After a simulated game, pitchers show a **voluntary activation deficit of the infraspinatus** — the nervous system can't fully recruit it, independent of the muscle's own condition. Post-game strength drops ~11% ER, ~18% IR, ~15% flexion. Shoulder IR, total motion, and elbow extension are **still depressed at 24 hours**. Meanwhile joint kinetics per pitch do *not* fall proportionally with velocity. Translation: **late in an outing the passive structures carry a larger share of an undiminished load.**

**3. Velocity is the LAST fatigue indicator, not the first.**
Order of appearance: command drift (arm-side misses, elevated FBs) → loss of breaking-ball finish → visible slot drop → longer between pitches → **velocity last**. Pull decisions keyed to the radar gun are keyed to the final signal. **[EMERGING as a formal hierarchy; ESTABLISHED that command/secondaries degrade first]**

**4. GIRD is mostly bone in this population — chase total motion instead. [ESTABLISHED / EMERGING]**
Humeral retrotorsion (~10–15° dominant-side) is laid down while the proximal humeral physis is open (~ages 8–16) and is **locked at skeletal maturity**. Much of an elite pitcher's measured GIRD is osseous.
- Wilk 2011 (**122 pro pitchers, 5 seasons**): **TRM deficit >5° = ~2.5× shoulder injury risk**; GIRD alone ~1.9× but **not significant (P=.17)**.
- A 17-study meta-analysis of preseason ROM screening found **no motion measure significantly predicted** in-season injury.
- **Do:** track *his own* ROM/ER-strength baseline longitudinally across a start cycle. **Don't:** screen against population norms, or stretch a deficit that's bone (you'll lengthen the anterior capsule you wanted to keep).

**5. Connective tissue is net-catabolic for ~36 h after loading. [ESTABLISHED, non-throwing tissue]**
Collagen synthesis peaks ~24 h and stays up ~72 h, but degradation rises earlier — **net collagen balance is negative to ~36 h, positive from ~36–72 h**. Reloading inside the negative window repeatedly = net matrix degradation. This is the best physiological argument against stacking high-intent throwing days. *Caveat: derived from patellar tendon/Achilles in non-throwers; never mapped for the UCL.*

### Fast corrections to common talk
- "Stretch out his GIRD" → **[FOLKLORE]** when it's osseous.
- "MRI is ugly, he's hurt" / "MRI is clean, he's fine" → **both wrong** here.
- "Run to flush lactic acid" → **[FOLKLORE]**; pitching is alactic, and lactate is fuel.
- "Ice after lifting" → **[ESTABLISHED harm]** — CWI attenuates resistance-training hypertrophy (Piñero 2024 meta-analysis). Tactical use only.
- "He wings, he's going to get hurt" → **[FOLKLORE]**; majority prevalence in asymptomatic pros. 2013 Scapular Summit: role "not clearly defined."
- "He's loose" → laxity ≠ instability; in this population laxity is often an asset.

### Practical carry-forwards
- **Count pens, flat-ground, long toss, showcases, and testing days as load.** Game pitch counts systematically undercount true workload.
- **Periscapular strength declines measurably across a collegiate season** (all groups down 3–14%; scaption −8%, p=.018). In-season maintenance isn't optional.
- **New ulnar-sided numbness should raise UCL suspicion**, not just nerve suspicion — laxity increases nerve traction; they co-travel.
- **Between-start map:** Day+1 no high intent (collagen still net-negative, ROM still down); Day+2 reintroduce; Day+3 highest intent **gated on his ROM/ER-strength returning to baseline, not the calendar**.
- **Highest-risk single exposure in this population:** maximal-intent showcase/pro-day outing, off-schedule, low chronic load, minimal warm-up, strong incentive to hide fatigue.

### ⚠ Immediate-refer flags
Medial elbow pain at MER · posterior elbow pain at release · posterosuperior shoulder pain in late cocking · ring/small finger numbness · arm heaviness with coldness/swelling/color change (**vascular TOS — urgent**) · posterior axillary pain with abrupt velocity loss (**lat/teres major strain**) · sudden lateral trunk pain with a pop (**oblique**) · "dead arm" that persists past a normal recovery window or carries any pain, paresthesia, night symptoms, or visible atrophy.

### Three questions for the biomechanist
1. Can mechanics decouple velocity from elbow varus torque **within an individual**, and by how much? All the coaching leverage is in that residual.
2. What is the real dose–response curve for high-intent throwing and **net collagen balance in the UCL specifically**? Our recovery reasoning rests on patellar-tendon data.
3. Is there a **dugout-measurable marker of posterior-cuff activation deficit** that fires before command drifts — and long before velocity drops?
