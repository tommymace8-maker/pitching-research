## 3. ⭐ THE RESEARCH — THE STANDARDIZED-EFFECT IMPORT FALLACY (F-473)

### 3.1 Run the obvious import and watch it explode

The pooled effect the literature reports (**SNIPPET-ONLY**): Simonsmeier, Androniea, Buecker & Frank (2020), *Int Review of Sport & Exercise Psychology* 14(1) — **d = 0.431, 95% CI [0.298, 0.563]**.

This corpus's own registered command scale: **F-186** — within-pitcher miss-distance SD ≈ **10 in**. **F-170** — one inch ≈ **0.3 FIP**.

```
Δ = 0.431 × 10 in  = 4.31 inches of command
     4.31 × 0.3    = 1.29 FIP
```

**A pitcher sits in a chair 15 minutes, three times a week, and takes 1.29 off his FIP.**

### 3.2 Two reductios

**(a) Against the registry.** That is the largest effect in 472 findings — **more than 4× a one-SD Stuff+ gain** (F-170), bigger than an entire season of command development (F-309: 4.52 pts), bigger than the mound (F-464: ~2.0 mph). For free, with no arm cost, using a technique sold since the 1980s.

**(b) Against detection.** A 4.31-inch effect needs **42 tracked pitches** to establish at 80% power (§4). **Two bullpens.** An effect that size would have been rediscovered independently by every pitching coach who ever lived. It has not been.

### 3.3 Where the import breaks — and it is general

A standardized effect is a **ratio**: `d = Δ/σ`. Importing `d` from population A into B and computing `Δ_B = d̂·σ_B` is valid **only if `Δ_A/σ_A = Δ_B/σ_B`** — only if **the raw benefit is proportional to the receiving population's scatter.** The samples underneath these meta-analyses are largely undergraduates and novices, who have both a larger σ *and* far more headroom. The import assumes a group with 3× the scatter gets 3× the raw benefit. **Nobody has claimed that, let alone tested it.**

The honest alternative is the **raw import** — keep the effect in inches. But:

> 🚨 **META-ANALYSES DISCARD THE RAW UNITS. STANDARDIZATION DESTROYS EXACTLY THE NUMBER YOU NEED.** `d = 0.431` cannot be recovered back into inches without going to each primary study — which is the whole reason the pooled figure gets quoted instead.

**THE RULE (F-473): when you meet a pooled `d`, ask what the raw units were before standardization. If you cannot recover them, you have a DIRECTION and not a MAGNITUDE — say so, and never multiply.**

This is a **new hazard class**, and it is general to every meta-analytic `d` this corpus will ever meet, in any topic. It sits beside F-465 (the unit-mislabelled table), F-382 (peer-reviewed laundering), F-373 (abstract contradicted by its own table).

**It also names a category the corpus lacked (F-474): VERIFIED LEVER, UNPRICED.** The usual failure is a marker sold as a lever. This is the mirror — a real lever whose size cannot be transported.

## 4. WHAT A PROGRAM COULD ACTUALLY DETECT (F-476)

F-186's power form, σ = 10 in: `n = 7.849·σ²/Δ²`

| Effect Δ | Pitches per condition | |
|---|---|---|
| 4.31 in (the naive import) | **42** | two bullpens — and it would already be famous |
| 2.0 in | **196** | ✅ reproduces F-186's ~200 exactly, validating the form |
| 1.0 in | **785** | a full fall, one condition |
| 0.5 in | **3,140** | not available to any college program |

**The clustering correction nobody applies.** Pitches within a bullpen are not independent. Design effect `1+(m−1)ρ`; at m = 25, ρ = 0.10 → **DE = 3.4**, so 785 becomes **~2,670**. ⚠️ **ρ has never been published for any population — 0.10 is an assumption, stated as one.**

**Inverted, at a realistic 400 tracked declared pitches per condition across a fall:**

```
MDE = 10·√(7.849/400)   = 1.40 inches       (independence assumed)
MDE = 10·√(7.849/117.6) = 2.58 inches       (at DE = 3.4)
```

> **A COLLEGE PROGRAM CANNOT DETECT AN IMAGERY EFFECT ON COMMAND SMALLER THAN ~1.4 INCHES — REALISTICALLY NOT BELOW ~2.6.**

**This is the cycle's hardest result and it argues against running the experiment at all.**

**And it reaches past this topic. F-186's ~200 — quoted throughout this corpus as the price of a 2-inch command claim — assumes independent pitches. If ρ > 0 it is an UNDERESTIMATE, and every command detection threshold in the registry is optimistic by up to 3.4×.** Registered as F-482 item 7 and escalated into the standing n ≥ 97 audit.

## 5. MECHANISM — the anatomist's deflation (F-477)

**SNIPPET-ONLY:** *Pitching-specific facilitation of upper-limb corticospinal excitability during motor imagery of sports motor skills*, **Exp Brain Res (2026), PMID 41557017**. TMS over M1, MEP amplitudes. Baseball MI facilitated **APB (abductor pollicis brevis)**; MI **with a model video** additionally facilitated **FCR**; other recorded muscles not facilitated vs rest. *n, stimulus intensity and magnitudes unknown — blocked.*

**THREE DEFLATIONS:**

1. **This is an acute state measured DURING the imagery, not a training adaptation.** MEP facilitation under motor imagery has been reproducible for thirty years and **has never by itself predicted a performance gain.**
2. **APB IS A THUMB MUSCLE.** Its contribution to ball velocity is nil. Its only plausible pitching relevance is the **ball–hand interface** (`library/ball-hand-friction.md`, 2026-09-21) — finger force and release. Interesting lead; not a velocity result.
3. **The muscles that actually make a pitch were not shown to be facilitated** — lats, pecs, subscap, the hip/trunk chain. TMS reaches distal representations easily and proximal ones poorly, so this is partly a **measurement-accessibility artefact**, not a physiological ranking. Either way the paper cannot be used to claim the prime movers were engaged, because they were not measured.

**Verdict: imagery engages the motor system in a pitching-specific, muscle-specific way. Nothing about velocity. Nothing about training.**

## 6. WHERE THE HEADROOM PROBABLY IS — and why it is graded WEAK (F-478)

**SNIPPET-ONLY:** Driskell, Copper & Moran (1994), *J Applied Psychology* 79(4):481–492 — **k = 35, overall d = 0.53**; mental practice more effective the more the task involved cognitive components (**r = 0.44**).

An 85+ arm's delivery is **automatized** — that is what "mechanics already good" means, and it is this program's population constraint. The execution layer is the low-cognitive case. What is *not* automatized: pitch selection and sequencing (F-294→F-303), count strategy (F-278→F-283), the runner-on-first decision set (F-316→F-325), times through the order (F-286→F-292), and the pre-pitch routine's restart after disruption.

**Prediction: imagery's headroom in this population is in the DECISION layer, not the EXECUTION layer.**

> ⚠️ **AND THE OBJECTION IS CORRECT AND IS RECORDED, NOT BURIED.** Driskell's moderator is measured **across tasks** (card-sort vs dart throw), not **across layers within one task**. Applying it to a within-task decomposition of pitching is **structurally the same move §3 just condemned.** Graded **WEAK**. Kept on cost, not on evidence. **Dispute #42** — where the anatomist's ruling is that the r = 0.44 should be deleted from the argument entirely and the claim rest on **automaticity** alone, which has independent support.

## 9. STEP 3 — THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

### Q1 (to the biomechanist): "You just killed the only number I had. What am I allowed to tell a pitcher imagery is worth?"

**Answer.** Nothing in inches. `d = 0.431` licenses *"it works"* and does not license *"it is worth X."* The moment you multiply it by our 10-inch SD you have asserted that a novice with three times the scatter gets three times the raw benefit — an empirical claim nobody has tested (F-473). And §4 says you will never measure it yourself: your floor is **1.4 inches** at best, **2.6** realistically.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — *"This has been tested properly, in people who aren't you, and it works. I can't tell you how much, and I'm not going to pretend I can."* **The drill is** the 15-minute session in §10. **On video the failure looks like** nothing — there is no video failure mode, which is exactly why this is seductive and why the anti-attribution clause matters. **Here's how we know it's working:** ⚠️ **WE WON'T.** F-476 says the measurement is unaffordable. Adopt it on cost (F-481), and **do not credit it for a good fall.**

### Q2 (to the anatomist): "Is there anything physically happening, or am I selling meditation?"

**Answer.** Something is happening: motor imagery of pitching raises corticospinal excitability in a **muscle-specific** way (PMID 41557017). But three caveats gut the coaching use (F-477): it is an **acute state during the imagery**, the facilitated muscle is **abductor pollicis brevis — a thumb muscle**, and the delivery's prime movers **were not shown to be facilitated at all.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — *"Your nervous system doesn't fully distinguish imagining the pitch from throwing it. That's real and it's measured. It does not mean imagining builds arm speed."* **The drill is** first-person, real-time imagery (not slow-motion, not watching yourself from the stands) — the specificity result is the one thing that argues the *content* matters. **On video the failure looks like** him doing it in third person or at half speed; ask him to describe what his glove hand is doing and if he answers from outside his own body, he's watching a movie. **How we know it's working:** we don't, at the performance level. The only honest check is that he can report **kinesthetic** detail — the feel of the seams, the front foot landing — not just pictures.

### Q3 (to both): "Fifteen minutes on what? If I only get one target, which one?"

**Answer.** The delivery is automatized, which is the low-headroom case; the decision layer is not (F-478). **But the biomechanist's objection to that reasoning is sustained** (Dispute #42), so the honest support is **automaticity alone**, not Driskell's r = 0.44.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — *"Don't spend it re-imagining your mechanics. You already own those. Spend it on the pitch you have to make when it's 3-2, runner on second, and you don't want to throw the fastball."* **The drill is** situational rehearsal: name the count, the runner, the hitter's handedness, the pitch, and the target, then run it in real time. **On video the failure looks like** — in a game — a long pause and a shake-off in exactly the situations he rehearsed, meaning the rehearsal never reached the decision. **How we know it's working:** **time to decision**, not location. Tempo between catcher's sign and set is measurable off any broadcast or bullpen video and does not need 200 pitches to move visibly — though ⚠️ **a tempo change is not a performance gain and must not be reported as one** (cf. `library/pitch-clock-tempo.md`).

### Q4 (to both): "The ABCA 'mental bullpen' says a guy can throw a pen in his head on his off day. Free reps or free lunch?"

**Answer.** **Nobody has ever tested it (F-482 item 4).** It is the most testable and most commercially loaded claim in the topic: it asserts that mental reps carry some of a throw's training benefit at none of its arm cost. The mechanism in §5 is the only thing in its favour and it is an acute excitability measure, not an adaptation.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — *"It costs you nothing, so do it. But it is not a substitute for a throw and I'm not taking a throw off your program because you did it."* **The drill is** the non-throwing pitchers running a mental pen alongside the live one, same tempo, same sequence. **On video the failure looks like** him doing it with his eyes closed and motionless — real-time is the whole point; if his pen takes four minutes when a live pen takes fifteen, he's skimming. **How we know it's working:** ⚠️ **UNKNOWN AND UNTESTED — say so.** This is the single cheapest study in the topic and it is unrun.
