## §1 THE RESEARCH

Full derivations in `library/catcher-framing-command-measurement.md`. Headlines only here.

### 1a. The called zone is a ball-centre zone (F-305)
**19.90 in × 24.50 in**, versus the 17 × 21.6 rulebook rectangle — **+33% area**, perimeter **88.8 in**. Horizontal geometry is exact and rule-derived. *Every* framing argument and *every* called-strike command metric lives on that perimeter.

### 1b. The physical framing lever is plate-to-glove travel (F-306)
The umpire judges the ball **where it is caught**, ~1.5–2.0 ft past the plate. Over that gap it keeps descending:

**4-seam 92 mph ≈ 2.0 in · sinker ≈ 2.4 in · slider ≈ 2.8 in · curveball ≈ 3.7 in. Horizontal ≈ 0.9 in.**

Two predictions fall out of the geometry alone: framing is **vertical-dominant** (2–4× horizontal) and **bottom-of-zone-dominant** (lever ∝ tan VAA). The industry believes both. **This cycle reached both without reading anyone**, which is worth more than one more assertion.

⚠️ **A bound on what is available, not proof anything is coachable. MARKER, NOT LEVER.**

### 1c. ★ The shift-times-density result (F-307)
Converted calls = **δ · f₀** — the shift times the density of taken pitches at the line — and **to first order it does not depend on umpire uncertainty σ_u at all.** A sloppy umpire converts more pitches near the line and fewer far from it; the two cancel.

**f₀ = 7.38% of taken pitches per inch** (calibrated: σ_p = 10 in/axis, swing model fit to 47/68/28; achieved 48.4/66.6/27.8). Cross-checks at wider bands agree to 1%.

> **Every inch of presentation ≈ 7 calls per 100 taken pitches.**

This is the cycle's most portable result **because it survives not knowing the thing nobody knows.**

### 1d. What it is worth — and the backward check (F-308)
Using the corpus's **own** F-270 table, one flipped call = 0.100 R. A 0.5-in catcher difference ≈ **8 runs per college season** — larger than the 0-2 waste pitch (~0.2 R), times-through-the-order (~1 R) and a sequencing tendency (0.8–6.4 R) **combined**.

**Run the reported ±15–20 R MLB framing spread backward and it implies only 0.35–0.70 in of presentation difference.** Against a ~2-inch available lever. **Nearly every professional catcher already captures most of the geometry; what separates them is the residual.**

### 1e. ★★ The finding of the cycle (F-309)
Pitcher command as execution scatter (F-182), converted to zone-rate points — **9.0 pts per inch of execution SD**:

| | pts of called-strike rate |
|---|---|
| A realistic **one-season command gain** (7.5 → 7.0 in) | **4.52** |
| **Changing catchers** (δ = 0.5 in) | **3.69** |

> **Ratio 0.82. The catcher is the same size as the thing you are trying to measure.**

Against the *large cross-sectional* command gap the catcher is only 15% — ignorable. **It is the small within-athlete change — the only kind development ever produces — where contamination equals signal.** The **F-094** between-vs-within pattern in a new venue.

**And it is bias, not noise.** Catcher assignment is not random. **More data makes the wrong answer more confident** — the corpus's **second** worked example after F-287.

### 1f. The fix is free this time (F-310)
**Zone rate / edge rate / miss distance from tracked location** is framing-immune by construction, uses 100% of the sample, costs nothing. **Contrast F-298**, where the unconfounded estimator cost 3–6× and failed the budget outright.

⚠️ But clean ≠ fast: **2.4 college starter-seasons** to confirm a realistic command gain. The fix removes the **bias**; the **noise wall** of F-273/F-282/F-286/F-295 is untouched.

### 1g. F-283 survives its strongest objection (F-311, F-312, F-313)
ABS reached this program's level: **SEC ran the challenge system in the 2026 conference tournament; NCAA approved all three divisions from 2027.** F-283's conditional is resolved.

The best argument *against* "never challenge on 0-2" is that the umpire is most wrong there — arXiv 2609.03786 reportedly finds **−17.17 pp on 0-2**. Put both terms in one expression: 0-2 needs an **18.2×** overturn advantage; the largest the reported bias can supply is **1.86×**. **Margin ≈ 9.8×. The rule stands**, now against a real attack rather than by default.

## §3 TRANSLATE — the coach interrogates the other two

**Q1 (coach → biomechanist): "You say the catcher is worth as much as the command gain. I've watched a kid clean up his delivery and throw more strikes. Are you telling me that was the catcher?"**
Partly, and only sometimes. For the **big** difference — an elite-command arm versus a poor one — the catcher is 15% and you can ignore it. It is the **small year-over-year move** where the two are the same size (4.52 vs 3.69 pts). So: if the kid's execution scatter genuinely tightened, you would see it in **tracked miss distance**, which no catcher touches. If you only saw it in strike percentage, you cannot separate the two.
> **So what I tell the pitcher is nothing** — this is my problem, not his. **The drill is** a reporting change: command comes off TrackMan location, not off the scoreboard. **On video the failure looks like** nothing; that is the danger — it is invisible by construction. **Here's how we know it's working:** we re-cut last year's numbers within catcher and see whether the "improvement" survives. If it halves, we learned something.

**Q2 (coach → anatomist): "Two inches of ball drop between the plate and the glove. Can a catcher actually do anything about that, given what a body can do in 13 milliseconds?"**
**No — not by reacting.** 13 ms is below voluntary reaction time by an order of magnitude; nothing about the catch is a response to the pitch at that timescale. Whatever a catcher does is **pre-programmed from the pitch call and the ball's early flight**, which is exactly why receiving is trained as posture and setup rather than as a reaction. The anatomist's caution: **the 2 inches is what geometry permits, not what a human can harvest**, and the gap between those is unmeasured.
> **So what I tell the catcher is** get the glove to the spot early and quiet — **the drill is** a receiving block that scores glove stillness at the catch, **on video the failure looks like** the glove travelling *with* the ball and recoiling after it, **and here's how we know it's working:** boundary-band called-strike rate, split by catcher, **~2,500 taken pitches each** to detect a 0.5-in difference.

**Q3 (coach → biomechanist): "You told me the umpire's precision cancels out. That sounds too convenient. Why should I believe it?"**
Because it is an identity, not an empirical claim: ∫[Φ((d+δ)/σ) − Φ(d/σ)]dd = δ for any σ. A wider umpire distribution spreads the conversions out but does not change their total. **The assumption that does the work is that pitch density is flat across the band**, which holds along the sides and fails at the corners. The correction is second-order and was not computed.
> **So what I tell the staff is** stop game-planning around whether tonight's guy is tight. **Here's how we know it's working:** we don't, directly — but we stop spending attention on a second-order term.

**Q4 (coach → both): "Eight runs a season for half an inch of catcher. That's bigger than everything you've given me in four cycles. Why am I only hearing it now?"**
Because the corpus optimised for *pitching* questions and this is a *roster* question. It is the right answer to a question this program was not asking. ⚠️ **And the number carries two unverified inputs** (the F-270 run table, the framing-spread anchor), so **the ordering is the finding and the cell is not.**
> **What I actually do with it:** hand it to whoever sets the lineup, and stop pretending it is mine to act on.

**② Anatomist → Biomechanist.** *"Your f₀ = 7.38%/in comes from a Gaussian pitch distribution you calibrated to remembered league rates, in a cycle where you read nothing. That is a model wearing a measurement's clothes."*
→ **CONCEDED ON THE VALUE, DEFENDED ON THE STRUCTURE.** f₀ is a model output and is now labelled so ("about 7 per 100 per inch," never 7.38). **What does not depend on it**: the σ_u cancellation (an identity), the 0.82 ratio's *order* (both numerator and denominator scale with f₀-adjacent assumptions and partially cancel), and the bias-not-noise argument (which is about assignment, not magnitude). **Logged as Dispute #23.**

**③ Coach → Biomechanist.** *"You found an 18.7-point framing gap from calledthird.com that backs into ~0.75–1.1 in, and your own independent check gave 0.35–0.70 in. You are about to call that corroboration. Two unverified numbers agreeing is exactly F-274."*
→ **CONCEDED IN FULL, AND RECORDED AGAINST INTEREST.** The convergence is noted in F-314 explicitly as **suggestive and nothing more**. One of the two inputs comes from a source whose only credibility signal is self-generated (F-314), which makes the agreement *weaker* evidence than two anonymous numbers would be, not stronger.

**④ Anatomist → Coach.** *"You are recommending zone rate as the command metric. A pitcher deliberately working off the plate has a low zone rate and may have excellent command. You have replaced a biased metric with a wrong one."*
→ **CONCEDED IN PART, AND NARROWED ON THE SPOT.** F-310 now carries the restriction: **compare a pitcher to himself over time at similar usage; never rank two pitchers.** The anatomist's stronger version — that usage drifts *with* command, so even the within-pitcher comparison is contaminated — is **not resolved** and goes to Dispute #23b.

**⑤ Biomechanist → Anatomist.** *"You said 13 ms is below reaction time, so framing must be pre-programmed. That is an inference about the catcher from a number about the ball. Nothing measured a catcher."*
→ **DEFENDED.** The claim is a *bound*, not a measurement: whatever a catcher does in that window cannot be a visually-driven correction, because no human closes that loop in 13 ms. It bounds the mechanism without identifying it.
