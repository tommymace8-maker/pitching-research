## 2. THE FIRST RESULT — "COLD IS ONE THING" IS FALSE, AND F-139 FUSED THREE INTERVENTIONS

The literature contains three interventions that share a temperature and share nothing else. F-139 merged them, and so does every clubhouse conversation.

| | **(A) Post-lift whole-body CWI** | **(B) Intra-outing local cooling** | **(C) Post-outing icing** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | 10–15 °C tub, 10–15 min, after resistance training | palm or forearm/shoulder cooling between innings | ice to the shoulder after the start |
| Why anyone does it | soreness, "recovery" | velocity retention late in the outing | arm "recovery" |
| Outcome measured | fibre CSA, 1RM, lean mass | ball velocity | **dynamometer torque** |
| Evidence | **13+ controlled trials, 2 meta-analyses** | 2 crossovers, **67–70 mph** | 1 crossover, n = 16, velocity never measured |
| Registered where | **new today, F-603→F-609** | **F-360** (already held) | **F-581, F-582** (already held) |
| Verdict | a real, measured adaptation cost | unusable on population | measured the wrong outcome |

**The practical consequence is not cosmetic.** The three have *opposite* recommendations. (B) says cool the arm *during* the outing. (A) says do not cool the trained muscle *right after lifting*. (C) says nobody knows. A single-line registry entry saying "CWI attenuates hypertrophy; use tactically" collapses all three into mush, and "use tactically" is unactionable because it never says tactically *when*.

## 3. THE RESULT OF THE DAY — THE SIGNIFICANT STRENGTH COST IS A **TRAINED-LIMB** FINDING, AND THE WAY PITCHERS ACTUALLY PLUNGE IS AN UNINFORMATIVE INTERVAL

**Grgic J (2023), *Eur J Sport Sci* 23(3):372–380, PMID 35068365.** Ten studies, **n = 170 total**, 92% male, 11 comparisons across 22 groups. Random-effects meta-analysis of CWI vs control on resistance-training strength gains. **SNIPPET-ONLY — abstract and secondary quotation of the abstract; methods never seen.**

| Analysis | ES | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overall** | **−0.23** | **−0.45, −0.01** | **0.041** |
| **CWI to the TRAINED LIMBS only** | **−0.31** | **−0.61, −0.01** | **0.041** |
| **WHOLE-BODY CWI** | **−0.08** | **−0.53, +0.38** | **0.743** |

Read the third row the way this corpus has learned to read rows like it (F-590). **The whole-body interval is not a null. It is an uninformative interval.** It runs from a −0.53 harm to a +0.38 benefit; its width (0.91) is *wider* than the overall analysis's (0.44). That is the signature of a small, underpowered subgroup, not of an established absence of effect. **What can honestly be said is: the significant, pooled strength cost in this literature is carried by studies that immersed only the trained limb, and for whole-body immersion — the thing an athlete does when he gets in a tub — the strength cost has never been established in either direction.**

**And the flagship study sits on the limb-only side of that line, which nobody says out loud.** Roberts LA et al. (2015), *J Physiol*, DOI 10.1113/JP270570, is the paper the entire "ice baths kill your gains" discourse rests on: n = 21 physically active men, 12 weeks of strength training 2×/wk, CWI **waist-deep**, ~10 °C, 10 min, initiated within 5 min post-exercise, vs 10 min low-intensity cycling. Active recovery gained isokinetic work **+19%**, type II fibre CSA **+17%**, myonuclei per fibre **+26%** (all p < 0.05); the CWI group gained **none of those significantly**. Those are large, clean, mechanistically coherent numbers — **and "waist-deep after lower-body training" is a trained-limb protocol, not a whole-body one.**

The complement is as important. **Fyfe JJ et al. (2019), *J Appl Physiol*, DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.00127.2019**, title included: *"...attenuates anabolic signalling and skeletal muscle fiber hypertrophy, **but not strength gain**, following whole-body resistance training."* n = 8 CWI vs 8 control, pair-matched on leg-press 1RM, 7 weeks. Fibre hypertrophy attenuated; **1RM strength was not.** One study at n = 8 per arm settles nothing — but it points the same way as Grgic's whole-body subgroup, from an independent design, and that is worth more than either alone.

## 4. THE HYPERTROPHY SIDE — AND THE BOUNDARY CONDITION THE INDUSTRY HAS ALREADY LOST

**Piñero A et al. (2024), *Eur J Sport Sci* 24(2):177–189, DOI 10.1002/ejsc.12074** — "Throwing cold water on muscle growth." **Eight studies.** Bayesian. **SNIPPET-ONLY.**

- Comparative effect: **cSMD₀.₅ = −0.22 [95% CrI −0.47, +0.04]**, favouring RT alone, with **p(<0) = 0.957** and **p(<−0.1) = 0.834**.
- Uncontrolled effects, which are the more arresting pair: **RT alone SMD₀.₅ = 0.36 [0.10, 0.61]** — reliable growth. **CWI + RT SMD₀.₅ = 0.14 [−0.08, +0.36]** — *an interval containing zero*. In eight studies, lifting produced hypertrophy you could bet on and lifting-plus-ice-bath did not.

Note the honest caveat on that second pair: uncontrolled (within-arm) effect sizes are not a between-group comparison, and the controlled estimate (−0.22) is the one with the −0.47-to-+0.04 interval that touches zero. **The direction is well supported; the magnitude is small and its interval includes "nothing."**

**🚨 AND NOW THE BOUNDARY CONDITION, which is the most useful sentence I retrieved all day.** Piñero's own stated scope, as quoted in secondary coverage: *"The results of analysis apply solely to CWI application within 15 minutes of exercise cessation, which may not accurately reflect practical applications."*

**Every number above is a number about plunging within fifteen minutes of racking the bar.** Nothing in either meta-analysis speaks to plunging two hours later, or six, or the next morning.

## 5. ⭐ THE "WAIT FOUR TO EIGHT HOURS" FIX IS UNTESTED, AND IT IS BEING SOLD AS A FINDING

The field has already converged on a remedy. It appears everywhere: *delay the plunge 4–6 h, or 8 h, or 24–48 h, and you keep the recovery and lose the cost.* The specific sources I found circulating it today:

- A 2026 narrative review — **Tornero-Aguilera JF, Lozano-Meca J, López-Moreno M, Sancho-Haro ES, Muñoz-López M, Baz-Valle E, López-Gil JF, Yáñez-Sepúlveda R, Clemente-Suárez VJ, "The cold-water immersion recovery–adaptation paradox," *Exp Physiol* (2026), DOI 10.1113/EP094042, PMID 42667675** — which recommends that during a hypertrophy mesocycle post-resistance CWI "should be avoided or delayed beyond approximately 8 hours, because repeated mTORC1 suppression accumulates into measurable losses of type II fibre cross-sectional area."
- Commercial and clinical restatements of a "4-hour rule," a "4–6 hour rule," and a clinic-sourced "24–48 hour buffer."

**Grade: the DIRECTION is a reasonable mechanistic inference. The NUMBER is FOLKLORE.** The 8 h figure is a recommendation inside a narrative review's reasoning, not the output of an experiment. **No trial in either meta-analysis used a delayed-CWI arm — the pooled evidence is 100% within-15-minutes by Piñero's own stated inclusion.** A review proposing a threshold and a review *measuring* one are different objects, and the number is already being quoted as though it were the second. Three different "rules" (4 h, 8 h, 24–48 h) circulating simultaneously is itself the tell: if any of them were measured, there would be one.

This is a cheap, obvious, unrun study: three arms, CWI at 0 min / CWI at +6 h / no CWI, hypertrophy and 1RM outcomes. It is the single thing that would convert this entire topic from "avoid" to "schedule."

## 6. THE ACUTE SIDE — CWI DOES NOT RESTORE STRENGTH, IT TRANSIENTLY TAKES EXPLOSIVENESS AWAY, AND THE SORENESS BIOMARKER DIES UNDER BIAS CORRECTION

Brand-new and directly relevant: **"Temporal dynamics of muscle strength recovery following acute cold-water immersion: a systematic review and meta-analysis," *PeerJ* 2025 (article 21537), PMID 42473449.** Databases searched to 2025-09-01. **Twenty-two RCTs.** **SNIPPET-ONLY** (`peerj.com` and PMC both blocked).

| Outcome | Pooled | Interval | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MVIC** | **g = 0.08** | −0.11, +0.26 (p = 0.42) | no overall and no time-dependent effect; **moderate certainty** |
| **CMJ, overall** | g = 0.01 | −0.25, +0.28 (p = 0.92) | — |
| **CMJ, 0 h post-immersion** | **g = −0.68** | **−1.16, −0.20** | significant subgroup difference across time, p < 0.01; **low certainty** |
| **Creatine kinase** | g = −0.43 → **−0.16** | −0.67,−0.19 → **−0.44,+0.12** | the benefit **does not survive trim-and-fill** |

Three things follow, and all three matter more to a pitching program than the hypertrophy argument.

1. **The thing CWI is used for — getting strength back — it does not do.** g = 0.08 at moderate certainty across 22 RCTs is as close to a real null as this corpus ever sees, and unlike the Grgic whole-body row its interval is *narrow* (0.37 wide). **This is a null, not an uninformative interval.** The distinction is the whole method and it should be read twice.
2. **Immediately after getting out, explosive output is impaired, medium effect, g = −0.68.** Mechanistically unsurprising — cold lowers nerve conduction velocity and motor-unit firing rate. A separate systematic review (*J ISAKOS* 2021, S2059-7754(21)00072-9, snippet-only) reports the same from the duration side: ice beyond ~10 min is linked to reduced muscular power.
3. **The soreness story's one objective leg is publication-bias-fragile.** CK's apparent reduction halves and crosses zero under trim-and-fill. What survives is the *subjective* benefit — soreness and perceived recovery — plus parasympathetic reactivation.

## 7. ⭐ THE DECISION, AND WHY IT DOES **NOT** NEED AN EXCHANGE RATE

This cycle is the structural mirror of 2026-10-05 (F-597, Dispute #60) and it comes out cleaner, for a reason worth stating.

Yesterday's problem was a *choice between two unpriced channels*: a low velocity-loss threshold buys CMJ height (unpriceable in mph, F-590's interval [−0.280, +0.404]) and a high one buys hypertrophy (r = 0.58 / r = 0.52 cross-sectional, never manipulated, F-504). Choosing required a conversion nobody has.

**Today does not, and here is the asymmetry in full.**

- **The cost of post-lift CWI** falls on the channel with the strongest registered *correlation* to velocity in this entire corpus — lean and total mass, F-001 r = 0.58, F-002 r = 0.52. **That link is CROSS_SECTIONAL and has never been manipulated (F-504). I cannot price the cost in mph and I am not going to pretend to.**
- **The benefit** is (i) soreness and perceived recovery — real, replicated, subjective; (ii) parasympathetic reactivation; (iii) a CK reduction that does not survive bias correction; and (iv) **not** strength restoration, which is a measured null at moderate certainty.

So: **the benefit channel contains nothing that this corpus can convert into mph either.** That is the point. When neither side of a trade is priceable in the outcome you care about, the deciding question is not "which is bigger" but **"which side has a demonstrated cost on a variable plausibly upstream of the outcome?"** Hypertrophy attenuation is demonstrated in controlled trials. The benefit side's demonstrated effects are perceptual. **Declining to plunge within 15 minutes of a lift therefore forfeits only perceptual benefit, and avoids a measured biological cost — and that conclusion survives without any mph conversion at all.**

**Stated at its correct strength and no stronger, exactly as F-597 required of itself:** this is **not** "skip the cold tub and you will throw harder." There is no such claim available and anyone who makes it is inventing a slope that does not exist. It is: **a habit whose only demonstrated returns are how you feel is being paid for with the adaptation you went to the weight room to get, and in a velocity-development block that is a bad trade — for a reason that needs no exchange rate.** Stopping a cost is not earning a return.

**The honest limit on that paragraph, which Dispute #61 exists to hold open:** everything above is solid for *trained-limb* immersion. For *whole-body* immersion after a *whole-body* lift, which is what the tub in the facility actually is, the strength cost is an uninformative interval and the hypertrophy cost rests on a −0.22 whose own interval touches zero.

## 11. THE THREE AGENTS

### ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY

The mechanism here is unusually well specified for this corpus, which is why the direction is credible even with the magnitudes soft. Resistance exercise raises mTORC1/p70S6K phosphorylation and recruits satellite cells; that signalling window is the adaptation. Cooling the trained tissue drives vasoconstriction, cuts perfusion, and blunts both the signalling and the satellite-cell response — Roberts measured reduced satellite cells at 24–48 h and blunted phosphorylation of anabolic kinases, alongside the +17% type II CSA gain that the cold group did not get. **Type II is the fibre population a 95 mph delivery is built from, and it is the one the histology says is affected.** That is a coherent story and I will defend it.

**Three places I will not let the coach overread it.**

**(1) Depth of cooling is the dose, and the tub is not the dose Roberts used.** Intramuscular temperature falls with immersion time, water temperature, and **tissue depth under subcutaneous fat**. Roberts cooled legs waist-deep after leg training. A 215 lb pitcher sitting chest-deep for 10 min at 12 °C is not cooling his pecs, lats or glutes to the depth a waist-deep leg protocol cools a quadriceps. **If the dose is depth, the whole-body subgroup being quieter is not a statistical accident — it may be the mechanism telling you the dose was smaller.** That is the strongest mechanistic argument available *against* my own headline, and it belongs to the biomechanist's dispute, not to me.

**(2) The pitching arm is not in the tub.** Nobody immerses a shoulder. The posterior cuff and forearm flexor-pronator mass — the tissues doing the work in a delivery — are above the water line in every protocol anyone actually uses. Everything in §3–§4 is about *legs and trunk*. That is still the force-production engine, so it matters; it is not the arm.

**(3) The acute impairment and the chronic cost are different physiology and should never be summed.** g = −0.68 on CMJ at 0 h is cold muscle and slowed nerve conduction; it is gone in hours and it is fully reversible. The chronic cost is a signalling deficit integrated across a mesocycle. **A pitcher who plunges on Friday has not lost anything on Saturday. The cost, if it is real, is counted in weeks.** Any version of this that says "the plunge cost him velocity that week" is wrong on the physiology.

### BIOMECHANIST

I will give the mechanism and then take the numbers apart, because the numbers are weaker than §3 reads on first pass.

**On the headline. A non-significant subgroup is not a different subgroup.** Grgic's whole-body row (−0.08, p = 0.743) and the limb-only row (−0.31, p = 0.041) were, as far as I can tell from an abstract, never compared *to each other*. A formal test of the subgroup difference is a different test, and in the absence of a significant interaction the correct statement is **"a smaller subgroup was underpowered"**, not **"whole-body immersion is different."** The intervals overlap heavily: [−0.61, −0.01] against [−0.53, +0.38]. **A difference of −0.23 between two estimates whose CIs overlap across most of their range is not a finding; it is a subgroup analysis doing what subgroup analyses do.** Today's whole headline is therefore **EMERGING at best, and it will stay there until somebody reads that methods section.** Dispute #61.

**On power, which is the quantity nobody states.** The pooled strength literature is **n = 170 across ten studies — a mean of 17 participants per study, across 22 groups.** By this corpus's own n ≥ 97 rule (F-519→F-532), *not one included study could detect anything smaller than a large effect*, and the pooled effect is −0.23, which is small. A pooled small effect assembled from a dozen underpowered trials is the exact object that bias correction has repeatedly driven to zero elsewhere in this registry — F-189's external-focus g = 0.26 → 0.01, F-190's self-controlled practice g = 0.44 → 0.107. **I cannot see a publication-bias assessment for either CWI meta-analysis, and in this literature that is not a footnote.** It is the single most informative missing number of the day.

**On the hypertrophy estimate.** −0.22 [−0.47, +0.04] **contains zero.** Piñero's Bayesian framing (p(<0) = 0.957) is a legitimate and more informative way to report it, and I accept the directional read — but note what p(<0) = 0.957 means: a 4.3% posterior probability that the true effect favours the ice bath. **That is not nothing in a literature with no bias assessment I can see.**

**On the one number I will fully endorse.** The *PeerJ* MVIC result, g = 0.08 [−0.11, +0.26] from 22 RCTs at moderate certainty, is a **narrow interval around zero** and it is the best-supported quantity in this entire cycle. **It is also the one that contradicts the actual reason athletes get in the tub.** The corpus should note that it arrived graded "moderate certainty" by the authors' own GRADE assessment, which is higher than almost anything else here.

**On what I would accept as settling Dispute #61:** one paragraph. Grgic's methods, reporting whether a between-subgroup test was run, with how many studies and participants sat in the whole-body cell. If the whole-body cell is three studies and forty men, the headline collapses into "underpowered" and §3 must be rewritten.

**And a check-4 note, because this corpus now runs one as a matter of course:** none of today's numbers is a geometric identity. These are independent constructs — a thermal intervention and a histological or strength outcome — with no shared terms. **Check 4 passes cleanly.** Check 1 fails everywhere (no velocity reported in any sample because no sample threw). Check 2 passes unusually well: **these are genuine INTERVENTIONS with control groups, which is rare in this registry and is the reason this cycle is worth having.** Check 3 is where the trouble is: the number measures fibre CSA and 1RM, and the sentence people want it to support is about mph.

### PITCHING COACH

Four things I can use and one I will not.

**What I can use.** This is the rarest thing in this vault: **a recommendation that costs nothing to implement and requires nothing of the athlete.** It is not a cue he has to feel, not a drill that has to survive a bullpen, not a mechanical change with a transition window. It is *moving an existing habit later in the day, or dropping it.* There is no adherence problem, no transfer lag, no re-learning cost, and **no way for it to make him worse** — the acute g = −0.68 on explosive output argues for *not* plunging before throwing, which points the same direction. When the downside of acting is zero and the mechanism is coherent, I do not need the effect size pinned to act.

**What I will not do.** I will not tell a pitcher the cold tub is costing him mph. I cannot price it, F-504 says nobody can, and a kid who hears "the plunge is why you are 91 and not 94" has been handed a story that will outlive the evidence. The honest sentence is in §7 and it is narrower than anyone wants.

**On the in-season exception, which is where I disagree with how the science reads.** During the season I am not buying hypertrophy. I am buying Saturday. If a pitcher throws Friday and we fly Saturday, a tub that makes him feel better and reactivates his parasympathetic system is not costing me an adaptation I was not purchasing that week. **The adaptation argument is an OFFSEASON and velocity-block argument.** The science does not say that — it has never looked at an in-season pitcher — but the periodisation logic is the right shape and §10's item 4 is the field reaching the same conclusion.

**On the thing that actually worries me.** Everything in §3–§6 is about *lifting*. My guys do not plunge after lifting. **They plunge after they throw.** Nobody has studied that — and the mechanism cuts differently there, because after an outing the adaptation I want is not hypertrophy, it is tissue recovery for the next start. §8 is the gap that matters to me and it is the one with nothing in it.

## 12. THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

**Q1 — "October. Fall ball is on and my guys are in a mass-and-strength block. Pitcher X, 89–91, 6'3", 195, needs fifteen pounds. He plunges after every lift because the older guys do. Do I stop him, and what do I actually say?"**

**Answer (anatomist):** Stop the *timing*, not the practice, and be straightforward about the strength of it. The signalling window you are protecting is the first hours after the lift; Roberts cooled within 5 minutes and that is the condition every pooled number describes. The cost is cumulative across the block, not per session — so this is a block-level housekeeping decision, not an emergency.

**Answer (biomechanist):** And say the magnitude honestly. **There is no mph figure. There is a −0.22 SMD on hypertrophy whose interval touches zero, and a −0.23 on strength driven by limb-only protocols.** Do not inflate it.

**→ So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Ice bath is not banned and it is not hurting your arm. It just does not go within a couple of hours of a lift — in that window your body is reading the lift and deciding how much muscle to build, and cold turns the volume down on that message. Lift, eat, then plunge later or tomorrow if you want it."*
**→ The drill is:** no drill. A **schedule change**: move the tub to a non-lifting window — after throwing, after practice, evening, or a non-lift day. Keep it available, because the soreness benefit is real and adherence to the hard parts of a block matters.
**→ On video the failure looks like:** nothing. **There is no video tell, and that is why this one is unusually honest and unusually easy — it costs no attention, no cue, no mechanical risk.**
**→ Here is how we know it is working:** **you do not measure this in mph and you must not try.** Measure the thing the intervention acts on: **body mass and lean mass weekly, same scale, same time of day, fasted, and the block's 1RM or 3RM.** Detection: the corpus has no within-athlete weekly mass SD for an in-season college pitcher (F-507 item 6, the sixteenth entry in that pattern) — so bracket it. At a plausible within-athlete weekly SD of 1.0–1.5 lb, detecting a 4 lb divergence from the expected block trajectory needs **roughly 6–8 weekly weigh-ins**, which is most of a block. **One week of weights proves nothing.** This is a decision you make on mechanism and re-examine at the end of the block, not one you validate in a fortnight.

**Q2 — "Is there any version of this where cold is the right call?"**

**Answer (biomechanist):** Three, and one is a trap.
1. **Congested schedule, adaptation not the goal.** Soreness and perceived-recovery benefits are the best-replicated effects in the topic. In-season, that is a legitimate purchase. **But know what you are not buying: strength restoration, g = 0.08, moderate certainty, a real null.**
2. **Never in the two hours before throwing or testing.** g = −0.68 on CMJ at 0 h. Cold muscle is slow muscle.
3. **The trap: the between-innings result.** F-360. 67–70 mph samples, n = 8 and n = 22, a post-hoc pain split, and a harm arm four times the benefit arm. **It is circulating in the industry with the magnitude quoted and the sample hidden. Do not act on it.**

**→ So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Cold is a tool for feeling better when the next outing is close. It is not a tool for getting stronger, and it is never a tool for the hour before you throw."*
**→ Here is how we know it is working:** for the in-season use the honest outcome is **subjective**, and that is fine — soreness and perceived recovery are what the evidence supports. Track them on a fixed 0–10 scale rather than inventing a performance claim. **Do not put a dynamometer on it and call it performance — that is the F-581/F-582 error exactly.**

**Q3 — "Everyone says wait four hours. Can I give my guys four hours as a rule?"**

**Answer (biomechanist):** You can give them a *heuristic* and you must not call it a finding. **Nobody has measured a delay.** Every pooled number is within-15-minutes by Piñero's own inclusion criterion. The circulating numbers are 4 h, 4–6 h, 8 h and 24–48 h, from four different sources, none of them an experiment — and **the existence of four competing "rules" is how you know none was measured.**

**Answer (anatomist):** Mechanistically, longer is safer and the 8 h figure from the 2026 *Exp Physiol* review is a defensible guess about when the acute signalling window has largely closed. **A guess from a review is still a guess.**

**→ So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Different hours away from the lift, and nobody actually knows which. Easiest version: plunge on days you did not lift, or at night."*
**→ Here is how we know it is working:** **we do not, and I am saying that out loud.** This is the cycle's cheapest unrun study and it is in §13's handoff.

**Q4 — "What if he says the plunge is why he sleeps and he is not giving it up?"**

**Answer (anatomist):** Then he keeps it, moved off the lift. **F-399→F-412 make sleep the highest-leverage recovery variable in this corpus by a distance**, and an unpriceable hypertrophy decrement is not worth trading against a sleep habit that works. The parasympathetic reactivation effect is real and is plausibly the route by which it helps him settle.

**Answer (coach):** And this is the right instinct generally. **Adherence to the block beats optimisation of the block.** The pitcher who lifts hard all winter because the tub makes the week tolerable beats the pitcher who quit in January with pristine mTOR signalling.

**→ So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"If it is how you come down at night, keep it — just not right after lifting."*
**→ Here is how we know it is working:** sleep duration and timing, which this corpus already knows how to track (F-399→F-412).

## 13. CROSS-EXAMINATION

**BIOMECHANIST → ANATOMIST + COACH.** *The claim:* "whole-body immersion looks safer for strength than limb-only." *Why I doubt it:* two overlapping subgroup intervals and no interaction test I can see, from an abstract. *What would settle it:* Grgic's methods and results section — whether a between-subgroup test was run, and the k and n of the whole-body cell. **ANATOMIST'S RESPONSE — CONCEDED, WITH A MECHANISTIC RIDER:** statistically you are right and I will not defend the subgroup as a finding. But there is an independent reason to expect the difference — **cooling dose is depth-dependent, and a whole-body dunk cools any given trained muscle less deeply than a dedicated limb immersion.** Fyfe 2019 found strength preserved under whole-body conditions in a second, independent design. **So: downgrade the statistical claim to EMERGING, keep the mechanistic expectation as a reason to look.** → **Dispute #61, OPEN.**

**ANATOMIST → COACH.** *The claim:* "there is no downside to acting, so act." *Why I doubt it:* there is one, and it is not physiological — **you are spending credibility.** If you tell a team to change a habit on the strength of a −0.22 whose interval touches zero, and next year a properly powered trial reports a true null, you have taught a clubhouse that your recommendations are fashion. **COACH'S RESPONSE — PARTIALLY CONCEDED:** fair, and it is why the sentence in §12 Q1 is *"this is not hurting your arm, it just does not go next to a lift"* rather than *"stop icing."* **A schedule nudge framed as a schedule nudge spends almost nothing.** But I defend acting: when the implementation cost is zero and the mechanism is coherent, waiting for a definitive trial in a literature that has produced none in a decade is not caution, it is paralysis. **OPEN as a calibration question.**

**COACH → BOTH.** *The claim:* that §3–§6 tells me anything about my pitchers. *Why I doubt it:* **every number in this cycle comes from somebody who lifted weights and then got in a tub. My pitchers get in the tub after they throw.** There is no resistance-training session in that sequence, no hypertrophy stimulus to blunt, and the adaptation at stake is tissue recovery for day five — a different biology with a different outcome. **Why are we confident this literature even addresses the behaviour we are trying to change?** **BIOMECHANIST'S RESPONSE — CONCEDED, AND IT IS THE BEST CHALLENGE OF THE DAY:** it does not address it. **It is directly applicable to the post-LIFT plunge and it is silent on the post-OUTING plunge.** The only post-outing pitching trial in existence is F-581 and it measured a dynamometer. **I accept the scope limit and log it as the cycle's headline gap (F-611) and as Dispute #62.** → **Dispute #62, OPEN.**

**COACH → BIOMECHANIST, second.** *The claim:* that the acute CMJ decrement matters to me. *Why I doubt it:* **nobody plunges before pitching.** A −0.68 at 0 h is a laboratory artefact of a behaviour no one performs. **BIOMECHANIST — DEFENDED:** mostly agreed on the practical point, with one real exception: **a cold tub between a lift and a throwing session on a two-a-day fall schedule is exactly that behaviour**, and that sequence does happen in college programs in October. The finding earns its place as a scheduling constraint even if it never earns one as a performance claim.

## 14. WHAT TO DO THIS WEEK

**One thing, for an 89–91 mph college arm in an October mass-and-strength block.**

**Move the cold tub off the lift.** Not banned — **moved.** Nothing within roughly two hours after resistance training; park it on non-lifting days or in the evening. Keep it available for soreness and for sleep, because those are the benefits that are actually supported.
