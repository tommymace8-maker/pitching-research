## 1. THE RESULT OF THE DAY — both sides of this argument have a broken citation

### 1a. The case against distance running (F-683)

The modern consensus traces, in public circulation, to **one 1992 study**: Potteiger, Williford, Blessing & Smidt, vol. 6:2–6 — journal given as *J Appl Sport Sci Res* by one citing source and *J Strength Cond Res* by others.

**Three mutually incompatible descriptions of the same paper:**

| Description | Source |
|---|---|
| "21 male NAIA collegiate baseball **players**," 4 ×/wk, 1-h sessions | UNLV 2023 thesis |
| "a collegiate **pitching staff** in two groups of **eight**," over a season; sprint arm 10–30 × 15–60 m, 10–60 s rest, 3 d/wk | ericcressey.com |
| "**18 Division II pitchers**" | third-party relay — **a CONFLATION** with a *different* study (battling ropes vs interval/sprint running, JASC, which found **no** effect on peak velocity or stamina) |

**And the magnitudes in circulation — +3.0% throwing velocity, +4.2% anaerobic power — trace to TopVelocity (2023-08-16), a site selling a velocity program.** They do **not** appear in the Cressey article, which reports only *"a significant improvement (p<0.05) in the pitching velocity and anaerobic power measures of the sprint groups."*

**The design kills it regardless of the sample.** The arms were **(weight training + sprints)** versus **AEROBIC DANCE**. No isolated endurance arm. No untrained control. **So the endurance variable was never isolated, and nobody in the study ran poles.**

⚠️ **Two re-import vectors logged:** the 3.0%/4.2% pair, and the "18 DII pitchers" descriptor. Neither may enter this corpus as a magnitude. This is precisely the failure mode the program's correction convention exists for.

### 1b. The case FOR an aerobic base (F-687)

**The only confirmed-elite object in the whole topic, and it is the cross-sectional one.** *Sports* 4(1):14 (2016), PMID 29910262: **n = 24 MLB pitchers (14 SP / 10 RP), seven seasons 2007–2013**, estimated VO2max (modified Bruce + CO₂ analyser). Starters above vs below group-mean VO2max differed on performance; VO2max correlated with strikeouts, ERA, WHIP; effect specific to **starters**. Via a citing thesis: **SP 49.49 ± 4.59, RP 45.28 ± 5.72 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹**, no AT difference. A second organisation reports pitcher mean **53.64** — **the two means disagree by more than an SD, so no population norm can be set.**

**CROSS_SECTIONAL. MARKER, NOT LEVER. Three independent inflation routes:**
1. **Median split** — 7 vs 7 starters, split on a between-subject variable that tracks body composition, age, role and training history.
2. **Multiplicity** — "many pitching metrics" against VO2max at n = 24, no correction retrieved.
3. **Survivorship** — seven seasons of panel data in which good pitchers keep reappearing. The arrow plausibly runs **performance → fitness**, or both from a third thing (health, years in a system).

**Never phrase this as an instruction.**

## 2. THE ONLY REAL EXPERIMENT EVER RUN ON PITCHERS, AND IT IS A NULL IN BOTH DIRECTIONS (F-684)

**Rabena & Moran (2019), *Int J Exerc Sci* 12(6):726–734.** Randomised, **n = 13 collegiate pitchers**, 11 weeks, **in season**, steady-state exercise vs interval training, pre/post.

| Outcome | p |
|---|---|
| One-mile run time (favouring steady state) | **0.007** |
| 30-m sprint | 0.15 |
| **Pitching velocity** | **0.25** |
| WHIP | 0.75 |
| Fatigue index | 0.79 |
| Soreness / readiness | 0.52 |

**CAUSALITY: MANIPULATED_NULL** — randomised, controlled, variable manipulated, outcome measured. One of very few INTERVENTION-grade objects in the topic.

**Read it carefully, because it cuts both ways.** Against the traditionalist: steady state bought no stamina — WHIP and fatigue index flat. Against the sprint-only camp: **steady state cost no speed — 30-m sprint p = 0.15 — the one direct test of "distance running makes a pitcher slow," in pitchers, and it failed to show the effect.**

⚠️ **AND THE HONEST CAVEAT, WHICH IS LARGER THAN THE RESULT.** At **n = 13** this could not have detected anything a program cares about. **F-681 prices 1 mph of mean velocity between groups at ~106 pitchers.** Thirteen is not an eighth of that. The correct reading is **not** "modality doesn't matter" — it is "the one real experiment was far too small to answer, and nobody has run a bigger one." ⚠️ **No effect sizes or CIs retrieved, only p-values; per standing rule, no design is inferred from a p-value.** ⚠️ **No sample mean velocity retrieved — the single most important missing number.**

## 3. WHAT THE GENERAL LITERATURE ACTUALLY SHOWS — the effect is real, small, and lands in the one column we sell (F-685)

**Schumann, Feuerbacher, Sünkeler, Freitag, Rønnestad, Doma, Lundberg (2022), *Sports Medicine* 52:601–612, DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7. 43 studies, n = 1,090.**

| Outcome | Pooled SMD | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal strength | ≈ −0.06 | 37 |
| Hypertrophy | ≈ −0.01 | 15 |
| **Explosive strength** | **−0.28, p = 0.007** | 18 |

⚠️ **The three coefficients were read from review summaries of the paper, NOT from the paper's own abstract. Author/journal/volume verified as existing; the numbers are NOT source-verified.** Corroborating direction from an independent updated SportRxiv meta-analysis: no interference with maximal strength or hypertrophy irrespective of modality, type, order, frequency, age or training status; explosive strength still reduced, worse in one session. **Outvoted and older:** Wilson 2012 (PMID 22002517) — hypertrophy ES 1.23 vs 0.85, strength 1.76 vs 1.44.

**This reframes the argument AGAINST BOTH CAMPS.** The traditionalist is wrong that aerobic work is free: the one column with a signal is explosive output. The sprint-only coach is wrong about the size: **−0.28 is small, and it is an SMD on jump and RFD tests in general trainees, not mph off a mound.**

⚠️ **DO NOT CONVERT −0.28 INTO MPH.** The corpus holds no transfer coefficient from a jump/RFD SMD to ball velocity, and **F-438 → F-449 is why: the jump–velocity link in pitchers is r ≈ 0.07 at n = 33.**

⚠️ **SAMPLE MISMATCH, TOTAL: 43 studies, zero throwing athletes, zero throwing outcomes.** When someone cites the interference effect at a pitcher, they are citing jump tests in general trainees.

## 4. THE ONE SENTENCE TODAY EARNS, AND IT IS A CALENDAR CHANGE (F-690)

Every candidate moderator failed across both pooled analyses — **modality, exercise type, exercise order, frequency, age, training status** — except one: **attenuation is more pronounced when aerobic and strength training are performed in the SAME SESSION.**

⚠️ **The separation intervals in circulation are UNSOURCED.** Secondary blogs give "worst under 3 hours" and "separate by 6+ hours"; **the two disagree with each other and neither was traceable to a primary.** Registrable: *same session worse than different sessions.* Nothing finer.

⚠️ **And it is still a between-study subgroup contrast.** Nobody has randomised the same athletes to same-session vs separated and measured explosive output. **That trial does not exist in any population.**

⭐ **STRUCTURAL NOTE: this is the fifth time the corpus has found a topic's only actionable output to be a SCHEDULING decision rather than a training decision** — after the between-start week, the between-inning break, the warm-up interval (F-419) and nap timing (F-646→). ## 6. THE RECOVERY MECHANISM — live, unmeasured, and it predicts the wrong outcome from what everyone tests (F-688)

**The mechanism is real** (F-132, untouched today): pitch ~1.5–2 s, high-velocity portion ~0.05–0.15 s, ~15–25 s between pitches, several minutes between innings; **PCr fast phase ~20–30 s, near-complete ~3–5 min**, rate governed by oxidative capacity.

**But:**
- **No PCr or ³¹P-MRS recovery measurement of a pitcher exists, at any level.** Nobody has shown PCr is incompletely restored at any point in an outing.
- **F-676 removed the indirect support.** In-game HR = **84.8 ± 3.9% of age-predicted HRmax** in 16 pro starters over 682 innings, in an **alactic** task. If HR that high accompanies an alactic task, HR reads **arousal**, not metabolic demand — so nobody has ever shown a pitcher approaching a metabolic ceiling. (And no **measured** HRmax exists in any pitcher study, so 84.8% is a percentage of an estimate.)
- **The timing arithmetic cuts the wrong way: 3–5 min restores PCr near-completely, and a half-inning is usually longer than that.** The mechanism's bite, if it has one, is **between pitches at 15–25 s** — inside the fast phase — which makes **the pitch clock**, not the conditioning program, the live variable.

**Critically: the mechanism does not predict more velocity. It predicts less velocity DECAY.** Which is the column nobody looked in — and that is the whole of §7.

## 7. ⭐⭐ THE CHEAPEST UNRUN TEST IN THE TOPIC — SWAP THE OUTCOME, NOT THE TRAINING (F-689)

Every conditioning study in pitchers chose an endpoint it could not see, got a null, and read the null as an answer. **F-289 already priced the endpoint the mechanism actually predicts.**

Fastballs needed **per bucket** to detect a within-outing drop (two-sample, α = .05, 80% power, SD = 1.0 mph):

| Drop | Fastballs / bucket |
|---|---|
| 0.5 mph | 63 |
| **1.0 mph** | **16** |
| 1.5 mph | 7 |
| 2.0 mph | 4 |

(At SD = 0.8 mph, a 1.0 mph drop needs **10**.)

**~16 fastballs in innings 1–2 and ~16 in innings 5–6 — a Friday starter supplies that with no special effort. Against ~106 pitchers for 1 mph of MEAN velocity (F-681). Roughly an order of magnitude cheaper, and it is the right endpoint.**

**THE DESIGN.** Within-athlete. Decay = (innings 1–2 fastball mean) − (innings 5–6 fastball mean), fastballs only, ≥16 per bucket, across **4–6 starts before** and **4–6 starts after** a conditioning block. Each arm is its own control.

⚠️ **TWO HONEST LIMITS, STATED NOT HEDGED.**
1. **The 1.0 mph within-outing SD is a CORPUS ASSUMPTION** and has never been published for an 85+ arm. **Measure it on the individual arm from the existing radar log first — it is the first number, not an input.**
2. **F-289 prices detecting the DECAY, not detecting a CHANGE IN the decay across a block**, which needs the between-start SD of the decay itself — a quantity the corpus does not hold.

⚠️ **Do not run it as a between-subject correlation across a 12-man staff.** Restriction of range (Luera 2020, n = 149 pros, r = .17–.29) plus n = 12 makes that hopeless.

## 11. WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK WITH AN 85+ ARM

**Do this one thing: pull the within-outing fastball SD for each starter off the radar log you already have, then stop bolting conditioning onto the end of the lift.**

- **What:** For each starter, from the last 4–6 starts, compute mean fastball velocity in innings 1–2 and in innings 5–6, and the **SD within each bucket**. That is a query, not a session.
- **Why that and not a conditioning change:** because the SD is the number that tells you whether you can see anything at all. If his within-bucket SD is 1.0 mph, a **1.0 mph** decay is visible in **16 fastballs per bucket** — one start. If it is 1.5 mph, you need **~35** and you are looking at three starts minimum before any claim.
- **How you'd know it worked:** you would have, for the first time, a per-arm decay number with an error bar on it. **That is the measurement this corpus has been missing in six topics, and it is free.**
- **The second thing, which costs only a calendar change:** separate conditioning from lifting into different sessions. Supported by the only surviving moderator in the pooled literature (F-690). ⚠️ **Do not claim a specific interval — "3 hours" and "6 hours" are both unsourced.**
- ⚠️ **What NOT to do: do not change anyone's conditioning modality this week and expect to see it.** 1 mph of mean velocity needs ~106 pitchers between groups. You do not have 106 pitchers, and neither did anyone who published on this.
