## 2. 🚨 CORRECTION #1 — THE GRIP-STRENGTH NULL MEASURED ELBOW TORQUE (F-465, F-466)

**F-013 as written:** *"No significant univariate association exists between any grip-strength variable and **ball velocity** in D1 pitchers."* Graded **ESTABLISHED**, **CONFIDENCE: high**, *"the closest sample-to-target match of any cross-sectional study in the corpus."*

**✅ SOURCE-VERIFIED — Barrack et al. 2024 read in full (PMC11590131).** The paper is titled *"Investigating the Influence of Modifiable Physical Measures on the **Elbow Varus Torque – Ball Velocity Relationship**."* Its dependent variable is **elbow varus torque**. Ball velocity is a **covariate**. Its only grip sentence:

> *"No grip strength variables were significantly associated with **EVT** in univariate analysis."*

**The population was right — n = 87 NCAA D1, 37.87 ± 1.55 m/s = 84.83 ± 3.47 mph, genuinely the best match in the registry. The sample size was right. The PMID was right. The dependent variable was wrong.**

Also read at source: **57 modifiable variables entered univariately, no multiplicity correction stated**; a-priori power targeted **R² ≥ 0.16 with 5 predictors, minimum n = 80** — a large effect *on EVT*, never aimed at velocity.

### The positive result hiding inside the null (F-466)
**Grip strength SYMMETRY predicted INCREASED elbow varus torque: +0.27 N·m per +1 N asymmetry, 95% CI [0.07, 0.48], P = .008**, controlling for velocity. Individual-arm grip entered neither the univariate analysis nor the final model. Authors: *"The relevance of grip strength on the nondominant arm is unclear, making interpretation difficult."*

⚠️ **Graded low-medium and treated with suspicion proportional to its provenance:** backward elimination from a 27-variable pool reduced from 57, CI lower bound 0.07, and an asymmetry term entering when neither component does is more likely a reduction artifact than a tissue fact. **A screen, not a program. Emphatically not a reason to train the glove hand.**

## 3. 🚨 CORRECTION #2 — THE FLAGSHIP STRIDE NULL NEVER TESTED THE CONDITION ITS CLAIM NAMES (F-467 → F-469)

F-043 / F-044 / F-045 is item 2 in `INDEX.md`'s **"If you only read five things."**

### 3.1 What F-044 said, and what the study did
**F-044:** *"pitchers threw at **±25% of self-selected stride** and hand and ball velocity were **equivalent across conditions**."*

**The Buffalo crossover ran TWO game conditions: over-stride and under-stride.** Desired stride was measured **in warm-up** and never pitched. **✅ Verified twice, independently, this cycle:**

1. **Crotin & Ramsey 2021** (PMC8486408 — the *same* n = 19 cohort, read in full), as an explicit limitation:
   > *"The disadvantage of this methodological design was the inability for comparing desired stride length data with the ± 25% stride conditions."*
   > *"Responses seen between stride length extremes also cannot infer changes occurring from desired stride length."*
2. **Matsuda et al. 2025** (PMC12011807, read in full), citing it as reference 13:
   > *"there was no significant difference in ball velocity **between the over-stride length and under-stride length conditions**."*

Stride values: **OS 1.40 ± 0.15 m (0.76 %BH); US 0.95 ± 0.14 m (0.52 %BH); desired 1.24 ± 0.17 m.**

### 3.2 ✅ The midpoint, supplied — F-045 source-verified in full (F-468)

| Condition | Stride (m) | %BH | Ball velocity (m/s) | vs NS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under-stride | 1.08 ± 0.13 | 62.52 ± 6.99 | **32.48 ± 1.72** | −1.42, **p = 0.03, d = 0.78** |
| **Normal** | 1.35 ± 0.12 | **78.20 ± 5.70** | **33.90 ± 1.86** | — |
| Over-stride | 1.56 ± 0.13 | 89.86 ± 6.58 | **32.48 ± 1.70** | −1.42, **p = 0.03, d = 0.79** |

**ANOVA p < 0.01, η² = 0.36 (large). US vs OS p = 1.00, d < 0.01. The penalty is 1.42 m/s = 3.18 mph in BOTH directions, identical to two decimals.**
**Power: MDE at n = 20 is d = 0.660; observed 0.79 → 91.8% achieved. Adequately powered for what it found — rare here.**

⚠️ **Constraints read at source and NOT previously recorded in F-045:** **flat ground, indoor laboratory, thrown to a target not a catcher**; sample **173.2 cm / 71.8 kg**, NS velocity **75.8 mph** — nine mph below the floor. **SAMPLE MISMATCH, directional only.**

### 3.3 The synthesis (F-469) — one curve seen twice
Both studies found **the extremes indistinguishable from each other.** Only one had the middle point, and it sits **3.2 mph above both.** **Stride length is an inverted U with a sharp vertex at the self-selected value.**

**The instruction is unchanged. The reason has inverted:** not *"stride doesn't matter"* but *"**it matters, symmetrically, and he is already at the top.**"*

⚠️ **F-048 is untouched.** *"A shorter stride cut heart rate 11.1 bpm at no velocity cost"* is an **OS-vs-US** comparison — the comparison that study *can* make. **Stride as a stamina trade survives intact.**

### 3.4 The mechanism, and the hole in it (F-472)
Matsuda's actual purpose was energy flow:
- **Total energy outflow, lower torso → trunk, stride + arm-cocking: p = 0.59, η² = 0.02 (negligible).**
- Inputs moved hard: lower-torso energy at SFC **OS > US d = 1.23**; at MER **US > NS d = 1.65**; stride-hip negative work **US < OS d = 2.66**; trunk positive work **OS > US d = 2.85**.

**Stride changed the TIMING of delivery to the trunk, not the TOTAL.** Authors: *"implying difficulties in explaining ball velocity only by the lower extremity mechanics."*

> ⚠️ **THE LOOP IS NOT CLOSED AND THIS CYCLE DECLINES TO CLOSE IT.** If trunk inflow was invariant, **this model does not explain the 3.2 mph.** The effect is real and its mechanism is unaccounted for.

## 5. NEW FROM THE FIELD, SOURCE-VERIFIED — grip as a fatigue instrument (F-474, F-475)

**Tremblay et al. 2025** (PMC11877241, *BMJ Open Sport Exerc Med*, read in full). Simulated 75-pitch outing, 5 × 15, 5 min between blocks, Rapsodo 2.0, turf mound.

| Measure | Pre | Block 5 | Change | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grip, dominant** | 55.67 ± 12.32 kg | 48.62 ± 12.25 | **−12.66%** | **p < 0.001**, ηp² = 0.299 |
| **Grip, NON-dominant** | 53.81 ± 12.08 kg | 49.94 ± 11.86 | **−7.19%** | p = 0.073 *(linear trend p = 0.029)* |
| **Velocity** | 119.87 ± 8.00 km/h | 118.75 ± 6.90 | **−0.93%** | p = 0.039, ηp² = 0.142 |
| Soreness, forearm flexors | 1.65 ± 1.16 | 4.19 ± 2.02 | +25.4% | p = 0.005 |
| Pressure pain threshold | — | — | **nothing significant anywhere** | flexors p = 0.060 |

**Velocity was NOT monotonic** — block 2 (120.35) exceeded block 1 before declining. Converges with **Crotin & Ramsey 2021** read the same cycle: **−1.9 kg / 45.1 kg = −4.2% over 80 pitches, p = 0.017, d = 0.28.**

### 5.2 🚨 The unintended control, and the discount (F-475)
**The non-dominant arm also lost grip — 7.19% — and it threw zero pitches.** It held a glove and performed **six maximal 3-second isometric grip efforts**, exactly as the dominant arm did.

Authors offer **cross-education** and **glove weight**. The parsimonious alternative — **a repeated maximal isometric test is itself fatiguing** — is never considered, and **no no-pitch control session exists** to separate them.

> **Throwing-attributable decline ≈ 12.66 − 7.19 = 5.5 pp, not 12.66.**
> **Corrected responsiveness ratio ≈ 5.9 : 1, not 13.6 : 1.**

Still ~6× better than radar, which is provably unresponsive (**F-082**: in-season velocity *rises*). **But a program using dominant-hand grip loss as a pull threshold is over-reading it by roughly 2×.**

### 5.3 What it is not
Authors' own limitation:
> *"This study did not directly evaluate the effects of a gradual decline in grip strength, and we were, therefore, unable to evaluate the relationship between the decrease in grip strength, muscle soreness perception and pitch count."*

**Nothing links grip decline to any outcome you care about. A responsive instrument with no validated criterion.** There is **no evidence anywhere** for a grip-loss threshold that should remove a pitcher from a game.

### 5.4 ⚠️ Population
**n = 30 recruited / 26 analysed, age 21.43 ± 8.86, RANGE 13–50**, mean velocity **74.5 mph**, pitch count from a **13U federation guideline**, indoors, to a nine-pocket net. **SAMPLE MISMATCH — directional only. No magnitude here is a norm.**

## 6. CROSS-EXAMINATION

**① BIOMECHANIST → COACH. "Your inverted-U is confounded and you can't see it." (F-473, Dispute #40)**
*The claim:* the self-selected stride is a mechanical optimum.
*Why I doubt it:* **in every instructed-stride design, the normal condition is the only one without an instruction attached.** NS beating both extremes by an *identical* margin is equally predicted by "both instructed conditions cost the same attentional tax." **F-192 — guidance hypothesis, k = 75, N = 2,228, PASSES n ≥ 97** — says directing attention to the body degrades skilled output. And **F-472 helps me**: if total energy to the trunk was invariant, the mechanical story has no obvious channel for 3.2 mph and the attentional story needs none.
*What would settle it:* **a sham-instruction condition** — tell him to hit a target equal to his own normal stride. One extra condition, ~20 pitches. **No study in the literature has it.**
**COACH: CONCEDED, on the sentence.** I can say *"instructing a stride change costs ~3 mph acutely."* I cannot say *"his stride is optimal."* **What I do this week is unchanged under either account.**
**ANATOMIST, partial defence:** the two accounts diverge on **time course** — a mechanical optimum persists; an attentional tax decays with practice. **Nobody has run a stride manipulation longer than a session**, so this cannot currently discriminate.

**② BIOMECHANIST → THE PAPER. "Your fatigue instrument fatigues the athlete." (F-475)**
*The claim:* grip strength is a better acute-fatigue instrument than velocity.
*Why I doubt the size:* **the arm that didn't throw lost 7.19%.** Six maximal isometric efforts is a training stimulus, not a passive reading, and there is no no-pitch control.
**ANATOMIST, defending:** cross-education is real and the glove arm does work. **NOT DISMISSED** — the study cannot separate them and neither can we. **Both agree the DIRECTION survives and the MAGNITUDE halves.** Practical output is the same either way: **test both hands, track the gap.**

**③ COACH → BIOMECHANIST. "You just told me two of my nulls are wrong. How many more?"**
*The claim:* the audit is done.
*Why I doubt it:* **you read two papers and found an error in both.** Type A is mechanised — fine. **Types B and C need a paper read each and there are ~55 to go.**
**BIOMECHANIST: CONCEDED, and it is written into the file as §8.** 2-for-2 on a *selected* pair is not a rate. **The audit is begun, not finished**, and saying otherwise would be the manufactured-consensus failure state.

**④ COACH → BOTH. "Does anything change on my mound Monday?"**
*Answer, honestly: almost nothing, and one thing.*
- **Stride:** unchanged — don't touch it. The *reason* changed and the reason is what you say to a pitcher who asks.
- **Grip trainers:** unchanged — still no. But **"it's null" is now "nobody tested it,"** and if a player asks you must say the second thing.
- **THE ONE CHANGE:** if you are already using grip dynamometry as a fatigue monitor, **you are reading it about 2× too hot, and the fix is to test the glove hand too.**

**⑤ BIOMECHANIST → THE CORPUS'S OWN PROPOSAL. (F-476, Dispute #41)**
**F-449** proposes measuring the within-athlete impulse→velocity slope from ~85 paired observations over a fall block. **A 2025 in-game study of 183 college pitchers reportedly finds the intrapitcher kinetics/velocity relationship neither strong nor stable across outings.** If so, pooling across outings estimates **a slope that may not exist as a stable quantity.**
**DEFENCE:** ⚠️ **it is a snippet — nothing read at source, no ICC, no n per pitcher, no sample velocity**, and this corpus has been burned three times by search-summary corruption. Also a different quantity (arm torque vs ground impulse).
**BUT THE DEFENCE IS WEAK WHERE IT MATTERS:** nobody has shown the impulse slope IS stable either, and **the burden sits with the untested proposal.**
**RESOLUTION, adopted regardless of whether the paper is ever read:** **F-449's protocol should fit the slope PER OUTING as well as pooled, and report the between-outing variance of the slope.** If per-outing slopes scatter, the pooled slope is an artifact — **and you learn it from data you were already collecting, at zero extra cost.**

## 8. WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO THIS WEEK WITH AN 85+ ARM

**The one change: if you use grip dynamometry as a fatigue monitor, test BOTH hands and track the DIFFERENCE.**

- **What:** bilateral Jamar, 3 × 3 s per hand, pre-outing and immediately post-outing. Record **dominant − non-dominant**, not the dominant value.
- **Why:** the non-dominant arm is a free within-session control for everything that is not throwing — test reactivity, time-on-task, arousal, hydration. **In the only study that measured it, the non-throwing arm accounted for 7.19 of a 12.66 pp "fatigue" signal (F-475).**
- **Failure mode on video:** none — this is invisible on video, which is the point. It is an instrument for what the camera and the radar gun both miss.
- **How you'd know it's working:** ⚠️ **you would not, yet, and that is the honest answer.** There is **no validated grip-loss threshold for removing a pitcher**, and the authors of the only two studies both call establishing one future work. **What you are building this fall is the baseline distribution you do not have:** the bilateral gap, pre and post, for each arm on your staff.
- **Detection arithmetic:** to detect a 2 kg within-athlete post-outing change against a test-retest SD of ~2.5 kg at 80% power you need **~15 paired outings per pitcher**. **One outing tells you nothing. A season tells you the shape of his curve.**
- ⚠️ **Do NOT set a pull threshold off this.** Nothing links grip decline to any performance or injury outcome. You are collecting, not deciding.

**Everything else this week is unchanged, and two sentences changed:**
- **Stride:** still don't touch it — now because *"20% either way cost 3.2 mph in the only study that checked,"* not because *"stride doesn't matter."*
- **Grip trainers:** still no — but if a player asks, the true sentence is **"nobody has tested it,"** not **"it's been shown not to work."**
