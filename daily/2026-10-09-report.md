## 2. ANATOMY–PHYSIOLOGY PASS — DOES THIS WORK GIVEN THE TISSUE?

### 2.1 The adaptation is real and it is a *material* change, not a *size* change

The pooled intervention evidence is coherent and it is not folklore.

**Bohm, Mersmann & Arampatzis (2015), *Sports Med Open* 1:7, DOI 10.1186/s40798-015-0009-9.** Meta-analysis, **27 studies, 37 interventions, 264 participants**. Weighted effects:

| Property | ES | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Tendon stiffness | **0.70** | 0.51, 0.88 |
| Young's modulus | **0.69** | 0.36, 1.03 |
| Cross-sectional area | **0.24** | 0.07, 0.42 |

Their conclusion: adaptation is *"more attributed to adaptations of the material rather than morphological properties,"* and **loading magnitude** is the key variable; effective interventions use high intensity over **>12 weeks**.

**Lazarczuk SL, Maniar N, Opar DA, Duhig SJ, Shield A, Barrett RS et al. (2022), *Sports Med* 52(10):2405–2429, DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01695-y** (PROSPERO CRD42019141299) replicates the shape: high-strain > low-strain, stiffness gains driven by **modulus, not CSA**, resistance training the most effective modality, **concurrent training SMD 0.03 [−1.03, 1.10]** (two intervention groups — a null with no power).

**Mechanism, verdict: ESTABLISHED.** A tendon's stiffness is trainable, the route is high mechanical strain, and the change is in the material.

⚠️ **But note the arithmetic nobody quotes: 37 interventions across 264 participants is ~7.1 participants per intervention** (F-629). The pooled ES is real; every brick in it is tiny.

### 2.2 ⭐⭐ AND THEN IT DISAPPEARS IN TRAINED ATHLETES — THE RESULT OF THE DAY

Lazarczuk's own limitations paragraph is the most important sentence retrieved today:

> *"only three studies recruited well-trained athletes, including highly trained distance runners, female endurance athletes, and strength-trained males, all of which displayed no change in PT stiffness even following high-intensity training"* — and *"caution should be applied when directly applying the findings of this review to highly trained populations."*

Three independent corroborations landed in the same sweep:

1. **Professional athletes, cross-sectional shear-wave study** (*Diagnostics* 2023, 13(15):2541, DOI 10.3390/diagnostics13152541, PMC10417388): *"PT load exercise and strength training have not shown an effect on stiffness properties in athletic individuals."*
2. **Adolescent handball players, 2021 functional high-load intervention** (PMC7987778): pain fell; there were *"no significant changes of tendon stiffness or maximum tendon strain in either group."* The clinical benefit did not come through stiffness.
3. **Professional female soccer, the PLACEBO arm** of Lee et al. 2025 (below): 10 weeks of pre-season **high-intensity** resistance training + plyometrics + pitch work moved patellar tendon stiffness **+4.6% (d = 0.32)**.

And every large published number in this literature comes from the opposite end of the training-status scale:

| Number | Population |
|---|---|
| +57% stiffness (67.5 → 106.2 N/mm), +36% rate of torque development — **Kubo et al. (2001), *J Appl Physiol* 91(1):26–32** | untrained young men |
| +57.7% stiffness at ~80% 1RM (0% at ~40%) | **older adults** |
| "up to 61.6%" — **Oranchuk, Storey, Nelson & Cronin (2019), *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 29(4):484–503, DOI 10.1111/sms.13375** | single-study maximum, mixed/untrained |
| +51% / +66% gastrocnemius tendon stiffness, 6 wk | **n = 11 per group**, recreational |
| +20.1 ± 20.5% high-load, 12 wk | Achilles **tendinopathy patients**, n = 15/arm |

🚨 **THE STRUCTURAL FINDING (F-630, F-631): THIS CORPUS'S RESTRICTION-OF-RANGE RULE APPLIES TO ADAPTATION MAGNITUDES, NOT ONLY TO CORRELATIONS.**

The vault has enforced one version of this since Luera 2020 (n = 149 pros, r = .17–.29): *in an all-hard-throwing sample, correlations collapse; published mixed-level coefficients are inflated relative to our population.* Today the same structure appears in a **training-effect** literature: in a heavily pre-loaded population the *adaptation* collapses, and published mixed-population effect sizes are inflated relative to ours.

A pitcher who already throws 85+ has spent years applying high strain to his Achilles and patellar tendons through sprinting, jumping, lifting and ~1.5× bodyweight of braking per pitch. **He is the "well-trained athlete" in whom the effect is measured at zero to +5%.** Every impressive figure in the table above was earned by someone unlike him.

⚠️ Honest limit on this: Lazarczuk's caveat rests on **three studies**, none of them pitchers, and a three-study subgroup is itself a weak basis. The direction is corroborated three further ways; the *magnitude* of the attenuation is not established. It is EMERGING, not ESTABLISHED.

### 2.3 ⭐ The shoulder has no route at all — F-064's spring is untrainable *as far as anyone has measured*

This is the anatomist's hardest point, and it is a gap, not a result.

- **Zero trials, in any population, report a tendon-stiffness outcome for the rotator cuff or the long head of the biceps.** Searched: the entire retrievable rotator-cuff loading literature (RoCTEx progressive high-load protocol PMC4318133, PASE trial PMC10880378, NCT01984203, NCT04073212) reports **DASH, pain and function** — never stiffness. Closest object in existence: **NCT05969652**, heavy slow resistance vs eccentric in rotator cuff tendinopathy, which lists *supraspinatus tendon structure* among its outcomes. **No results posted.** (F-636)
- **No in-vivo strain-energy figure exists for the rotator cuff**, and therefore no comparison with the Achilles is possible. The only mechanical rotator-cuff measurement retrieved is a compliance-validation study (Hatta et al. 2014, *J Ultrasound Med* 33(9):1641, tendon/reference strain ratio vs compliance r = 0.73, P < .01) — the instrument works; it quantifies nothing about energy.
- Every modality ranked for stiffness adaptation is **lower-limb**: Achilles and patellar. Those are long, series-elastic, high-strain-energy tendons. The cuff tendons are short and broad.

**So: the one place the corpus has a documented elastic mechanism (the shoulder, F-064) is the one place with no adaptation literature; and the places with a rich adaptation literature (Achilles, patellar) are not where F-064's −201 J is stored.** Nobody has bridged this, and the gap was not previously recorded.

⚠️ **AND F-064'S OWN PREMISE IS SOFTER THAN THE REGISTRY HAS BEEN TREATING IT.** −201 J of negative work at the shoulder during cocking is **not** evidence that the storage element is *tendon*. Capsule, ligament and eccentrically-loaded muscle all absorb negative work and none of them is addressed by a tendon-stiffness protocol. F-064 already carries the population flag (~62 mph, 10 m target throws, not off a mound); the *tissue attribution* flag is new and is registered today (F-636).

## 3. BIOMECHANIST PASS — ATTACKING THE DATA

### 3.1 The stiffness → RFD link is CORRELATIONAL. It has never been shown causal.

The whole commercial argument is a two-step: *isometrics raise tendon stiffness* → *stiffer tendon transmits force faster* → *higher RFD* → *higher velocity*. Step one is an intervention. **Step two is a correlation, and the people who study RFD say so in print.**

**Maffiuletti NA, Aagaard P, Blazevich AJ, Folland J, Tillin N, Duchateau J (2016), "Rate of force development: physiological and methodological considerations," *Eur J Appl Physiol*, DOI 10.1007/s00421-016-3346-6:**

- *"existing data are inconsistent and a causative relationship has not been clearly demonstrated."*
- Achilles stiffness vs RFD: **r² = 0.29 – 0.90, depending on the time interval to which RFD was computed.** ⚠️ A relationship that ranges from 29% to 90% of variance explained **as a function of an analyst-chosen time window** is not one magnitude; it is a family of magnitudes, and a vendor will quote the top of it.
- ⭐⭐ **THE NUMBER THAT PRICES THE WHOLE TOPIC:** *"changes in tendon stiffness induced by strength training (typically <50%) are small when compared to the variability observed within a population (~500%)."*
- **Direct counterexample:** a bed-rest detraining study found reductions in vastus lateralis tendon stiffness **not correlated** with the decline in RFD (n = 6).

### 3.2 ⭐ The marker-vs-lever arithmetic, derived in-cycle

Take Maffiuletti's two figures at face value. The **between-subject spread** of tendon stiffness in a population is ~500%. The **trainable displacement** is <50%. The trainable slice is therefore **~10% of the observed range** — and in a trained athlete, per §2.2, **~1%**.

This is the exact geometry that killed stride length (F-043 → F-045, F-522 → F-524) and extension: a cross-sectional coefficient fitted across a range the athlete **cannot traverse**, then sold in the imperative voice. **Anyone showing you a stiffness-vs-performance scatter is pricing a range your pitcher cannot move through.** (F-634)

Four-check audit on the keeper numbers:

1. **Sample's actual velocity?** Not applicable to most — the tendon literature has no throwers. Where throwing samples exist they are junior-high (F-445), high-school or unspecified-college. **No tendon or muscle stiffness measurement exists in a confirmed 85+ sample.**
2. **Control group, variable manipulated?** Yes for the tendon-property outcome (Bohm, Lazarczuk, Kubo, Lee, Fu — all intervention syntheses or trials). **No for every performance outcome.** The manipulation stops at the tendon.
3. **Does the number measure what the sentence says?** No, twice: (a) "stiffness predicts RFD" is r² over an analyst-chosen window; (b) §3.4 — stiffness and elastic energy *output* are different variables and can move in opposite directions.
4. **High R² a geometric identity?** No identity found this cycle. But the r² = .29–.90 range is the softer cousin of the same problem: the number is a choice, not a measurement.

### 3.3 THE GAP, STATED FLATLY: nobody has ever related tendon or muscle stiffness to pitch velocity

Four-angle sweep, all negative:

- **Tendon stiffness + pitching velocity:** nothing. What exists is stiffness as a *consequence* of throwing — post-outing shear-wave studies.
- **Shear-wave elastography in pitchers:** real and growing, and it has the **wrong outcome** every time. n = 32 college players, 20 throws, 13 shoulder muscle sites (PMC11012453) — teres minor and latissimus stiffness rose after throwing; no velocity result retrievable. Flexor digitorum superficialis/profundus elasticity rose immediately post-pitching (PMID 39611120). Division IA UCL shear-wave velocity fell at mid-season and stayed down (PMID 37426937). Collegiate scalene elasticity higher in ABER than neutral (PMC9393373). High-school infraspinatus: **SWV vs pitch count r = 0.467, p = 0.003** (PMC11940899) — the only correlation in the set, and it is against **workload**, not velocity.
- **Nearest overhead sports:** handball and javelin relate throwing velocity to lower-limb **power and strength**, not tendon stiffness. The 2026 javelin paper (Köykkä, Ihalainen, Saari, Waller & Cronin, DOI 10.1177/17479541261448854) *measures* vertical stiffness (6,102 ± 2,049 N/m) and the correlation against throw performance was not retrievable. **That is the single cheapest open item in this topic: one correlation, in a dataset that already exists.**
- **Leg stiffness in pitchers:** one sample, and it is adolescent — see §3.4.

**Dispute #27 reaches its ELEVENTH venue.** Across the entire tendon-stiffness literature and the entire pitcher elastography literature: **zero accuracy or command outcomes.** Not one study measured where the ball went.

### 3.4 ⭐ EXTENSION OF F-445 — leg stiffness *was* measured in pitchers, it *did* correlate, and it *dropped out of the model*

F-445 registered the adolescent CMJ study (J Human Kinetics 2025/26, PMID 42211802, PMC13215227, n = 32 junior-high pitchers, Kistler 2,500 Hz) with a truncated predictor list. Today's snippet completes it: the correlates of ball velocity were **braking force, peak force, braking RFD, braking impulse, propulsive impulse, peak power, rate of power development, and LEG STIFFNESS.** The regression retained **absolute braking force + body height = 43.3%** of variance. **Leg stiffness did not survive.**

That is the marker-vs-lever point in miniature, inside a pitching sample: leg stiffness is a **correlate of braking force**, not an independent channel. ⚠️ Junior-high — SAMPLE MISMATCH, hard, directional only. It remains the **only** leg-stiffness-vs-pitching-velocity datum in existence. (F-642)

## 4. WHAT THE INTERVENTION EVIDENCE ACTUALLY PRESCRIBES — AND WHY A COLLEGE PROGRAM CANNOT EXECUTE IT

### 4.1 The 2026 network meta-analysis — the best-powered object in the topic

**Fu et al. (2026), "The effect of exercise training modalities on the morphological and mechanical properties of the Achilles tendon: a systematic review and network meta-analysis," *Front Sports Act Living*, DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1782503, PMID 41908219, PMC13017926.** 49 studies; **31 RCTs, n = 1,388** in the network; 5 intervention categories, 15 subtypes; searches to 18 Feb 2025.

| Outcome | Winner | SUCRA |
|---|---|---|
| **Stiffness** | long-term isometric plantar flexion (LIPFC) | **89.3%** |
| Stiffness, 2nd | **low-load blood flow restriction (LL-BFR)** | — |
| Stiffness, last | short-term eccentric (SECT) | lowest |
| **CSA** | short-term plyometric jump training | **95.6%** (SMD 1.33 vs control) |
| Length / elongation / strain | gait retraining | sparse network |
| — | stretching-focused programs | low across all outcomes |

Authors' own caution: heterogeneity, sparse networks, *"well-powered head-to-head RCTs with harmonised outcome definitions are needed."*

⭐ **AND NOTE THE COLLISION WITH YESTERDAY.** LL-BFR ranks **second for Achilles tendon stiffness** — one day after F-615→F-627 established that the entire randomised BFR evidence base **in the shoulder** is 74 participants across two trials, and that the only pitcher trial reports at its own detection floor. **"BFR" is not one claim.** Its tendon-stiffness evidence is a different, better-populated literature than its proximal-hypertrophy evidence, and a coach who heard yesterday's verdict should not transfer it here. (F-638)

### 4.2 🚨 THE PRESCRIPTION IS DEFINED IN A UNIT THE FIELD CANNOT MEASURE

The Arampatzis-line protocol, as described by downstream sources:

- **Stimulus target: tendon strain 4.5–6.5%**, which *"corresponds in average to a tendon loading of 90% MVC."*
- **Protocol: 5 sets × 4 reps, 3 s contraction / 3 s relaxation, ~2 min inter-set rest, 3–4 sessions/week.**
- **Time course:** 2024 *Scientific Reports* study (DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-56840-6, PMC10960029) at 90% MVC — stiffness change *"occurred already within the first eight weeks, and no further significant changes were found thereafter"* across a 16-week design; ramp was 70% MVC for 2 weeks, then 90% for 13.
- **Dose-response:** Arampatzis-line 14-week comparison — 90% MVC leg reached **4.55 ± 1.38%** strain vs **2.85 ± 0.99%** at 55% MVC; stiffness moved only in the 90% leg.
- **Screening threshold offered:** maximum tendon strain during MVC **> 9.0%** is read as tendon stiffness too low relative to the muscle's strength — i.e. a muscle–tendon imbalance.

⭐⭐ **AND HERE IS THE NUMBER THAT BREAKS THE PRESCRIPTION:** to achieve an average tendon strain of 6.4%, *"there was a substantial variation in the relative training loads (i.e., between **42 and 90% of the MVC**)."*

**%MVC is a 2:1-invalid proxy for the actual stimulus.** Prescribe "90% MVC long holds" to twelve pitchers and you deliver the intended tendon strain to some of them, a double dose to others, and nothing to a third group — with no way to tell which is which, because the only way to know is ultrasound tendon-strain measurement synchronised to a dynamometer. **A college weight room cannot measure the dependent variable, cannot measure the independent variable, and cannot measure the outcome.** The protocol is scientifically specified and operationally unverifiable. (F-639)

This is **Dispute #41's eighth encounter** — *if the magnitude cannot be measured, is "adopt it anyway" a finding or an escape hatch?* — and today it arrives in a sharper form than before: not "we cannot measure the effect," but **"we cannot measure the dose."**

### 4.3 ⭐ Collagen: the only lever that moved a trained athlete's tendon — and its trial sits at its own detection floor

**Lee, Robshaw & Erskine (2025), "High-intensity resistance training and collagen supplementation improve patellar tendon adaptations in professional female soccer athletes," *Exp Physiol*, DOI 10.1113/EP092106, PMC12576002.**

- **n = 11** first-team players from a Women's Championship club, pair-matched: **6 collagen / 5 placebo**.
- 30 g hydrolysed collagen (or energy-matched maltodextrin/fructose) **+ 500 mg vitamin C** before each session, **3×/week for 10 weeks** of pre-season; sessions = high-intensity lower-limb resistance + plyometrics + pitch work.
- **Patellar tendon stiffness +15.4% (collagen) vs +4.6% (placebo); Young's modulus +14.2% vs +3.4%; within-group d = 0.81 vs 0.32; P = 0.002.**
- **Neither group showed tendon hypertrophy.**

🚨 **APPLY THIS CORPUS'S OWN RULE — F-439, extended to positives by F-616 YESTERDAY.** Derived in-cycle: for two independent groups at n = 6 and n = 5, effective n per group = 2(6)(5)/11 = **5.45**, so the minimum detectable effect at α = .05 two-tailed and 80% power is **d ≈ 1.70**. The reported between-group separation is nowhere near that. The P = 0.002 therefore comes from the **repeated-measures structure** (a group × time interaction gains power as the pre–post correlation rises), not from group size — so the exact MDE is design-dependent and I will not claim a single number for it. What **is** claimable: **an 11-athlete trial cannot estimate a magnitude precisely, and a significant result from one has passed through the significance filter.** Treat +15.4% as the **ceiling of a wide interval**, not an expectation. (F-640)

⭐ **This is the SECOND INDEPENDENT APPLICATION of F-616's rule, in an unrelated literature, one day after the rule was invented.** Yesterday it diagnosed a BFR trial in pitchers. Today it diagnoses a collagen trial in soccer players. The rule generalises.

**And it is contradicted.** **Jerger et al. (2022), *Scand J Med Sci Sports*, DOI 10.1111/sms.14164** — specific collagen peptides + resistance training, Achilles: tendon stiffness and muscle strength rose in **both** groups, **no between-group difference**. A 2025 systematic review (*J Funct Morphol Kinesiol* 11(1):130, PMC13028264) splits it by dose: **15–30 g/day → significant between-group effects; ~5 g → within-group only**, with limitations *"small sample sizes, heterogeneous protocols, and short intervention durations."* **→ Dispute #65.** (F-641)

### 4.4 ⭐⭐ AND THE TARGET MAY BE WRONG FOR A BALLISTIC SKILL

Two retrieved results, both snippet-only and one blog-mediated, point the same uncomfortable direction:

1. **An equal-work duration comparison** (1 s pulses vs 20 s sustained holds, one leg each): *"tendon stiffness increased significantly for the long duration exercise, but not for the short duration exercise... the short duration exercise did show a **higher increase in elastic energy output**."* ⚠️ Identity of this trial not confirmed — reported through a physiotherapy blog. **UNVERIFIED as to source; recorded because of what it implies.**
2. **Plyometric vs isometric head-to-head** (PMC5555899, n = 11/group, 6 weeks): both raised gastrocnemius tendon stiffness (+51% / +66%, no between-group difference), RFD gains were **trends only** — and *"plyometric training (but not isometric training) enhances the extensibility of tendon structures during **ballistic** contractions and active muscle stiffness during fast stretching."*

**So: stiffness and elastic-energy output are separate variables and the protocol that maximises one did not maximise the other; and the modality that ranks FIRST for static stiffness (isometrics, SUCRA 89.3%) is the one that did NOT improve tendon behaviour under ballistic contraction.** A pitch is a ballistic contraction. **"Maximise tendon stiffness" is an untested target that may be the wrong one, and the Achilles literature's own compliance trade-off says so independently:** *"a more compliant tendon stores more elastic energy for a given force... however, compliant tendons also have lower restoring forces, which might attenuate propulsion."* There is an optimum, its location is unknown for throwing, and nobody selling stiffness mentions it. **→ Dispute #66.** (F-637)

### 4.5 A prescription variable the field does not distinguish at all

**Oranchuk et al., "'Pushing' versus 'holding' isometric muscle actions; what we know and where to go: a scoping and systematic review with meta-analyses,"** medRxiv DOI 10.1101/2024.11.04.24316609 (⚠️ **PREPRINT** — no peer-reviewed citation confirmed; 54 studies 1995–2024, n = 856; later version searched to Aug 2025).

- Time to task failure, 23 studies, n = 407: **pushing (PIMA) longer than holding (HIMA), g = −0.74, p < 0.001** — except axial muscles, where it reverses hard (g = 1.78–3.59).
- No absolute differences in HR, MAP or RPE across 6–11 studies (g = −0.11 to 0.18) except RPE at 50% of TTF greater in PIMA (n = 164, g = −0.31, p = 0.01).
- Divergent neuromuscular signatures; authors suggest HIMA and PIMA *"resemble the ones for muscle lengthening and shortening, respectively."*
- A **2026 within-subject RCT in JSCR (PMID 42647753)** reports **PIMA better for strength, HIMA better for morphological adaptation.**

**Practical consequence:** "do isometric holds" is underspecified in a way that has measured consequences. Pushing into an immovable object and resisting an external load are different interventions. No baseball source retrieved makes the distinction. (F-644)

## 6. TRANSLATION — THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

### Q1 (to the anatomist): "F-064 is the line I use most. Do I keep saying it?"

**Keep it, with one word changed.** Say *"a better spring"* — do **not** say *"a stiffer tendon."* The architecture claim (ESTABLISHED) is that the shoulder's acceleration power exceeds contractile capacity, so stored energy must be returning. The tissue claim — that the storage element is tendon and that tendon stiffness is the trainable handle — **has never been tested at the shoulder** (§2.3). There is no trial, in any population, that changed rotator-cuff tendon stiffness and measured anything.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"The arm returns energy you loaded into it; your job upstream is to load it well — nothing you can do to the tendon itself has ever been shown to change that."*
> **The drill is:** no new drill. This answer removes a drill.
> **On video the failure looks like:** nothing — there is no video signature, which is itself the point.
> **How we know it's working:** we don't, and nobody does. Spend the block elsewhere.

### Q2 (to the biomechanist): "Guys sell isometric holds for velo. Yes or no?"

**No, for the arm. Not yet, for the legs. And the reason is not that isometrics don't work — they do, on tendons.** The chain breaks in three places: (1) in trained athletes the stiffness adaptation measures **0 to +5%** (§2.2); (2) stiffness → RFD is **correlational**, explicitly uncausal in print, with a trainable range **~10%** of the between-subject range (§3.1–3.2); (3) **no study has ever related any stiffness measure to pitch velocity** (§3.3).

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Long holds build tendons. Nobody has shown they build velocity, and in guys already trained like you the tendon barely moves."*
> **The drill is:** keep heavy strength work, which raises stiffness as a by-product (Bohm ES 0.70) and is justified on its own terms by the registry's strength findings. Do not displace it with a dedicated stiffness block.
> **On video the failure looks like:** a pitcher who spent eight weeks on isometric holds and whose radar reads the same, which is also what eight weeks of **anything** looks like (F-622: offseason velocity SD = 1.83 mph, nearly 3× the mean gain).
> **How we know it's working:** you can't, on velocity, at any n you have — F-623's wall. **Validate a strength block against the strength quality, never against predicted velocity (F-018).**

### Q3 (to both): "Then is there ANY version of this I should run?"

**Yes, one, and it is at the front leg — and it is a braking-capacity block, not a stiffness block.** The honest chain the vault actually supports: **F-006** (drop jump R² = 0.30 in n = 53 professionals, RSI p = .002, where CMJ and squat jump are null) + **F-445/F-642** (braking force is the surviving CMJ predictor; leg stiffness drops out) + **F-050** (lead knee extension ≈ 0.035 mph/degree between professionals). The lead leg's block is a **~50–80 ms braking event** that an Achilles and a patellar tendon serve directly — and it is the one place in pitching where the lower-limb tendon literature even applies.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"We're not training tendon stiffness, we're training how hard and how fast you can put the brakes on. The front leg is a post, not a shock absorber."*
> **The drill is:** short-contact reactive work — drop jumps from a controlled box height, repeat hops, and the heavy strength work that underwrites them. Fu 2026 says long-hold isometric plantar flexion is the top-ranked *stiffness* modality if you want to add it, at 5×4, 3 s on / 3 s off, 3–4×/wk, understanding §4.2: **you cannot verify the dose.**
> **On video the failure looks like:** lead knee flexing *through* foot contact instead of arresting — the collapse, visible at 240 fps as the knee angle continuing to close after the foot is down.
> **How we know it's working:** **force plate, not radar.** Measure **braking RFD and braking peak force on a single-leg or CMJ protocol** across the block. That is a strength-quality readout with a detectable signal at squad size; velocity is not (F-623: 106 pitchers to detect 1 mph, 422 to detect 0.5 mph). ⚠️ And command, if you track it at all, needs **~200 tracked pitches** to detect a 2-inch gain (F-186) — one bullpen proves nothing.

### Q4 (to the anatomist): "What about collagen? Guys are already taking it."

**It is the only thing in this cycle that moved a *trained* athlete's tendon, and its trial has 11 people in it.** The best case: 30 g hydrolysed collagen + 500 mg vitamin C before training, 3×/week, 10 weeks → **+15.4% vs +4.6%** patellar tendon stiffness in professional footballers (Lee 2025). The case against: **Jerger 2022 found no between-group difference**, and dose matters (15–30 g beats ~5 g). Nobody has run it on a pitcher or measured a throwing outcome.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"It's cheap, the downside is money, the tendon evidence is real but small, and nobody has ever shown it does anything for how hard you throw."*
> **The drill is:** if a pitcher already buys it — 15–30 g with vitamin C, **before** the session, not after. Below 15 g the between-group evidence disappears.
> **On video the failure looks like:** nothing. This is a supplement, not a mechanic.
> **How we know it's working:** **you don't**, without an ultrasound and a dynamometer. Honest framing: an un-measurable bet with a plausible mechanism and a small price. ⚠️ **Do not let it become the thing the block is built on.**

## 7. CROSS-EXAMINATION

### Challenge 1 — BIOMECHANIST → ANATOMIST: "Your trained-athlete collapse rests on three studies and you are treating it as a law."

**Named claim:** §2.2 / F-630 — that tendon stiffness adaptation is attenuated to near zero in trained athletes.
**Why I doubt it:** Lazarczuk's caveat is a **three-study subgroup**, none of them pitchers, and three studies is the sample size this corpus routinely refuses to accept (F-439's n ≥ 97 rule would discard it instantly). You are also leaning on Lee 2025's **placebo arm, n = 5**, which is a detection floor, not a measurement. And there is a counter-case inside your own section: Lee's placebo arm *did* move (+4.6%), and the 2023 *Diagnostics* professional-athlete result is **cross-sectional** — it cannot speak to adaptation at all.

**ANATOMIST — PARTIALLY CONCEDED.** The **magnitude** of the attenuation is withdrawn as a quantity. Three studies plus an n = 5 arm cannot establish "zero to +5%" as a number, and the cross-sectional professional study is correctly disqualified as evidence about adaptation. **What survives, and why:** (1) the attenuation's **direction** is what the whole adaptation literature's own loading-threshold logic predicts — if stiffness responds to strain *above habitual*, a decade-trained tendon has a higher habitual, so the same absolute protocol delivers a smaller relative overload; (2) **every large figure in the literature comes from untrained, older or patient samples** (the §2.2 table), which is an observation about the whole evidence base and not about three studies; (3) the review's own authors wrote the caution, against their own interest.
**Settled by:** a tendon-stiffness intervention stratified by training history — or, at a fifth of the cost, **reporting the baseline stiffness of every arm in every future trial**, which would let anyone test the attenuation by meta-regression on data that already exists. **Graded EMERGING, direction only, per the concession.**

### Challenge 2 — COACH → BIOMECHANIST: "You priced the trainable range at ~10% off one sentence in a review."

**Named claim:** §3.2 / F-634 — trainable stiffness displacement is ~10% of the between-subject range, ~1% in a trained athlete.
**Why I doubt it:** it is **one parenthetical** in Maffiuletti 2016 — "(typically <50%)" against "(~500%)" — with no citation retrievable behind either figure, read through a search snippet, in a cycle where **no primary text was opened**. And the two numbers may not even be commensurable: a 500% population range in *what unit*, measured on *what tendon*, in *what sample*? If one is Achilles and the other patellar, the ratio is meaningless.

**BIOMECHANIST — CONCEDED AS A QUANTITY, DEFENDED AS A STRUCTURE.** The **10% figure is withdrawn**; it should never have been given two significant figures from a parenthetical. The commensurability objection is correct and unanswerable without the primary text. **What survives:** the *structure* does not need those numbers. Any variable where between-subject spread greatly exceeds within-athlete trainable displacement will produce a cross-sectional coefficient that **cannot be realised by training** — and this corpus has established that pattern independently four times (F-043→F-045 stride, F-510's exchange rates, F-001/F-442's size confound, F-622's offseason SD). Stiffness has the profile. **Reframed as: "the trainable fraction of the observed range is unknown and probably small, and no published stiffness scatter has ever reported both quantities."** That reframing is what goes in the registry.
**Settled by:** one paper reporting, in the same trained sample, (a) the between-subject SD of tendon stiffness and (b) the within-athlete pre-post change. **Both quantities exist in every intervention dataset ever collected and neither is ever reported together.**

### Challenge 3 — ANATOMIST → COACH: "You recommended drop jumps and reactive work off a cross-sectional R² = 0.30. That is the exact error this program exists to prevent."

**Named claim:** Q3 — the front-leg braking block.
**Why I doubt it:** F-006 is **CROSS_SECTIONAL**, n = 53, and its own COACHING field says *"no RSI-training intervention with a velocity outcome exists."* F-445's braking-force result is **junior-high**. F-004 is a **jump-height transfer null** that has already killed four channels in six cycles (F-438→F-449). You have assembled three insufficient things and called it a recommendation. Stride length died exactly this way.

**COACH — DEFENDED, WITH THE CLAIM NARROWED IN PRINT.** The objection would land if I had said *"train braking force and velocity will rise."* I did not, and the narrowing is the point: **the recommendation's outcome measure is braking RFD on a force plate, not velocity.** That makes it a **strength-quality block validated against the strength quality** — exactly F-018's rule — and it is immune to the stride-length failure because **no velocity exchange rate is being asserted or implied.** Second defence: the braking event is the one place where the tendon mechanism has a plausible *mechanical* home in pitching (a 50–80 ms deceleration served by series-elastic lower-limb tendons), so even if the stiffness story is wrong the training is justified by the lift and the block, not by the tendon. Third: **it costs nothing new.** Reactive lower-body work is already in every college program. I am not adding a block; I am declining to add a stiffness block and telling him to keep what he has.
**Where I concede:** the words *"this will help your velocity"* must not be attached to it, by me or anyone, and F-006's cross-sectional status goes in the same sentence as the recommendation whenever it is repeated.

### Challenge 4 — BIOMECHANIST → ANATOMIST: "If elastic energy output and stiffness can diverge, your entire §4.4 undercuts §2.2's relevance — and you logged it as a finding anyway."

**Named claim:** §4.4 / F-637 — stiffness and elastic energy output moved in opposite directions in a duration comparison.
**Why I doubt it:** the duration study's **identity is unconfirmed** — it reached us through a physiotherapy blog, with no author, journal or year. This program has caught **16 named fabrications**, including a fake position statement. An unattributed trial described second-hand, whose result happens to be rhetorically convenient, is precisely the profile of the things we have had to retract. It should be labelled UNVERIFIED and **left out of the findings**, not registered.

**ANATOMIST — CONCEDED ON PROVENANCE, DEFENDED ON REGISTRATION.** The study is **UNVERIFIED** and F-637 says so in its NUMBERS field; no magnitude from it is quoted anywhere. **But the claim it supports does not depend on it,** and that is why it is registered: the *same divergence* appears in a **named, indexed** trial — PMC5555899, where isometrics raised static tendon stiffness and **plyometrics, not isometrics**, improved tendon behaviour under ballistic contraction. Two independent routes to "stiffness is not the same variable as ballistic elastic behaviour," one named and one not. And the Achilles compliance trade-off is stated in a peer-reviewed review in its own words. **F-637 stands on the named sources; the blog-mediated trial is recorded as a lead, flagged, and is not load-bearing.**
**Settled by:** anyone reporting tendon stiffness **and** elastic energy output from the same intervention arms. ⚠️ **Note what this challenge exposes: the divergence, if real, means the industry's target variable is wrong — so the cost of leaving it unregistered is higher than the cost of registering it flagged.**

- **F-439 / F-616 (the detection-floor rule) — ⭐⭐ MOVED, DECISIVELY, AND IT GENERALISES.** Yesterday the rule was extended from nulls to positives for the first time, which opened Dispute #63 about whether that extension is legitimate. - **Dispute #41 (if the magnitude cannot be measured, is "adopt it anyway" a finding or an escape hatch?) — ⭐ MOVED, AND INTO A HARDER FORM.** Eighth encounter. Every prior instance was *we cannot measure the effect*. §4.2 is **we cannot measure the dose**: the prescription is specified in tendon strain, the field substitutes %MVC, and the substitution is 2:1 invalid between individuals. **A protocol whose dose is unverifiable is weaker than one whose outcome is unverifiable, because you cannot even confirm the intervention happened.**
- **Dispute #27 (the accuracy-outcome absence) — ELEVENTH VENUE, HOLDS WITHOUT EXCEPTION.** Tendon-stiffness adaptation syntheses, the pitcher elastography literature, the handball/javelin literature, the Fu network meta-analysis. Outcomes found: stiffness, modulus, CSA, strain, length, RFD, pain, DASH, time to task failure, ball velocity. **Command: zero.**
- **The restriction-of-range rule (Luera 2020) — ⭐⭐ GENERALISED TO A NEW CLASS OF EVIDENCE.** Until today it governed **correlations**. F-631 extends it to **intervention magnitudes**. This is the cycle's most portable output and it should be read against every positive training finding in the registry, not just this one.
- **F-623's detection wall — NOT MOVED, but given a cheaper way around.** The wall stands (106 pitchers for 1 mph). What §6 Q3 adds is that **a force-plate braking metric is detectable at squad size where velocity is not** — so the wall is an argument for changing the outcome variable, not for abandoning the block.
- **Dispute #55 / #49 (threshold vs linear; marker vs lever) — NOT MOVED.** Nothing retrieved bears on either.
- **F-064 — ⚠️ NOT MOVED, BUT NEWLY FLAGGED.** Its architecture survives. Its **tissue attribution** (that the −201 J is stored in tendon) is now recorded as an untested premise.
