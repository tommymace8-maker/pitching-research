## 3 — THE RESIDUE: EIGHT THINGS THE 09-02 SWEEP DID NOT REACH

### 3.1 ⚠️ A citation conflict between the two cycles (F-260)

**The two cycles agree on every identifier, every number and every result of the PLOS ONE manipulation — and disagree on its authors.**

- **09-02** records **"Kashiwabara et al."**
- **09-03** records **Kidokoro S, Matsuzaki Y, Akagi R** — from a search summary naming a **Shibaura Institute of Technology institutional-repository page** and listing all three with given names.

Everything else matches exactly: PLOS ONE **15(3):e0230385**, PMID **32182276**, n = 26 HS hitters, FB 34.3 ± 1.3 m/s, Continuity 15 / Random 30 / Open 20.

**Neither cycle opened the paper. PLOS ONE is fully open access — one click, the moment egress returns.** Both names are recorded and dated; **neither overwritten.**

**Why this matters out of proportion to its size:** it is this corpus's most-repeated lesson at the smallest possible scale. A PMID, a DOI, a sample size and two p-values, agreed on by two independent passes — **with the author line still in dispute.**

### 3.2 The minimax claim has been reversed twice in 2024 (F-261, Dispute #19)

09-02 records Kovash & Levitt's over-alternation result as *contested*. **It is stronger than contested.** Two independent 2024 analyses report that **payoff equality and absence of serial correlation hold for the majority of players** — the deviations lie elsewhere (batters swing *less* than theory predicts; pitchers throw *inside the zone* more).

- ScienceDirect PII **S2773161824000168**, *"Professionals do play Minimax"* (2024) — ⚠️ **authors and journal unretrievable**
- **White DR & Smith BO (2024)**, American Behavioral Scientist, **DOI 10.1177/00027642241235829**

⚠️ Attribution risk between the two is **logged and unresolved**.

**What it touches:** *"he alternates too much"* — now an unproven diagnosis at elite level.
**What it does NOT touch:** the count audit, which rests on **usage in a given count**, not serial dependence between adjacent pitches. **Different quantities; only one reversed.**

**The portable idea, worth taking either way: move the question from pitch TYPE to pitch LOCATION.** Three pitch types all landing in the same third of the zone is more predictable than the mix suggests, and no pitch-type analysis sees it.

### 3.3 The seven-class baseline, and two sample sizes for the audit (F-262)

**Sidle & Tran (2018)**, DOI 10.3233/JSA-170171. **~1,340,000 pitches, 287 pitchers**, ≥500 pitches in both 2014 and 2015. **Seven-class naive baseline 54.38%.** Top features again include **the previous pitch**.

⚠️ **Not poolable** with 09-02's binary ~71% / 3-class 61.3% — different label sets.

Two sample sizes to sit alongside the existing ±10 pp figure (that one answers *how precisely can I estimate*; these answer *how big a change can I detect*):

| Question | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Detect **70% → 55%** in one count's fastball usage (80% power, α = .05) | **≈ 83 pitches in that count** |
| Detect lag-1 **serial correlation r = 0.10** (80% power) | **≈ 790 pitches** — a full season |

### 3.4 The TTOP framing to adopt, and the cheapest study on the board (F-263)

**"The third time through the order" is a BIN BOUNDARY.** Every pre-2023 analysis binned plate appearances by time-through and compared bin means — **a design that cannot distinguish a step from a slope.** That is Brill/Deshpande/Wyner's own critique.

**This is F-043 in the time domain.** Stride length was a real cross-sectional pattern read as a mechanism that would survive intervention. F-094 is the general warning.

**The test nobody has run:** F-125/F-127 say command degrades before velocity under fatigue and that fatigue neurally inhibits the infraspinatus. So a **fatigue**-driven TTOP should show in **location quality, smoothly, ahead of any outcome signal** — a **familiarity**-driven one should not. Computable from public data, no new collection. **Cheapest high-value study identified this cycle.**

### 3.5 ABS challenge splits — one operational rule (F-264)

⚠️ *The 09-01 branch has an ABS entry (its F-257) flagging a circulating effect figure as not credible. This is the complementary half.*

**Two challenges per team over nine innings**; pitcher, catcher or batter may initiate. Overturn: **Triple-A 2025 50%, spring 52%. Catchers 56% · batters 50% · pitchers 41%.** Player sentiment (The Athletic, n = 134): **63.4% opposed, 17.2% in favor.**

> **If your conference adopts a challenge system, the catcher owns the challenge.** Pitchers are the worst challengers at 41% — worst view of the plate, most invested in the call.

Beyond the tactic: F-182 says framing contaminates command measurement, and that confound sits under Dispute #12. **This is the first public umpire-independent ground truth on borderline pitches.** Limit: **challenged pitches are a selected subset** — challenges are spent strategically.

### 3.6 The vertical slider (F-265)

Driveline (Connor White): hard, gyro-heavy, little horizontal movement, **6–9 inches of additional drop at ~84 mph**, **within 10 mph of the pitcher's own fastball** — a smaller velocity tax than a conventional breaking ball. **~1% of pitches** currently. No run values, no whiff rates, no acquisition data.

**PROMISING on the physics** — and it points the same way as 09-02's shape-diversification finding. **The claim to refuse: that it "isn't especially difficult to acquire."** Spin efficiency was sold that way and is ~65% fixed over three years (F-235). **Watch item; do not build an off-season around it.**

### 3.7 Folklore killed and NOT replaced (F-266)

*"A pitcher sitting 88 in the first inning often dips 3–5 mph by the sixth."* Commercial training blog, **88 mph** baseline, **uncorroborated across three searches.**

**Note the family resemblance:** this corpus already catches facilities quoting "3–5 mph" as a *training gain* to an 88 mph arm, extrapolated from 15-year-olds (F-072). **Same number, same strawman, now recycled as a within-start loss.**

**The corpus has no replacement number and I am not inventing one.** This blocks pricing the fatigue side of Dispute #18: **we have no figure for a quantity every coach believes he knows.**
