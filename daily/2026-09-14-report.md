## §2 — THE BIOMECHANIST'S PASS: THE SPLIT-OF-A-SPLIT PROBLEM

### 2.1 The structural result, stated before any numbers

A platoon split is **a difference of two sample means**. That does two things at once, in opposite directions:

- **The noise DOUBLES.** Var(D) = σ²/n_opp + σ²/n_same. You are not measuring one noisy thing, you are subtracting two.
- **The signal SHRINKS.** True *platoon skill* varies far less between pitchers than true *overall quality* does. (Derived below: ~18 wOBA points for RHP against ~30–50 for overall quality.)

Reliability is signal over signal-plus-noise. **A split attacks it from both ends simultaneously.** This is not a fact about platoon splits. It is a fact about **every split statistic** — times-through-order, home/road, count, RISP, first-half/second-half. **F-350.**

That is worth pausing on, because the corpus has now derived the same wall three separate times by three separate routes: F-273 (mix, 60,000 pitches), F-286 (TTO, 790 seasons), and now this. **F-286's 790-season result is a special case of F-350.** Three independent derivations converging is the strongest internal consistency check this corpus has ever produced.

### 2.2 The per-PA noise floor

Recomputed independently this cycle from standard wOBA weights and an ordinary outcome distribution (BB .095 @ .70, 1B .142 @ .89, 2B .044 @ 1.27, 3B .004 @ 1.62, HR .032 @ 2.10, outs remainder):

- mean wOBA **0.3224**, per-PA SD **0.5269**

F-286 holds **0.531**. **Agreement to 0.8% on a different weight set.** The corpus's number is sound; I adopt 0.531 below for internal consistency.

### 2.3 The college starter's actual budget

F-286's college weekend-starter budget: **~308 batters faced per season** (14 starts × ~22 BF).

| Opposite-handed share | n_opp | n_same | Harmonic PA | **SE of the observed split** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40% | 123 | 185 | 148 | **61.8 pts** |
| 45% | 139 | 169 | 152 | **60.8 pts** |
| 50% | 154 | 154 | 154 | **60.5 pts** |
| 55% | 169 | 139 | 152 | **60.8 pts** |

**≈61 wOBA points of pure sampling noise on a full season's split, and it barely moves across the whole plausible range of lineup handedness.** That robustness matters: you do not need to know your opponents' handedness mix to know the number is useless. **F-337.**

For scale: **61 points is more than twice the entire league-average platoon effect.** The measurement error is bigger than the thing being measured, by a factor of more than two.

## §3 — THE FIELD SWEEP MEETS THE ARITHMETIC (and they agree)

### 3.1 The find

WebSearch surfaced **Jared Cross, "Forecasting Pitcher Platoon Splits," The Hardball Times, 14 Aug 2015** (also carried by SABR). Both hosts refused. Two independent search retrievals returned the same figures:

> A mixed-effects model suggests right-handed pitchers' observed platoon splits should be regressed by adding **~1,670 harmonic plate appearances**; left-handed pitchers, **~570**.

**"Harmonic PA" is exactly the right unit** — Var(D) = σ²(1/n_L + 1/n_R) = 2σ²/H, where H is the harmonic mean of the two counts. That is not a coincidence of phrasing; it means Cross's constant is directly comparable to a quantity I can derive without him.

### 3.2 The independent check — and it lands

The regression constant *c* in r = H/(H+c) satisfies c = 2σ²_PA / σ²_true. Running it the other way, Cross's constants imply:

| | Cross's *c* | **Implied σ_true of platoon skill** |
|---|---|---|
| RHP | 1,670 harmonic PA | **18.4 wOBA points** |
| LHP | 570 harmonic PA | **31.5 wOBA points** |

And forward, from my own bracket of σ_true, with **no reference to Cross at all**:

| σ_true assumed | Derived *c* |
|---|---|
| 15 pts | 2,506 |
| **18 pts** | **1,741** |
| 20 pts | 1,410 |
| 25 pts | 902 |
| 31 pts | 587 |

**σ_true = 18 pts gives c = 1,741 against Cross's published 1,670 — a 4% miss. σ_true = 31 pts gives 587 against his 570 — a 3% miss.**

This is the most satisfying result of the cycle, and it cuts **both** ways, which is the honest reading:

- It **validates the corpus's per-PA variance** (F-286's 0.531) against an outside empirical fit. F-286's 790 seasons is built on that number; it just survived a check it did not know it was taking.
- It **does not validate Cross's constants**, because I have not opened his page. What it establishes is that his numbers are *arithmetically consistent with a plausible true-skill spread* — which is exactly what a fabricated number usually is not. It raises confidence; it does not substitute for reading it. **QUEUED. F-338.**

### 3.3 The LHP/RHP asymmetry — a genuinely new coaching distinction

σ_true is **1.7× larger for left-handers**. Lefty pitchers differ from each other in platoon skill far more than righties do. Consequence:

| | Harmonic PA for 50% reliability | **College seasons to get there** |
|---|---|---|
| RHP | 1,670 | **11.0** |
| LHP | 570 | **3.7** |

**A left-hander's platoon split is roughly three times faster to learn than a right-hander's — and a four-year lefty starter very nearly gets there.** He is the *only* pitcher on the staff about whom the handedness numbers will ever say anything. For every right-hander, at every college career length, the answer is no. **F-341.**

**Mechanistic note, and it is speculative:** the LHP spread is plausibly *arsenal variance*, not handedness magic. Left-handers face a mostly right-handed world, so a lefty who survives to D1 has been selected on having *something* for opposite-handed hitters, and the ways of having it (changeup, cutter, high slot, crossfire) differ a lot. Righties face a more mixed diet and converge. **Untested. This is a hypothesis, labelled as one.**

## §5 — THE LEVER, AND IT IS NOT THE SPLIT

### 5.1 What the field says (snippet-only, CROSS-SECTIONAL)

From the same Cross work and a FanGraphs arm-angle line:

- Pitchers with small or reverse splits **throw more changeups and curveballs, fewer sliders**.
- **Pitches with more VERTICAL movement → smaller or reverse splits.** Pitches with more **LATERAL movement → larger splits.**
- **Sidearm right-handers show especially large wOBA gaps**; higher arm angles show smaller splits.

### 5.2 This independently corroborates the vault — which is the point worth making

The corpus arrived at the same place from a completely different data source:

- **F-157**, the sweeper platoon table (ESTABLISHED): RHP→RHB sweeper RV/100 **−0.94**; RHP→LHB sweeper **−0.05** versus the ordinary slider's **−0.35**. *Against opposite-handed hitters the sweeper is the worst pitch in the comparison.* Maximum lateral movement, maximum split.
- **F-158**: the league's answer to the sinker's platoon problem was to **stop throwing it** — 21% → 9.7% RHP-to-LHB. Lateral movement again.
- **F-071**: lower slot = same-handed weapon, contraindicated against opposite-handed hitters.

**Three vault findings and one outside model, different data, same physics: lateral movement is a same-handed weapon and buys you nothing the other way.** That is about as close to establishment as this corpus gets.

### 5.3 ⚠️ AND IT IS STILL A MARKER

**Nobody has manipulated an arsenal and measured the resulting change in a pitcher's platoon split.** Not Cross, not Driveline, not anyone found this cycle. Every line in §5.1 is **CROSS_SECTIONAL** — pitchers who *already* throw more changeups *already* have smaller splits. The causal arrow could run the other way entirely: pitchers who happen to survive opposite-handed hitters get *allowed* to keep throwing the changeup.

**This is precisely the F-043/F-044/F-045 stride-length structure, and the corpus is required to name it before recommending anything.** What rescues it from being *only* a marker is that the mechanism is **physical and forward-derivable** — the movement direction of a pitch relative to a hitter's barrel path is geometry, not correlation — which is a stronger footing than stride length ever had. **But the honest label is: strong mechanism, zero intervention evidence. F-346.**

## §6 — THE ANATOMIST'S PASS: WHERE PHYSIOLOGY ACTUALLY ENTERS

The anatomist's first contribution is to **decline the question**. There is no tissue-level account of a platoon split. Nothing adapts. No motor unit recruits differently against a left-handed hitter. **The split is a geometry-and-perception phenomenon and the anatomist has no business claiming it.** Saying so is the finding — the corpus's standing failure mode is an agent manufacturing relevance.

**But there is one real physiological handle, and it is sharp:**

The opposite-hand weapon is almost always a **changeup or splitter** — a pitch whose entire effect depends on a *velocity and spin differential held by forearm and wrist positioning*, not on gross force output. **F-127** establishes the fatigue hierarchy: **command and secondary-pitch finish degrade BEFORE velocity.** Therefore:

> **The opposite-hand weapon is the first thing fatigue takes away.** A starter's platoon disadvantage should *widen within an outing*, and widen **before** any velocity decline is visible on the gun.

This is a **MECHANISM** claim with a measurable consequence: **fastball-minus-changeup velocity separation, bucketed by pitch number.**

| Within-pitcher release-speed SD | Detect 1.0 mph separation loss | 1.5 mph | 2.0 mph |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 mph | 10 CH per bucket | 4 | 3 |
| **1.0 mph** (corpus working assumption, F-289) | **16** | **7** | **4** |
| 1.2 mph | 23 | 10 | 6 |

A college starter throws ~12–18 changeups per start → **~6–9 per early/late bucket.** So: **a large separation collapse (≥1.5 mph) is visible in a SINGLE START. A subtle one (0.5 mph) needs ~60 per bucket — about eight starts pooled.** **F-348.**

That is a rare thing in this corpus: a fatigue tell that does not need a season. It belongs beside F-320 (five pitches) and F-331 as the corpus's third fast instrument.

⚠️ **And it exposes an eighth gap of the recurring kind: the corpus holds no within-pitcher SD of FB−CH velocity separation for an 85+ arm.** It is in every program's TrackMan log. Nobody has published it. That is now **eight** instances of *already in your data, an afternoon to compute, gating a whole topic's detection arithmetic, published by nobody* (F-264, F-289, F-307, F-295, F-320, F-336's two, and now this).

## §7 — THE NUMBERS A COACH WILL ACTUALLY BE HANDED

### 7.1 Reliability of a college split

σ_true = 18.4 pts (RHP), noise from §2.3:

| Sample | Harmonic PA | Reliability | **You keep** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 season | 152 | 0.084 | **8%** |
| 2 seasons | 305 | 0.154 | 15% |
| 3 seasons | 457 | 0.215 | 22% |
| **Full 4-year career** | 610 | 0.267 | **27%** |

**After four years you throw away three-quarters of what you see.** After one, you throw away 92%. **F-337.**

### 7.2 One in three of your pitchers will look like a reverse-split guy

With a true league split of +20 to +35 points and SD(observed) = 63.5:

| True league split | P(observed split is negative) | **On a 12-man staff** |
|---|---|---|
| 20 pts | 0.376 | **4.5 pitchers** |
| 27 pts | 0.335 | **4.0 pitchers** |
| 35 pts | 0.291 | **3.5 pitchers** |

**Every season, roughly four of your twelve pitchers will show an apparent reverse split — and it will be a different four next year.** The turnover is the tell, and it is free to check: it costs one afternoon with two years of your own game logs, and it is the single most convincing demonstration you can show a staff. **F-339.**

### 7.3 The result that closes the question

A pitcher shows a **100-point reverse split** across a full season. Bayes, with r = 0.084:

| Observed split | Posterior mean | **P(he is truly reverse-split)** |
|---|---|---|
| −100 pts | **+16.4 pts** | 0.176 |
| −150 pts | **+12.2 pts** | 0.244 |
| −200 pts | **+8.0 pts** | 0.324 |

**Even after a 200-point observed reverse split, the best estimate of his true split is still POSITIVE — a normal platoon split of +8 points — and he is still only one-in-three to be genuinely reversed.**

To get to a coin flip you would need to observe **−296 points**. That is **5.1 SD** below the mean: p = **1.9 × 10⁻⁷** per pitcher-season, **0.00001 expected occurrences on a 12-man staff over four years.**

> ### **No college right-hander will ever, in a college career, generate the evidence required to conclude he has a reverse platoon split.** Not a weak conclusion. Not an unlikely one. **Arithmetically unavailable.** **F-340.**

### 7.4 The doctrine: estimate what is estimable

Same pitcher, same plate appearances, two quantities:

| Quantity | σ_true | Reliability at ~250–308 PA |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall quality (q)** | 30–50 pts | **0.44 – 0.73** (call it ~0.6) |
| **Platoon split (s)** | 18 pts | **0.084** |

**Seven times the reliability, from the very same events.** Hence the rule:

> **Spend all your estimation effort on q. Assign every pitcher the league-average s and move on.** The information is in the overall number. The split is a way of destroying it. **F-342.**

## §8 — THE COACH'S PASS: FOUR QUESTIONS AND THE TRANSLATION

### Q1 (Coach → Biomechanist): *"Ole Miss has a lefty reliever. Bases loaded, seventh, a lefty hitting. Do I bring him?"*

**Answer.** Write each pitcher's performance as q ± s/2. Facing a LHH: the righty gives q_R + s/2, the lefty gives q_L − s/2. The switch gains **(q_R − q_L) + s**. So:

> **The handedness matchup is worth ~27 wOBA points. Make the switch UNLESS your lefty is more than ~27 points of true quality worse than the righty already out there.**

And since q is ~7× better estimated than s, **the quality gap is the term you can actually know.** Priced out: 40–80 matchup decisions × 1.0–2.0 PA each, at 0.027/1.25 = 0.0216 runs per PA:

**≈0.9 – 3.5 runs per season, most likely ~1.3 – 2.6.** **F-343.**

Which sits exactly where the running game (F-319), times-through-order and count leverage landed. **The corpus has now priced four separate tactical questions and every one came back between 1 and 3 runs a season.** That consistency is itself worth recording — these decisions are real, they are roughly equal, and none of them is where a season is won.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — nothing. This one is a dugout decision, and the pitcher should never be told he is being lifted because of a *split*.
> **The drill is** — build the reliever depth chart on overall quality in December, with league-average splits applied on top. One sheet. Never rebuilt off in-season handedness numbers.
> **On video the failure looks like** — a coach on the rail reading a laminated split card with 40 PA in the denominator.
> **Here's how we know it's working** — we do not, and we must not pretend to. 60 decisions a year at ~0.02 runs each is **invisible forever** (F-273's wall). **Audit the DECISIONS against the rule, not the outcomes.** 100% compliance is achievable and checkable in an afternoon; the run value is not checkable in a career.

### Q2 (Coach → Anatomist): *"My Friday starter gets crushed by lefties the third time through. Is that his split, or is he tired?"*

**Answer: measurable, and the answer is not in the wOBA.** §6 says his opposite-hand weapon dies before his velocity does. The third-time-through lefty damage and the fatigue channel make the *same* prediction in outcomes — and per F-286 the outcome split needs 790 seasons, so outcomes cannot separate them. **The input can.**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — "your changeup gets *faster* when you get tired, and that's what they're hitting. It isn't the lefties, it's pitch 70."
> **The drill is** — chart FB and CH release speed by pitch number, every start, all season. Bucket early (1–40) vs late (60+).
> **On video the failure looks like** — the changeup arm action shortening; the arm-speed tell of F-256; the finish coming off it.
> **Here's how we know it's working** — **a ≥1.5 mph separation loss is detectable in ONE start with 7 changeups per bucket.** A 0.5 mph drift needs ~60 per bucket, about eight starts pooled. **Both are affordable. Neither requires a single at-bat outcome.**

### Q3 (Coach → Biomechanist): *"Then what DO I do about a guy lefties hit?"*

**Answer: the arsenal, and know that you are acting on a marker with a mechanism, not on a proven lever (§5.3).**

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — "you need one pitch that goes *toward* a lefty's hands or away from his barrel. Your sweeper is not that pitch and never will be — it's a righty-killer" (F-157: −0.94 vs RHB, −0.05 vs LHB, *worse than an ordinary slider*).
> **The drill is** — A/B changeup and splitter grips in a bullpen and read the SHAPE, not the results. F-168's rule: use a stuff model as a **shape screen at 80–250 pitches, within-pitcher, within-season, within one model vintage**. Target vertical separation, not lateral.
> **On video the failure looks like** — arm speed dropping on the changeup (F-256); or the pitcher "fixing" it by dropping his slot, which per F-071 makes the platoon problem **worse** while raising elbow varus torque (F-070).
> **Here's how we know it's working** — **COMPLIANCE, NEVER OUTCOME.** A usage shift from 9% to 22% against opposite-handed hitters is detectable at 80% power in **120 opposite-handed pitches** — about four starts. At 400 opposite-handed pitches, usage is pinned to **±4.1 points at 95%**. The run-value benefit is never detectable in this athlete (F-273). **Verify he threw it. Never try to verify it worked.**

### Q4 (Coach → both): *"Fine. Which of my guys is the exception?"*

**Answer: possibly your four-year left-hander, and nobody else.** §3.3: a lefty reaches 50% reliability in **3.7 college seasons** against a righty's **11.0**. A senior lefty with four years of your own game logs is the one pitcher on the roster whose handedness numbers are worth opening — and even then you regress him halfway to league average.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is** — nothing, again. This is roster information.
> **The drill is** — pool your seniors' four years, compute harmonic PA, apply r = H/(H + 570) for lefties and H/(H + 1670) for righties, shrink toward league average, and *throw away every righty column*.
> **On video the failure looks like** — nothing. This one is a spreadsheet failure, not a mound failure.
> **Here's how we know it's working** — the year-over-year turnover test of §7.2. If your "reverse-split guys" are a different four names every season, the list was noise. **If the same lefty keeps appearing across four years, that is the one signal in the whole exercise.**

## §9 — CROSS-EXAMINATION

### Challenge 1 — BIOMECHANIST → COACH. *"Your §8 Q3 recommendation is a cross-sectional finding worn as an instruction. That is the thing this program exists to stop."*

**The claim:** "Build a vertical-movement pitch to shrink his platoon split" rests on §5.1, which is entirely observational. **Stride length died exactly here.**

**Coach's response — PARTIALLY CONCEDED, and the concession is load-bearing.** Conceded that there is **no intervention evidence**, that this must never be phrased as "adding a changeup will shrink your split by X points," and that no magnitude may be quoted. **Defended** on two grounds: (i) the mechanism is **forward-derivable geometry** — where a pitch moves relative to a barrel path is not an association, it is a trajectory — which is a footing stride length never had; (ii) the recommendation's *measurable check is compliance-only*, so it cannot launder itself into a false outcome claim. **Net: the recommendation stands, stripped of every magnitude, and is tagged CROSS_SECTIONAL in F-346 with the stride-length precedent named in the finding itself.**

### Challenge 2 — ANATOMIST → BIOMECHANIST. *"Your 4% agreement with Cross is circular. You fed his own variance structure back to him."*
