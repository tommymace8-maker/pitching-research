## 3. THE ANATOMY AND BIOMECHANICS PASSES — the two derivations that carry the cycle

### 3.1 🚨 The quiet-eye criterion is twice as wide as the entire strike zone (F-450)

The standard definition (identical across two independent snippets): final fixation **within 3° of visual angle** for **≥100 ms** before movement initiation. The **100 ms is a clock** and travels between tasks. **The 3° is an angle, and an angle is not a size** — the region it admits scales with distance, and every flagship quiet-eye task is near-field while pitching is far-field by an order of magnitude.

`s = 2D·tan(1.5°) = 0.05241·D`

| Task | Eye→target | 3° window | Target width | **Window ÷ target** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Golf putt | 0.9 m | 4.7 cm | hole 10.8 cm | **0.44** |
| Darts | 2.4 m | 12.6 cm | board 34 cm | **0.37** |
| Basketball FT | 4.4 m | 23.0 cm | rim 45.7 cm | **0.51** |
| **Pitching → zone** | **18.44 m** | **38.0 in** | **17 in** | **2.24** |
| **Pitching → mitt** | 18.44 m | 38.0 in | ~12 in | **3.2** |

And the angular budget the pitch actually has, at the corpus's 54.5 ft ball-travel convention:
- **1 in of lateral miss = 0.0876° = 5.26 arcmin**
- **6-in miss = 0.53°**
- **the ENTIRE strike zone = 1.489° from release, 1.341° from the eyes**

> **3° tolerance ÷ 1.3–1.5° zone = 2.0–2.2×.** A pitcher satisfies the textbook criterion while his gaze wanders across the glove, the mask, both of the catcher's shoulders and a foot of dirt either side. **The measurement does not resolve the thing it is named for.** A criterion as demanding as putting's, relative to its target, would have to be ≈ **0.75°** at 60.5 ft — inside the ~2° fovea and at the edge of what field eye-trackers resolve.

**This does not prove gaze is irrelevant. It proves the effect sizes cannot be imported.** It cuts both ways, and Dispute #39 is about exactly how far it cuts.

### 3.2 🚨 At 60 feet the eyes cannot tell him how far away the target is (F-451)

IPD ≈ 6.3 cm; vergence `θ = 2·atan(IPD/2D)`:

| Distance | Vergence | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|
| 0.9 m (putt) | 4.009° = **240.5 arcmin** | 1 cm depth → **2.67 arcmin (160 arcsec)** |
| 4.4 m (FT) | 0.820° = 49.2 arcmin | — |
| **18.44 m** | 0.196° = **11.7 arcmin** | **1 ft depth → 0.194 arcmin = 11.6 ARCSEC** |

Against a good-observer stereoacuity threshold of **20–30 arcsec** that degrades with distance:

> **Binocular depth information about the mitt is at or below threshold. The pitcher's aiming problem is two-dimensional and angular.** Quiet eye's proposed mechanism — *parameterise the movement from a precise target localisation* — has rich depth input in every flagship task and **effectively none on a mound.** The distance to the plate is a constant he **knows**, not one he **sees**.

**The coaching consequence is immediate and larger than the finding:** vertical command error is dominated by release **timing**, and the eyes supply nothing about it. **Stop treating a high miss as a target-focus failure.** He is not looking in the wrong place; he is letting go early.

⚠️ The stereoacuity threshold is asserted from general knowledge and was **not verified at source.** If the true threshold is worse than 20–30 arcsec, the conclusion strengthens; it cannot weaken.

### 3.3 What the literature adds, all of it snippet-only

- **Lebeau 2016 (F-453):** d = 1.04 expert-vs-novice and d = 0.58 successful-vs-unsuccessful are **CROSS_SECTIONAL — markers.** d = 1.04 is the exact between-groups design **F-094** calls the wrong denominator. The only **INTERVENTION** number is **d = 0.84 on k = 9 articles** — the identical profile to the external-focus literature that went from g = 0.26/0.58 to bias-corrected **g = 0.01/0.15** (F-189), and to OPTIMAL theory's pillars that went to zero (F-190). **No bias-corrected quiet-eye estimate exists.**
- **The darts intervention (F-454):** training moved **quiet-eye duration** and **did not move throwing accuracy** — a **manipulated dissociation in a throwing task**, and the most relevant intervention result located. Its n is unknown, so under F-439 it is a **detection floor, not an absence.** Top of this topic's verification queue.

## 4. TRANSLATE — the coach's four questions

### Q1 — "So do I stop telling him to look at the glove, or not?"

**Answer (anatomist + biomechanist, agreeing):** No. F-450 is a **measurement** result, not a behavioural one, and it degrades the evidence in both directions — the pitcher-side *nulls* are as weak as the pitcher-side positives, for the same reason. The defensible position is **agnostic**, exactly as **F-192** concluded about feedback frequency and for the same structural reason.

> **What I tell the pitcher:** *"Keep looking where you've always looked. Nobody has ever studied where a pitcher looks on anyone who throws like you — three men in one abstract is the whole file."*
> **The drill:** none. **This question's output is the refusal of a training block, not the addition of one.**
> **On video the failure looks like:** nothing — there is nothing here to see.
> **How we know it's working:** we don't, and F-456 is why: **one 30-pitch bullpen has 2–4% power** against a 2-inch command shift.

### Q2 — "If I wanted to prove a gaze cue worked, what would it cost?" (F-456)

**Answer (biomechanist):** More than you have. α = .05, power .80, DEFF = 2.45 at 30 pitches/pen and ρ = .05:

| Target | Raw n/condition | ×DEFF | **Total pitches** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-in mean radial-miss shift, σ = 6 in | 141 | 346 | **692** |
| 2-in shift, **σ = 8 in** | 251 | 615 | **1,230 ≈ 41 bullpens** |
| 2-in shift, σ = 10 in | 392 | 961 | **1,922** |
| **10% cut in location SD** | 708 | 1,735 | **3,470 — not affordable** |
| 20% cut in location SD | 159 | 390 | **780 ≈ 26 bullpens** |

> **What I tell the pitcher:** *"If a guy tells you he watched a target-focus cue work in a pen, he watched noise. That pen had a three-percent chance of seeing a real two-inch change."*
> **How we know it's working:** you can't, below ~700 tracked pitches, and only then for an effect nothing in this topic predicts.

⚠️ **σ is bracketed because the corpus still does not hold the within-pitcher location SD for an 85+ arm.** That is **F-457: the TWELFTH detection table in this corpus blocked by a dispersion statistic already sitting in every program's TrackMan log.** The bracket moves the required sample by **2.8×**.

### Q3 — "Then is there anything about the eyes I *can* use this week?" (F-458, F-459)

**Answer (biomechanist, reluctantly; coach, enthusiastically):** Yes — but it is the **other** gaze question. Not *does looking longer help command*, but **does where he looks give the pitch away.**

arXiv 2603.04874: **80.4%** pre-release pitch-type accuracy from **broadcast** 3D pose over **119,561 professional pitches**; **head/eyes reportedly 19.0%** of predictive signal. ⚠️ **Unread, and the snippet contradicts itself** (it calls wrist "the most informative joint group" at 14.8% while giving head/eyes 19.0%). **Use the order of magnitude, never the ranking.** This is the exact summariser corruption that produced the fabricated *"Park et al., 12.4 million pitches"* in `idea-scouting.md` §2.

Detection for a tell is **cheap**, because it is a classification test rather than a 2-inch shift:

| Grader accuracy to detect (2 pitch types) | **Frames needed** |
|---|---|
| 60% | 189 |
| **65%** | **80** |
| 70% | 42 |

> **What I tell the pitcher:** *"We're not going to work on your eyes. We're going to check whether they're talking."*
> **The drill:** two bullpens. Phone on a tripod beside the mound at hitter's-eye height, ~100 pitches, catcher logs pitch type. Cut a still at the **same event every pitch** — max leg lift is easiest to find by eye, foot contact is better and harder. Strip the labels. Hand 80–100 stills to an assistant who does not know the order and have him call **fastball / offspeed**.
> **On video the failure looks like:** head or eye line drifting glove-side on the breaking ball; the head settling **earlier** on the fastball.
> **How we know it's working:** the grader sits at **chance — 50% ± 10% on 80 frames.** At **65%+ you have a tell.**

**And then stop**, because of Q4.

### Q4 — "If the check is positive, do I coach his eyes?"

**Answer (biomechanist, and this is Dispute #38):** **No.** A feature-importance share out of a gradient-boosted ensemble over **229 correlated inputs** is not a channel. Head orientation is mechanically driven by **trunk lateral tilt** — which the same paper names its **single most discriminative feature** — and a tree ensemble will hand a redundant proxy a large share. **The 19.0% may be trunk tilt wearing the head's clothes.**

**Anatomist, partially dissenting:** the head is not a passive rider. The vestibulo-ocular and cervicocollic reflexes **actively stabilise** it against trunk rotation — that is their job, and it is why a pitcher's head stays comparatively level through a violent action. **A structure under active stabilisation is partially decoupled** from the segment beneath it, so independent signal is plausible. *"It must be inherited"* is not the safe default it sounds like.

> **What I tell the pitcher:** *"Your delivery is saying something. We're going to find out what part of it is saying it before we touch anything."*
> **The drill:** none yet — the next step is diagnostic, not corrective. Compare the positive frames for **trunk lateral tilt and glove-arm deceleration** before ever mentioning his eyes.
> **On video the failure looks like:** if the tell co-varies with tilt, you will see the **torso** differ by pitch type in the same frames the head does.
> **How we know it's working:** you don't yet — read arXiv 2603.04874 first. **It is free, it has been the corpus's named highest-value unread item since 2026-09-07, and it is still unread.**

**The coach's own line, sharper than either agent's:** *"You cannot fix a trunk-tilt tell by telling a man to move his eyes. If I coach the eyes and the signal lives in the tilt, I've spent a block making a pitcher self-conscious about the one thing in his delivery he wasn't thinking about — which is the classic route to making command **worse**."*

## 8. ✅ WHAT THE THREE PRIMARY READS CHANGED

### 8.1 The darts intervention (PMC7739699) — **worse for quiet eye than its own title says** (F-454, F-463)

**n = 36 dart NOVICES**, four groups (10/9/7/10), 2.37 m, WDF board 34 cm, 30 pretest throws + 3 × 50 training + posttest, SMI glasses at **60 Hz**.

- **QE IS TRAINABLE:** 574 → 747 ms, **F(1,32) = 6.93, p = .01, f = 0.43.** Not in dispute.
- **ACCURACY IS NOT MOVED:** 8.7 → 8.2 cm, **F(1,32) = 1.13, p = .30, f = 0.13.**
- 🚨 **THE ASSOCIATION RUNS BACKWARDS.** The group that gained most QE (external-visual, 499 → 946 ms) **got worse** (6.6 → 6.7 cm). The group that **lost** most QE (external-kinesthetic, 686 → 565 ms) improved most (10.8 → 9.6 cm), and the authors say so themselves.
- 🚨 **AND THAT IS CONFOUNDED.** Pretest accuracy differed at **F(1,32) = 20.75, p < .01, f = 1.47** — visual 6.6–6.8 cm vs kinesthetic 10.8–11.0 cm. Randomisation with 7–10 per group produced a gap larger than any target effect; **regression to the mean alone predicts the pattern.** **Do not reverse this into "long quiet eye is bad."**
- 🚨 **THE POWER CLAIM IS OFF BY A FACTOR OF EIGHT (F-463).** The paper prints **`1 − β = 0.95`** beside effect sizes down to **f = 0.03**. **Recomputed: 11.6% at f = 0.13, 48.8% at f = 0.33.** 80% needs **N ≈ 465** and **N ≈ 72**. **This is F-439's rule caught in print, and it is the first time this corpus has verified at source that a paper's own power statement is the thing misleading the reader.** New operating rule in F-463: **recompute every printed `1 − β` before grading a null.**

**Net: the accuracy null is a detection floor, not an absence — and no evidence anywhere shows quiet-eye training improves throwing accuracy.**

### 8.2 The tipping paper (arXiv 2603.04874) — **queue head closed; one objection dead, two sharper** (F-459)

**80.4% on EIGHT classes against a 32.3% majority-class baseline**, 119,561 pitches, XGBoost over 229 features at three detected events. **Head joints (both eyes + nose) 19.0%; both wrists 14.8%; trunk 5.3%; arms 40.5%; upper:lower 64.9:35.1.**

- ✅ **THE SEARCH SUMMARY HAD THE RANKING BACKWARDS.** It called wrist *"the most informative joint group."* The paper says head joints are *"higher than any single limb group."* **The cycle's instruction — use the magnitude, never the ranking — was right, and the ranking it refused was wrong. Third documented search-summary corruption in this corpus.**
- ✅ **THE TIPPING FRAME IS THE AUTHORS' OWN:** importance rankings *"as batter-visible 'tells' for anticipation training."*
- ❌ **DISPUTE #38's ORIGINAL FORM IS DEAD.** Head orientation is not inheriting from trunk lateral tilt: **trunk 5.3% vs head 19.0%, a 3.6× gap.** Conceded.
- ⚠️ **DISPUTE #38, AMENDED, IS ALIVE AND IS NOW THE PRINCIPAL OBJECTION.** Importance is **XGBoost gain**, and **the ablations are by feature CATEGORY only — there is no leave-one-joint-out ablation, so the head's 19.0% is never tested by removing it.**
- ⚠️ **NEW AND UNDISCUSSED BY THE PAPER: no held-out-PITCHER evaluation.** The split is over pitches, so the same pitcher is in train and test; the only generalisation check is handedness. **⭐ This makes the week's recommendation MORE necessary, not less — the paper cannot tell you about your pitcher.**
- ⚠️ **Event-detection error:** release 9.6 ms (90.7% within ±10 ms) but **foot plant 46.9 ms (22.1%)** and **MER 46.5 ms (31.4%)**. Conservative in direction; do not read the per-event breakdown finely.
- **F-256 implication:** FF↔FT is the ceiling — **grip-defined variants are not separable from pose.**

### 8.3 xCTRL (arXiv 2508.19184) — **the circularity objection survives** (F-460)

Statcast 2008–2023, bins of **pitcher × pitch type × batter handedness × season**, **min 250 pitches/bin**, GMM by EM, **K = K(x) tuned over K ∈ {1,…,6} by validation log-likelihood**, **1.5·IQR outlier trim before fitting (outliers still scored)**, xCTRL = posterior-weighted distance to inferred target centres.

- 🚨 **VALIDATION LOG-LIKELIHOOD DOES NOT BREAK THE LOOP — IT OPTIMISES IT.** It selects the K that best *describes* the location cloud, which is the same quantity the command score is built on. **No simulation study and no validation against a known target appears in the paper.**
- ✅ **One point in the paper's favour the cycle did not anticipate:** the pre-fit IQR trim means the wildest pitches do not widen the components. **Mitigates, does not close.**
- ⚠️ **The near-tautology stands:** BB/9 and WHIP are near-definitional for a location metric. **FIP and IP are the informative comparisons.**
