## 1. WHY THIS TOPIC

Zero coverage in 482 findings. `grep -i "front arm"` → 0. `grep -i "lead arm"` → 0. Every "glove" hit in the registry belonged to the catcher/framing topic or to glove-tagging as a command metric. Meanwhile it is the oldest cue in pitching instruction, taught in **two mutually contradictory versions** — "pull the glove / firm front side / equal and opposite" versus "chest to glove / keep it out front" — with the same stated mechanism: **glove arm → trunk rotation → arm speed → ball speed.**

This brief tests all three links. **None of them survives in an 85+ population.**

## 2. THE STRUCTURAL FACT (F-483)

The glove arm is **not in the elite pitching model.** Variable lists enumerated at source:

- **ASMI / BioPitch** (McCutcheon, Slowik & Fleisig 2025, PMC11789100, read in full): **523 pitchers, 425 professional, 21 kinematic variables, ZERO glove-arm variables.** The only glove-*side* variable is trunk contralateral tilt — a trunk variable.
- **KinaTrax in-game NCAA D1** (PMC13519164, read in full): **ZERO.**
- **Driveline OpenBiomechanics**: **FOUR** — and they are the only elite glove-arm data that exists.

⚠️ **Population check #1 on the largest elite dataset there is: ASMI's 523 pitchers average 37.1–38.0 m/s = 83.0–85.0 mph.** "425 professional pitchers" means ~84 mph in the lab. The in-game D1 set is the only unambiguously on-population sample at **90.2 mph**, and it has no glove data.

### The flagship paper's self-contradiction (F-484) — read it slowly

Barfield 2018 **never prints ball velocity**, yet analysed *"the fastest fastball delivered by each participant"* — so speed was measured, ranked, and discarded. Second instance of **F-466's pattern**. And the hedge decays across three sections of one paper:

- Abstract: *"**could prove to be** more advantageous"*
- Discussion: *"**could be** more advantageous"*
- **Conclusion: *"**is** more advantageous to performance."***
- **Limitations, two paragraphs earlier: *"future studies should consider... glove arm kinematic variations on ball velocity to determine the glove arm's influence on PERFORMANCE."***

**The paper's own limitations section says it did not measure performance. Its conclusion claims to have established it.** The industry quotes the conclusion.

**OPERATING RULE EARNED: READ THE LIMITATIONS BEFORE THE CONCLUSION. WHEN THEY DISAGREE, THE LIMITATIONS ARE THE FINDING.**

Its two headline correlations (rs = ±0.52, P = .002) sit against its own **detection floor of 0.471**, among **~78 uncorrected tests** (Bonferroni would need |rs| ≈ 0.57). **95% CI on rs = 0.52 at n = 33: 0.215 to 0.733 — R² anywhere from 4.6% to 53.7%** (F-485). And its glove-arm SDs (38.65°, 47.25°) are **2.6× to 5.8×** those of the ≥85 sample (8.14–14.85°), so the spread it correlated across is maturation, not technique (F-486).

## 4. ⭐ THE PRIMARY-DATA RESULT

**Driveline OpenBiomechanics, downloaded and analysed 2026-09-26. 411 fastballs, 100 pitchers, 84.70 ± 4.72 mph (69.5–94.4). ≥85 subset: 59 pitchers, 88.05 ± 2.21 mph.** Pitcher-level means throughout.

### Link 1 — glove shoulder → ball speed. **NULL.**
`horizontal_abduction_fp` **−0.100**; `abduction_fp` **−0.007**; `external_rotation_fp` **+0.112**; `abduction_mer` **−0.020**. Largest R² = **1.3%**. 95% CI −0.148 to +0.358. ICC-disattenuated: 0.112 → 0.116, so noise is not hiding it (F-487).

The one nominal hit in the mixed sample (`external_rotation_fp`, r = −0.215, p = .031) **flips sign** to +0.112 at ≥85. Textbook restriction of range.

### Link 2 — trunk rotation → ball speed. **ALSO NULL at 85+, and this is the finding with reach (F-488).**

| | mixed (n=100) | **≥85 (n=59)** |
|---|---|---|
| `max_torso_rotational_velo` | +0.328, R² 10.8% | **+0.108, R² 1.2%** |
| `max_shoulder_internal_rotational_velo` | +0.292, R² 8.5% | **+0.086, R² 0.7%** |

**Any cue justified by "it increases trunk rotational velocity" is justified by a variable explaining 1.2% of velocity variance in this population.** That covers a large slice of the cueing industry. Same variables, same instrument, one sample split at 85 — the cleanest Luera-2020 demonstration this corpus holds.

### Link 3 — glove arm → trunk rotation. **The one robust signal, and it's a dead end.**
`glove_shoulder_external_rotation_fp` vs `max_torso_rotational_velo`: **r = −0.525 (n=100) and −0.465 (n=59), p < .0005 both, survives Bonferroni in both, Spearman −0.467/−0.470.** R² 22–28%. Real. And per Link 2, worth nothing in mph.

Barfield's direction echoes weakly: `abduction_mer` vs arm speed **r = +0.269** at ≥85 (their youth analogue +0.52) — doesn't survive correction, and reaches the ball at r = −0.020. **The marker propagates exactly one link, then dies.**

## 5. MECHANISM — the anatomist's brief argues both ways

⚠️ **DERIVED IN-CYCLE, not cited.** One arm ≈ 5% BW = **4.7 kg** at the 93.7 kg ASMI mean; CoM ~0.45–0.55 m from the long axis abducted, 0.15–0.30 m tucked → **ΔI ≈ 0.6–1.0 kg·m²** against a trunk-plus-head longitudinal inertia of order **1–2 kg·m²**. **The figure-skater intuition is sound physics and the effect is tens of percent.**

Three reasons it still delivers nothing:
1. **Angular momentum is not conserved** — the lead leg applies external ground torque throughout. The skater spins on a frictionless pivot; the pitcher does not.
2. **The timing is anatomically unavailable as a conscious act.** MER→release is **~30–50 ms**; electromechanical delay is the same order. **A voluntarily initiated glove pull cannot be timed inside the window it is supposed to act in.** Whatever the glove arm does in acceleration was pre-programmed before foot contact — which makes it a *position* variable, which is what §4 measured.
3. **No headroom.** Peak torso rotational velocity is **1074 ± 83 °/s** — a between-pitcher SD of **7.7% of the mean.**

**The honest reading: a large mechanism producing r ≈ 0 means the system compensates.** Pitchers self-organise around glove-arm position rather than converting it to rotational speed. That is more interesting than a small mechanism would have been.

## 7. THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

**Q1 — "Give me the honest answer: is there a front-side velocity gain or not?"**
**No.** Four glove-shoulder variables, 59 pitchers at 88 mph, largest R² = 1.3%, and reliability is high enough that noise cannot be hiding it (F-487, F-490). The mechanism is real (§5) and the transfer is zero. **CI upper bound is r = 0.358, so a moderate effect is not formally excluded at n = 59** — but there is no positive evidence at any sample size in this population, ever.
→ **I tell the pitcher:** *"Your glove arm is not where your missing 2 mph is. We're not spending a block on it."* **Drill:** none — the deliverable is the block you *don't* run. **On video the failure looks like:** a coach cueing the front side after a bad outing because it is the most visible thing in the delivery. **How we know it's working:** we don't measure it; we measure whatever we spent the block on instead.

**Q2 — "Then why does every good pitcher have a good front side?"**
Because it is a reliable *signature*, not a cause. **ICC 0.91–0.95** (F-490): a pitcher's glove-arm position is nearly fixed. You are seeing the same thing you see with stride length — a stable individual pattern that correlates with being good across a mixed sample and stops correlating with anything inside the elite band (F-488 shows the same collapse for trunk rotation itself).
→ **I tell the pitcher:** *"That's your signature, not your engine."*

**Q3 — "Is there anything here worth having?"**
Yes, two things, and neither is velocity. **(a)** F-179 — *already registered* — says glove-shoulder abduction *consistency* at foot plant associates with lower miss distance. That supports using the glove arm to **set position at foot plant**, which is a command/organisation use, not a power use. **(b)** The cost figure: within-pitcher SD is **1.8–4.1°**, so a deliberate change is verifiable in **2–11 pitches per condition**.
→ **I tell the pitcher:** *"Different every time on the way there, same every time when the foot lands."* **Drill:** variable leg-lift / variable-tempo bullpen, all arriving at the same front-side position at foot contact. **On video:** glove shoulder in a different place at foot plant on consecutive pitches from the same intent. **How we know:** 10 pitches, phone at hitter's-eye height, freeze at foot plant.

**Q4 — "How many pitches to know I changed something?"**
**About ten** — against **~200** for a 2-inch command claim (F-186) and **~85 paired observations** for the impulse→velocity slope (F-449). 5° at `abduction_mer`: **2 pitches**. 3°: **6**. 5° at `external_rotation_fp`: **11**.
→ **⚠️ AND THIS IS THE TRAP.** The glove arm is **the cheapest thing in the delivery to verify you changed and the thing least likely to pay you for changing it.** A coach who likes measurable progress will see the change every single time, and it will mean nothing. **Say that out loud to yourself before you run the block.**
