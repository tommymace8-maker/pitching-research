## 3. THE RESEARCH

### 3.1 ⭐ The lever — mound beats flat ground by ~2 mph (F-464) · **SOURCE-VERIFIED, READ IN FULL**

**Dowling et al. (2020), *Orthop J Sports Med* 8(12), PMID 33354584, [PMC7734513](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734513/).**

21 HS varsity pitchers, 5 fastballs at each of 4 conditions (mound/flat × 60.5/50.5 ft). Quoted at source: *"The order of the throwing conditions was randomized for each pitcher, to eliminate any bias related to testing order."* **Every pitcher his own control. This is a MANIPULATION, not an association.**

| Ball velocity, m/s | 50.5 ft | 60.5 ft |
|---|---|---|
| Mound | 33.9 (1.9) | **34.2 (1.9)** |
| Flat ground | 33.2 (1.9) | **33.3 (1.8)** |

**+0.9 m/s = +2.0 mph at regulation distance.** Arm slot **+3.0° higher on the mound** (P = .02). No effect on elbow varus torque, arm speed or arm rotation.

🚨 **CHECK #1, RUN, FAILED: 34.2 m/s = 76.5 mph.** "High school varsity" means a **76 mph** fastball — nine below our floor. **SAMPLE MISMATCH, directional only.** Whether it survives to 85+ is Dispute #40.

### 3.2 ⭐ The headline number is in the wrong units (F-465) · **DERIVED FROM A SOURCE-VERIFIED PAPER**

Table 2's coefficients should equal the Table 1 cell differences. Differencing them:

| Row | Raw Δ @50.5 | Table 2 | Raw Δ @60.5 | Table 2 | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arm slot, deg | +1.0 | +1.0 | +2.9 | +3.0 | ✅ |
| Arm rotation, deg | −0.5 | −0.5 | −1.4 | −1.4 | ✅ |
| Elbow torque, N·m | +0.3 | +0.3 | −1.0 | −0.9 | ✅ |
| Arm speed, "deg/s" | +30.6 | +5.2 | +80.4 | +14.2 | ❌ **raw ÷ 6 = RPM** |
| Ball speed, "m/s" | +0.70 | +1.6 | +0.90 | +2.0 | ❌ **raw × 2.2369 = MPH** |

Three rows reproduce exactly. The two that don't are exact unit conversions **to the printed digit** (0.70 × 2.23694 = 1.57 → "1.6"; 0.90 × 2.23694 = 2.01 → "2.0"; −38.4 ÷ 6 = −6.4 → "−6.4").

**The printed 95% CIs exclude the paper's own Table 1 differences.** Read as printed, the mound is worth 3.6–4.5 mph. Read correctly, **1.6–2.0 mph.**

And the abstract compounds it: *"...with pitches at 60.5 ft having higher velocity (+0.7 m/s)"* — that **+0.7 is the 60.5-vs-50.5 contrast within the mound condition**, and it is **0.7 mph**, not 0.7 m/s. **A WebSearch summariser this cycle reported it as the mound-over-flat effect in m/s: wrong contrast AND wrong unit, 4.5× off, from a correctly-cited open-access paper.**

> **NEW HAZARD CLASS: THE UNIT-MISLABELLED TABLE.** Journal real, DOI resolves, open access, three of five rows consistent. Every fabrication defence passes.
> **RULE ADDED: when a paper prints both cell means and model contrasts, difference the cells and check.** One minute; it caught a published unit error today. Cf. F-382, F-373, F-375.

### 3.5 ⭐ ~100 joules in, ~5 joules out (F-468) · **DERIVED IN-CYCLE, pure arithmetic**

Rule geometry: rubber top 10 in; from 6 in in front, 1 in/ft for 6 ft. A lead foot `s` ft out sits at `10 − (s − 0.5)` in. **Net extra downhill vs flat: 4.4 in at a 4.9 ft stride → 6.0 in at 6.5 ft.**

| | Free PE (`mgΔh`) |
|---|---|
| 73.6 kg (Dowling's men), 4.4 in | **80.7 J** |
| 73.6 kg, 6.0 in | **110.0 J** |
| 90 kg (an 85+ arm), 4.75 in | **106.5 J** |
| 90 kg, 6.0 in | **134.6 J** |

**Ball KE at 90 mph: 117.4 J.** So **the downhill supplies more energy than the ball ever carries.**

**What the ball keeps:** Dowling's measured gain is **4.40 J** against 80.7–110.0 available → **4.0–5.5%**. A 90 kg arm needs **5.27 J** for +2 mph from 90, against 89.7–134.6 → **3.9–5.9%**. **The two bands coincide at populations 14 mph apart.**

✅ An energy argument **cannot dismiss** the mound. ✅ It **cannot sell** it either — ~95% goes to eccentric absorption, braking and heat.
🚨 **NO DRILL FOLLOWS.** "Fall down the hill" proposes to raise a transfer efficiency **nobody has measured within an athlete**, and F-063 already retired *"get down the mound faster."* Filed **MECHANISM**, deliberately generating no prescription — the F-392 posture.

### 3.6 ⭐ The mound's biggest effect is PLANE (F-469) · **DERIVED against F-151**

Release sits on ~4.0–5.5 in of residual mound. Two routes:
- **Trigonometry** over 16.8 m: **0.35° / 0.39° / 0.48°**
- **F-151's regression** (R² = 0.999, n = 6,110): 1.055 × (4.5/12) = **0.396°**

**They agree. Call it 0.40°.** Via **F-152** (1 SD = 0.5°) that is **0.79 SD** of VAA-above-average. Via **F-151's** exchange rate it is **≈ 4.3 inches of IVB-equivalent.**

⚠️ **Declared: this is a GEOMETRIC IDENTITY, not a discovered effect** (check #4). ⚠️ But one term was **measured**: arm slot **+3.0° on the mound**. Posture moves the same way, so 0.40° is a floor.

### 3.7 A bullpen mound off by an inch moves the pitch an inch (F-470)

Release-height error → **one-for-one** vertical arrival error. 0.5 in → 0.5 in; 2 in → 2 in.

**Scale:** F-309's realistic one-season command gain is **4.52 pts** of zone rate; its catcher-contamination term is **3.69 pts**. A mound discrepancy is **the same order as both** — and unlike catcher assignment it is **constant, unidirectional and free to remove**. Per F-309 this is **BIAS, NOT NOISE**: more bullpens make you *more* confident in a contaminated number.

## 4. THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

**① "Is flat-ground work costing my guys velocity, and should I stop it?"**
*(biomechanist)* On the only randomised evidence, yes — **about 2 mph, at 76 mph.** But that is a statement about **what the radar gun reads during the session**, not about training effect. Nothing anywhere has tested whether flat-ground *training* produces less mound velocity.
**→ SO WHAT I TELL THE PITCHER IS:** *"your flat-ground number is not your number — the floor is worth about two miles an hour."* **THE DRILL IS:** unchanged; keep flat ground for what it's for. **ON VIDEO THE FAILURE LOOKS LIKE:** a coach writing down a flat-ground velo and treating it as a data point in a velocity program. **HOW WE'D KNOW:** we wouldn't yet — it's Dispute #40.

**② "If I move a guy to flat ground for a week, what actually changes about his pitch?"**
*(biomechanist)* **Plane, by about 0.40° — 0.79 SD of VAA, ~4.3 in of IVB-equivalent (F-469).** That is a much bigger deal than the 2 mph for anyone doing pitch design.
**→ SO WHAT I TELL THE PITCHER IS:** *"flat ground tells you about your arm, not about your pitch."* **THE DRILL IS:** grade shape off the mound only. **ON VIDEO/TRACKMAN THE FAILURE LOOKS LIKE:** a four-seamer that "gained ride and flattened out" in a flat-ground session and reverts on the mound — **he didn't change; the floor did.** **HOW WE'D KNOW IT'S WORKING:** you stop generating that false positive. Zero pitches required.

**③ "Can I coach him to use the slope better?"**
*(anatomist)* **No, and this corpus will not let you try.** The mound hands him ~106 J and the ball keeps ~5 J (F-468). The other 95% is eccentric absorption at the lead leg — real, necessary work, not waste to be recovered. **Nobody has ever measured that efficiency within an athlete**, and F-063 already retired *"get down the mound faster."*
**→ SO WHAT I TELL THE PITCHER IS:** nothing. **THE DRILL IS:** none. **This is the F-392 posture, taken deliberately:** promoting a mechanism to a prescription is what cost this corpus stride length and extension.

**④ "Then what do I actually do this week?"**
*(coach)* **Measure your own mounds.** See §5.

## 5. WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO — THE MOUND AUDIT

**Twenty minutes, one level, one tape, one afternoon. It removes a BIAS, not a noise term.**

For the game mound and **every** bullpen and indoor mound in the program, record three numbers:
1. **Height at the front edge of the rubber** above plate level (spec: 10 in).
2. **Drop at 5 ft** in front of the rubber (spec: 4.5 in of fall → 5.5 in above plate).
3. **Drop at 6 ft** (spec: 5.5 in of fall → 4.5 in above plate).

**WHAT IT BUYS.** Any height discrepancy `δ` puts a **fixed `δ`-inch vertical bias** into every command number taken off that mound (F-470). At a 0.5–2 in discrepancy that is **the same order as F-309's entire realistic one-season command gain (4.52 pts)**.

**HOW HE'D KNOW IT WORKED — and this is where the honesty goes.** You will **not** detect this in a bullpen. Against this corpus's σ = 6/8/10 in bracket for within-pitcher vertical location (F-457), separating a 1-inch mean shift needs **hundreds of tracked pitches per condition**. ~200 tracked pitches is the floor for a 2-inch command claim.
**So the audit is not a measurement you validate — it is a bias you eliminate by construction.** The check is that the tape reads the same on both mounds. That is the whole verification, and it is worth having precisely because the alternative — proving it with pitches — is unaffordable.

⚠️ **AND THE LIMIT, STATED:** if he has adapted to a wrong mound, the audit doesn't fix the adaptation, it stops the rehearsal. **Dispute #40b.**

**THE SECOND THING, IF YOU WANT DATA:** run Dowling's protocol on your own roster — mound/flat at 60.5 ft, order randomised within pitcher, 5+ fastballs each, one radar gun. At F-289's within-pitcher velocity SD of 0.8–1.2 mph a 2 mph within-athlete gap is visible in **a handful of pitches per man**. **It would settle Dispute #40 for the population that matters, in one afternoon, and nobody has done it.**

**① BIOMECHANIST → COACH, on F-464.** *"You are about to say 'the mound is worth two miles an hour.' The sample throws **76.5 mph**, and the mound's contribution is an **energy-transfer efficiency** — the quantity an 85+ arm is already best at. **The direction of error is not neutral; it is more likely to shrink than grow.**"*
**PARTIALLY CONCEDED.** The coach gave up the magnitude and kept the direction: **F-468's free PE scales with mass (90 kg gets 106 J where 73.6 kg gets 81), and F-469's plane effect is trigonometry — identical at 76 and 96.** Two of three channels grow or hold. **→ Dispute #40.**

**② ANATOMIST → BIOMECHANIST, on the 4–6% coincidence.** *"You are treating the agreement between the measured efficiency (4.0–5.5%) and the efficiency a 90 kg arm would need for +2 mph (3.9–5.9%) as corroboration. **It is not. Both numbers are `ΔKE / mgΔh` with mass and drop scaling together — it would look identical if the true effect at 85+ were zero.**"*
**CONCEDED IN FULL** and written into F-468 and Dispute #40. **This is the F-274 pattern — two numbers agreeing is not two pieces of evidence** — caught before it was published rather than after.

**③ COACH → BIOMECHANIST, on F-469.** *"You have handed me a 0.40° number and called it a geometric identity, which in this file usually means 'ignore it.' **But I cannot ignore it — it is the reason a flat-ground shape report lies to me.** Either it is actionable or the identity label is doing work it shouldn't."*
**DEFENDED, and the distinction sharpened.** The identity label means **no new pitching fact was discovered** — it forbids the claim "the mound improves your fastball." It does **not** forbid using the identity as a **measurement correction**, which is what F-469's coaching line is. **The recommendation is a warning, not a lever, and it is labelled as one.**

**④ BIOMECHANIST → BOTH, on F-467's null.** *"You are half-quoting a paper nobody read to support the story that height doesn't matter but existence does. **A null at n = 20 with no reported power analysis is a detection floor, and F-463 showed that printed power figures can be off by 8×.** Stop leaning on it."*

**⑤ ANATOMIST → COACH, on the audit.** *"Your one recommendation has **no measurable check** — you say so yourself. This corpus's standing rule is that every recommendation carries a detection table."*
**DEFENDED, and the defence is the interesting part.** The rule exists to stop unfalsifiable *effect* claims. **The audit claims no effect.** It removes a bias **by construction**, the way calibrating a radar gun does. **The detection arithmetic (F-457: hundreds of pitches for 1 inch) is exactly why the tape is the right instrument and the bullpen is the wrong one.** **ACCEPTED by both**, on condition that the report states plainly that this is not a measured win — which §5 does.
