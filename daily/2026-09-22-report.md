## 0. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH RESULT

**F-004 is not a null. It is an uninformative interval that this corpus has been reading as a zero.** At r = 0.07 with n = 33, the 95% confidence interval on the population correlation runs **[−0.280, +0.404]** — an upper bound at which jump height would explain **16.3% of fastball-velocity variance** and rank third in King 2025's own table. The study could not have reported *any* true correlation below **r = 0.344** as significant, and had **~20% power** at r = 0.20 and **~40%** at r = 0.30. Worse, the inference the corpus has been drawing from it is structurally the **mirror image of the error this program exists to prevent**: F-004 is a *between-subject* null, and all four channels it has killed are *within-athlete, constant-mass, acute state changes*. Those are not the same quantity — and the mechanism says they diverge in a specific, predictable direction, because **jump height is body mass divided out of impulse, and body mass is the strongest single correlate of velocity in the same sample (F-001, r = 0.58)**. Within an athlete at constant mass, the division is a constant, and **+2 inches of vertical is +3–4% of CMJ concentric impulse — the r = 0.71 variable (F-003)**. The four channels do not become true. They move from **FALSIFIED to UNMEASURED**, which is a different instruction to a coach.

### 1.1 The confidence interval nobody computed

| | r | R² |
|---|---|---|
| Lower bound | **−0.280** | 7.8% |
| Point estimate | 0.070 | 0.5% |
| **Upper bound** | **+0.404** | **16.3%** |

**Read the top of that interval against the paper's own table.** The same 33 pitchers produced absolute CMJ peak power **r = 0.43** and Wingate absolute peak power **r = 0.44** — the two findings King *endorses*. ⚠️ **Stated honestly:** 0.404 < 0.43, so the interval does not literally contain them. And a formal test of the *difference* between two dependent correlations (Steiger) needs r(jump height, CMJ peak power), which the vault does not hold and today's cycle cannot retrieve. **The claim here is that the two are indistinguishable at this n, not that they are equal.**

**r_crit = 0.344.** Any true correlation below 0.344 — i.e. below **11.8% of variance** — was *guaranteed in advance* to be reported as "NS."

## 2. THE ANATOMY–PHYSIOLOGY PASS — why the null is mechanically expected, and why it does not transfer

### 2.1 Jump height is body mass divided out of impulse

Net vertical impulse in a CMJ: **J = m · v_takeoff**. Jump height: **h = v²/2g → v = √(2gh)**. Therefore

> **J = m · √(2gh)**

Jump height is the *mass-free* half of impulse. F-004's own COACHING line already says this ("a mass-normalized quantity in disguise") — and then draws the opposite conclusion from it.

**Here is the suppression, explicitly.** In the same n = 33 sample, **body mass r = 0.58** (F-001) and **lean mass r = 0.52** (F-002) are the two strongest correlates of velocity. So in a between-subject comparison, jump height *systematically removes the strongest predictor from itself*. A 170 lb pitcher and a 205 lb pitcher with identical 30-inch verticals differ by ~20% in impulse and, per F-001, in expected velocity. **Jump height's between-subject correlation is not measuring a weak relationship; it is measuring a strong one with the signal deliberately divided out.**

### 2.2 Within an athlete, the division is a constant

Across a fall training block, **body mass is approximately constant** (and if anything rises). Hold m fixed and **J ∝ √h**:

| Vertical gain | Impulse gain at constant mass |
|---|---|
| 24 → 26 in | **+4.1%** |
| 28 → 30 in | +3.5% |
| 30 → 32 in | +3.3% |
| 32 → 34 in | +3.1% |
| 24 → 26 in **and** 200 → 205 lb | **+6.7%** |

**So the correct sentence is not "he moved a variable with r = 0.07."** It is: *he moved his CMJ concentric impulse 3–4%, and concentric impulse is the highest jump-test correlate of fastball velocity in the literature (r = 0.71, F-003).*

The **only** case in which "r = 0.07" is the right frame is the athlete who added two inches **by losing body mass** — which is precisely the between-subject situation F-004 measured.

⚠️ **This is a correction to F-004's COACHING line, not to F-004's number.** The number stands. The instruction built on it does not.

### 3.1 The second pillar is underpowered too

F-359 (Yanai group 2018, PMC6028199, n = 18, source-verified 09-15) reported **no squat-jump variable moved** after 117 max-effort pitches. Audit it:

- **Minimum detectable effect, paired t, n = 18, 80% power: d = 0.660.**
- Largest observed jump effect: **d = 0.25** (height). Approximate power at d = 0.25: **~15%**.
- The study was built to detect a *large* effect and reported the absence of a large effect.

⚠️ **And a scale problem the corpus has not flagged.** F-359's velocity decline is reported as **d = 0.32, P = 0.026** and **d = 0.38, P = 0.001**. At n = 18 paired, d = 0.38 carries only ~31% power — those p-values are not reachable from those d's on the same SD. The near-certain explanation is that **the velocity d uses the between-subject SD (±6.2 km/h) while the significance comes from a within-subject comparison**, whereas the jump d's are pre/post pairs. **The velocity and jump effect sizes in F-359 are not on a comparable scale, so the contrast between them cannot establish a physiological dissociation.** The within-subject SDs are not in the vault and could not be retrieved today.

**Net: both pillars of "jump doesn't matter for pitchers" are underpowered nulls, one of them additionally confounded by a normalisation that deletes the signal.**

## 5. THE COACH'S PASS — four pointed questions

**Q1 (Coach → Biomechanist): "You just told me F-004 is basically uninformative. So do I train the vertical jump or not?"**
**No — and the reason has not changed, only the honesty of it.** You do not train jump *height*, because height is the mass-free half of impulse and you want the mass in. You train **absolute impulse**: get bigger and get more forceful, and let jump height be a *readout* of whether impulse moved at constant mass. What changed today is the sentence you say when he adds two inches.
> **TELL THE PITCHER:** "Two inches on your vertical at the same body weight is about four percent more impulse into the ground. That's the number that actually tracks velocity across pitchers. What nobody has measured — not us, not anybody — is what four percent of impulse is worth in mph."
> **THE DRILL:** unchanged. Absolute-load lower-body work plus the braking/short-contact emphasis F-006 supports.
> **ON VIDEO THE FAILURE LOOKS LIKE:** a pitcher whose vertical goes up while the scale goes down. That is the one case where "r = 0.07" is the right frame, and it is a warning, not a win.
> **HOW WE KNOW IT'S WORKING:** see Q3.

**Q2 (Coach → Anatomist): "Then are the four things we threw away back on the table?"**
**They are not back. They moved from FALSIFIED to UNMEASURED, and that is a different instruction.** Re-warm-up, mid-outing fatigue, sleep, pre-game cooling: the corpus said the transfer step was *broken*; it should have said the transfer step was *unmeasured*. **Broken means stop. Unmeasured means nobody has looked, and the channel is open and cheap to test.** No magnitude is licensed for any of the four today, and this cycle produces none.

**Q3 (Coach → Biomechanist): "Fine — how would I find out, here, this fall?"**
Pair every CMJ test session with a radar-logged bullpen and regress **within-athlete deviations** (each man's session value minus his own season mean), not raw values across the room. That deletes body mass and deletes between-athlete talent in one step.
**Sample size, 80% power, α = .05:**

| Within-athlete r to detect | Paired observations |
|---|---|
| 0.40 | **47** |
| 0.30 | **85** |
| 0.20 | **194** |

**With 12 arms that is ~7 paired sessions each for r = 0.30 — one fall block.** ⚠️ **These are floors.** Repeated measures inside athletes are clustered; ignoring that inflates significance, so budget **~1.5–2×** or fit a mixed model with athlete as a random effect. And note the noise: session-to-session fastball SD is bracketed at **0.8–1.2 mph** (F-289), so a one-session comparison is worthless — this is a *block* measurement, like every other one in this corpus.

**Q4 (Coach → both): "Can you just give me mph per pound of impulse?"**
**No, and it is worth knowing exactly why.** **King 2025 and Sakurai 2024 both report correlations and neither reports the regression slope or the velocity SD.** Without either, r cannot be converted into mph by anybody — this corpus included. **Anyone who hands you an mph-per-unit-power figure has manufactured it.** F-004's original COACHING line was right to forbid the conversion; it was wrong about the reason. The conversion is **unavailable**, not **zero**.
