## 1. THE RESEARCH

### 1.1 What the topic actually contains — three separate claims wearing one word

"Breathing work for pitchers" is sold as one thing. It is three, with three different mechanisms, three different literatures and three different verdicts:

| Channel | Mechanism | Target outcome | Literature exists? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Ventilatory capacity** | Inspiratory muscle training (IMT/RMT) delays inspiratory muscle fatigue and attenuates the respiratory metaboreflex | Endurance, within-outing retention | Yes, large, meta-analysed |
| **B — Mechanical / intra-abdominal pressure** | Breath state sets trunk stiffness and therefore force transmission | Velocity | One manipulated study, not in throwers |
| **C — Autonomic / arousal** | Slow-paced breathing raises cardiac vagal activity, lowers state anxiety | Command | Yes, but wrong populations and wrong outcomes |

**They do not stand or fall together, and the industry sells them as a bundle.** Separating them is most of this cycle's work.

### 1.2 CHANNEL A — THE VENTILATORY CHANNEL HAS NO MECHANICAL PURCHASE ON A PITCH

**The anatomy-physiology agent attacks the mechanism first, before any number.**

IMT's performance mechanism is not "stronger lungs." It is two specific things: (i) delaying fatigue of the diaphragm and external intercostals during **sustained** high ventilation, and (ii) attenuating the **respiratory metaboreflex** — the sympathetically-mediated limb vasoconstriction that fatiguing respiratory muscles trigger, which steals perfusion from locomotor muscle. Both require ventilation held near maximum for **minutes**.

Now price the task. From **F-132** (ESTABLISHED, MECHANISM, already in the vault): the pitch is **phosphagen-dominant**; the high-velocity portion from front-foot strike to release lasts **~0.05–0.15 s**; the whole delivery is **~1.5–2 s**; the interval between pitches is **~15–25 s**; between innings, minutes. A pitcher never sustains the ventilation that generates a metaboreflex, and his inspiratory muscles are never the limiting tissue.

→ **F-672. Prediction: zero velocity effect from IMT in this population, on mechanism grounds, before reading the literature.**

⚠️ **SELF-FLAG, AND IT IS THE CYCLE'S STRUCTURAL CONTRIBUTION.** This is the **third consecutive cycle** in which a registered finding was used as a prior over an unread literature (F-406 on 10-10, F-004 on 10-11, **F-132 today**) — **and all three discounted.** INDEX.md already flags this: *"a prior that always discounts is not a prior, it is a policy."* The prediction audit is now the **eighth queued audit** and today is the strongest argument yet for running it. **I am registering the prediction and the indictment of the prediction method in the same cycle, because I cannot tell from inside which one is right.** Logged against Dispute #68.

**What the literature says (snippet-only, all of it):**

- IMT with **linear-workload devices** in athletes: pooled sports performance **SMD = 0.64 [0.35, 0.92], p < 0.0001**; maximal inspiratory pressure **MD = −29.42 cmH₂O [−35.65, −23.20], p < 0.001**, independent of sport modality. ([Systematic review, Phys Ther Sport / ScienceDirect S1466853X18301652](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466853X18301652))
- The **same body of work shows no VO₂max effect** across 13 studies that measured it on an incremental test.
- PowerBreathe-specific meta-analyses report MIP and FVC gains with **"disparate"** performance effect sizes (individual studies d = 1.81, d = 0.89) and are **device-manufacturer-linked**. ([PMC9855123](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9855123/), [PMC8297193](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8297193/))

**THE DECISIVE OBSERVATION IS WHAT THE OUTCOMES ARE.** Every pooled performance effect in channel A is a **fixed-distance time trial** — 400 m, 800 m, swim splits. **MIP moves reliably; the thing MIP is supposed to buy moves only in continuous endurance tasks.**

→ **F-673. No respiratory muscle training trial, in any population, has ever had a throwing outcome.** Searched; the gap is total. The nearest randomised evidence in overhead athletes is **core/trunk** training (10 RCTs, improved standing-throw velocity, no benefit for step or jump throws), which is a different intervention entirely and is already adjacent to this corpus's strength-training holdings.

**MARKER VS LEVER:** channel A's SMD = 0.64 is a **lever** — these are randomised interventions with controls, Check #2 passes cleanly. It fails **Check #3 decisively**: the number does not measure what the pitching claim says it measures.

### 1.3 CHANNEL A′ — CO₂ TOLERANCE AND APNEA WORK MOVE THE ONE ENERGY SYSTEM THE PITCH DOES NOT USE

- 2022 meta-analysis, **7 studies, n = 126**: apnea training **"might increase peak blood lactate concentration,"** no clear aerobic benefit, *"further evidence required."* ([Front Physiol, PMC9551563](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9551563/))
- 2025 review: breath-hold training **"does not seem to improve haematological variables nor aerobic performance in apnoea novices"** and is **"questionable in apnoea-trained individuals."** ([Bouten 2025, Exp Physiol](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/EP091905))

Cross-reference **F-132**: the pitch is alactic and **produces little lactate**. The single outcome apnea training has any claim on is **peak blood lactate / glycolytic capacity** — the energy system the pitch does not use.

→ **F-674.** This is **structurally identical to F-660** (heat acclimation improves everything except peak anaerobic power, the one thing a pitcher sells). **Second instance in two days of an adaptation whose demonstrated outcome class and the pitcher's outcome class are disjoint.** That is now a pattern worth naming, not a coincidence.

**And note the shape of the apnea result: breath-hold capacity improves, performance does not.** Test-specific adaptation — **the identical pattern to F-571** (every mobility intervention in existence aimed at ROM and measured ROM). Third domain in which this corpus has caught it.

### 1.4 ⭐ CHANNEL A″ — BOLT IS DEBUNKED IN PRECISELY THIS POPULATION

The industry's measurement instrument for "CO₂ tolerance" is the **BOLT score** (Body Oxygen Level Test, a ~30 s comfortable breath-hold after a normal exhale), with thresholds of **<20 s = "disproportionate breathlessness"** and **>30 s = adequate**.

**n = 49 elite speedskaters, BOLT against a Wingate test and a cardiopulmonary exercise test: no significant relationship with exercise performance.** Age, somatic indices and training experience were also non-significant. The authors state BOLT **"has not yet been scientifically validated in sports settings,"** that its link to baroreceptor and chemoreceptor function **"remains to be established,"** and that it **"requires developing an appropriate methodology to ensure the validity of the assessment."** ([PMC11406178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11406178/))

→ **F-675. VERDICT: DEBUNKED for performance prediction in trained athletes; the 20 s and 30 s cutoffs are MARKETING.** They trace to the test's commercial promoter ([Oxygen Advantage](https://oxygenadvantage.com/blogs/science/measure-bolt)), not to any validation study. A second commercial page calls BOLT **"the simplest reliable marker of how efficiently you breathe"** and cites nothing.

⚠️ **Note the direction of the sample:** this is a **HIGHLY-TRAINED** sample, which is the rare case where restriction of range works *for* us rather than against us — the null was found in exactly the population that matters, not inferred down from a mixed one.

### 1.5 ⭐⭐ CHANNEL B — THE ONLY MANIPULATED DATA IN THE TOPIC CONTRADICTS THE INDUSTRY'S CUE

**The cue, as the industry states it.** A mental-training site prescribes: *inhale as you receive the sign and set your grip, hold briefly as you come set, and **exhale through the delivery***, which it claims *"stabilizes the core, reduces muscle tension in the shoulders."* It cites **no data**. ([mindandmuscle.ai](https://mindandmuscle.ai/blog/mental-training/breathing-exercises-for-baseball-players)) Variants of this cue are near-universal in pitching instruction, usually bundled with "draw the belly button in" or "hollow."

**The only study that manipulated breath state against rotational output.** Kimura et al. (2026), *IJSPT* 21(3):262–271, **crossover randomised, n = 15 healthy young adult males**, seated isometric trunk rotation, catheter-type IAP sensor, custom trunk-rotation dynamometer:

| Condition | Intra-abdominal pressure | Peak trunk rotation torque |
|---|---|---|
| **Spontaneous rotation** | **96.7 ± 31.4 mmHg** | **68.7 ± 20.2 Nm** |
| **Abdominal drawing-in** | **34.9 ± 15.9 mmHg** | **49.1 ± 17.7 Nm** |
| Difference | **−63.9%**, p < 0.01, **d = 2.48** | **−28.5%**, p < 0.01, **d = 1.03** |

([PubMed 41777431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41777431/); [IJSPT 156439](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/156439-the-effects-of-the-abdominal-drawing-in-maneuver-on-intra-abdominal-pressure-and-torque-during-trunk-rotation) — **PDF NOT OPENED, egress-blocked; numbers are snippet-level from two independent summaries that agreed**)

The authors attribute the loss to drawing-in reducing inspiratory volume and abdominal circumference, which **removes the mechanical basis for pressure generation**. Converging evidence: lumbar stiffness rises with respiratory effort and is **greatest at maximum expiration**, while during **tidal breathing** the IAP rise is **"not sufficient to improve spinal stiffness"** ([J Appl Physiol 2002](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00939.2002)); and in lifting, IAP rises **"to a greater extent with breath holding."**

→ **F-670.** **The cue and the data point opposite ways at the one instant that matters.** "Exhale through the delivery" moves the trunk toward the low-pressure, low-stiffness state at front-foot strike — the ~50–80 ms braking event where the lead leg's force has to transmit through the trunk (cf. F-642, F-645). The only manipulated trunk-rotation data says the abdominal state that cue produces costs **28.5% of rotational torque**.

**THE BIOMECHANIST'S THREE OBJECTIONS, AND THEY ARE SERIOUS:**

1. **POPULATION.** n = 15 healthy young adult males. Not pitchers, not 85+, training status unstated. **SAMPLE MISMATCH — DIRECTIONAL ONLY.**
2. **CONSTRUCT.** **SEATED ISOMETRIC** trunk rotation against a dynamometer. A pitch is standing, ballistic, unresisted, with trunk angular velocity on the order of **900–1,200 °/s**. IAP's job in the measured task is transmitting torque to an **external resistance**; a thrown ball offers almost none. **This is the most severe construct swap the corpus has logged since F-564.** It does not make the result wrong; it makes it a hypothesis about pitching rather than a measurement of it.
3. **DETECTION FLOOR.** → **F-671.** Paired design, n = 15, α = 0.05 two-tailed, 80% power: **d_min = 2.80/√15 = 0.72.** The **IAP** result (d = 2.48) clears its own floor by **3.4×** and is robust. The **torque** result (d = 1.03) clears it by **1.42×**. By F-439/F-616 logic that is the result that fails to replicate first, and **torque is the half of the paper that matters to us.**

**MARKER VS LEVER:** this is genuinely a **MANIPULATED** variable with a randomised crossover order — one of the few in the whole corpus. **CAUSALITY: INTERVENTION.** The weakness is entirely in population and construct, not in design. That is unusual here and worth saying plainly.

### 1.6 ⭐⭐ CHANNEL C — AND THE REFRAME THAT ORGANISES THE WHOLE TOPIC

**Cornell et al. (2017), *JSCR*: 16 professional starting pitchers, 682 innings (381 home / 301 away). In-game heart rate = 84.8 ± 3.9% of age-predicted HRmax.** Home starts ran higher in the **first and second innings** than all others; first-inning home **87.3 ± 3.6%** vs away **85.8 ± 3.8%**. The authors read the overall figure as evidence pitching is **"mainly an anaerobic task."** ([JSCR 2017;31(1), Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/fulltext/10.1519/jsc.0000000000001465~in-game-heart-rate-responses-among-professional-baseball))

**Put that next to F-132 and the two statements cannot both be metabolic.** A task that is phosphagen-dominant, produces little lactate, works for 0.05–0.15 s at a time and rests 15–25 s between efforts **cannot** generate a genuine 85% HRmax metabolic demand.

→ **F-676. THEREFORE IN-GAME HEART RATE IN A PITCHER IS AN AROUSAL SIGNAL, NOT A METABOLIC ONE** — and the home/away first-inning gap, which is a **context** difference with no metabolic content whatever, is a small piece of direct evidence for that reading.

**The consequence reorganises the topic.** If in-game HR is sympathetic rather than metabolic, then:

- **Channel A is aimed at a limiter that does not exist in this task.** (Consistent with F-672, F-673.)
- **Channel C is the only channel with a live mechanism** — and its target outcome is **command**, via arousal, not velocity via ventilation.
- **The entire respiratory-training literature is built for channel A.**

⚠️ **CAVEAT THE BIOMECHANIST INSISTS ON.** **Age-predicted** HRmax (220 − age) carries a standard error of estimate of roughly **±10–12 bpm** in individuals. At a true HRmax of 195, a ±11 bpm error moves "84.8%" to anywhere from ~80% to ~90%. **The 84.8% figure is not a measurement of relative intensity; it is a measurement divided by a guess.** The qualitative dissociation survives — nothing moves 85% HRmax to a level an alactic task would explain — but **do not quote the number as precise.**

**What channel C's literature actually contains (all snippet-only):**

- **Acute, 75 athletes:** cardiac vagal activity **significantly higher in all slow-paced-breathing conditions vs control** (RMSSD, LF power). **Single lab session. Outcome is HRV, not performance.** ([Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10484-023-09605-2))
- **4 weeks slow-paced breathing during recovery from weightlifting, young male baseball players from a regional academy:** improved **resting HRV**; co-primary outcome **anaerobic performance**. ([Science & Sports 2026, S0765159726000559](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0765159726000559)) **⚠️ SAMPLE MISMATCH — "young," academy-level, not 85+. And the outcomes are resting HRV and anaerobic power, i.e. channel A′ outcomes bolted to a channel C intervention.**
- **HRV biofeedback, 18 Taiwanese university baseball players, 20 min/day × 10 days, randomised:** **cognitive anxiety significantly reduced** vs no change in controls. The performance outcome was **BATTING**, and no performance effect was confirmable from the snippets. ([J Funct Morphol Kinesiol 2025;10(1):65, PMC11843864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11843864/))
- **Musicians, randomised:** slow-breathing groups improved HF and LF/HF during performance anticipation, **and adding biofeedback to slow breathing produced no differential result** — i.e. the device adds nothing to the breathing. ([PMC3464298](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3464298/))

→ **F-677.** **Channel C's literature is about the right mechanism, in the wrong populations, measuring the wrong outcomes.** Not one study in it has a pitching outcome of any kind.

→ **F-678. THE DETECTION FLOOR ON THE ONE BASEBALL RCT.** 18 participants, two arms → **9 per arm**. α = 0.05 two-tailed, 80% power: **d_min = 2.80 × √(2/9) = 1.32.** That trial could only have detected an effect of **d ≥ 1.32**. Its anxiety finding is therefore either enormous or a false positive, and **it is uninformative about magnitude either way.** The seventh application of F-439's logic in the corpus.

## 3. TRANSLATE — THE COACH INTERROGATES THE OTHER TWO

### Q1 (coach → anatomy-physiology): "I have guys who gas out in the fifth. You're telling me breathing training does nothing for that?"

**Answer.** Not quite — I'm telling you the fifth-inning problem is not ventilatory. F-132: the pitch is alactic; PCr resynthesis is ~half-restored in 20–30 s and near-complete in 3–5 min, which is exactly the rhythm the game gives you. Your inspiratory muscles are not the tissue that fails. What fails is more likely neuromuscular and mechanical. **F-676 is the useful part: his HR reading 85% HRmax is telling you about arousal, not fuel.** Treating it as a conditioning signal is a category error, and it's the same error F-132 already killed for distance running.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** nothing about his lungs. **The drill is** no drill — I don't add anything. **On video the failure looks like** mechanics degrading in the fifth with breathing that looks unchanged from the first. **Here's how we know:** if it were ventilatory, a between-inning breathing protocol would restore him; this corpus's own between-inning work says the budget is 120 s and there are better uses for it.

---

### Q2 (coach → biomechanist): "Then is the 'exhale through the delivery' cue actively hurting my guys? Because I say it."

**Answer.** **Unknown, and that's the honest answer — but the one piece of manipulated data points against you.** Kimura 2026 (n = 15, randomised crossover): drawing-in dropped IAP 63.9% and rotational torque 28.5% (d = 1.03). Expiration and a hollowed abdomen are the low-pressure state; a held breath and an expanded abdominal wall are the high-pressure state. **Three caveats you must carry:** the sample is 15 untrained-status young men; the task is seated isometric rotation, not a pitch; and the torque effect sits only 1.42× above its own detection floor (F-671).

**Here is the line I will defend.** The cue is **UNPROVEN in both directions** and I am not going to replace one unproven cue with its opposite — "hold your breath through the delivery" is exactly the kind of instruction that produces a stiff, slow pitcher, and nobody has measured it either. **What I will do is stop saying the cue at all,** because I have been giving a mechanical instruction with zero supporting data and now have one manipulated study suggesting it runs the wrong way.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** nothing — **I retire the cue.** If he asks, "breathe however you breathe; don't suck your stomach in." **The drill is** removal, not addition. **On video the failure looks like** a visible abdominal hollow or a rib flare at the set position, plus an audible exhale that starts *before* front-foot strike rather than after release. **Here's how we know it's working:** we don't, at a single-athlete scale — F-681 says 106 pitchers for 1 mph. **This is a cue retirement on grounds of having no evidence to begin with, not a treatment with a measurable payoff.** That is the honest framing and I will not dress it up.

---

### Q3 (coach → both): "My freshman with the first-inning problem — 87% HRmax at home in the first, per that study. Is the between-inning breathing thing worth the 120 seconds?"

**Answer.** This is the one place the topic has a live mechanism. Channel C is real: slow-paced breathing at ~6 breaths/min reliably raises cardiac vagal activity acutely in athletes (n = 75, all conditions vs control), and the one randomised baseball trial reduced cognitive anxiety. **But read F-678 before you believe a magnitude: that trial was 9 per arm, d_min = 1.32.** And every outcome in the whole channel is HRV or self-report; **not one is a pitch.**

The cost, though, is genuinely near zero — unlike channel A's device work (F-680). It needs no equipment, fits inside a budget you already have, and the musician trial says the **biofeedback device adds nothing over the breathing itself**, so don't buy anything.

> **So what I tell the pitcher is:** *"Between innings, before you pick up the ball: six breaths, four seconds in through the nose, six out. That's it. It's for the first inning and for innings after you've been scored on."* **The drill is** six breaths at ~0.1 Hz, in the dugout, before the warm-up throws — not instead of them. **On video the failure looks like** the pitcher walking off the mound and straight into the dugout rail talking to someone, i.e. never actually doing it; and in-game, a tempo that accelerates after a hit. **Here's how we know it's working:** ⚠️ **at the individual level, we don't.** F-374/F-681: a 10-pp zone-rate change needs ~392 pitches per condition, and ~1,330 once you correct for within-bullpen clustering at ρ = 0.1. **What is measurable, if you have an HR strap, is the proximal step only** — did his between-inning HR recovery actually change, yes or no. That is a process check, not an outcome check, and I will label it as such rather than let it masquerade as evidence the pitch got better.
