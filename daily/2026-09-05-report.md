## 2. The model

Built to be **continuous with F-251**, using its exact constants: m = 0.145 kg, r = 0.0366 m, A = 4.2078e-3 m², Cd = 0.33.

**Air density** (not assumed — computed):
- ρ = p_d/(R_d·T) + p_v/(R_v·T), with R_d = 287.058, R_v = 461.495 J/(kg·K)
- Saturation vapour pressure, Buck: p_sat = 610.94·exp(17.625·T_c/(T_c+243.04)) Pa
- Station pressure from ISA: p = p₀·(1 − 2.25577e-5·h)^5.25588

**Continuity check.** At 0 ft / 68 °F / 50% RH the model returns **ρ = 1.1988 kg/m³**. F-251 used **1.196**. They agree to 0.2%. Incidentally this identifies an unstated assumption in F-251: its "sea level, 20 °C" figure of 1.196 corresponds to *humid* air; **dry** air at those conditions is 1.2041.

**Trajectory:** full RK4 integration in the vertical plane, dt = 1e-4 s, with gravity, quadratic drag, and a Magnus force perpendicular to velocity (F_M = ½·ρ·A·C_L·v²).

**Induced vertical break (IVB)** is computed the way Statcast conceptually defines it — the Magnus trajectory minus the *same trajectory with spin removed* — so gravity and drag cancel out of the difference.

**The C_L honesty problem, and how it is handled.** No lift-coefficient model could be verified this cycle. So C_L is **not asserted** — it is *calibrated* so that the sea-level case reproduces a league-typical 16.0 in IVB for a 95 mph four-seam, and then **held fixed while only the air changes.** This makes the *absolute* inches an assumption and the ***ratios* the result.** §4 shows the ratios are invariant to that choice.

Script: `/tmp/pr/air.py`, `/tmp/pr/check.py`, as run this cycle.

## 4. Robustness — is this just the force law restated?

**Partly, and that must be said plainly.** F_Magnus ∝ ρ, so deflection ∝ ρ *to first order* is expected, not discovered. The corpus's own check #4 (is a high R² a geometric identity?) applies to me here, and I am flagging it against myself.

**But there is a real second-order question the force law does not answer**, and it is the reason the simulation was worth running. Thinner air does two competing things to movement:
- **less Magnus force per unit time** → less break
- **less drag → the ball keeps more speed → shorter flight** → *less time to deflect* → less break again
- but **less drag also means higher v throughout, and F_M ∝ v²** → *more* break

I expected the net to come out **below** the density ratio. It does not. **The corrections cancel to under 0.1%.**

| Sea-level calibration | C_L | AFA IVB as % of SL | ρ ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.0 in | 0.1293 | 79.09% | 79.06% |
| 16.0 in | 0.1725 | 79.10% | 79.06% |
| 20.0 in | 0.2158 | 79.11% | 79.06% |

| Cd | AFA IVB as % of SL | flight time SL → AFA |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 79.10% | 404.4 → 400.8 ms |
| 0.33 | 79.10% | 406.1 → 402.1 ms |
| 0.36 | 79.10% | 407.9 → 403.5 ms |

**The ratio is invariant to both free parameters, across their entire plausible ranges.** That is what licenses using the density ratio directly, with no simulation, as a field tool: *your movement scales with the density ratio, full stop.* The absolute inches remain calibration-dependent and are not claimed.

## 5. The finding that actually matters: **altitude is nearly all downside**

The two effects of thin air point in opposite directions for the pitcher, and they are wildly unequal in size.

**Sea level → Air Force Academy (6,600 ft), 95 mph four-seam:**

| Quantity | Change | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **IVB** | 16.00 → 12.66 in | **−3.34 in (−20.9%)** |
| Flight time | 406.1 → 402.1 ms | −4.0 ms (−0.98%) |
| Plate speed | 86.8 → 88.6 mph | +1.76 mph |

The "thin air makes my fastball play faster" consolation is **4 milliseconds**. Set that against F-252: the hitter's commit window sits at 130–175 ms, and the *uncertainty band on that single input* is 45 ms — **eleven times larger than the entire altitude effect on the clock.** The 4 ms is not merely small, it is unmeasurable against the noise in our own model of the hitter.

Meanwhile the movement loss is a fifth of the pitch's shape.

**So: at altitude a pitcher trades 21% of his movement for a timing gain that disappears inside the error bars of the hitter's clock.** There is no compensation. The fastball plays flatter, not faster.

## 6. The perception/detection split — and why he must not chase this mid-game

Two computations, pointing opposite ways, and the coaching depends entirely on holding both.

**(a) It is invisible pitch-to-pitch.** F-171 gives a within-pitcher elevation release-angle SD of ±1.1°, and 30 cm of plate movement per degree. That is **13.0 in of vertical scatter at 1 SD from his own command noise alone.**

| Environmental shift | IVB loss | as a fraction of his own 1 SD scatter |
|---|---|---|
| Air Force Academy vs sea level | 3.34 in | **0.26 SD** |
| Coors Field vs sea level | 2.93 in | 0.23 SD |
| Oxford February vs June | 1.99 in | 0.15 SD |

**Even the most extreme venue in college baseball moves his fastball about a quarter of one standard deviation of his own pitch-to-pitch noise.** He cannot feel this. Any single pitch that "didn't have its usual life" in Colorado is overwhelmingly likely to be ordinary variance he would also have thrown in Oxford.

**(b) It is trivially visible on the TrackMan aggregate.** Two-sample, α = .05, 80% power, pitches of that type per group:

|---|---|---|---|

## 9. The three agents

### 9.1 ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY

*"Nothing in this cycle is about the athlete, and that is the point I want on the record."*

Air density does not touch the pitcher. It does not change force production, motor unit recruitment, elastic energy return, or adaptation. **A pitcher at 6,600 ft produces the same pitch; the air does something different with it.** This is the cleanest MECHANISM finding the corpus has registered in weeks precisely because the human is not in the causal path.

One physiological caveat that is *not* in this model, and I want it flagged rather than smuggled in: **altitude does affect the athlete**, just not through aerodynamics — reduced arterial oxygen saturation, elevated ventilatory demand, and disturbed sleep in the first 48 h at 5,000+ ft. For a *pitcher* — an athlete doing ~100 high-intent efforts with 20+ s of rest between them, an almost purely alkaline-phosphagen task — the aerobic penalty should be close to irrelevant within an outing. **But recovery between outings on a Colorado road trip is a different question, and this cycle did not address it and has no data on it.** Do not let §3 be read as "altitude only affects the ball." It is "altitude only affects the *pitch*."

### 9.2 BIOMECHANIST

*"I accept the arithmetic and I want three limits stated."*

**One.** The result is a **model**, not a measurement. There is no Statcast validation in this cycle — none could be fetched. §8's Nathan snippet agrees, but a snippet is not a validation. The honest grade is ESTABLISHED-as-physics, **UNVERIFIED-as-empirical-magnitude.**

**Two.** The model is 2-D and treats C_L as constant. Real C_L depends on spin factor S = rω/v, and S rises as the ball decelerates. In thinner air the ball decelerates *less*, so S at the plate is slightly lower, which would push real movement slightly *below* my numbers. I estimate this as sub-0.1 in and it does not touch the ratio — but it is an unmodelled term and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

**Three, and this is the one I would actually fight about.** The corpus's headline framing — "IVB scales with air density" — is **very close to a geometric identity restated**, and this program has been burned by exactly that (the R² = .945 incident). F_M ∝ ρ is the definition of the force. **I will sign off on it only with the second-order result attached**, because *that* is the non-trivial part: the drag coupling cancels to <0.1%, which is not obvious and which I would have guessed wrong. Strip that out and this finding is a freshman physics problem wearing a lab coat.

Finally: I want the **detection numbers in §6(b) read as the floor, not the answer.** n ≈ 6 assumes the *only* thing that changed between the two samples is the air. On a road trip it never is — different mound, different ball, different rest, different catcher, adrenaline. The six-pitch figure is what the statistics permit under an assumption the road trip violates.

### 9.3 PITCHING COACH

*"This is the rare one I can use on Monday without lying to anybody."*

Most of what this program produces is a marker I am told not to cue. This is different: **it is not a lever at all, and it is not pretending to be.** No pitcher is going to change the air. So the entire value is in **expectation-setting and in stopping bad in-game decisions** — which is a real category and one I deal with every road trip.

Here is the concrete problem it solves. We go to a high-elevation park. My guy's slider doesn't finish in the first inning. He is 20 years old. He concludes his slider is broken, and he starts **manipulating it** — trying to get more on it, wrapping it, overthrowing it. By the fourth inning he has genuinely broken it, and the damage follows him home to sea level where the air was never the problem. **I have watched this exact sequence.** I have never before had a number to put against it.

Now I do: *the air took a fifth of it, you cannot get that back by trying harder, and the pitch is fine.*

What I push back on: **§6(a) is the important half and §6(b) is the dangerous half.** If I hand a college pitcher a table showing six pitches can detect the effect, he will start reading TrackMan between innings and pitching to the number. The 0.26-SD figure is the one that belongs in his head. The six-pitch figure belongs in mine, after the game.

## 11. What goes to the athlete this week

**Situation:** an 85+ arm on a high-elevation road trip, or the first hot afternoon after a cold-weather February.

**What I tell the pitcher** — in the pre-series meeting, once:
> "The air here is about four-fifths as thick as at home. Everything you throw is going to move about four-fifths as much — that's the air, it's not you, and it's happening to their pitchers too. Don't try to get it back. If you try to make the slider bite, you'll wrap it, and you'll take that home with you. Throw it exactly like you throw it in Oxford and let it be smaller."

**The drill:** there isn't one, and that is the point. **The intervention is a prohibition, not an addition.** The only active change is a **usage** change, decided by me and not by him mid-game: at 80% density, the pitch that loses the most absolute movement is the biggest-breaking one, so the curveball is the pitch to de-emphasise and the pitch whose value degrades least is the one that never depended on Magnus — a cutter, a firm slider, or anything living off velocity and location. **Velocity separation is unaffected by air density** — it is a timing quantity, and per F-254 it is the *linear* channel and the one that survives to the commit instant. **Thin air degrades the movement channel and leaves the velocity channel intact.** That is the arsenal adjustment, and it follows from the corpus's own prior work rather than from anything new.

**On video, the failure looks like:** a longer, more deliberate arm action on the breaking ball; visible extra effort on a pitch that used to be thrown casually; and — the tell I actually watch for — **the breaking ball's release point drifting from the fastball's**, because a pitcher trying to add break almost always adds it by getting around the ball, which moves the hand.

**How we know it worked** — and both halves get stated:
- **Measurable (cheap):** on TrackMan, **6–8 fastballs of that type** settle whether the movement loss occurred, per §6(b). Compare road IVB against his own season baseline, *not* against a staff average.
- **Measurable (the one that matters):** his IVB and horizontal break should come back to baseline **on the first outing back at sea level.** If they do, the air did it and he is fine. **If they don't, he changed the pitch on the road and brought it home** — which is the actual failure this whole recommendation exists to prevent, and it is detectable within one start.
- **Not measurable:** whether any of this bought outs. Per F-257's logic, the outcome question needs thousands of swings and a road trip supplies dozens. **Do not go looking for it.**
