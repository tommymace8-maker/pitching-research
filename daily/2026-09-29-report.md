## 1. ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY — does the mechanism survive the tissue?

The glove arm is not a decoration. It is roughly 5% of body mass held at a long radius from the rotational axis, and pulling it in reduces the moment of inertia of the trunk-plus-arms system. Angular momentum being conserved across the acceleration phase, that raises angular velocity for free. **This is real physics and it is Barfield's own stated rationale.**

Order of magnitude, computed here: for a 90 kg pitcher, moving a ~4 kg arm from a 0.35 m radius to 0.15 m changes that segment's contribution by roughly `4 × (0.35² − 0.15²) ≈ 0.4 kg·m²`, against a trunk-plus-arms inertia on the order of 1–2 kg·m². **Not negligible — 20–40% of the system inertia — but nowhere near a mile an hour on its face, and the timing matters more than the magnitude.** The inertia reduction only buys anything if it happens *during* trunk acceleration. Pull early and you have simply changed the starting configuration; pull late and the ball is gone.

**Against the mechanism, three tissue-level objections:**
1. **The glove arm is not free.** It is attached to a trunk that is simultaneously being decelerated by the lead leg. The "pull" a coach sees may be a *consequence* of trunk deceleration rather than a cause of trunk acceleration. This becomes Dispute #47.
2. **A violent glove pull loads the same tissue you are trying to protect.** Forceful scapular retraction and horizontal adduction on the lead side at the instant of maximum external rotation is the classic hyperangulation setup on the throwing side. *One line, then move on:* the corpus's own injury constraint is unchanged and this is not the topic's mission.
3. **Conscious placement of a limb that is moving at over 900 deg/s is not obviously possible.** By MER the glove-arm humerus is in the ballistic phase. What a pitcher can actually set is the *starting* configuration and the *intent*, not the instantaneous angle.

**Grade: the moment-of-inertia mechanism is ESTABLISHED as physics and UNMEASURED as a pitching effect.**

## 2. BIOMECHANIST — the data, and two failures at source

### 2a. 🚨 The only glove-arm study in baseball has no radar gun in it

**Barfield, Anz, Andrews & Oliver (2018)**, Orthop J Sports Med 6(7), PMID 30023405, **PMCID PMC6047254 — READ IN FULL.**

n = 33 right-handed **youth** pitchers, **age 13.6 ± 2.0 y**, 169.4 ± 14.3 cm, 63.5 ± 13.0 kg. Electromagnetic tracking. Three fastballs, **only the fastest analysed.**

**Check #1 — what was the sample's actual velocity?** **Unanswerable, because the study never recorded it.** A full-text search returns no mph, no m/s ball speed, no radar. Every occurrence of "ball velocity" is in the introduction or discussion citing *other* papers. The outcome variables are joint forces normalised to body mass and the **segment** velocity magnitudes of pelvis, torso, humerus and forearm.

**Check #3 — does the number measure what the sentence says it measures?** **No.** The conclusion reads *"more advantageous to performance."* "Performance" means **humeral angular velocity**. The paraphrase circulating in the field — *"for maximum ball velocity to be achieved… a pitcher needs to maintain an active glove arm"* — **inserts an outcome that was never recorded.** → **F-508.**

### 2b. And nothing in it survives its own multiplicity

**Check #2 and the asterisk audit.** Tables 2–4 are **3 event pairings × 12 dependent variables × 2 glove-arm variables = 72 Spearman tests.** No alpha stated; no correction applied. **14 flagged at P ≤ .05; 3.6 expected by chance.**

| Threshold | Critical \|r\| at n = 33 | Survivors |
|---|---|---|
| Uncorrected α = .05 | 0.344 | 14 / 72 |
| **Bonferroni α = .00069** | **0.5604** | **0 / 72** |
| BH-FDR, q = .05 | — | **3 / 72** |

🚨 **The largest correlation in the paper is 0.52. The Bonferroni critical value is 0.5604. Nothing survives.** BH retains three, knife-edge: all three carry the rounded `P = .002` against a rank-3 threshold of `.002083`. **At a true p of .0021 the table empties.** And all three survivors are a kinetic, a segment velocity and a joint angle — **none is a pitching outcome.** → **F-509.**

### 2c. ⭐ The F-050 correction — a 23× "dispute" that was never a disagreement

**Dowling et al. (2024)**, Orthop J Sports Med 12(8), PMID 39157018, **PMCID PMC11329978 — READ IN FULL.** This is the exact source of F-050's "+1.05 mph per degree."

| Group | n | Knee ext. | Ball velocity | | Varus torque |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HS-Low | 17 | −7 ± 5° | 31.2 ± 1.8 m/s | **69.8 mph** | 56.3 ± 12.2 N·m |
| HS-High | 16 | 18 ± 6° | 34.1 ± 2.6 m/s | **76.3 mph** | 64.2 ± 14.7 N·m |
| PRO-Low | 16 | 1 ± 8° | 39.3 ± 1.3 m/s | **87.9 mph** | 95.4 ± 13.3 N·m |
| PRO-High | 18 | 33 ± 7° | 39.8 ± 1.1 m/s | **89.0 mph** | 85.3 ± 10.7 N·m |

**Within-level slopes, computed here because the paper did not compute them:**

- Within HS: 6.49 mph / 25° = **0.260 mph/deg**
- **Within PRO: 1.12 mph / 32° = 0.035 mph/deg**
- *Published, pooled across both levels: **1.05 mph/deg** (R² = 0.22)*

🚨 **The published slope is 30× the within-professional slope**, fitted across a **bimodal 69.8–89.0 mph** sample. It is measuring the competition-level gap.

**And the resolution.** F-050 registered a 23× conflict against **Solomito 2024, n = 121 collegiate, single level, 0.045 mph/deg**, and blamed marker conventions. **Dowling within-professional = 0.035. Solomito = 0.045. Ratio 1.29×. The two studies agree.** → **F-510, Known Correction #9.**

**Two further defects underneath.** (i) The **elbow-torque sign reverses between levels** — within-PRO torque *fell* 95.4 → 85.3 N·m as knee extension rose (−0.32 N·m/deg) while within-HS it *rose* (+0.32); the paper reports the interaction at **P = .005** and then publishes a pooled slope carrying the high-school sign. **F-050's price clause is false for an 85+ arm** → **F-511.** (ii) The four groups total **67, not 100** — the middle ±0.5 SD was deleted, an extreme-groups inflation stacked on top of the pooling, disclosed in neither abstract → **F-512.**

### 2d. The newest front-side paper, and a p-value that does not check out

**PMCID PMC13431036 (2025), contralateral trunk tilt in HS pitchers — READ IN FULL.** n = 19, 2D video, **ball velocity 75.2 mph — ten mph under the floor.** Headline **r = 0.47, p = 0.004.**

⚠️ **At n = 19, r = 0.47 gives t = 2.195 and p = 0.042 — not 0.004. A p of 0.004 at n = 19 requires \|r\| = 0.628.** At n = 57 (3 pitches × 19 pitchers) it gives 0.0002, also not 0.004, and pseudo-replication besides. **Neither reading reproduces the printed p.** To the authors' credit the paper states its own a priori requirement of **42 participants** and reports on **19**, which makes its whole null table uninterpretable under F-439. → **F-514.**

## 5. THE COACH'S FOUR QUESTIONS

**Q1 — "So is the glove arm worth anything, or am I wasting reps?"**
*Biomechanist:* Unknown, and that is the honest answer. The only study measured 13-year-olds and never turned on a radar gun (F-508), and nothing in it survives its own multiplicity (F-509).
**→ What I tell the pitcher:** "The front side keeps you organised. Nobody has shown it's worth velocity, so we're not spending a block on it." **The drill:** flat-ground at 70% with one constraint — glove finishes over the front knee, lead elbow down at your side. **On video the failure looks like:** glove elbow above shoulder height at foot plant, or the glove crossing the midline toward the throwing side before release. **How we know it's working:** we don't measure velocity, we measure tidiness — the **SD of glove-elbow height at plant** across 25 pitches, and even that is a look, not a result (F-186's ~200 pitches, and F-482 item 7 says the ICC it assumes is unpublished).

**Q2 — "I've been coaching the block for velocity. How much is it actually worth?"**
*Biomechanist:* **0.035 mph per degree**, between pitchers, at the professional level. The 32° spread between the most and least extended professionals in the study is worth **about one mile an hour** (F-510).
**→ What I tell the pitcher:** "Your block is about staying over the front side and repeating, not about finding velocity. The velocity in it is about a mile an hour across the entire range of professional baseball." **The drill:** none new — stop running one. **How we know it's working:** we don't chase it.

**Q3 — "What about the elbow? I've always told them the block costs them something."**
*Anatomy-physiology:* At the professional level the data points the other way — torque fell as extension rose (F-511). *One line, then move on.*
**→ What I tell the pitcher:** nothing. **What I stop telling the staff:** the price clause on F-050. It was fitted on high schoolers.

**Q4 — "Then what IS worth doing with the front side this week?"**
*Coach, answering himself:* The one live idea is that **consistency** of the front side, not its position, is what tracks command (F-515) — and it is a blocked, unverifiable industry post with no sample size. **We do not act on it.** What we do is the cue in Q1, graded UNPROVEN and named as such to the pitcher, and **we run the experiment in F-518 item 1 in the fall** — twelve 85+ arms, a radar gun, two glove-arm conditions, one afternoon. **It would be the first manipulation of a glove-arm position in the history of the sport, and we own the equipment.**

## 6. CROSS-EXAMINATION

**BIOMECHANIST → PITCHING-COACH: "the glove arm may be trunk rotation measured twice."** Every association in F-508 is with a trunk or pelvis quantity, and the glove arm is rigidly attached to the trunk. **Coach concedes the passive case, defends the handle case:** the trunk is not directly instructable and the glove arm is. **→ Dispute #47, OPEN.** Settled by the F-518 experiment — *provided it records trunk rotation as well as velocity*, since both positions predict the same null otherwise.

**BIOMECHANIST → THIS CORPUS: "F-515 is Dispute #36 happening for the fourth time."** A topic with no velocity effect producing a variance-flavoured command claim is exactly the move Dispute #33 and #36 flagged. **Anatomy-physiology concedes on procedure:** the mechanism story is cheap and would have been equally tellable in reverse. **Registered as a lead with no coaching action attached. → Dispute #48, OPEN and parked.**

**ANATOMY-PHYSIOLOGY → BIOMECHANIST: "your Bonferroni is too harsh."** Seventy-two tests on two predictor variables are not independent; Bonferroni assumes they are, and a correlated family means the true critical value sits below 0.5604. **Biomechanist concedes the principle, defends the conclusion:** the BH-FDR result is the answer to exactly that objection, and it retains **three of 72 on a knife-edge**, none of which is a pitching outcome. **The conclusion does not depend on which correction you prefer.**

**PITCHING-COACH → BIOMECHANIST: "you just killed F-050 using group means, which is the thing you complain about."** Fair, and **conceded in the finding itself** — F-510 carries the caveat in its NUMBERS block. The within-level slopes are between-pitcher estimates computed from an extreme-groups split, not within-athlete slopes. **What survives regardless: the published 1.05 was fitted across a 19 mph level gap, and that is visible in the paper's own Table 2.**
